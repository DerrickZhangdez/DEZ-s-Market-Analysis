## Executive Summary

The bond market is once again running the equity market. A Federal Reserve that has pivoted from cutting to *hiking* — 25bp on September 16 to 3.75%–4.00%, the first increase since 2023 — has pushed the 10-year Treasury above 5.11% and the 5-year through 5% for the first time since 2007, and equities are discounting duration accordingly. Wednesday saw broad-based selling led by small caps (Russell 2000 −1.77%) and long-duration tech (Nasdaq −1.13%), with the S&P 500 off 0.75% to 7,706.03. Thursday's pre-market extends the move, with Nasdaq-100 futures down roughly 0.8–0.9% as rate anxiety outweighs both Middle East diplomacy and the still-intact AI capex cycle.

The day's two crosscurrents are Xi Jinping's state visit to Washington (Sept 23–25, his first since 2015) and an oil market that remains hostage to Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb disruption risk. Neither has resolved. Our read: the index-level story is now about the discount rate, not earnings — and that argues for rotating toward cash-generative financial infrastructure trading at single- and low-double-digit multiples rather than paying 30x for growth that a 5% risk-free rate punishes.

## Macro & Monetary Environment

The regime has changed, and it is worth stating plainly: **the Fed is tightening again.**

- **September 16 FOMC:** Unanimous (12–0) 25bp hike to **3.75%–4.00%** — the first hike in over three years. The statement framed the move as supporting "a more timely return" to the 2% target.
- **Dot plot:** 16 of 18 officials see at least one more 25bp hike this year; four pencil in two more. Futures price roughly **4.2% by December 2026** and **~4.7% by September 2027**.
- **Inflation forecasts revised up:** headline PCE **3.7%** for 2026 (from 3.6%), core PCE **3.4%** (from 3.3%). Notably, 2027 forecasts were left unchanged (2.3% headline / 2.5% core) — the Committee is treating the overshoot as a level problem, not a re-anchoring problem. That distinction is the entire bull case for duration, and it is fragile.
- **Curve:** With 2s at 4.85% and 10s at 5.11%, the curve has bear-steepened. This is the textbook worst configuration for unprofitable growth, highly levered balance sheets, and small caps — which is exactly what Wednesday's tape showed.

**What to watch this week:** Costco (COST) after the close tonight is the cleanest read on the high-income consumer; Darden (DRI) this morning on the middle-income one. Beyond that, every Treasury auction is now a market event — a weak long-end auction into this yield backdrop would do more damage to equities than any single earnings print.

**Strategist's note:** A 5% risk-free rate mathematically compresses the fair multiple on every long-duration asset. The market spent 2024–2025 learning to pay up for AI terminal value; it is now being asked to discount that terminal value at a rate 150bp higher than it assumed. Not every AI thesis breaks — but every AI *valuation* gets marked.

## Geopolitical Factors

**Middle East / energy.** The 2026 conflict cycle produced one of the sharpest oil shocks in recent memory, with Brent surging toward **$120/bbl** before easing on anticipated policy responses and reserve releases. The current state is best described as *uneven disruption* rather than either open war or durable peace: renewed attacks around the Gulf and the **Bab el-Mandeb** chokepoint continue to impede normalization of flows, and the IEA has cut both supply and demand projections for the remainder of the year. Academic work on this cycle estimates that a geopolitically driven 1% decline in oil production pushes prices up ~11.5% on average, and that oil volatility during high-geopolitical-risk regimes runs roughly **twice** its calm-period level. Thursday's tape carried some hope of diplomatic progress — that hope is not yet a position.

**US–China.** President Xi Jinping is in Washington September 23–25 on his first US state visit since 2015. Markets are treating this as a low-probability/high-impact event: a tariff or export-control de-escalation would be a genuine upside catalyst for semis and industrials, while a breakdown would hit the same names. Position sizing, not prediction, is the right response.

**Inflation transmission.** The critical linkage today is that the energy shock and the Fed's hiking cycle are the *same trade*. Any renewed spike in crude feeds directly into the headline PCE path the Fed just revised upward, which pulls forward the next hike, which lifts the 10-year, which compresses equity multiples. Oil is no longer just an energy-sector story; it is the macro variable.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: Fintech, Payments & Financial Data

*(Rotation: day-of-year 267 mod 6 = 3)*

This is an unusually interesting moment for the sector, because the rate move that is hurting most of the market **bifurcates** fintech rather than uniformly damaging it. Understanding which side of that split a name sits on is the whole game today.

**The winners from higher rates and higher volatility:**

Exchanges and market-infrastructure operators are in an outright boom. Through Q2 2026, US options daily volume averaged nearly **71 million contracts**, well ahead of last year's record pace (~61 million ADV in 2025, itself the sixth consecutive record year). The drivers — geopolitical uncertainty, violently shifting rate expectations, AI-theme repositioning, and a reopened IPO window — are precisely the conditions currently unsettling the equity indices. Volatility is the raw input to this business model.

The numbers are striking. **Cboe** posted record Q2 net revenue of **$732 million, +25% YoY**, with adjusted diluted EPS up **45% to $3.60**; index options ADV rose **32% to 6.2 million contracts**, and the derivatives franchise set a quarterly record with net revenue +30%. Q1 was arguably better: +29% net revenue, +48% adjusted EPS. **Nasdaq** reported $1.5 billion net revenue, +15% YoY, with market services +11% and index options revenue more than doubling YoY for a fourth consecutive quarter. Every surge in trading translates into transaction fees, clearing revenue, and — crucially — *higher demand for market data*, which is the annuity underneath the cyclical volume.

**The losers from higher rates:**

Consumer lenders face rising funding costs and a credit cycle that has not yet been tested at a 4% policy rate. Balance-sheet-light BNPL and subprime models are the most exposed. Any fintech that relies on cheap wholesale funding is now running a shrinking spread.

**The bifurcation nobody is pricing correctly — financial data.**

The most interesting dislocation in the sector is in financial data and analytics, where an *AI-disruption narrative* has collided with a rate-driven de-rating. **FactSet** has fallen roughly **60%** from its 2025 high near $450, trading down toward the $185–$225 range on fears that AI will commoditize the financial terminal. The market has compressed a >95%-retention subscription business from 25–30x historical earnings to roughly **15x forward**. Meanwhile the underlying business printed **7% organic revenue growth** with what management describes as AI-driven subscription momentum, shifting from seat-based to usage-based pricing. The bear case is real and should not be dismissed — but it is a *terminal-value* argument being applied to a business with contractual near-term revenue, and the price already embeds a great deal of it.

This is the same disease that produced the broader "SaaSpocalypse" (below): a single narrative — AI disintermediates software — applied indiscriminately across enterprise software, vertical software, information services, and online classifieds, without discriminating between businesses that AI genuinely threatens and businesses that own proprietary data AI needs.

**Payments: unglamorous, cash-generative, and cheap.**

The payments complex has de-rated to levels that look disconnected from cash generation. **WEX** trades at roughly **7.1x–9.7x forward earnings** and **5.2x EV/EBITDA** while guiding to ~24% YoY earnings growth and printing Q2 adjusted EPS +35.4%. **Shift4** trades near **8.2x forward** with a **0.49 PEG** — though we would note honestly that it *cut* full-year guidance after its Q2 double-beat, which is why the multiple is what it is, and short interest sits above 20%. **ACI Worldwide** raised 2026 guidance with real-time payments growing over 20%.

**Structural tailwinds worth underwriting:** The GENIUS Act (enacted July 2025) established the first comprehensive US regulatory framework for payment stablecoin issuers, requiring 100% liquid reserves and full AML programs — which converts stablecoins from a regulatory risk into an addressable enterprise product. FedNow and RTP continue scaling. Cross-border settlement is compressing from days to minutes. 2026 is the year the sector shifts from experimentation to production.

**Spotlight conclusion:** Own the volatility beneficiaries and the mispriced data/payments infrastructure. Avoid the funding-cost-exposed consumer lenders except where growth and underwriting quality are demonstrably exceptional.

### Artificial Intelligence

The capex cycle remains intact in nominal terms but is increasingly **financed** rather than self-funded — a distinction that matters enormously at a 5% risk-free rate.

The marquee structure: **Nvidia is backing up to $105 billion in financing** for an OpenAI data center at the PORTS-Pike Technology Campus in Pike County, Ohio, supporting an initial **4.25 GW** with an option for a further **3.75 GW**. SB Energy builds and manages under a 20-year lease to OpenAI; Nvidia supplies the compute; capacity phases in from **2028**. This followed earlier talks of a backstop as large as $250 billion. Aggregate hyperscaler capex (Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta, Amazon, OpenAI) could reach **$527 billion in 2026**.

The analytical point investors keep skipping: when the chip vendor finances the customer's purchase of its own chips, revenue quality deteriorates even as revenue grows. Reported figures earlier this year showed at least one OpenAI/Nvidia data center arrangement landing **$145 billion below** initial reporting — which markets read, correctly, as a signal about how much of the demand is genuinely independent. At a 3% cost of capital this structure is clever financial engineering; at 5% it is a materially riskier proposition. We are not calling a top on AI infrastructure. We are saying the financing structure deserves the scrutiny the market has so far declined to apply.

### Technology (Broad)

Rate-driven multiple compression is doing the work. The Nasdaq-100's −0.87% Wednesday and ~−0.8% pre-market Thursday are not earnings-driven; they are discount-rate-driven. Analyst activity Thursday reflects heavy dispersion rather than directional conviction: **Wells Fargo raised Okta's price target from $200 to $230**; rating changes crossed on CoreWeave, Etsy, Dropbox, Synopsys, Nebius, Dollar General, Welltower, and VICI Properties. **Graphic Packaging (GPK) rose ~3.5%** on a JPMorgan upgrade to Overweight explicitly citing valuation after a 19% post-Q2 decline — a small but telling data point that the market is again willing to pay for cheapness.

### Hardware & Semiconductors

Semis remain the market's highest-beta expression of the AI trade, and the September 2 **Broadcom** print was the sector's warning shot: the company *reiterated rather than raised* 2026 guidance and delivered a softer-than-expected AI chip outlook, sending shares down as much as **15%** intraday and dragging Intel, AMD, and Micron with it. After a rally that carried many chip names to record highs, the market has become unwilling to pay for "in line."

**Micron** is the sector's key upcoming catalyst, reporting **September 30**. The setup is extraordinary and precarious in equal measure: the stock is up roughly **228% YTD** (and ~696% over twelve months) to around $935, having crossed **$1 trillion** in market capitalization for the first time last month. Memory pricing has been the single best AI derivative of 2026. A stock that has moved that far requires not merely a beat but a materially raised outlook; anything less is likely to be sold, as Broadcom demonstrated. We would not initiate ahead of that print.

### Software & SaaS

The damage here has been historic. Per JP Morgan, 2026 has delivered **the largest non-recessionary software drawdown in over 30 years**, with roughly **$2 trillion** of market capitalization erased and software's S&P 500 weighting falling from **12% to 8.4%**. Median public SaaS EV/Revenue sits at **6–7x**, back to 2015–2016 levels and far below the 18.6x peak of late 2021.

Two forces compounded: the AI-disintermediation narrative, and the duration repricing that a hiking Fed imposes on long-dated cash flows. The dispersion beneath the median is where the opportunity lives — data infrastructure still commands premium multiples on AI-driven demand, while vertical software and information services have been sold indiscriminately. A 6x EV/Revenue median across an industry with 90%+ gross margins and high retention is not a normal state of affairs. It is either a correct repricing of terminal value or one of the better entry points of the decade, and the honest answer is that it will be different for each company.

## Risk Factors

**1. The Fed hikes more than the market has priced — SEVERE.** Sixteen of eighteen officials see at least one further hike this year and four see two. If core PCE fails to decelerate from 3.4%, the 2027 forecasts that markets are relying on (2.3%/2.5%) lose credibility, and the entire "level problem, not re-anchoring problem" framework collapses. Every long-duration asset reprices again.

**2. Long-end disorder above 5% — SEVERE.** The 10-year at 5.11% and the 5-year through 5% for the first time since 2007 have already produced bear-steepening. A poorly received long-end auction, or forced selling from levered rate positions, would transmit directly into equities. Wednesday's Russell 2000 at −1.77% is the leading indicator to watch, as small caps carry the most floating-rate debt.

**3. Energy re-escalation feeding the inflation path — HIGH.** Brent reached toward $120 earlier in this cycle, and with Bab el-Mandeb and Hormuz disruption unresolved, oil volatility runs roughly double its calm-regime level. Because energy now feeds directly into the Fed reaction function, an oil spike is simultaneously an inflation shock *and* a rate shock — the two channels compound rather than offset.

**4. AI capex quality and circular financing — HIGH.** Vendor-financed demand (Nvidia backstopping up to $105B for OpenAI's Ohio build) is materially more fragile at a 5% risk-free rate than at 3%, and the $145B gap between reported and actual deal size on an earlier arrangement suggests the market is already re-examining how much AI demand is genuinely independent. Broadcom's 15% single-day decline on *merely reiterated* guidance shows how little cushion these valuations carry.

**5. Micron's September 30 print as a sector-wide event — MODERATE-TO-HIGH.** A name up ~228% YTD that recently crossed $1T in market cap needs a raise, not a beat. Given memory's status as the purest AI derivative of 2026, a disappointment would likely be transmitted across the entire semiconductor complex rather than contained.

**6. US–China summit outcome — MODERATE (two-sided).** Xi's visit concludes tomorrow. A tariff or export-control de-escalation is a genuine upside catalyst for semis and industrials; a visible breakdown is a downside shock to the same names.

## Bottom Line

This is a discount-rate market, not an earnings market: with the Fed hiking into 4%, the 10-year above 5.11%, and the 5-year through 5% for the first time since 2007, the multiple compression hitting long-duration tech is arithmetic rather than sentiment, and it is not finished. The constructive response is not to abandon equities but to rotate toward businesses whose cash flows are near-dated, whose revenue is contractual, and — best of all — whose economics are *long* the volatility damaging everything else, which is why today's picks concentrate in exchanges, payments infrastructure, and mispriced financial data rather than in the AI complex. We would be patient on semiconductors ahead of Micron's September 30 print, skeptical of vendor-financed AI demand, and willing to buy the indiscriminate selling in information services where a terminal-value narrative has been applied to businesses with contracted near-term revenue.
