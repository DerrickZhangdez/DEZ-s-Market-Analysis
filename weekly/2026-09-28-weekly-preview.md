# Weekly Market Preview — Week of Monday, September 28 to Friday, October 2, 2026
**Published:** Sunday, September 27, 2026 | Overnight futures open

---

## The Week in One Paragraph
The tape enters the final week of Q3 caught between a resilient equity complex and a bond market that is flashing warning lights. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq just booked their best week since early August (+1.2% and +2.1% respectively), yet they did so *against* a Treasury market where the 10-year yield ripped to a fresh 19-year high near 5.17–5.23%. That divergence — stocks up, long rates up hard — is the dominant story, and it will not resolve quietly. The catalysts are stacked: a five-day macro gauntlet running from Monday's Consumer Confidence and JOLTS through Wednesday's PCE/GDP and capping with **Friday's September jobs report**, a parade of Fed speakers recalibrating the market to a hawkish Fed (funds at 3.75–4.00% after September's 12-0 hike, with roughly 75% odds priced for *another* hike on October 28), and a light-but-pointed earnings slate led by **Micron (Wed)** and **Costco (Thu)**. Layer on Brent above $104 as US–Iran negotiations over the Strait of Hormuz stay tense, a VIX curve building a hump into the November midterms, and the October 1 federal fiscal-year funding deadline, and you have a week where liquidity reprices off the *prints*, not the speeches. Base case: moderately constructive, but the rope is short — a hot NFP or another leg higher in yields is the fastest path to a down week.

## This Week's Critical Calendar

### Federal Reserve Watch
- **FOMC meeting this week? NO.** The next decision is **October 27–28, 2026** (announcement Wed Oct 28, 2:00 PM ET; presser 2:30 PM ET). It is a *non-SEP* meeting — no dot plot — so it confirms trajectory rather than resetting it.
- **Current policy rate:** target range **3.75%–4.00%**, following the September 15–16 meeting's unanimous (12-0) 25 bp hike.
- **Implied odds:** Markets and the sell side (BofA) peg the probability of *another* 25 bp hike on October 28 near **~75%**. This is a tightening-bias Fed, and every data point this week is filtered through "does this raise or lower the odds of the October hike?"
- **Fed speakers:** Expect a heavy speaking schedule ("a Fed-speaker parade") across the week. With the October decision live and the SEP absent, listen for how officials frame the yield surge, the labor market, and their reaction function to Friday's jobs number. Treat individual headlines as noise around the data; the prints, not the podiums, will set the rate path.

## Geopolitical Risk Monitor
Ranked by near-term market impact:

- **Middle East / Strait of Hormuz — ELEVATED | Direct energy-price transmission.** Iran's FM floated reopening Hormuz to commercial traffic within seven days conditional on the US lifting blockades; President Trump rejected the ceasefire terms, and throughput remains constrained (~10–15 vessels/day vs. a ~130+ pre-war baseline). This is the single most tradable geopolitical variable this week — it moves Brent, which moves equity futures.
- **US federal funding deadline (Oct 1) — MEDIUM/RISING | Data + sentiment risk.** A funding lapse would delay economic data and pressure risk sentiment at exactly the moment the market needs clean labor prints.
- **US–China trade — MEDIUM | Slow-burn.** The October 2025 truce is holding; targeted restrictions signal future divergence but are calibrated to avoid immediate damage. Watch for any escalation headlines tied to tech/semis export policy.
- **Russia–Ukraine — MEDIUM | Episodic.** Strike-and-counterstrike intensity remains near war-highs; the market impact is mostly via energy and periodic risk-off spikes rather than a persistent bid.
- **November 3 midterms — BUILDING | Volatility term-structure.** Not this week's story, but the VIX futures curve already shows a hump into early November — a reason hedges are getting more expensive by the day.

## Sector Themes to Watch

### AI & Technology
The clearest divergence in the market: **semis are winning, software is not.** Over the last ~20 sessions semiconductor ETFs are up ~5.9% while software ETFs are down ~3.9%. The narrative supports it — AI cloud price hikes and long-term memory agreements are providing fundamental support to hardware, while software/SaaS grapples with an "AI disruption" overhang. Micron's Tuesday print is the week's cleanest AI-infrastructure read-through.

### Hardware & Semiconductors
**Micron (MU) reports Tuesday after the close** — the marquee semi event. The debate is memory pricing (DRAM/HBM) and whether AI-driven demand offsets softness in industrial, auto, and comms end markets. Industry commentary remains "supply-constrained" on AI infrastructure with "extremely robust" hyperscaler capex; any confirmation of tight HBM supply and firm pricing is bullish for the whole memory and AI-hardware complex.

### Software & SaaS
The soft spot. Cloud and cybersecurity ETFs lagged into the weekend (cloud −1.3%, cyber −2.2% on the week). With rates at 19-year highs, long-duration software multiples are under the most valuation pressure of any tech sub-group. Expect continued rotation *out of* unprofitable/high-multiple SaaS and *into* cash-generative semis until the yield story turns.

### Energy & Commodities
Oil is the swing factor. Brent ~$104–106, WTI ~$93, both bid on Hormuz risk. Note the tension with fundamentals: the EIA's September STEO sees Brent averaging ~$90 in 2H26, and J.P. Morgan models Brent at ~$80 in Q4 → ~$78 year-end. **The geopolitical premium is fighting a soft supply/demand backdrop** — headline-driven, two-way risk. Energy equities get a tactical tailwind while the premium holds.

### Financials & Rates
This is where the whole week hinges. The **10-year at ~5.17% (19-year high)** and the ~1 percentage-point rise year-to-date is the gravitational force on equity valuations. Higher-for-longer helps bank net interest margins but pressures credit-sensitive borrowers and every long-duration equity. The yield curve's message — long rates rising on a hawkish Fed and heavy supply — is the market's chief anxiety. If yields add another 5–15 bp/day as they did last week, equities struggle regardless of earnings.

## Positioning & Strategy
**Respect the bond market, lean into the leadership.** The path of least resistance for equities is *sideways-to-up* only if the 10-year stops climbing. Until yields stabilize, favor the market's proven leadership — cash-generative semiconductors and AI-hardware — over long-duration, unprofitable software that de-rates fastest as discount rates rise. The Micron print early in the week is a tone-setter for the entire AI-infrastructure trade.

**Barbell the risk.** Pair the semis leadership with selective defensives and energy. Energy equities carry a tactical tailwind while the Hormuz premium keeps Brent above $100, but size it as a *headline* trade given the soft fundamental oil backdrop into Q4. Defensives (staples like GIS) offer ballast if the data disappoints — but recognize they fight the yield headwind, so treat them as rotation vehicles, not core longs, until rates cool.

**Hedge into the gauntlet.** With VIX near 15 and the curve building a midterm hump, protection is cheap relative to the event risk stacked into Friday's NFP and the ongoing yield surge. Consider low-cost index put spreads or VIX-call structures targeting the payrolls print and the run-up to the October 28 FOMC. The asymmetric risk this week is a hot jobs number that cements the October hike and sends the 10-year decisively through 5.25%.

## Bottom Line
**Watch the 10-year, trade the jobs report.** Equities can grind higher only if Treasury yields stop rising; the single most important call for the week is that **Friday's September payrolls** is the fulcrum — a hot number pushes the 10-year through ~5.25%, cements the ~75% odds of an October 28 hike, and puts the recent rally at risk, while an in-line-to-soft number lets stocks extend. Own the semis leadership, keep defensives and energy as ballast, and carry cheap hedges into the print.
