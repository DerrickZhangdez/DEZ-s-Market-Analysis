## The Week in One Paragraph

Markets enter the week bruised and on edge. Last week delivered the sharpest tech-led drawdown since the summer: the S&P 500 fell 1.82% on Friday to 7,580.07, the Nasdaq slid 2.86% (its worst day since June 5), and more than $1 trillion of Big Tech market value evaporated as investors finally pushed back on the pace of AI capital spending. Alphabet logged its worst single-day drop on record and Intel cratered ~29% on a grim guide. Into this fragile tape lands a brutal three-headed catalyst week: **(1) the two biggest earnings prints of the season — Apple and Palantir Monday after the close, then AMD, Qualcomm, Arm and a wave of others; (2) the U.S. midterm elections Tuesday, November 3; and (3) the October jobs report Friday, November 6.** The macro regime is hawkish and unusual: under Chair Kevin Warsh the Fed held at 3.75–4.00% in a contentious 10–2 vote (the dissents favored a *hike*), and futures now price roughly a 70% chance of a **rate increase** at the December 9 meeting — a tightening bias, not an easing one. Layered on top is a genuine geopolitical shock: an ongoing Iran–U.S. conflict has driven Brent toward $115–118 and WTI above $100, with tanker attacks near the Strait of Hormuz sustaining a fat war-risk premium. The biggest risk this week is a volatility feedback loop — a soft Apple/AMD guide or a contested, slow-to-resolve election count colliding with already-broken technical support. The biggest opportunity is that extreme short-term pessimism, hawkish-but-stable policy, and a seasonally strong post-midterm historical pattern could set up a sharp relief rally if the earnings and election hurdles clear cleanly.

## This Week's Critical Calendar

### Federal Reserve Watch

- **FOMC meeting this week? NO.** The Fed met October 28–29 and held the target range at **3.75–4.00%**. The next decision is **Wednesday, December 9** (two-day meeting Dec 8–9). FOMC minutes from the October meeting are due **November 18**.
- **The October decision was hawkish and split 10–2** — notably, both dissents (reported as Gov. Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack) favored a **quarter-point hike**, not a cut. This is a Fed leaning *against* further easing.
- **Current rate | implied December odds:** Target range **3.75–4.00%**. Market pricing (per futures cited in coverage) shows roughly a **70–72% probability of a 25 bp hike on December 9** — an unusual tightening bias driven by sticky inflation and the oil shock. Verify against the live CME FedWatch tool before trading the front end.
- **Fed speakers to watch (schedule varies by source — confirm on the Fed calendar):**
  - **Monday:** Chicago Fed's **Goolsbee** (non-voter) — typically dovish; watch for pushback on the hike narrative.
  - **Thursday:** Cleveland's **Hammack** (voter, dissented hawkish), Richmond's **Barkin** (non-voter), Dallas's **Logan** (voter) — Logan has publicly argued for *at least two more* 25 bp hikes. Hawkish chorus risk.
  - **Friday:** Vice Chair for Supervision **Bowman** on supervision.
  - **What to listen for:** Any crack in the hawkish bloc, or conversely confirmation that a December hike is close to a done deal. With the AI trade wobbling, a hawkish surprise is a double negative for long-duration tech.

## Sector Themes to Watch

### AI & Technology
The entire week is a referendum on AI spending discipline. After $1T+ was erased last week (Alphabet's record drop central), the market wants evidence that hyperscaler/accelerator demand justifies the capex. **Apple (Mon)** and **AMD (Tue)** are the fulcrums; **Palantir (Mon)** tests whether premium-valuation AI software can still command its multiple. Watch guidance language on 2027 capex plans above all.

### Hardware & Semiconductors
Chips led the decline (Intel ~-29%, AMD ~-11% Friday, Nvidia -4%+). AMD's MI350/MI450 roadmap and data-center GPU commentary are the key read-through into Nvidia's later-month print. Memory pricing, server supply chain (SMCI), and any U.S.–China export-control headlines are swing factors. Relative strength in tech/communications is the weakest of any sector — this group needs a catalyst to stop the bleeding.

### Software & SaaS
Palantir and Shopify headline. The debate: does AI *disrupt* seat-based SaaS, or do these names monetize AI as an accelerant? With rates near 5% on the 10-year, long-duration software multiples are vulnerable to any yield back-up. Expect dispersion — winners and losers, not a single trade.

### Energy & Commodities
The standout sector by macro tailwind. With Brent pushing toward $118 on the Iran war premium and OPEC+ holding output, integrated and E&P names have a live, this-week catalyst. Watch for a continued value-rotation bid into energy as a geopolitical hedge. Copper and gold saw sharp moves last week — monitor for risk-off/haven flows.

### Financials & Rates
Big-bank earnings are mostly mid-month, so this week financials trade on rates. The 10-year near ~4.93% (down ~36 bp in October, its first monthly decline in eight months) and a hawkish December setup create a push-pull: a steeper curve helps net interest income, but recession-risk credit concerns cap enthusiasm. Credit spreads bear watching if the equity selloff resumes.

## Positioning & Strategy

**Respect the tape, but don't chase the panic.** The technical picture is damaged and the event calendar is dense, so the base case is *elevated, two-way volatility* rather than a clean trend. Into Monday's Apple/Palantir prints and Tuesday night's AMD + election double-header, carrying outsized directional risk is unattractive. Keep gross exposure moderate, raise the quality bar on new longs, and let the earnings/election fog clear before adding aggressively. The asymmetry favors patience: if the hurdles clear, there is room for a sharp relief rally given washed-out positioning and the strong historical post-midterm seasonality (the S&P has been higher six months after all 19 prior midterms since 1950).

**Lean into a barbell.** On one side, use the energy complex (E&P value, integrateds) as both a geopolitical hedge and a rare sector with a positive macro catalyst — the Iran premium and OPEC+ discipline are tailwinds, and energy is uncorrelated with the AI-capex worry crushing tech. On the other side, keep a core in quality mega-cap tech that has been indiscriminately sold (Alphabet-type names at compressed multiples), accumulating on weakness rather than at the open. Favor value semis (QCOM) over the most AI-capex-dependent GPU names until guidance proves the spending thesis.

**Hedges worth considering.** With the VIX off its lows but term structure not yet in deep backwardation, defined-risk downside hedges (put spreads into Friday's jobs number and the election) are reasonably priced for the event density. Watch credit spreads as the real tell — if IG/HY spreads widen alongside equities, de-risk further. The single biggest portfolio threat is a *contested election count + soft AMD guide* combination; size positions so that scenario is survivable.

## Bottom Line

**This is a prove-it week, and AMD's Tuesday-night guidance is the hinge.** The market has pre-emptively punished the AI trade and is bracing for a hawkish Fed, an oil shock, and an uncertain election. If Apple steadies the ship Monday and AMD's data-center outlook holds Tuesday, washed-out positioning and favorable post-midterm seasonality can fuel a sharp relief bounce off the 7,500 support shelf. If AMD disappoints or the election count turns messy, the next stop is the 200-day near 7,325 — and Wilson's 7,100 is live. **Stay defensively positioned with an energy hedge into the catalysts; be ready to add quality tech into a capitulation, not into strength.**
