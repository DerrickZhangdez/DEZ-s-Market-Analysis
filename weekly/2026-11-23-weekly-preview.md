## The Week in One Paragraph

Wall Street limps into Thanksgiving week off its **worst five days since April 2025** — the S&P 500 shed roughly **8.6%** to about **6,910** and the Nasdaq Composite fell about **10%** into **correction** near **22,620**, as the year's dominant AI/data-center trade violently unwound. The proximate triggers were a tangle of AI-bubble anxiety, hawkish Fed repricing, politics (post-midterm regulatory fears, data-center moratoriums) and a bruising, poorly-received Anthropic IPO debut that failed to stabilize sentiment. Yet **Sunday night futures bounced hard** (S&P futures up ~1.5–2.1%, Nasdaq futures up ~2.5–2.9%), setting up a classic oversold-rebound tape into a thin holiday week. The dominant cross-current is a **Fed that is tightening, not easing**: markets now price a **~90%+ probability of a 25bp hike** at the December 9 FOMC to 4.00–4.25%, a regime that pressures long-duration tech multiples even as growth holds up. The single most important data point of the week is **Wednesday's October core PCE** (the Fed's preferred gauge, seen near 3.2% y/y) landing alongside the **Q3 GDP second estimate** and **durable goods** — all before a long weekend. Biggest risk: a hot PCE that cements the December hike and reignites the de-rating of crowded AI names. Biggest opportunity: a tradeable, breadth-led bounce in oversold, fundamentally-anchored names if PCE comes in benign and Dell's Tuesday-night print reassures on AI demand.

## Sector Themes to Watch

### AI & Technology
The **epicenter of the selloff and the bounce.** The AI/data-center complex just suffered its sharpest drawdown of the cycle — Nvidia reportedly fell ~14% on Friday 11/20 alone and sits well below its early-October high; the group shed trillions in market cap peak-to-trough. The bear case crystallized around **capex sustainability** (Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Oracle committed ~$750B in 2026 capex), Nvidia-specific noise (a reported SEC accounting inquiry; CEO-succession chatter), export-control risk, and the failed Anthropic IPO stabilization (stock fell ~9% but held above its $83 IPO price). **This week's tell:** whether Dell's Tuesday print and a benign PCE can convert an oversold bounce into a durable base, or whether rallies get sold into the half-day Friday.

### Hardware & Semiconductors
The SOX had its worst day since March 2020 earlier in November (down ~10–12% in a session). Watch **Dell (Tue), HPE (Mon), Analog Devices (Tue)** — ADI offers a cleaner read on the **non-AI, industrial/auto analog cycle** that's less exposed to bubble de-rating. **Memory pricing** is a live swing factor (a tailwind for memory makers, a margin headwind for OEMs like Dell). Export-control headlines from the APEC backdrop are a daily risk.

### Software & SaaS
**Workday and Autodesk (both Tue after close)** test whether the "AI-disrupts-SaaS-seats" narrative is fundamentally dentable or just sentiment. Watch cRPO/billings durability and margin discipline. Software held up relatively better than hardware in the rout; a stabilization here would broaden any bounce.

### Energy & Commodities
**Oil down ~5–6% last week** (WTI ~$82, Brent ~$91) despite Hormuz attacks — surplus fears dominating. **Gold had its worst week since March** on Fed-hike repricing and forced de-leveraging/selling. Lower crude is a **disinflationary tailwind** that, if sustained, supports the soft-PCE/bounce case. OPEC+ posture and any Hormuz escalation are the two-way risks.

### Financials & Rates
The **10-year Treasury yield fell ~15–20bp to ~4.95%**, dipping back below 5% after an extended run above it — a bond rally driven by the equity selloff and falling oil. **DXY ~103.7** (off a 19-month high near 103.97). For tech valuations the tension is acute: a hiking Fed caps how far yields can fall, keeping a lid on multiple re-expansion even as the flight-to-quality bid helps near term. No major bank earnings this week.

## Positioning & Strategy

**Trade the bounce, but travel light.** The setup is a textbook oversold mean-reversion into the single thinnest liquidity window of the quarter. That argues for **participating in the rebound with reduced size and defined risk** rather than pressing directional bets. The two gates on the week are binary and both land Tuesday–Wednesday: **Dell's print (Tue PM)** and **core PCE (Wed AM).** Clear both benignly and the oversold rally can extend into a seasonally friendly late-November/December window; fail either and rallies should be sold into Friday's half-day. Avoid initiating large new positions into Wednesday's data drop given you'll be carrying them over a three-day weekend with no ability to react.

**On rotation:** This is still a market deciding whether the AI-capex super-theme is a bubble deflating or a growth story consolidating. Barbell it: pair **selective, beaten-down quality in the AI/semis complex** (Dell, ADI — names with real cash flows, not story stocks) against **defensive and value ballast** (Medtronic-style med-tech, low-multiple energy, staples) so you own the bounce without betting the account on the AI trade re-inflating. Underweight the most speculative, cash-burning AI derivatives until breadth and the capex narrative stabilize.

**Hedges worth considering:** With the VIX only in the low-20s, **downside protection is still reasonably priced** relative to realized volatility — modest index puts or put-spreads into/through the PCE print are sensible insurance for a long-weekend hold. A small **energy/oil exposure** (or EXE-type name) hedges the Hormuz tail. Keep **dry powder**: forced de-leveraging can create better entry points than a Sunday-night gap-up.

## Bottom Line

**This is a "prove-it" week for the bounce.** The AI-led rout was violent enough to leave the market deeply oversold and primed for a rebound — but the rebound only earns a second week if **Dell reassures on Tuesday night** and **core PCE cooperates on Wednesday morning.** With a hiking Fed, a holiday-thinned tape and a three-day-weekend carry, **lean modestly long into oversold quality with hedges on and size down** — and let Wednesday's PCE, not Sunday's futures gap, tell you whether to press or fade.
