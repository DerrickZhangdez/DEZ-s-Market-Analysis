# Weekly Market Preview — Week of Monday, October 19 to Friday, October 23, 2026
**Published:** Sunday, October 18, 2026 | Overnight futures open

---

## The Week in One Paragraph

Wall Street enters the week nursing its worst five sessions since June, and the battleground has shifted decisively from macro to micro. The AI trade cracked last week — the Nasdaq Composite fell 4.5% and the Philadelphia semiconductor complex shed roughly 11% as investors finally began pricing the gap between hyperscaler capital spending and the profits it is supposed to produce. Into that nervousness marches the single most important earnings cluster of the quarter: **Netflix Tuesday, then Meta, Tesla, IBM, Texas Instruments and Lam Research on Wednesday**. These reports are no longer about beating EPS by a penny — they are a referendum on whether the capex-to-cash-flow story holds. Overlay a genuinely hostile macro backdrop — September CPI reaccelerated to 3.7%, PPI spiked to a multi-year high of 6.3%, the 10-year yield is camped near 5.1%, and Brent oil punched to $110 for the first time since May on Strait of Hormuz attacks — and you have the classic late-cycle setup: good news on earnings may not be enough, while any disappointment gets punished twice. The Fed is in its pre-meeting blackout ahead of the **October 27–28 FOMC**, so there is no official voice to calm nerves this week. Biggest risk: a hyperscaler guide-down on AI returns that validates the bears. Biggest opportunity: capitulation in high-quality, cash-generative names that got sold with the index but have catalysts of their own this week.

## This Week's Critical Calendar

### Federal Reserve Watch

- **FOMC meeting this week? No.** The next decision lands **Wednesday, October 28** (a two-day meeting Oct 27–28). Critically, it is a **non-SEP meeting** — no updated dot plot or Summary of Economic Projections.
- **Fed speakers:** Expect **silence**. The pre-meeting communications blackout runs into the Oct 27–28 decision, so there are no scheduled policy speeches this week. That removes the usual mid-week "Fed-speak" shock absorber — the tape has to trade earnings and data on their own.
- **Rate backdrop:** The target range is widely reported at **3.75%–4.00%**, following a September 16 hike (the first increase since 2023) in response to re-accelerating inflation. *(Note: one data provider still shows 3.50%–3.75%; treat the exact level as subject to confirmation against the Fed's own release.)*
- **Market-implied odds for Oct 28:** Pricing is unsettled and source-dependent — roughly a **20%–25%** implied probability of another hike, though hot September CPI/PPI has pushed some desks higher. The hawkish risk is live; the September FOMC minutes read hawkish and official commentary has hardened. Verify current CME FedWatch probabilities before positioning.

## Sector Themes to Watch

### AI & Technology
The central event risk of the week and the quarter. The bull case (capex = future cash flow) and bear case (capex = margin destruction + debt) collide Wednesday night with **Meta**. Watch AI-financing stress specifically: **CoreWeave (CRWV)** and **Nebius (NBIS)** fell ~15% over five sessions, and Goldman flagged growing stress in the AI debt market. The question framing the tape, per the Street: *can hyperscaler profitability grow faster than capex?* Meta's answer moves everything.

### Hardware & Semiconductors
The epicenter of the damage — chips fell ~11% last week, with the sector index reportedly down for eight straight sessions, its longest streak since 2008. **Lam Research** and **Texas Instruments** (both Wed) are the direct reads: LRCX on wafer-fab equipment / memory capex, TXN on the broad analog/industrial cycle. A reassuring LRCX order book could mark a tradable bottom; a cut would confirm the de-rating.

### Software & SaaS
Relative safe harbor if the capex-heavy names wobble. **IBM** (Wed) is the tell — software + AI-consulting backlog with a far cheaper multiple than the hyperscalers. Watch for the "AI-disruption-of-SaaS" narrative to intensify or fade depending on META/IBM commentary; names like Adobe and Intuit screen as relatively cheap growth and may catch a rotation bid.

### Energy & Commodities
The week's momentum trade. **Brent ~$109 / WTI ~$101** after a >10% weekly WTI rally. Drivers: US crude inventories drew ~8.6M bbl (week ended Oct 9) plus Mideast risk premium. Integrated majors and E&Ps are the obvious beneficiaries; the risk is a geopolitical de-escalation that unwinds the premium quickly. Refiners (e.g., Ampol-type margins reportedly doubling) are benefiting from crack-spread strength.

### Financials & Rates
The quiet winner. With the curve steepening on inflation fears and the Dow outperforming, banks are back in favor — **Bank of America** was upgraded (Baird → Outperform, $62 PT) and **Blackstone** to Buy (Citi). The catch for *everything else*: a **10-year near 5.1%** is a valuation vice on long-duration tech. The single most important macro number for equities remains the long bond — a push back toward the 5.31% early-October high would pressure multiples hard.

## Valuation-Based Stock Ideas for the Week

*Names with an identifiable catalyst THIS WEEK and defensible fundamentals. Valuation metrics are approximate and should be confirmed against live data. Informational only — not investment advice.*

**1. Texas Instruments — TXN**
- **Catalyst this week:** Q3 earnings Wed 10/21 after close (est. EPS ~$2.39).
- **Valuation:** Forward P/E in the low-to-mid 20s; rich on absolute terms but near the low end of its own multi-year range as the analog cycle troughs.
- **Why attractive now:** Best-in-class analog franchise, fat free-cash-flow generation once the capex build normalizes, ~3% dividend, and *far* less tied to the fragile AI-capex narrative than the hyperscaler-levered names. A cyclical-bottom read here is a lower-beta way to own a semi recovery.
- **Key risk:** Guidance disappoints and drags it down with a sector already off 11%.
- **Rating:** **Accumulate**

**2. IBM — IBM**
- **Catalyst this week:** Q3 earnings Wed 10/21 after close (est. EPS ~$2.87).
- **Valuation:** Forward P/E roughly high-teens to ~20, a steep discount to mega-cap AI peers, with a ~3%+ yield.
- **Why attractive now:** A "value" way to own the AI theme — software + consulting backlog converting to revenue, minimal data-center-capex balance-sheet risk, and defensive characteristics if the hyperscaler trade keeps unwinding. Exactly the profile that outperforms in a tech-to-value rotation.
- **Key risk:** Consulting demand softens in a slowing economy; FX drag.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

**3. Bank of America — BAC**
- **Catalyst this week:** Momentum from the Baird upgrade (Outperform, $62 PT) into a steepening curve; rides the financials-over-tech rotation.
- **Valuation:** Roughly ~11–12x forward earnings, near/above tangible book — undemanding versus the market.
- **Why attractive now:** A steeper curve and higher-for-longer rates expand net interest income; credit has held; capital return is robust. Direct beneficiary of the exact macro (sticky inflation, high long yields) that is *hurting* tech.
- **Key risk:** A sharp growth scare that widens credit spreads and flattens the curve.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

**4. General Motors — GM**
- **Catalyst this week:** Q3 earnings Tue 10/20 before open.
- **Valuation:** Low single-digit-to-mid forward P/E — a classic deep-value screen.
- **Why attractive now:** Priced for pessimism; aggressive buybacks shrink the share count, and any moderation in EV-loss guidance or resilient truck pricing is upside surprise fuel. Low correlation to the AI story.
- **Key risk:** Tariff/cost pressure, oil-driven demand hit, EV cash burn.
- **Rating:** **Accumulate** (value/contrarian)

**5. Netflix — NFLX**
- **Catalyst this week:** Q3 earnings Tue 10/20 after close (est. EPS ~$0.82 vs $0.59).
- **Valuation:** Premium — forward P/E in the 30s+; the growth here must be *delivered*, not assumed.
- **Why attractive now:** The one mega-cap growth print that is *not* a capex-vs-profit debate. Ad-tier scaling, password-sharing monetization and ~1,000 cost-cutting job reductions point to margin leverage. A clean beat could be the circuit-breaker that steadies sentiment before Wednesday's gauntlet.
- **Key risk:** Rich multiple leaves no room for a soft guide; subscriber decel.
- **Rating:** **Buy (higher risk)** — size accordingly given the valuation.

## Positioning & Strategy

**Respect the rotation, don't fight it.** The tape is screaming that leadership is broadening *out* of mega-cap AI and into value, financials, energy and defensives — the Dow's weekly gain against the Nasdaq's 4.5% drop is not noise. Until Meta and the chip names prove the capex story on Wednesday night, the prudent posture is a **barbell**: keep quality AI-infrastructure exposure but pair it with cash-generative, lower-multiple names (financials benefiting from the steep curve, energy riding the Mideast premium, and "value tech" like IBM) that can hold up if the growth cohort breaks. Avoid adding to the most financing-dependent AI names (CoreWeave, Nebius) into the debt-stress headlines.

**Hedge the Wednesday event.** With Meta, Tesla, IBM, TXN and LRCX all after Wednesday's close — and VIX options expiring the same day — the mid-week vol is concentrated and knowable. Consider trimming into Monday/Tuesday strength, holding dry powder, and using defined-risk structures (put spreads on the Nasdaq-100, or a small VIX call position) rather than chasing the overnight futures bounce. The asymmetry favors patience: good earnings may be sold (season already running 83%+ beats, so beats are priced in), while misses get hit hard against a 5.1% 10-year.

**Keep oil and the 10-year on the top of the screen.** These are the two exogenous variables that can override earnings. A tanker incident in Hormuz or a 10-year back above 5.25% would swamp even good corporate results. Energy exposure doubles as a portfolio hedge against exactly that Mideast tail.

## Bottom Line

**This is a stock-picker's week disguised as an index week — and the whole quarter hinges on Wednesday night.** Meta's capex-versus-profit verdict, flanked by Tesla, IBM and the chip pair, will decide whether last week's AI wobble was a healthy shakeout or the start of a genuine de-rating. With the Fed silent in blackout, inflation hot, the 10-year at 5.1% and oil at $110, there is no macro cavalry coming — the market must earn its footing one print at a time. Lean toward quality, cash flow and the value/financials/energy rotation; keep powder dry for Wednesday; and let the mega-caps tell you whether to re-engage the growth trade or keep hedging.
