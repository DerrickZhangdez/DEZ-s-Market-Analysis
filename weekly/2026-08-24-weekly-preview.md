# Weekly Market Preview — Week of August 25 to August 29, 2026
**Published:** Sunday, August 24, 2026 | Overnight futures open

---

## The Week in One Paragraph
This is the single most consequential week of the summer, and it hinges on two questions that markets cannot answer until Wednesday night and Friday morning. First, does the AI capex super-cycle still have a second gear? Nvidia (Wed, after close) and Marvell (Thu, after close) report into a tape that just punished chip and AI-infrastructure names as long-end Treasury yields surged toward 20-month highs. Second, will new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh validate the market's ~80% odds of a September rate cut when he delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote Friday morning — the same morning July core PCE prints. The backdrop is uncomfortable: the S&P 500 snapped a three-week winning streak with a 1.4% weekly loss, the Nasdaq shed 2%, and the 10-year yield near 4.74% is doing the tightening the Fed has signaled it may soon stop. Add a still-hot Middle East (Strait of Hormuz disruptions keeping Brent near $95) and pending new U.S. sanctions on Iran, and you have a week where a benign PCE print plus a strong Nvidia guide plus a dovish Warsh could reignite the melt-up — while any one of the three disappointing could turn a wobble into a genuine correction. Position for two-way volatility; do not be a hero into Wednesday's close.

## This Week's Critical Calendar

### Federal Reserve Watch
- **FOMC meeting this week? NO.** The next FOMC decision is **September 15–16, 2026**. This week's Fed event is **Jackson Hole**.
- **Jackson Hole Economic Policy Symposium: Aug 27–29.** Theme: *"Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy."* The main event is **Friday, Aug 28 (~10:00 a.m. ET): Chair Kevin Warsh's first keynote** since being sworn in (May 22, 2026). With the September FOMC three weeks out, this is effectively the last major opportunity for the Chair to shape rate-cut expectations. Listen for: how heavily Warsh weights labor-market cooling vs. sticky inflation, any nod to the long-end yield surge, and whether he blesses or pushes back on the market's cut pricing.
- **Rate & market-implied odds:** Current target range **4.25%–4.50%**. CME FedWatch (pre-symposium) implies roughly an **~80% probability of a 25 bp cut in September** and a comparable probability of a further cut by December. Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan and Goldman all forecast a September cut followed by a steady easing path. **These probabilities will be repriced in real time by Warsh's speech and the same-morning PCE print** — the highest-variance 90 minutes of the week.

## Geopolitical Risk Monitor
Ranked by near-term market impact:

- **Middle East / Strait of Hormuz** | *Escalated, active* | **HIGH.** Ongoing tanker-transit disruptions (~0.6 mb/d) keep Brent near $95 and inject an inflation/energy tail-risk directly into the PCE and Fed narrative. Watch Treasury Secretary Bessent's expected comments on Iran.
- **Pending new U.S. sanctions on Iran** | *Imminent* | **HIGH.** Sweeping new economic sanctions are being prepared; an oil-supply headline could spike crude and the long end simultaneously — the worst combination for high-multiple tech.
- **Tariffs / trade policy** | *Ongoing* | **MEDIUM-HIGH.** Sustained U.S. tariff and non-tariff barriers continue to pressure margins and capex; watch for any EU/China headlines.
- **Russia-Ukraine** | *Grinding, year four* | **MEDIUM/LOW.** Largely priced; episodic energy-infrastructure risk.
- **China-Taiwan** | *Simmering, de-escalated* | **LOW.** Post Trump–Xi summit, not seen as a 2026 flashpoint.

## Sector Themes to Watch

### AI & Technology
The whole complex trades off Wednesday night. Nvidia's data-center guide and any commentary on Blackwell supply, sovereign-AI demand, and the "digestion vs. durability" debate will set risk appetite for the entire market. A strong guide + dovish Warsh = the melt-up resumes; a soft guide into a 4.7%+ 10-year = the correction deepens. Note the AI-financing feedback loop: heavy AI-related corporate debt issuance is itself part of what is pushing long-end yields up.

### Hardware & Semiconductors
Marvell (Thu) is the key "breadth" read — custom ASICs and AI networking. Semis were hit hardest in the yield-driven selloff, so positioning is lighter and the bar arguably lower than for NVDA. Watch memory pricing and any supply-chain commentary. SOX-adjacent names will amplify both NVDA and MRVL reactions.

### Software & SaaS
Salesforce and CrowdStrike (both Wed AMC), plus Workday and Intuit, test the "AI eats SaaS" fear against real monetization. The debate: are agentic-AI features additive (seat/consumption upside) or cannibalistic? Cheap-ish multiples mean a clean beat-and-raise could see outsized rallies; soft guides confirm the bear case.

### Energy & Commodities
Brent ~$95.30 / WTI ~$87.06, elevated on Hormuz risk. EIA/IEA forecast a glide lower (Brent ~$85 in Q3, ~$78 by Q4) *if* disruptions ease into early 2027 — but sanctions headlines can override the fundamentals near term. Energy equities are a natural hedge against both the geopolitical tail and the rotation-into-value trade.

### Financials & Rates
The 10-year at ~4.74% (2-year ~4.17%) is the market's dominant variable — a bear-steepening driven by AI/deficit issuance and inflation worry. Treasury's plan to at least double long-maturity buybacks to $4B next quarter offered only temporary relief. **A move toward 4.85–5.00% would compress equity multiples, tech worst.** Conversely, a soft PCE + dovish Warsh could pull the 10-year back under 4.60% and re-rate the whole market higher. Banks benefit from the steeper curve; rotation into financials/value was last week's tell.

## Positioning & Strategy
Respect the asymmetry of the calendar. Three binary, high-variance events (NVDA guide Wed night, Warsh keynote + core PCE Fri morning) are stacked into 60 hours, and implied vol (VIX 16) is *not* pricing them adequately. That argues for keeping some dry powder, avoiding oversized single-name bets into Wednesday's close, and considering cheap optionality/hedges (index puts or a small VIX-call position) that are inexpensive precisely because complacency is high.

On rotation: the prior week's leadership — Dow up, Nasdaq down — reflected capital moving from long-duration tech into value/cyclicals/financials as the long end rose. If yields keep climbing, that rotation is the trade: favor financials (steeper curve), energy (Hormuz premium + inflation hedge), and cheap consumer/value names with their own catalysts (DKS, CMCSA). If instead PCE comes in soft and Warsh is dovish, yields fall, and the barbell flips back toward beaten-down quality software (CRM, INTU) and semis (MRVL) — buy the names that were hit hardest by the yield spike.

Hedging specifically: an energy overweight is the cleanest hedge this week because it pays off on the one scenario that hurts everything else — an Iran-sanctions oil spike that lifts crude *and* the long end together. Keep gross exposure moderate; let Wednesday night and Friday morning tell you which regime you're in before pressing.

## Bottom Line
**The week is a referendum on the two pillars holding up this market — AI earnings power and the September rate cut — and both get tested within 60 hours.** The single most important call: don't over-commit into Wednesday's close; let Nvidia's *guidance* and the Warsh/PCE double-header on Friday define the regime, then lean into the winning rotation (value/energy if yields rise, quality-tech/semis if they fall). The tail risk that trumps all of it is an Iran oil-supply shock — own some energy as insurance.
