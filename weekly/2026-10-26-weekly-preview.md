# Weekly Market Preview — Week of Monday, October 26 to Friday, October 30, 2026
**Published:** Sunday evening, October 25, 2026 | Overnight futures open

## The Week in One Paragraph

This is the single most important week of the fourth quarter. Three mega-forces collide inside 72 hours: a **Federal Reserve decision on Wednesday (Oct 28)**, the **heaviest slate of Big Tech earnings of the season** (Alphabet and Microsoft Wednesday after the close; Amazon and Intel Thursday), and the **first look at Q3 GDP plus the September PCE inflation print on Thursday**. The backdrop is tense rather than euphoric: the S&P 500 just logged its **second straight weekly decline** and sits roughly **2.5% below its October 6 record of 7,818.93**, the AI/semiconductor trade cracked last week (the chip gauge posted its longest losing streak in nearly a year), the **10-year Treasury yield is pinned near 5.00%**, and **oil has surged** — Brent above $116, its highest since April — reviving inflation anxiety just as the Fed is set to stand pat. The dominant theme is *AI capex ROI*: after roughly $190–205B annual capex guides from the hyperscalers, the market wants proof that spending is converting into cloud revenue and margins. Overnight futures are pointing modestly higher, an attempt to stabilize after a bruising week. The biggest risk is a "hawkish hold" from Powell colliding with a hot PCE number and a disappointing cloud guide; the biggest opportunity is a relief rally if earnings reassure and the Fed signals a December move is still on the table.

## This Week's Critical Calendar

### Federal Reserve Watch

- **FOMC meeting? YES — October 27–28, 2026.** Decision released **Wednesday, Oct 28 at 2:00 PM ET**, Chair Powell press conference ~2:30 PM ET.
- **Expectation:** **Hold.** CME FedWatch has shown roughly an **87–88% probability of no change** this meeting, with only a ~9–12% chance of a hike.
- **Current target range:** **3.75%–4.00%** (the Fed raised 25 bps at its September 16 meeting).
- **No new Summary of Economic Projections (SEP/"dot plot")** at this meeting — markets will parse the statement language and Powell's tone for signals on December.
- **December path:** Futures are pricing roughly an **80% probability of a hike at the December 9 meeting.** The key question: does Powell lean into the oil-driven inflation risk (hawkish) or emphasize data-dependence and growth (neutral)?
- **Fed speakers this week: NONE.** The FOMC communications **blackout period runs Oct 17–29**, so there are no scheduled Fed speeches. All of the signal comes from Wednesday's statement and presser.

**What to listen for:** Any shift in the characterization of inflation (oil at multi-month highs complicates the picture), the balance-sheet/QT language, and whether Powell pre-commits or pushes back on a December hike. A "hawkish hold" — standing pat while signaling December is live — is the base case and is largely priced; the surprise risk is either an explicit hawkish tilt (negative for long-duration tech) or a dovish "we can be patient" (risk-on fuel).

## Sector Themes to Watch

### AI & Technology
The AI trade is on the back foot into the one week most able to re-rate it. Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon report with a combined FY capex well north of $500B — the market has shifted from "capex = growth optionality" to "show me the ROI." Watch cloud growth rates (Azure, Google Cloud, AWS), margin trajectory under heavy depreciation, and any commentary on 2027 spending (both Alphabet and Microsoft have signaled meaningfully higher spend next year). A reassuring cloud guide is the most powerful bullish catalyst available this week; a miss extends the drawdown.

### Hardware & Semiconductors
Last week's tape was unkind: the chip gauge logged its longest losing streak since ~November, and **Nvidia closed ~$217, about 9% off its Oct 6 record**, pressured by worries that OpenAI's revenue run-rate undershot. Yet the fundamental backdrop stays tight — **TSMC posted Q3 revenue of ~$46.7B with 66.4% gross margin and said demand remains far above supply with "no sign of an AI slowdown."** Memory is the key sub-theme: HBM now reportedly ~48% of accelerator COGS, memory makers are pushing price hikes, and **AMD (~+10%) and Nvidia (~+15%) have signaled chip price increases** — supportive for memory/foundry suppliers, a margin headwind for PC OEMs. The GlobalFoundries–TSMC ~$2B CoWoS interposer deal underscores packaging as the real bottleneck. Intel's Thursday print is the wildcard for the foundry-turnaround thesis.

### Software & SaaS
The read-through from Microsoft's Copilot adoption and Azure AI consumption will set the tone for the broader software complex. The persistent debate — whether generative AI is a tailwind (consumption, new SKUs) or a disruption risk (seat-based models, commoditization) — gets fresh data Wednesday. Recent analyst upgrades in the space (e.g., Manhattan Associates to Overweight with $250 targets; Etsy upgraded on AI-search engagement) show selective appetite despite the macro.

### Energy & Commodities
**Energy is this week's quiet leader.** Oil's spike (Brent >$116, highest since April; WTI ~$103) on Strait-of-Hormuz disruption fattens upstream cash flows just as **Chevron and Exxon report Friday**. Higher energy prices are simultaneously the inflation villain that complicates the Fed and the earnings hero for the energy sector. Watch nat-gas and the broader commodity complex for the inflation read-through into the Thursday PCE reaction.

### Financials & Rates
With the **10-year near 5.00%** (it briefly dipped below 5% on Oct 22 for the first time in a month before rebounding), the rates backdrop is the gravity on tech valuations. Investor unease about heavy Treasury issuance to fund AI-data-center buildout has kept the long end elevated. Visa (Tue) offers a consumer-spend read; the yield curve's reaction to Wednesday's Fed and Thursday's data will matter more for equity multiples than any single bank print. A sustained break above 5% on the 10-year is the level that historically rattles equities.

## Positioning & Strategy

**Respect the event risk, don't fight the tape blindly.** This is a week to size positions for volatility rather than to make an all-in directional bet. The sequencing matters: Fed Wednesday 2 PM → Alphabet/Microsoft Wednesday after close → GDP/PCE Thursday morning → Amazon Thursday after close. Each is a discrete volatility event, and they compound. Consider keeping some dry powder into Wednesday and letting the hyperscaler reactions clarify whether the AI-trade drawdown is a dip to buy or the start of something larger.

**Barbell the two dominant macro forces.** The week's gravity is *inflation via oil* and *AI-ROI via earnings*. A sensible barbell pairs **energy (CVX/XOM into Friday prints, a natural inflation hedge)** against **selective quality tech into earnings (GOOGL as the relative-value play).** Add **real-economy capex beneficiaries (CAT)** that monetize the AI buildout without carrying full hyperscaler-multiple risk. For hedges, index put spreads or a modest VIX-call overlay make sense given cheap-ish vol (19.45) ahead of a known catalyst cluster; a close above VIX 22 is the signal to de-risk further.

**Watch the rates–tech linkage above all.** With the 10-year at 5%, the single most important cross-asset relationship is yields vs. long-duration growth. If a hawkish hold plus hot PCE drives the 10-year decisively through 5%, trim high-multiple software and lean further into energy, financials and cash-flow compounders. If yields ease and cloud guides reassure, the beaten-down AI/semis complex (NVDA ~9% off its high, TSMC fundamentals still tight) offers the most upside on a relief rally.

## Bottom Line

**The week hinges on Wednesday evening.** A Fed that holds without sounding alarmed on inflation, followed by Alphabet and Microsoft delivering cloud growth that justifies the AI capex, unlocks a relief rally back toward the S&P's 7,750 resistance and the October 6 record. The opposite combination — a hawkish-leaning hold, a hot Thursday PCE, and a hyperscaler cloud guide that disappoints — confirms last week's AI-trade breakdown and puts 7,500 in play. **Position for the first scenario but hedge for the second: own the energy/inflation hedge and the cheapest quality tech, keep dry powder into Wednesday, and let 7,600 on the S&P and 5.00% on the 10-year be your risk tripwires.**
