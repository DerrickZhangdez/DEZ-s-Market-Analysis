# Weekly Market Preview — Week of August 3 to August 7, 2026
**Published:** Sunday, August 2, 2026 (overnight into Monday, August 3) | Overnight futures open

---

## The Week in One Paragraph
The market pivots from the Big Tech gauntlet into a **jobs-and-AI-hardware crossroads**. Two forces dominate: **Friday's July nonfarm payrolls report** — the single most important macro print of the month with no FOMC meeting to hide behind — and a **second-tier AI earnings wave led by Palantir (Mon) and AMD (Tue)** that lands while the semiconductor complex is still nursing a violent late-July drawdown. The tape enters the week with a split personality: the S&P 500 closed July at a **record 7,489.72 (up ~9% YTD)** and Q2 earnings have been blockbuster (~85% of the ~300 reporters beating, aggregate profit growth tracking north of **+45% YoY**), yet the AI-hardware cohort shed **more than $1 trillion of value in late July** after Nvidia's reported plan to backstop up to **$250 billion of OpenAI financing** reignited fears the AI build-out is turning circular and capex-heavy. Overnight futures are modestly higher (Nasdaq 100 leading) helped by a **sharp drop in oil** after President Trump said Sunday he called off a planned strike on Iran — a genuine geopolitical de-escalation that pulled Brent back toward $84. The dominant risk this week is a **soft payrolls print colliding with rich AI valuations**; the dominant opportunity is that a *cooling-not-collapsing* labor number would cement September rate-cut odds and reward beaten-down rate-sensitive value. August and September are seasonally the weakest months of the year — respect the calendar, keep hedges on, and let Friday 8:30 a.m. set the tone.

## This Week's Critical Calendar

- **FOMC meeting: NO.** There is **no FOMC meeting in August**. The Committee last met **July 28–29** and held the target range at **3.50%–3.75%** (the fifth consecutive hold). The next decision is **September 15–16**, which *will* carry an updated Summary of Economic Projections (dot plot).
- **The signal event on the horizon:** **Jackson Hole (Aug 20–22)**. This week is a *set-up* week — the data (especially Friday's jobs report) will shape what Chair Powell can say at Jackson Hole about a September cut.
- **Fed speakers:** With the blackout lifted, expect scattered regional-Fed commentary through the week. Listen for how officials frame the **labor-market cooling vs. sticky-services-inflation** trade-off — any dovish tilt after a soft payrolls print would be jet fuel for rate-sensitive sectors.
- **Current rate | Implied odds:** **3.50%–3.75%.** Markets are leaning toward a **September cut**, but the probability is **data-dependent on Friday's payrolls**. A weak number pushes odds decisively higher; a hot number revives the "higher-for-longer" tail and pressures the long end.

## Geopolitical Risk Monitor
Active issues ranked by near-term market impact:

- **Iran / Strait of Hormuz — DE-ESCALATING (net positive this week).** President Trump said Sunday he **canceled a planned strike on Iran**, sending oil sharply lower. This follows the mid-2026 U.S.–Iran MOU that reopened the Strait and is bringing shut-in production back online. *Market impact:* Lower oil = disinflationary tailwind and risk-on support — but the situation remains **fragile and headline-driven**; a reversal would spike Brent and re-arm the inflation trade.
- **U.S.–China trade & critical minerals — ELEVATED, SLOW-BURN.** Tariff/export-control friction persists around **advanced semiconductors (U.S. leverage) and rare earths/critical minerals (China's leverage)**. A **U.S. Supreme Court ruling on the scope of executive tariff powers is pending in 2026** and is a genuine tail risk/opportunity. *Market impact:* Sector-specific (semis, industrials, materials); binary headline risk.
- **Russia–Ukraine — ENTRENCHED, year four.** No near resolution, but **U.S.-mediated ceasefire diplomacy continues** (possible Türkiye/Gulf/India involvement). *Market impact:* Mostly energy/ags and European risk premia; low direct U.S.-equity impact absent a breakthrough or breakdown.
- **Israel–Hamas — FRAGILE CEASEFIRE.** Holding but precarious across the broader Near East. *Market impact:* Low unless it re-entangles Iran and oil.

## Sector Themes to Watch

### AI & Technology
This is the week's **swing factor**. The AI-hardware complex lost **>$1 trillion in late July** after reports that **Nvidia would guarantee up to $250 billion in financing for OpenAI's data-center build-out** — reviving "circular financing" and capex-sustainability worries (NVDA slid toward ~$197). **Palantir (Mon AMC)** is the marquee AI-*software* test: at ~60x sales and ~80% revenue growth, it is the purest read on whether the market still pays up for AI monetization, or whether the July de-rating in hardware bleeds into software. A strong Palantir guide-up could re-energize the whole AI trade; a merely-good print risks a "great-but-priced-in" fade.

### Hardware & Semiconductors
The binding constraint remains **High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM)** — SK Hynix and Samsung are racing to add capacity, and memory names (incl. **SanDisk/NAND**) are leveraged to the cycle. **AMD (Tue AMC)** is the key catalyst: watch the **MI-series data-center ramp** (including the reported ~$60B Meta MI400 arrangement), **gross-margin trajectory (~56%)**, and any share commentary vs. Nvidia. Backdrop: hyperscalers (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta) are collectively pointing to **~$650B of AI infrastructure spend in 2026 (~+80% YoY)** — bullish for demand, but the July sell-off shows the market is newly sensitive to *how that spend is financed*.

### Software & SaaS
Palantir headlines, but the broader debate is **AI-disruption vs. AI-monetization**: does generative AI cannibalize seat-based SaaS, or do application-layer names (Shopify's AI-commerce tooling, vertical software) convert AI into revenue? Guidance and net-retention commentary matter more than the quarter. Expect elevated single-name dispersion.

### Energy & Commodities
Oil is the macro release valve: **Brent ~$84 / WTI ~$81** after Sunday's Iran de-escalation, with the structural story a **2026 oversupply** (OPEC+ restoration + resilient non-OPEC) capped by geopolitics. J.P. Morgan sees Brent ~$86 in Q3 easing toward high-$70s into year-end; the EIA is lower still. *Read-through:* softer oil = disinflationary, supports the rate-cut case and consumer, but pressures energy-sector earnings momentum.

### Financials & Rates
The **10-year Treasury yield near ~4.2%–4.3%** (down from the ~4.6%+ highs earlier in the year) and **DXY around ~101** frame the backdrop. The yield-curve and September-cut odds hinge entirely on **Friday's jobs report**. Lower yields would relieve pressure on high-multiple tech and lift rate-sensitive value (regional banks, REITs, homebuilders); a hot payrolls print does the opposite. Credit spreads remain contained — a sign the bond market is not (yet) pricing a growth scare.

## Positioning & Strategy
**Barbell into the binary.** The week is bookended by AI-single-name risk (Mon–Tue) and macro-labor risk (Fri), with elevated valuations leaving little cushion. Favor a **barbell**: keep core exposure to **quality compounders and AI-monetization winners** on one end, and **defensive-growth/quality-value** (staples, healthcare, select industrials with AI-infrastructure exposure) on the other. Trim the most stretched, momentum-only AI-hardware longs that have *no* earnings catalyst this week — let the ones with prints (AMD) prove themselves.

**Respect the seasonality and keep hedges cheap.** August–September is historically the weakest stretch of the calendar, the VIX just flashed a caution signal, and the tape is one soft payrolls print away from a rotation. With volatility having cooled off its late-July spike, **index puts/collars are relatively inexpensive insurance** into Friday. Consider using the Sunday-night futures pop to *fund* protection rather than chase.

**Play the rate-cut fork deliberately.** If Friday's jobs number is *soft-but-orderly*, the September-cut trade broadens the rally into rate-sensitive value — regional banks, homebuilders, REITs, and small caps — and that rotation is where the fresh money likely goes. If it runs *hot*, expect a yield-driven de-rating in long-duration growth; in that scenario, energy (despite soft oil), financials, and cash-flow value outperform. Position modestly ahead, add decisively after the print.

## Bottom Line
**Friday's jobs report is the whole ballgame** — it decides whether the September rate-cut narrative hardens into a broadening rally or snaps back into a higher-for-longer, high-multiple-tech-under-pressure regime. Into it, the single most important call is **discipline over chase**: take the Sunday-night strength and rich AI valuations as a reason to *fund hedges and lean toward quality*, not to press beta. Own the AI winners that have to *earn* it this week (AMD), pair them with defensive-growth ballast (LLY, COST), and let the payrolls print — not the futures pop — dictate how aggressive you get.
