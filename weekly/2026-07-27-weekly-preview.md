# Weekly Market Preview — Week of July 27 to July 31, 2026
**Published:** Sunday, July 27, 2026 | Overnight futures open

---

## The Week in One Paragraph
This is the single most consequential week of the summer. Four companies representing roughly **17% of the entire S&P 500's market capitalization — Microsoft, Meta, Apple, and Amazon — report within a 48-hour window**, colliding with a **two-day FOMC meeting (July 28–29)** and the **June core PCE print (Friday)**. The macro backdrop is a genuine tug-of-war: a fragile-but-holding US–Iran ceasefire pulled Brent back below $100 (from a spike toward $101), which cooled the reflation trade even as the 10-year Treasury yield sits near **4.67%, its highest since January 2025**. Overnight futures are rallying (Nasdaq 100 leading, +1.2%) on the oil relief. But the Nasdaq enters the week bruised: Alphabet's and Tesla's results last week spooked investors over surging AI-infrastructure capex, and this week's mega-cap prints will either validate the AI spending cycle or deepen the correction. The dominant risk is a "sell-the-capex" reaction to Microsoft/Amazon cloud spending; the dominant opportunity is that Q2 S&P 500 earnings growth is tracking a blistering **~38% year-over-year**, far above expectations. Position for volatility around Wednesday 2:00 p.m. and each after-the-close mega-cap release.

## This Week's Critical Calendar

### Federal Reserve Watch
- **FOMC meeting: YES** — two-day meeting **Tuesday July 28 – Wednesday July 29**, decision **Wednesday July 29 at 2:00 p.m. ET**, followed by Chair Powell's press conference at 2:30 p.m.
- **Expectation: HOLD.** Economists (FactSet poll) expect the Fed to leave the target range unchanged at **3.50%–3.75%** — the fifth consecutive hold. Markets price a high probability of no change.
- **The twist:** Resurgent, oil-driven inflation has flipped the risk skew *hawkish*. The probability of a **rate hike later in 2026 jumped to ~38%, up from ~12% a week earlier**, as Brent briefly cleared $100. Powell's tone on whether the energy spike is "transitory" vs. broadening is the market-moving variable.
- **No SEP:** There is **no Summary of Economic Projections (dot plot)** at this meeting — all the signal comes from the statement language and the press conference.
- **Fed speakers:** None. The FOMC **communications blackout** runs through the decision, so there are no scheduled Fed speeches this week — the statement and Powell presser are the only Fed inputs.
- **Current rate | Implied odds:** 3.50%–3.75% | No-change is near-certain for July; the live debate is the September/year-end path and the hike tail-risk.

## Geopolitical Risk Monitor
Ranked by near-term market impact.

- **US–Iran ceasefire (fragile)** | *Status: Holding, negotiations ongoing in Switzerland; China/Pakistan reportedly helping revive talks* | **Market Impact: HIGH.** This is the single biggest swing factor for oil, and therefore for the inflation/Fed narrative. Analysts see a **60-day rollover as the base case** rather than a formal breakdown, but two spoilers loom: ambiguous terms on Iranian oil-export MoU and Iran's Lebanon demands. A collapse would re-spike crude, strain Asian AI supply chains, and force a hawkish central-bank posture.
- **Strait of Hormuz normalization** | *Status: Slow re-opening; crude/LNG/fertilizer carriers returning first, containers lagging* | **Market Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH.** Shipping/insurance premiums keep a risk floor under energy.
- **US trade/tariff deadline** | *Status: A Trump trade deadline falls this week* | **Market Impact: MEDIUM.** Watch for last-minute deals or new tariff announcements that hit autos, industrials, and import-heavy retail.
- **US–China** | *Status: Entangled with the Iran file via oil diplomacy; tech-export controls persist* | **Market Impact: MEDIUM.** Key overhang for semicap names (LRCX) and China-exposed hardware.
- **Russia–Ukraine** | *Status: Grinding; sanctions framework intact* | **Market Impact: LOW-MEDIUM.** Mostly an energy/ag tail risk this week.

## Sector Themes to Watch

### AI & Technology
The whole week is an AI-capex referendum. Alphabet and Tesla already set a nervous tone last week by flagging surging infrastructure costs. Now **Microsoft, Meta, Apple, and Amazon** must show that hyperscale AI spending is translating into revenue and defensible margins — not just rising depreciation. The tell will be in *forward capex guidance*: markets have grown allergic to "we're spending more than expected." A clean beat-and-raise from Microsoft Azure and Amazon AWS would relieve the Nasdaq; soft cloud growth alongside higher capex is the bear case.

### Hardware & Semiconductors
Chips led Friday's selloff on the oil/inflation scare and enter the week jumpy. **Wednesday is semicap night**: Lam Research (options imply a ±9.5% move) reports into a WFE/memory-capex debate, alongside **Qualcomm** (custom-silicon hyperscaler engagement on track for December-quarter shipments; auto guided +50% YoY) and **Arm**. China export-control headlines remain the sector's swing risk. Memory pricing and datacenter accelerator demand are the themes to track.

### Software & SaaS
Microsoft anchors the group. The market narrative pits durable enterprise/Azure demand against the fear that generative-AI capex compresses free-cash-flow margins. Watch commentary on AI monetization (Copilot attach rates, AWS/Azure AI revenue run-rates) — that is what re-rates or de-rates the whole software complex.

### Energy & Commodities
The week's macro fulcrum. Brent slid back below **$100 (~$91)** and WTI eased from the low-$90s as the Iran truce held — directly relieving inflation pressure and supporting equity multiples. **Exxon and Chevron report Friday**; with crude still elevated versus pre-war levels, cash returns and buyback pace are the focus. Any re-escalation that re-spikes oil is the fastest route to a hawkish Fed repricing.

### Financials & Rates
The **10-year yield near 4.67% (highest since Jan 2025)** is the quiet pressure on tech valuations — every basis point higher tightens the discount rate on long-duration growth names. **Visa** (Tue) offers a real-time read on consumer spending and cross-border volume. The yield-curve and credit-spread backdrop stays benign for now, but a hawkish Powell surprise plus a hot core PCE Friday could jolt the long end and cap the equity rally.

## Positioning & Strategy
**Stay constructive but hedged into the two-way risk.** The tape wants to rally — futures are green, oil is cooperating, and Q2 earnings growth (~38% YoY) is exceptional — but the calendar stacks a Fed decision and 17% of the index's market cap into 72 hours. The asymmetric mistake is being maximally long, unhedged, into Wednesday and Thursday's closes. Given a complacent VIX, index puts or put-spreads are cheap insurance for anyone carrying full mega-cap exposure through the prints.

**On rotation:** the market is quietly broadening away from crowded mega-cap tech toward value/traditional cyclicals — a theme worth leaning into as a barbell. Pair a core of quality compounders (Visa) and cheap cash-flow (Ford) against your AI beta (Qualcomm, Lam), and hold an energy sleeve (Chevron) as a live hedge against Iran-truce failure. That energy position is doing double duty: it profits from a crude re-spike that would simultaneously hurt your rate-sensitive tech longs.

**On the Fed:** don't fight the hold, but respect the hawkish tail. With hike odds for later in 2026 back near ~38% on oil-driven inflation, the risk is a Powell who sounds less dovish than the July "hold" implies. If he leans hawkish *and* Friday's core PCE runs hot, trim duration risk in growth names and let the 10-year tell you when to re-add.

## Bottom Line
**This is a "show-me" week for the AI trade, refereed by the Fed.** The single most important call: **let Microsoft's Azure/capex and Amazon's AWS guidance set the market's direction — position for volatility, not conviction, into Wednesday–Thursday.** A hold from a not-too-hawkish Powell plus clean cloud beats re-opens the path to new highs; disappointing capex guidance or a hawkish surprise turns last week's chip wobble into a genuine tech correction. Carry cheap hedges, keep an energy anchor against the Iran wildcard, and be ready to add to quality on any capex-driven overreaction.
