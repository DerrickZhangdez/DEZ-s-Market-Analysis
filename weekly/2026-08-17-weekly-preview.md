# Weekly Market Preview — Week of August 17 to August 21, 2026
**Published:** Sunday, August 16, 2026 | Overnight futures open

---

## The Week in One Paragraph

Equities enter the week at record highs and in a decidedly upbeat mood — the S&P 500 closed Friday at 7,798.99, its 27th all-time high of 2026, capping a third straight winning week, while the Russell 2000 punched to fresh records in a healthy broadening of leadership. The relief rally has been powered by two benign inflation prints: an in-line July CPI followed by a **flat** July PPI (headline 0.0% vs. +0.2% expected), which together dampened fears that new Fed Chair **Kevin Warsh** would need to *raise* rates. That is the defining tension of this cycle: this is a hawkish Fed that dropped forward guidance, left the funds rate at 3.50–3.75% in July, and whose June dot plot still leaned toward *hikes*, not cuts. Markets now price roughly a 30% probability of a September hike (down from ~44% a week ago) and a ~70% hold, with meaningful cuts potentially pushed into 2027. The week's marquee events are **Wednesday's FOMC minutes** (from the July meeting — the first under Warsh's guidance-light regime) and a wave of **big-box retail earnings** (Home Depot, Target, Lowe's, TJX, Walmart) that will x-ray the health of the U.S. consumer and the housing complex. Layer on **August options expiration Friday**, a low-14s VIX signaling complacency, a 10-year yield stubbornly near 4.68%, and simmering geopolitical risk (Strait of Hormuz tanker attacks, fresh tariff deadlines) — and you have a tape that is priced for perfection into a data-heavy, catalyst-rich week. The biggest risk is not a single headline but the asymmetry: with sentiment euphoric and volatility cheap, the pain trade is a hawkish minutes surprise or a retail-guidance miss that punctures the soft-landing narrative.

## This Week's Critical Calendar

### Federal Reserve Watch

- **FOMC meeting this week? — NO.** The next policy decision is **September 15–16, 2026** (a meeting that will carry an updated Summary of Economic Projections / dot plot).
- **The week's Fed centerpiece is the July FOMC *Minutes*, released Wednesday, August 19 at 2:00 p.m. ET.** These are the first minutes to capture the internal debate under Chair Warsh, who has scrapped explicit forward guidance in favor of data-dependence. Read them for (1) how many participants still favor *hikes* this year, (2) the balance of inflation-vs.-labor-market concern, and (3) any hint on the pace of balance-sheet runoff.
- **Fed speakers:** A relatively quiet speaking calendar this week as officials observe the run-up to Jackson Hole. Note: **the Jackson Hole Economic Policy Symposium is August 27–29 — NOT this week.** Chair Warsh delivers his first keynote as Fed Chair Friday, August 28; theme: "Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy." Positioning ahead of that event will build all week.
- **Current policy:** Fed funds target **3.50%–3.75%** (unchanged since the July 29 meeting).
- **CME FedWatch (implied):** ~70% probability of a **hold** at the September meeting; ~30% priced for a **25 bp hike**. A September cut is essentially off the table under the current regime; the debate is hold-vs-hike, with cuts a 2027 story per several trackers.

## Sector Themes to Watch

### AI & Technology
The AI mega-cap catalyst is **deferred to next week** (Nvidia 8/26, Palo Alto 8/24, Marvell 8/27, Broadcom ~9/2). This week the AI trade coasts on momentum with no single-name earnings gate — meaning tech beta will trade off the *macro* (Fed minutes, yields) rather than company fundamentals. Watch for positioning/hedging into the Nvidia print to begin building by Thursday–Friday. A hawkish minutes surprise would hit long-duration growth hardest.

### Hardware & Semiconductors
Quiet on scheduled earnings, but the setup matters: guidance from Nvidia/Broadcom/Marvell next week will "reinforce or contradict" the AI-infrastructure demand narrative, so any pre-announcements, supply-chain checks (TSMC monthly sales, memory pricing), or China-tariff headlines on chip gear will move SOX. Taiwan chokepoint risk lingers as a tail.

### Software & SaaS
Late-cycle earnings trickle (cybersecurity and data names cluster next week — PANW 8/24, and cloud/data names into early September). The dominant narrative remains AI monetization vs. seat-based disruption. With rates elevated, unprofitable/high-multiple SaaS stays rate-sensitive; quality/profitable software is the preferred expression.

### Energy & Commodities
Crude is the sector to watch given Hormuz. WTI ~$82, Brent ~$85 (EIA sees Brent averaging ~$85 in Q3), with a supply-deficit narrative as Middle East shut-ins rise. Energy equities offer a natural hedge against the geopolitical tail and against any inflation re-acceleration that would validate the Fed's hawkishness. Watch nat gas on Ukraine-driven European supply risk.

### Financials & Rates
Bank earnings are past, but the **rate backdrop is the key transmission channel**: the 10-year at 4.68% (2-year 4.17%) keeps the curve only modestly positive, and a hawkish Fed caps the "lower rates lift valuations" thesis that growth bulls rely on. Watch credit spreads (still tight) and any minutes-driven repricing of the front end. Higher-for-longer supports bank NIMs but pressures long-duration equity multiples — a rotation tailwind for value over growth if yields grind higher.

## Valuation-Based Stock Ideas for the Week

Given retail-earnings week, the highest-conviction *dated* catalysts sit in the consumer/value complex. All figures are approximate consensus-based estimates.

**1. Target (TGT) — Buy**
- **Catalyst this week:** Q2 earnings **Wednesday 8/19 (BMO)**; consensus ~$2.25 EPS on ~$26.1B revenue (EPS +~10% y/y).
- **Valuation:** Forward P/E ~13x, P/S ~0.5x, EV/EBITDA ~7x — a discount to the retail group and to the S&P.
- **Why attractive now:** Deeply out-of-favor with low expectations; any evidence of traffic stabilization and the ongoing operational reset (inventory, digital, Roundel ad revenue) could drive an outsized relief move off a cheap base. Dividend aristocrat with a well-covered yield.

- **Rating: Buy**

**2. Home Depot (HD) — Accumulate**

- **Valuation:** Forward P/E ~mid-20s x, EV/EBITDA ~17x — a quality premium, not a value screen, but reasonable for the category leader.
- **Why attractive now:** The best-in-class housing/remodel proxy. With Fed cuts pushed out, big-ticket demand has been suppressed — setting a low bar. Pro-customer strength (incl. SRS/complex-pro) is the differentiator vs. Lowe's; a bottoming signal would re-rate the whole housing complex.
- **Key risk:** Higher-for-longer rates keep remodels deferred; a soft guide pressures the group.
- **Rating: Accumulate**

**3. Lowe's (LOW) — Accumulate**

- **Valuation:** Forward P/E ~18–19x, EV/EBITDA ~13x — a discount to HD with a higher buyback yield.
- **Why attractive now:** More DIY-weighted than HD, so a leveraged rebound candidate if the consumer holds and rates eventually ease; aggressive capital return supports EPS. Trades cheaper than HD on nearly every metric.
- **Key risk:** DIY skews more discretionary/rate-sensitive than Pro; a weak comp reads worse here than at HD.
- **Rating: Accumulate**

**4. TJX Companies (TJX) — Buy**

- **Valuation:** Forward P/E ~26x — a premium, but justified by consistent high-teens ROIC and counter-cyclical positioning.
- **Why attractive now:** The structural winner of *trade-down*. In a cost-pressured, hawkish-Fed environment where consumers hunt value, off-price gains share and enjoys the best inventory-availability environment in years. A rare "offense and defense" name.

**5. Comcast (CMCSA) — Buy (value/defensive diversifier)**
- **Catalyst this week:** No scheduled earnings — thesis is *macro*: a low-multiple, high-free-cash-flow defensive that benefits from any value-over-growth rotation if the Fed minutes read hawkish and yields grind higher.
- **Valuation:** Forward P/E ~8–9x, FCF yield double-digits, EV/EBITDA ~6x — among the cheapest large-caps in the S&P.
- **Why attractive now:** Priced for terminal decline; broadband/Peacock/theme-parks optionality and a growing dividend + buyback provide a floor. A classic ballast if euphoric growth names wobble on a hawkish surprise.

- **Rating: Buy**

## Positioning & Strategy

**Stay constructive but hedged.** The primary trend is up — record highs, broadening participation (small caps leading), and benign inflation are a powerful trio, and history (per CFRA's Stovall) favors further gains after clusters of new highs. But the risk/reward for *chasing* here is poor: the VIX at 14 makes downside protection cheap precisely when the calendar is dense (Fed minutes, retail guidance, OpEx, a Canada-tariff deadline, live Hormuz risk). The asymmetric trade is to own the trend while financing tail protection with a low-cost VIX/put structure into Friday's expiration.

**Lean value/quality over long-duration growth this week.** With the AI mega-cap catalyst deferred to next week and a hawkish Fed capping the multiple-expansion thesis, the marginal edge sits in cash-generative, reasonably valued cyclicals and defensives — the retail names above, plus energy as a geopolitical hedge. If the minutes read hawkish and the 10-year breaks 4.75%, expect a rotation *out of* high-multiple software/AI beta and *into* value, financials, and energy; position ahead of, not after, that pivot.

**Respect the consumer read.** The five big-box reports are the week's real fundamental signal. A broad beat-and-hold (especially Walmart's grocery/ad engine and any HD/LOW bottoming language) would validate the soft landing and let the melt-up continue. A guidance cut cluster — particularly on tariff-driven margin pressure or discretionary weakness — would be the fundamental crack that a complacent, richly valued tape is least prepared for. Size retail exposure for two-sided risk into the prints.

## Bottom Line

**The single most important call:** This is a "buy the trend, rent the hedge" week. Own the record-high momentum and the cheap value/consumer names with dated catalysts, but use sub-15 VIX to layer cheap downside protection into Wednesday's Fed minutes and Friday's OpEx — because the real risk isn't a cut that won't come, it's a hawkish minutes surprise (or a retail-guidance miss) that pushes the 10-year through 4.75% and forces a violent value-over-growth rotation into a market priced for perfection.
