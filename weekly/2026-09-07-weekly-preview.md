# Weekly Market Preview — Week of September 8 to September 11, 2026
**Published:** Sunday, September 7, 2026 | Overnight futures open
**Note:** U.S. equity and bond markets are **closed Monday, September 8** for the observed Labor Day holiday session cadence — the cash trading week runs Tuesday through Friday. (Labor Day fell on Monday, Sept. 7; the compressed calendar shifts several data releases.)

## The Week in One Paragraph

This is an **inflation week that will set the table for the September 15–16 FOMC**, and it arrives with the market leaning the "wrong" way for a change: after August nonfarm payrolls printed a hot **+162,000** (versus roughly +53,000 expected), traders are no longer debating rate *cuts* — they are pricing the odds of a **rate hike**, now around a coin-flip (~50%) after peaking near 66–70% earlier. The 10-year Treasury yield sits at **~4.76%**, having brushed a three-year high of 4.81% last week, and that is the single most important number for equity valuations right now. Into that backdrop, **PPI and CPI both land this week** and are the last major inflation reads before the Fed decides. Layered on top is a genuinely heavyweight earnings slate — **Oracle and Adobe both report Thursday** — that will test whether the AI-capex narrative can keep carrying a market trading at rich multiples in the historically weakest month of the year (and a midterm-election September to boot). The setup: complacent volatility (VIX ~14.5), stretched valuations, firm-to-rising yields, and two data prints that could swing hike odds hard in either direction. Opportunity lives in AI-infrastructure and memory names with real earnings power; the biggest risk is a hot CPI that forces the market to fully price a hike and pushes the 10-year decisively through 4.80%.

## This Week's Critical Calendar

### Federal Reserve Watch

- **FOMC meeting this week? NO.** The decision is **next week, Wednesday, September 16, 2026 (2:00 p.m. ET)**, followed by a press conference at 2:30 p.m. This is a **Summary of Economic Projections (dot plot)** meeting.
- **Fed speakers this week? NONE.** The FOMC **blackout period runs Saturday, Sept. 5 through Thursday, Sept. 17.** No public commentary from Fed officials — meaning this week's price action is driven purely by the **data**, with no ability for the Fed to steer expectations.
- **Current policy rate:** target range **3.50%–3.75%** (held since December 2025).
- **CME FedWatch implied odds (Sept. 16):** roughly a **coin flip — ~50% for a 25 bp hike, ~50% hold** (down from ~66–70% earlier last week after dovish-leaning commentary, but propped back up by the hot jobs number). A hike would be a notable hawkish move; the fact it is even on the table is this cycle's defining feature.

## Sector Themes to Watch

### AI & Technology
The week's narrative pivots on **Oracle and Adobe Thursday night**. Oracle is the purest large-cap read on AI-training infrastructure demand — watch **RPO (remaining performance obligations)** and OCI growth for confirmation the capex supercycle is intact. Adobe is the referendum on whether incumbent software can monetize generative AI faster than it disrupts them; **Firefly monetization and pricing durability** are the swing factors. Expect elevated single-name volatility Friday.

### Hardware & Semiconductors
The strongest fundamental story in the market. **Broadcom's** AI semiconductor revenue **tripled to $16.7B (+221% y/y)**, with management guiding toward ~$230B AI revenue by 2028 and holding **70–80% share of custom AI ASICs** (Google, Meta, ByteDance, OpenAI, Anthropic among customers). **NVIDIA** posted ~$96B revenue in its latest quarter and guided the next to ~$108B. **Memory is the tightest link:** DRAM/HBM prices up as much as **~90%** early in 2026, with Samsung, SK Hynix and **Micron sold out through 2027**. **TSMC** capacity remains unable to meet demand. This is where fundamentals most clearly justify valuations.

### Software & SaaS
Adobe headlines a group where the "AI disruption vs. AI monetization" debate is unresolved. Oracle blurs the line between software and infrastructure and will be read as an AI-capex proxy. Watch analyst reactions for read-through to the broader SaaS complex, which has lagged the semiconductor leadership.

### Energy & Commodities
**WTI ~$90 / Brent ~$94**, held up by Hormuz/Red Sea disruptions even as **OPEC+ added 188k bpd for September**, completing the rollback of 2023 voluntary cuts. J.P. Morgan sees Brent averaging ~$86 in Q3. Elevated crude is an inflation complication landing squarely in a CPI week. **Gold and silver** are bid as macro hedges — a signal worth respecting.

### Financials & Rates
The **10-year at ~4.76%** (near a three-year high) is the pressure gauge for equity multiples. With the curve's front end repricing toward a possible hike, any move **above ~4.80%** on a hot CPI would pressure long-duration/high-multiple tech. A firm dollar (DXY) tends to track yields higher. No bank earnings of note this week; rates *are* the financials story.

## Positioning & Strategy

**Respect the asymmetry into CPI.** The market has swung from pricing cuts to pricing a possible hike, so a *hot* CPI is the higher-pain surprise: it would validate a September hike, push the 10-year through 4.80%, and hit the exact long-duration AI/software leadership that has led the tape. With **VIX at ~14.5**, downside protection is unusually cheap — this is a sensible week to **carry a modest hedge** (index puts or a put spread) rather than chase, especially given Monday's holiday-thinned liquidity and thin books into Tuesday's open.

**Barbell the AI trade with value.** Stay long the highest-conviction AI-infrastructure fundamentals — **semiconductors and memory (MU, and the Broadcom/NVIDIA complex)** where earnings power is demonstrably outrunning valuation — but **pair with un-correlated deep value (CI)** that can hold up if rates spike and multiples compress. This is not the week to be maximally long high-multiple, zero-hedge growth.

**Trade the catalysts, not the noise.** Thursday night (ORCL + ADBE) is a discrete, high-information event — size single-name exposure for the implied move and let the prints, not pre-positioning, do the work. Keep dry powder for a CPI-driven dislocation; September's seasonal weakness historically rewards buyers of quality on volatility spikes rather than momentum chasers.

## Bottom Line

**Thursday's CPI is the fulcrum of the entire month.** With the Fed in blackout and no speakers to soften the blow, a hot core print pushes the September 16 decision toward a live hike, drives the 10-year through 4.80%, and pressures the AI leaders that hold the market up — while a soft print re-opens the "hold" path and fuels relief. Position for that two-sided risk: **own the AI-infrastructure and memory names where fundamentals clearly justify valuation, hedge cheaply while VIX sits near 14.5, and keep dry powder for a CPI-driven dislocation.**
