# Weekly Market Preview — Week of September 14 to September 18, 2026
**Published:** Sunday evening, September 13–14, 2026 | Overnight futures open

---

## The Week in One Paragraph

This is the most consequential Fed week in nearly three years. The FOMC meets Tuesday–Wednesday (Sept. 15–16) and markets are pricing an **85–90% probability of a 25 bp rate HIKE** to 3.75%–4.00% — what would be the **first Fed rate increase since 2023** and the first policy move of the Kevin Warsh era. A hotter-than-expected August core CPI (+0.3% m/m), Brent crude topping $100 on the Iran conflict, and the 10-year Treasury yield at **4.98% — its highest since October 2023** — have flipped the debate from "when do they cut" to "how many hikes are coming." Yet equities enter the week resilient: the S&P 500 closed Friday at a record-ish **7,656.98 (+0.86%)**, the VIX collapsed to **15.84**, and the forward P/E has compressed to ~19x, its lowest since April 2025. The dominant themes: (1) a hawkish Fed and the new dot plot/SEP; (2) a record **$9.6 trillion "triple witching"** options expiration Friday that could amplify moves; (3) the AI capex supercycle (~$800B hyperscaler spend in 2026) that keeps a floor under quality tech; and (4) an energy/geopolitics shock centered on the Strait of Hormuz. The biggest risk is a "hawkish hike + higher dots" combination that pushes the 10-year decisively through 5%. The biggest opportunity is dispersion — value, energy, and reasonably priced AI names into a volatility event that has cheapened valuations broadly.

## This Week's Critical Calendar

### Federal Reserve Watch

- **FOMC meeting? YES.** Two-day meeting **Tuesday Sept. 15 – Wednesday Sept. 16**; statement and rate decision **Wednesday 2:00 p.m. ET**, Chair Warsh press conference 2:30 p.m. ET.
- **This is a Summary of Economic Projections (SEP) meeting** — a fresh **"dot plot"** will be released. This is arguably more market-moving than the decision itself: it will reveal whether officials see *one* insurance hike or the start of a *series*.
- **Current rate:** 3.50%–3.75% (held since December 2025). **Market-implied:** ~85–90% probability of +25 bp to **3.75%–4.00%** (CME FedWatch / fed funds futures as of Sept. 11).
- **Fed speakers:** NONE — the FOMC is in its **communications blackout** through Thursday. The next Fed voices come *after* the decision.
- **What to listen for:** (1) the 2026 and 2027 median dots; (2) whether Warsh frames the move as a one-off "insurance" hike against sticky inflation or an open-ended campaign; (3) any language on the balance sheet; (4) how the Committee squares hiking with softening July jobs/retail data. Note the political backdrop — the White House has publicly pressured Warsh against tightening, raising the stakes on Fed independence messaging.

## Sector Themes to Watch

### AI & Technology
The structural bid remains intact: top-five hyperscaler capex is tracking toward **~$800B in 2026** and ~$1.3T in 2027, cloud backlog exceeds **$2T**, and Nvidia's Jensen Huang has called this a "supply-constrained" market at least through FY28. The key nuance this week: AI names where valuations are *not* stretched can keep working even as the Fed hikes, because the earnings trajectory is doing the heavy lifting. Watch for any partnership/capex headlines from the mega-caps that reaffirm the spend.

### Hardware & Semiconductors
BofA reiterates Nvidia, Broadcom, and Lam Research as top 2026 chip picks, forecasting ~30% growth toward **$1T in semiconductor sales** and another 50%+ year in AI silicon. Broadcom already reported (Sept. 2; AI revenue ~$10.8B, +143%); **Micron reports Sept. 30** (not this week) but trades at a forward P/E near 13 with sales projected up sharply — a name to position around. Memory pricing and custom-ASIC momentum (Marvell, PT $365 at BofA) are the sub-themes.

### Software & SaaS
Rate sensitivity is highest here — long-duration cash flows get repriced when the 10-year pushes toward 5%. The offsetting narrative is AI monetization. Expect dispersion: profitable, cash-generative platforms hold up; unprofitable growth stays under pressure. Procore drew a fresh undervalued upgrade (Sept. 4) as an example of selective value emerging in the group.

### Energy & Commodities
Oil is the macro swing factor. Brent ~$99–100, WTI >$90 on Hormuz risk; EIA sees Brent averaging ~$90 in 2H26 before easing toward $77 by 2Q27 as Middle East exports normalize. OPEC+ is expected to hold October policy unchanged after completing its 1.65 mb/d rollback, while both the IEA and OPEC have trimmed 2026 demand forecasts. Energy equities are a natural hedge for a hawkish-hike + oil-shock week.

### Financials & Rates
A steeper/higher curve is a double-edged sword: net-interest-margin positive for banks, but wider credit spreads and a 10-year flirting with 5% pressure valuations everywhere. The 10-year at 4.98% is the number that matters — a decisive break above **5.00%** (last seen 2023, with 5.25% the 2006–07 zone) would raise the equity opportunity cost that has been absent all cycle.

## Positioning & Strategy

**Respect the event, don't fight the tape into it.** With ~85–90% of a hike already priced, the decision itself is largely discounted; the *dots and Warsh's tone* are the true catalyst. The asymmetric risk is a "hawkish hike" — a raise paired with a 2026–27 dot plot that signals more to come — which would jam the 10-year through 5% and hit long-duration growth and housing hardest. A VIX at 15.84 makes **index put spreads or a modest VIX-call hedge** unusually cheap insurance for a week that also carries a record $9.6T witching.

**Lean into dispersion, not direction.** This is a stock-picker's and sector-rotation week. Overweight **energy** (a natural hedge to the oil/geopolitics + inflation combo), **reasonably valued financials** (curve beneficiaries), and **quality AI/semis where the multiple is sane** (MU, FDX-style cyclicals, JBL). Underweight or hedge **unprofitable long-duration software** and **rate-sensitive housing** unless you're deliberately playing a dovish-dots surprise via LEN. The S&P's forward P/E at ~19x (lowest since April 2025) means valuation compression has already done some of the work — selloffs into Wednesday are more likely to be buyable than the start of a trend break, *provided* the 10-year holds below 5%.

**Manage the witching.** Friday's expiration can produce mechanical, non-fundamental moves; avoid initiating large new positions into the Friday close and expect the "real" post-FOMC trend to clarify early the following week.

## Bottom Line

**The single most important call: the market has priced the hike but not the hawkishness.** Trade the dots, not the decision — watch the 10-year's 5% line as the true risk switch, use historically cheap volatility to hedge, and favor energy, sane-multiple AI/semis, and value over long-duration growth into a week that ends with the largest options expiration on record.
