# Weekly Market Preview — Week of Monday, August 10 to Friday, August 14, 2026
**Published:** Sunday, August 10, 2026 | Overnight futures open

---

## The Week in One Paragraph

The market enters the week riding its strongest run since April, with the S&P 500 at a fresh record high (7,757.64) and the Nasdaq Composite pushing to 26,690.62 — but the tape is being carried on an unusual and slightly uncomfortable macro premise. Friday's July jobs report was a genuine shock: nonfarm payrolls **contracted by 23,000** against expectations of roughly +80,000, with 103,000 in downward revisions to prior months and labor-force participation sliding to a five-year low of 61.4%. Equities rallied anyway, because a weak labor market took the Fed's flirtation with a *rate hike* off the table and pulled the 10-year yield down to 4.6%. That sets up the single most important event of the week: **Wednesday's July CPI**. The market is now threading a needle — it wants inflation soft enough to keep the Fed from hiking, but not a print so hot that it revives hike odds while growth is visibly cooling. Layer on a heavy AI-earnings slate (CoreWeave Tuesday, Cisco Wednesday, Applied Materials Thursday), a near-term overbought technical setup, and a live geopolitical bid under oil from the US–Iran/Strait of Hormuz situation, and you have a week where the risk is asymmetric: a lot is priced for perfection into a data-and-earnings gauntlet. The dominant theme is **stagflation-lite** — softening jobs against sticky prices — and how the Fed, and this rally, navigate it.

## This Week's Critical Calendar

### Federal Reserve Watch

- **FOMC meeting this week? NO.** The Fed last met July 29 and held the target range at **3.50%–3.75%**. The next decision is **September 16**.
- **The narrative just flipped.** Coming into August, sticky inflation had several officials openly discussing a *hike*, and CME FedWatch showed roughly **57–62% odds of a September rate increase**. Within hours of Friday's payroll contraction, those hike odds collapsed toward **~44%**. The realistic September debate is now **hike vs. hold**, not hold vs. cut — an unusual, late-cycle stance driven by the collision of cooling jobs and above-target prices.
- **What to listen for:** Any Fed commentary this week will be parsed for whether officials treat the jobs miss as noise (revisions, participation drop) or signal. The late-August **Jackson Hole Economic Symposium** looms as the next set-piece for Chair Powell to frame the fall policy path — this week's data is the input to that message.
- **Current rate:** 3.50%–3.75% | **Implied September:** roughly a coin-flip on a hike, skewed toward hold after the jobs data.

## Sector Themes to Watch

### AI & Technology
This is the week AI infrastructure demand gets a real-time audit. **CoreWeave (Tue)** is the purest read on GPU-cloud demand; **Cisco (Wed)** on AI networking order flow; **Applied Materials (Thu)** on the equipment layer. Strong results + confident capex/order guidance would validate the leg that drove the Nasdaq's +5.2% week; any crack in the AI-spend narrative is the fastest route to a pullback given how much the mega-cap complex carried the tape.

### Hardware & Semiconductors
Focus on **AMAT's WFE (wafer-fab-equipment) commentary**, leading-edge and HBM/memory demand tied to AI, and the China export-control drag. Semis are the highest-beta expression of this week's AI verdict and are already extended — implied moves into earnings are large.

### Software & SaaS
Cisco straddles hardware and software/subscriptions; the market wants its recurring/networking-software mix and AI-orders backlog to keep expanding. Broader software leadership (it led Friday's tape) is sensitive to the CPI/rates read — lower yields are a direct tailwind to long-duration software multiples.

### Energy & Commodities
WTI is holding **~$84–85**, caught between **OPEC+ restoring supply (+548k b/d in August)** — a structural oversupply/glut narrative — and a **geopolitical premium** from Iran/Hormuz. The two-sided setup means energy is a hedge against the inflation-shock tail this week even as the medium-term supply story is bearish. Watch crude on any Middle East headline as a live CPI-adjacent risk.

### Financials & Rates
The 10-year at **4.6%** (down 7bp Friday) is the key cross-asset variable: falling yields underpin equity valuations, especially tech. A hot CPI that pushes yields back up is the main threat to the record-high multiple. Credit spreads remain contained; the yield curve's response to CPI is the thing to watch for the read-through to bank margins and tech valuations.

## Positioning & Strategy

The rally is real but the setup is asymmetric. After the best week since April, the S&P is at a record, the VIX is at 16.5, and the market has largely cashed in the "no September hike" relief. Into that, it must clear Wednesday's CPI, a Thursday PPI confirmation, and three AI-infrastructure earnings reports — a lot of binary catalysts against an overbought, complacent tape. The prudent posture is to **stay constructive but stop chasing**: hold core exposure, but resist adding beta at the highs the day before CPI.

**Sector rotation:** Falling yields favor long-duration growth (software, semis), which is exactly what led last week — but that leadership is now extended and event-exposed. Consider **barbelling** the AI beta with cheaper, cash-generative ballast: energy (also an inflation hedge), and quality/defensive dividend payers that benefit if the "soft jobs → lower yields" theme dominates. The July jobs contraction argues for trimming the most economically-sensitive cyclicals (retail, leisure hiring was soft) in favor of names with pricing power and durable demand.

**Hedges worth considering:** With the VIX at 16.5, downside protection is inexpensive relative to the event risk. A modest CPI-week hedge (index puts or a small VIX-call position) is reasonable insurance given how much good news is priced. Energy exposure doubles as a natural hedge against the week's genuine tail — an oil-led hot CPI from a Hormuz escalation. Keep some dry powder for a technical "checkback" toward 7,600, which would be a better entry than the record high.

## Bottom Line

**Wednesday's CPI is the whole week.** The market has decided a weak labor market means the Fed can't hike — but that thesis only holds if inflation cooperates. An in-line or soft core print keeps the record-high rally intact and validates buying the AI-earnings winners; a hot print revives hike odds into a visibly slowing economy — the stagflation-lite scenario that this complacent, overbought tape is least prepared for. Stay invested, stop chasing, and keep a cheap hedge on through Wednesday morning.
