## Macro & Monetary Environment

This is the most important macro setup of the year, and it is the inverse of what consensus expected in January.

**The inflation picture has stopped cooperating.** July core PCE — the Fed's preferred gauge — rose 0.2% month-over-month and **3.3% year-over-year**. Headline PCE came in at **3.7%**, slightly above the 3.6% consensus. Consumer spending stalled in the same report. That combination — sticky prices with softening real activity — is the least comfortable configuration a central bank can face, because it removes the easy trade-off. The July FOMC minutes were explicit: "many" participants indicated rate hikes would "likely be necessary if inflation did not decline."

**The market is now pricing hikes, not cuts.** CME FedWatch showed roughly **40% odds of a September 16 hike**, rising to about **72.7% cumulative odds by December**. For most of the last two years the debate was over the pace of easing. That debate is over.

**Warsh's debut.** Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivered his first Jackson Hole keynote at 10:00am ET today. Expectations going in were deliberately low-information: prediction markets on Kalshi assigned an 89% chance he would discuss inflation but only 16% that he would mention the bond market. A CNBC survey found 80% of 31 economists and strategists wanted him to explain his economic views in more detail — a striking vote of no-confidence in the clarity of Fed communication under the new chair. Warsh's July press conference, where he repeatedly pointed to sharply higher bond yields, triggered a further rise in long rates and, by most accounts, damaged market confidence in the Fed's strategy. The market's calm response today — equities up, VIX down, yields marginally higher — suggests he cleared a low bar by not surprising hawkishly.

**The long end is the pressure point.** The 30-year yield above 5.30% — the highest since June 2007 — against a $40 trillion federal debt load is the structural issue no Fed chair can talk away. A CNBC report that Treasury could use its General Account to fund buybacks moved yields earlier in the week, an indication of how sensitive the long end has become to any hint of demand-side intervention.

**What to watch next week:** the September 16 FOMC is now a genuine live meeting. Any upside surprise in the next CPI print materially raises hike probability and would hit long-duration equities — which, in 2026, means the entire AI complex.

## Geopolitical Factors

**Oil is trading on a geopolitical premium, and the range is wide.** Brent fell below $80 on August 4 (settling at $79.36) as renewed US-Iran talks eased tensions, then reversed hard to close above $90 on August 17 at $90.87 — a greater than 14% swing inside two weeks on headlines alone. Goldman Sachs projects Brent in an $80–$90 range. The volatility itself is the signal: geopolitical risk is now a structural component of energy pricing rather than an episodic shock.

**Strait of Hormuz remains the tail risk that matters.** Roughly 20% of global oil supply transits the strait. Any escalation threatening transit would trigger immediate and severe repricing across energy and equity markets — and, critically in the current regime, would collide directly with an inflation problem the Fed has already said it may need to hike into. This is the single most dangerous unhedged scenario for a long-equity portfolio right now.

**Realignment in the Gulf.** On August 7, Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan signed the Mecca Mutual Defense Agreement — a signal of Saudi movement away from dependence on US security guarantees. This adds a layer of complexity to regional stability that is not priced into energy markets.

**Russian export restrictions persist.** Diesel exports from non-producers and motor gasoline exports are banned through January 31; jet fuel exports are restricted through end-November 2026. Refined product markets remain structurally tighter than crude.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: Semiconductors & Semicap Equipment

*(Day-of-year 240; 240 mod 6 = 0 → Semiconductors & semicap equipment)*

This is the most interesting sector in the market right now precisely because it has stopped trading as a single asset.

**The August round trip.** The SOX surged more than 100% into a record high in late June, then fell as much as **29% from that peak** as investors questioned whether valuations had outrun earnings. In a single stretch, 20 of the world's most valuable chip stocks shed **$1.3 trillion** — Nvidia alone lost $238 billion, with SK Hynix ($176B), Samsung ($173B) and Micron ($113B) close behind. The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell almost 10% in August, roughly twice the Nasdaq 100's loss, even as it gained double digits at points early in the month. The proximate causes stacked up simultaneously: weaker-than-expected AI chip guidance from Broadcom, higher oil, and rising bond yields.

**The structural question.** The core bear argument is not that AI demand is fake — it is that **AI infrastructure investment may grow faster than AI revenue**. With aggregate US hyperscaler capex for 2025–2026 approaching **$700 billion**, and OpenAI at a ~$40B annualized run rate against Anthropic's reported ~$47B ARR, the revenue base is real and compounding fast — but it is not yet $700 billion. The gap is the whole debate.

**Why the "picks-and-shovels" layer is where the risk/reward sits.** Here is the dispersion that defines the current tape:

- **Nvidia** beat and guided strongly — shares +8.7% Thursday.
- **Marvell** delivered Q2 FY27 revenue of **$2.739 billion, up 37% YoY** and $39M above the guidance midpoint, with **data center growth accelerating to 46% YoY**, non-GAAP EPS of $0.94, Q3 guidance of $3.150 billion, and raised FY27/FY28 outlooks — and **fell over 7% after hours**.

When a company grows data center revenue 46%, beats, and raises, and the stock falls 7%, the market is telling you that expectations, not fundamentals, are the binding constraint on the front-line AI names. That argues for moving *down* the value chain to where the same volumes are captured at a fraction of the multiple.

**The semicap spending backdrop is strengthening.** Morgan Stanley raised its 2026 global WFE (wafer fab equipment) market forecast from **$122 billion (+5% YoY) to $128 billion (+10% YoY)** — a meaningful upgrade. More importantly, the *composition* of that spend is shifting toward exactly the process steps that advanced packaging and HBM require: additional deposition, etch, annealing, bonding and metrology. Heterogeneous integration is process-step-intensive in a way that monolithic scaling was not, which structurally raises equipment intensity per wafer.

**Advanced packaging is the genuine bottleneck.** The evidence is now unambiguous across multiple independent reporters:
- **Onto Innovation** raised its full-year advanced-packaging growth outlook to **~80%**, up from a prior 50% projection, with inspection revenue up 30% sequentially on 2.5D logic and HBM, and backlog exceeding **$1.1 billion** extending into 2027.
- **Amkor** guides its AI advanced packaging portfolio to **triple in 2026**.
- **ACM Research** raised full-year guidance on a very large first-half order increase tied to advanced packaging.
- **Veeco** booked a **$200 million advanced packaging order** for 2027 delivery.
- **Kulicke & Soffa** expects thermocompression bonding revenue above $100M in FY26 and is building toward ~$400M in Advanced Solutions revenue.

Four independent companies at different points in the packaging flow all reporting the same acceleration is a far more reliable signal than any single guide.

**Where the valuation discipline bites.** Not everything in this sector is investable at current prices. Screening the spotlight universe on valuation rather than narrative:
- **Veeco** trades at a P/E of ~139 with implied single-digit revenue growth in its FY guidance of $780–810M — the order book is real but the multiple already reflects it. **Pass.**
- **MACOM** — outstanding execution (data center revenue +81.5% YoY to $137.6M) but forward EV/EBITDA of ~46x, 58% above its five-year average, and ~50x forward P/E. **Pass on price.**
- **Lattice** — 62% growth and record revenue of $201.1M, but ~57x forward P/E. **Pass on price.**
- **Nova** — 43x forward P/E with unpriced geopolitical exposure. **Pass.**
- **Rambus** — forward P/E above 60 while royalty revenue actually *declined* to $69.64M from $74M and operating margins compressed from 46% to 42%. The narrative and the numbers are diverging. **Pass.**

That screen is what produced today's picks: the names below are the ones where the packaging/HBM thesis is intact *and* the multiple has not already discounted it.

## Stock Picks — Valuation-Based

All five picks sit in today's spotlight sector but span five genuinely distinct sub-sectors of the semiconductor value chain. Every name below is outside the Magnificent Seven, and exactly one (Micron) carries a market cap above $1T. The unifying thesis: **buy the process-step and capacity bottlenecks of AI compute, not the front-line compute names whose expectations bar has outrun their fundamentals** — a thesis Marvell's post-beat 7% decline validated this week.

### 1. AMKR — Amkor Technology, Inc.
*Sub-sector: OSAT / outsourced advanced packaging & test*

**Thesis:** Advanced packaging is the real physical bottleneck in AI compute, and Amkor is the largest US-listed pure-play on it — yet it trades like a commodity assembly-and-test business rather than the capacity constraint it has become. Management guides the AI advanced packaging portfolio to **triple in 2026**, with the Arizona facility ramping in H2 as a near-term catalyst.

**Valuation Metrics:** Forward P/S of **~2.2–2.35x** — the cheapest meaningful exposure to advanced packaging in the public market. Trading at 2.35x forward revenue against 27.5% YoY growth implies a growth-adjusted multiple well below the semicap peer group, which broadly sits at 35–45x forward earnings. Sell-side price targets of **$100–110** imply 40–55% upside.

**Growth Profile:** Q2 2026 revenue **+26% YoY**; advanced products revenue of **$1.56 billion**, up from $1.23 billion a year earlier. Management targets **$9B revenue and 17.5% gross margin by 2028**, scaling to **$11B and 22% by 2030** — note that the margin trajectory, not the revenue trajectory, is what re-rates this stock.

**Competitive Moat:** OSAT is a capital-intensity and qualification-cycle moat. Requalifying a packaging partner for a leading-edge AI accelerator is a multi-quarter process customers avoid unless forced. Amkor's Arizona footprint also gives it a structural advantage as onshoring requirements tighten around advanced packaging specifically.

**Key Risk:** Margin structure. Amkor's gross margins are far below the semicap equipment names, so the thesis depends on mix shift toward advanced products actually converting to margin — the 2028 target of 17.5% is credible but not yet demonstrated. A slip here means the revenue growth arrives without the re-rating.

**Rating: Strong Buy**

---

### 2. ONTO — Onto Innovation Inc.
*Sub-sector: Process control — metrology & inspection*

**Thesis:** Onto is the measurement layer for exactly the two processes that are scaling fastest — HBM and 2.5D advanced packaging — and management just raised full-year advanced-packaging growth guidance from 50% to **~80%** mid-year, an unusually large upgrade that the multiple has not fully absorbed.

**Valuation Metrics:** Forward P/E of **~36–37x**, essentially in line with (and per one source ~3% below) the semiconductor industry median of ~37.4x. That is the key point: Onto trades at a *market-median* multiple while growing revenue at 35% and advanced packaging at 80%. PEG on that basis is roughly **0.5–0.9x** depending on which growth rate you underwrite — inexpensive for the quality.

**Growth Profile:** Record Q2 2026 revenue of **$343 million, +35% YoY**, beating consensus of $326.2M. Advanced-node revenue +~50% sequentially; inspection revenue (Dragonfly family) +30% sequentially. Advanced packaging and specialty devices now account for nearly half of quarterly revenue. **Backlog exceeds $1.1 billion and extends into 2027**, with H2 revenue growth guidance raised to at least 25%.

**Competitive Moat:** Process control is the highest-switching-cost layer in the fab. Metrology recipes are co-developed with the customer's process and embedded in yield management; displacing an installed inspection tool means requalifying the process itself. A single OSAT customer placed **$200M+ in Dragonfly inspection orders** — that is a decade-long relationship being locked in, not a transaction.

**Key Risk:** Customer concentration in HBM. If HBM capacity additions pause — and memory has historically been the most violently cyclical end market in semis — the backlog converts more slowly than the current multiple assumes. The $1.1B backlog cushions timing risk but does not eliminate it.

**Rating: Strong Buy**

### 3. MU — Micron Technology, Inc.
*Sub-sector: Memory — DRAM/HBM/NAND (integrated device manufacturer)*
*This is the portfolio's single >$1T market cap position, per the diversity mandate.*

**Thesis:** Micron is the cheapest large-cap in the entire technology sector on forward earnings, and the market is explicitly refusing to capitalize its earnings because it does not believe memory profitability is structural. The 2026 HBM book being **entirely sold out under binding contracts** is the strongest counter-evidence available, and it is not reflected in a ~10–14x multiple.

**Valuation Metrics:** Forward P/E of roughly **10–14x** against consensus FY2026 EPS of ~**$33.92** and revenue of ~**$76B (+103% YoY)**. Shares around $1,211, up 284% YTD, with market cap crossing $1 trillion. The entire investment question reduces to one thing: is 10–14x the correct multiple for peak cyclical earnings, or a mispricing of a newly structural franchise? Note the asymmetry — at 14x you are paying almost nothing for multiple expansion, and even a modest re-rate toward 18–20x is a large return.

**Growth Profile:** Combined HBM, high-capacity DIMM and LP server DRAM revenue reached **$10 billion, a greater than five-fold increase** over the prior fiscal year. Data center now exceeds **56% of total sales** — a mix shift that materially changes the company's cyclicality profile. HBM TAM is projected to reach **$100B by 2028 at a ~40% CAGR**.

**Competitive Moat:** Memory is a three-player oligopoly with capital intensity that precludes new entrants. Micron's specific edge is technical: ~**21% HBM share** on 12-high 36GB HBM4 stacks consuming **~30% less power** than competing parts. In a data center where power is the binding constraint, power-per-bit is a real and defensible differentiator, not a spec-sheet detail.

**Key Risk:** This is the classic memory value trap setup — a low P/E on peak earnings is not cheap, it is a warning. If HBM pricing normalizes as SK Hynix and Samsung add capacity into 2027, EPS could fall by half and the "cheap" multiple doubles without the stock moving. Micron lost $113 billion of market value in the August selloff, which is a fair preview of the downside velocity. Size this position accordingly.

**Rating: Buy**

### 4. ACLS — Axcelis Technologies, Inc.
*Sub-sector: Wafer fab equipment — ion implantation (power, mature-node, memory)*

**Thesis:** Axcelis is the contrarian, non-correlated position in this basket — an ion implant franchise whose end markets (silicon carbide power, mature node, memory) are emerging from a genuine downcycle just as AI data centers create a *new* demand vector for SiC. It is the one pick here that does not depend on the AI packaging narrative holding, which is precisely why it belongs.

**Valuation Metrics:** **P/E of ~20.3x** — roughly half the semicap peer group median of ~37x — with a **15.9% profit margin** and **13.6% ROE**. You are buying a recovering cycle at a trough multiple rather than an accelerating cycle at a peak multiple.

**Growth Profile:** Q2 2026 revenue of **$215 million** with non-GAAP diluted EPS of **$1.06**, both above management's expectations. Guidance for Q3 revenue of ~**$230 million** (sequential improvement), and the company **raised full-year guidance to mid-single-digit revenue growth** from a prior forecast of roughly flat. Power bookings improved sequentially and exceeded the average of the past two years. Management sees improving demand through 2027, with strong YoY growth expected in memory and momentum continuing as new cleanroom investments accelerate.

**Competitive Moat:** High-energy ion implantation is a concentrated niche with deep process-recipe entrenchment, and Axcelis holds a dominant position in silicon carbide implant specifically. SiC implant is technically distinct from silicon implant — the material's hardness requires different energy profiles — which narrows the competitive field considerably.

**Key Risk:** The bull case leans heavily on electric vehicles as the primary SiC driver, and EV demand has been the single most disappointing forecast in industrials for two years running. The emerging AI data center 800-volt architecture demand is real and strategically important but is not yet large enough to offset an EV shortfall. Margin pressure was also visible in Q2 results.

**Rating: Accumulate**

### 5. ACMR — ACM Research, Inc.
*Sub-sector: Wet processing — wafer cleaning, electrochemical plating (ECP)*

**Thesis:** Every additional packaging process step requires additional cleaning steps, making ACM a direct second-derivative play on the same advanced-packaging acceleration driving Onto and Amkor — and its ECP franchise is directly levered to the plating intensity of advanced packaging. Guidance was raised on a very large first-half order increase.

**Valuation Metrics:** **P/E of ~43.2x** against 25–30% revenue growth — a PEG of roughly **1.4–1.7x**. This is the most expensive name in today's basket on a headline basis and I am sizing it as the smallest position accordingly. Analysts lifted fair value estimates to **$70.50 from $51.87** on higher long-term revenue growth and margin assumptions.

**Growth Profile:** Full-year 2026 revenue guidance raised to **$1.125–1.175 billion, implying 25–30% growth**. Q1 2026 revenue of **$231 million, +34% YoY**, driven by ECP and advanced packaging applications.

**Competitive Moat:** ACM's SAPS/TEBO megasonic cleaning technology is differentiated in cleaning delicate high-aspect-ratio structures without damage — a problem that gets materially harder as packaging structures become more three-dimensional. The moat strengthens as the industry roadmap advances.

**Key Risk:** **Geopolitical, and it is severe.** ACM's revenue base is heavily concentrated in China, which exposes it to export controls, entity-list actions, and the broader fragmentation of semiconductor trade routes — any of which could impair a large share of revenue with no warning. Margin pressure also emerged in Q1 results even as revenue grew 34%. This is a smaller-position, higher-variance holding, and the China exposure is the reason it is rated Accumulate rather than Buy despite excellent operating momentum.

**Rating: Accumulate**

## Risk Factors

**1. A September Fed hike — SEVERE, and the market is only half-pricing it.**
At ~40% odds for September and >72% by December, a hike is close to a coin flip that most equity positioning does not reflect. With core PCE at 3.3% and headline at 3.7%, the Fed has explicitly signaled hikes are "likely necessary if inflation did not decline." The most vulnerable assets are precisely the long-duration, high-multiple AI names that have led the market. This is the number one risk into September.

**2. AI capex outrunning AI revenue — SEVERE, and structural rather than cyclical.**
Roughly $700 billion in aggregate hyperscaler capex for 2025–2026 against an AI revenue base measured in tens of billions. Nvidia reportedly backing ~$100B of OpenAI financing raises a genuine circularity concern about how much of the demand is supplier-funded. The August selloff — SOX -29% from peak, $1.3T of value erased — was a preview of how fast this repositions. An OpenAI S-1 exposing weak unit economics would be the catalyst.

**3. The long end of the curve — HIGH.**
The 30-year above 5.30%, its highest since June 2007, against $40 trillion of federal debt. Rising long rates compress equity multiples mechanically and independently of earnings. This risk does not require anyone to be wrong about AI — it operates through the discount rate alone.

**4. Strait of Hormuz / energy shock — MODERATE probability, SEVERE impact.**
Brent's greater than 14% swing in two weeks in August shows how thin the geopolitical premium's stability is. A Hormuz disruption affecting ~20% of global oil supply would be uniquely damaging in the current regime because it would force the Fed to hike into a supply shock — the one scenario with no policy escape.

**5. Expectations risk in AI semis — HIGH and immediate.**
Marvell beat, raised, grew data center revenue 46% YoY, and fell 7%. When good results are insufficient, the risk is no longer fundamental — it is positioning. This is the specific risk today's picks are constructed to reduce by moving down the value chain to lower-multiple bottleneck assets.

## Bottom Line

The index-level calm on the last trading day of August conceals a market undergoing two simultaneous regime changes: a Fed that may hike rather than cut, and an AI trade where beating expectations is no longer sufficient to be rewarded. Marvell's 7% decline on a 37% revenue growth quarter is the single most instructive data point of the week — it says the front-line AI names are now priced for perfection while the bottleneck layers beneath them (advanced packaging, metrology, HBM capacity) capture the same volumes at half the multiple. Position down the value chain, keep duration risk deliberately small into the September 16 FOMC, and treat any Hormuz headline as the scenario that breaks the correlation assumptions in every model on the Street.
