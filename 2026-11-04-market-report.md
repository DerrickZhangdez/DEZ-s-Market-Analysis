## Executive Summary

The dominant story this morning is political, and it cuts in two directions at once. Democrats took control of both chambers of Congress in Tuesday's midterms, and after an initial overnight risk-off lurch — S&P futures were down as much as 1.4% — contracts reversed hard to roughly **+1%** once President Trump indicated he would accept the results, removing the contested-outcome tail risk that had been the market's actual fear. ## Geopolitical Factors

**Oil is the live wire, and the risk premium is unstable in both directions.**

- Brent fell **more than 7% on November 2** on US–Iran deal hopes, after ending October at a six-month high. It sits near $103 this morning.
- That optimism is fragile. A **US strike on Iran's Qeshm** island lifted prices on November 3, and the UK Maritime Trade Operations Centre has logged **at least four attacks on commercial vessels in early November**. Strait of Hormuz risk is also showing up in EU gas contracts.
- Gulf crude exports have recovered to pre-war levels, but **war-risk insurance, freight and rerouting costs are keeping Brent structurally above $100** even with physical supply normalized. This is the key point: the floor under oil is now a *cost* floor, not a supply floor, and it is stickier.
- Forecast dispersion is unusually wide — UBS sees Brent at $110 end-2026 falling to $85 in 2027; EIA sees $105 in Q4 2026 and $84 in 2027; Trading Economics models $116.80 by quarter-end.
- **US-facilitated Ukraine–Russia talks in Abu Dhabi are scheduled for November 10–11**, with continued Russian strikes on Ukrainian energy infrastructure as a second energy-risk vector.

Energy is the transmission channel from geopolitics into the Fed's reaction function. Brent sustained above $110 would likely push core inflation expectations up enough to turn the Fed's hawkish dissent into a hawkish majority.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: AI Infrastructure & Data Centers

*(Rotating spotlight: day-of-year 308 → 308 mod 6 = **2** → AI infrastructure & data centers — power, cooling, networking, interconnect, memory.)*

This is the most consequential day for this sector in at least a year, and the reason has nothing to do with earnings.

#### The political shock

Voters across the country just told the AI buildout "no," decisively and at scale:

- **Ballotpedia counted 41 local data center measures across 10 states**; Redbud Consulting's tracker counted 44.
- **Stateline reported 33 restriction measures passed or leading** as of Tuesday night; the **Washington Post put the confirmed figure at 29+**. Counts remain provisional.
- Measures ranged from outright bans to moratoriums, energy-use caps, and mandatory voter approval for new construction.
- **Capacity reported that exactly one local measure nationwide — in North Dakota — went the way of developers.**
- **Ohio was the epicenter**: 18 municipalities voted on 21 measures. In Wilmington, roughly **80% of voters rejected rezoning** for a data center project across four separate referendums.
- Every one of the six measures decided earlier in 2026 (New Hampshire, Wisconsin, Michigan, California) also went against development.
- A **UMass Amherst poll found just 11% of Americans would support an AI data center in their community.**
- Texas Governor Greg Abbott, formerly a vocal data center advocate, **issued a moratorium on new data center permits last month** under electoral pressure.
- Still ahead: **Clarksville, Arkansas votes in December** on $55B in bond ordinances for a ServerFarm project.

#### Why this matters more than it looks

The consensus reaction will be "local zoning fights are noise; hyperscaler capex is a $700–800B freight train." I think that is half right and dangerously incomplete.

What actually changed is the **marginal cost and timeline of siting**, and the **option value of already-permitted capacity**. Specifically:

1. **Greenfield US construction risk repriced.** Developers now face referendum risk on top of permitting risk. That lengthens timelines and raises the cost of capital on unbuilt projects.
2. **Already-permitted and already-contracted capacity became more valuable**, not less. Scarcity in a supply-constrained market accrues to incumbents.
3. **Electricity price is now an electoral issue.** This is the real mechanism. Voters are not objecting to AI in the abstract; they are objecting to their utility bills. That pushes the buildout toward on-site generation, grid upgrades, and efficiency — and toward jurisdictions that will have it.
4. **Retrofit and density beat greenfield.** If you cannot easily build a new campus, you cram more compute into the one you have. That is structurally bullish **liquid cooling, high-density power distribution, and high-speed interconnect** — the equipment layer — relative to the **real estate and construction layer**.

This is the single most important asymmetry in today's report, and it shapes every pick below. **I am deliberately avoiding the greenfield-construction-levered names** — Comfort Systems (FIX, ~50x trailing, 58% of H1 revenue from technology/data centers, record $14.1B backlog up 73%) and EMCOR (EME, ~$17.1B RPOs up 44%) are superb businesses with genuine momentum, but they sit at the exact point of maximum political friction at full multiples. Great companies; wrong entry point this morning.

## Stock Picks — Valuation-Based

That constraint turned out to be a gift: it forced me down the value chain into the **distribution, grid and interconnect layer**, which is precisely where the data center backlash *least* impairs the economics. ### 1. WCC — WESCO International *(Electrical & industrial distribution)*

**Thesis:** The cheapest credible way to own AI infrastructure growth. WESCO distributes the electrical guts of data centers without taking construction, siting, or technology-obsolescence risk — and it just beat Q3 badly while trading around 20x forward earnings, roughly a third of the multiple on the equipment makers it supplies.

- **Valuation:** Trailing P/E ~21.8 (TTM EPS $16.11); forward P/E ~18.2–20.2 depending on source and date. Market cap ~$17.1B. P/S well under 1.0x on ~$28B+ annualized revenue — a structurally low-margin distribution model, so judge it on earnings, not sales. EV/EBITDA and PEG: not verified.
- **Growth:** Q3 revenue $7.23B vs $6.87B consensus; EPS $5.77 vs $4.69 — a **23% earnings beat**. Data center sales **+55% YoY to $1.8B**, now ~23% of trailing sales. FY guidance raised to 13–14% organic growth and **$18.00–$18.50 EPS**.
- **Moat:** Scale in a fragmented distribution market, deep supplier relationships, and inventory availability that is *itself* the moat when lead times stretch to years. Switching costs are low per-transaction but high at program level.
- **Key risk:** **Leverage.** This is the clearest risk in the group — WESCO carries meaningful debt, and a distribution business with high leverage is dangerous if volumes roll over. Also a classic late-cycle amplifier: distributors see the downturn before manufacturers do.
- **Rating: Strong Buy**

### 2. HUBB — Hubbell Incorporated *(Utility T&D / grid infrastructure)*

**Thesis:** The purest backlash-resilient asset in the group. Hubbell's Utility Solutions segment sells transmission and distribution equipment to utilities — demand driven by grid capacity and electrification broadly, which *rises* whether a given data center gets approved in Ohio or relocated to North Dakota. The stock fell 4.75% on a Q3 beat, which is the opportunity.

- **Valuation:** ~21x 2026 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint ($20.50) at the ~$437.60 premarket print. **This multiple is my own arithmetic from a single premarket quote, not a sourced figure — treat as approximate.** Note the large GAAP/adjusted gap: adjusted EPS excludes ~$2.50/share amortization and ~$0.60/share transaction and integration costs; GAAP guidance is $17.30–$17.50 vs adjusted $20.40–$20.60. **Judge this one on GAAP too — roughly 25x — and the stock is less cheap than the adjusted number suggests.** Forward P/E on 2027 consensus, P/S, EV/EBITDA, PEG: not verified.
- **Growth:** Q3 adjusted EPS $5.91 vs $5.76 consensus. Organic growth 11%, with **Electrical Solutions +22%** and Utility Solutions +5%. **Data center sales up more than 80% organically.** FY total sales growth 18–19%.
- **Moat:** Specified-in, code-compliant utility equipment with long qualification cycles and entrenched utility relationships. Utilities do not switch T&D vendors casually, and regulatory approval processes lock in incumbents for years.
- **Key risk:** Acquisition integration and balance sheet. Average debt rose on the **NSI Industries (June 2026)** and **DMC (September 2025)** deals. The meters business also hit a component-availability constraint — temporary per management, but worth tracking. A serial acquirer with a widening GAAP-to-adjusted gap warrants scrutiny.
- **Rating: Buy**

### 3. CLS — Celestica *(Electronics manufacturing / hyperscale ODM)*

**Thesis:** Hyperscale compute and storage revenue grew 242% YoY, yet the stock trades near 28.5x earnings — a multiple that would be unremarkable for a business growing 15%, let alone 83%. Celestica builds the racks and systems that go *inside* already-permitted facilities, insulating it from siting politics.

- **Valuation:** P/E ~28.5 against a ~$31.8B market cap. An older source (roughly 10 months stale) cited forward P/E ~50.9 and a 223% one-year return — **the two figures are hard to reconcile and may reflect different earnings bases or a meaningful de-rating; verify before acting.** P/S roughly 1.5x on the $21B revenue guide. EV/EBITDA, PEG: not verified.
- **Growth:** Q3 revenue **$5.83B, +83% YoY**; adjusted EPS **$3.30, +109%**. Compute and storage **+242%**, driven by hyperscale AI ramps and stronger-than-expected storage demand. FY2026 guidance raised to **$21B revenue** (from $20.5B) and **$11.55 adjusted EPS** (from $11.30).
- **Moat:** This is the **weakest moat in the group and I want to be direct about that.** EMS is structurally low-margin with real customer concentration. What Celestica has is engineering co-design intimacy with a handful of hyperscalers and qualified capacity at a moment when qualified capacity is scarce — a *position*, not a fortress.
- **Key risk:** Customer concentration plus the commodity nature of EMS. If a major hyperscaler in-sources or dual-sources, revenue leaves quickly. The 242% growth rate is also an impossible comparison base for 2027.
- **Rating: Buy**

### 4. NVT — nVent Electric *(Electrical connection, protection & liquid cooling)*

**Thesis:** The direct beneficiary of the "density beats greenfield" dynamic. If new campuses are harder to permit, operators push power and cooling density inside existing ones — which is exactly what nVent's liquid cooling and power distribution products enable. Management just raised full-year organic guidance by six percentage points mid-year, which is rare.

- **Valuation:** Forward P/E ~32–35 (sources: ~32.5 and ~35). Richer than WCC or HUBB and I am not pretending otherwise — this is a growth multiple that requires the growth to show up. Against $5.36–$5.39 adjusted EPS guidance. P/S, EV/EBITDA, PEG: not verified.
- **Growth:** Q3 sales $1.5B, **+44% reported and organic**. GAAP EPS $1.23, adjusted $1.52 (+67%). **Record backlog $2.6B.** FY guidance raised to **48–49% reported / 38–39% organic** growth, up from 32–34% organic prior. Adjusted EPS guidance to **$5.36–$5.39** from $5.00–$5.10. Q4 organic growth guided 26–29%. Data center sales expected above **$2B** this year.
- **Moat:** Qualified, specified-in thermal and electrical components with long design-in cycles. The **Maverick Power acquisition (closed October 1)** adds a power distribution platform. Capex is being raised 50% to ~$140M specifically to expand liquid cooling and power utility capacity — management is investing into visible demand, not hope.
- **Key risk:** **Margin mix pressure in Electrical Connections**, which management is offsetting with price. Price-led margin defense works until volume growth slows, and the Q4 guide already implies deceleration from 44% to 26–29% organic. At 32–35x, there is little room for a miss.
- **Rating: Buy**

### 5. CIEN — Ciena *(Optical networking & data center interconnect)*

**Thesis:** Optical interconnect is the chokepoint when compute gets distributed across multiple sites — which is precisely what happens when you *cannot* build one giant campus. Ciena's "in and around data center" revenue has quadrupled year-to-date against a 3x target, its backlog is $7.7B with nearly all new orders queued for 2027, and the stock fell 11.3% on November 2.

- **Valuation:** **The most expensive and most contested name here — sources diverge wildly.** Trailing P/E ~63.5 (vs 5-year median 47.0). Forward P/E estimates range from **24.2x to 48.3x** depending on provider, date, and GAAP vs non-GAAP basis; one outlier showed 123x. Price $284.34 at the November 2 close; 52-week range $168.22–$637.51 — a 3.8x spread that tells you everything about the volatility. GuruFocus rates it significantly overvalued. P/S, EV/EBITDA, PEG: not verified.
- **Growth:** FY2026 revenue guidance raised to **$6.3B ±$100M, +32% YoY** at midpoint. Fiscal Q2 revenue $1.57B, **+40% YoY**. **Backlog $7.7B**, with nearly all new orders scheduled for 2027 delivery. 400G/800G pluggable interconnect revenue on track to **more than double** from 2025 levels.
- **Moat:** Coherent optical DSP technology is genuinely hard — a small number of credible suppliers, long qualification cycles, and carrier/hyperscaler relationships measured in decades. The backlog is contracted revenue, not a pipeline.
- **Key risk:** **Valuation, unambiguously.** At 63.5x trailing against a 47x median, with a stock that has traded between $168 and $638 in twelve months, you are paying a premium for execution that must be flawless. Marvell's acquisition of Lumentum also signals consolidation and intensifying competition in optical.
- **Rating: Accumulate** *(the lowest rating here, deliberately — I want the exposure, but position sizing should reflect that the valuation case is the weakest of the five and that I could not pin down a reliable forward multiple)*

## Risk Factors

**1. Hawkish Fed repricing — HIGH.** Two FOMC dissents favoring a *hike* and a Cleveland Fed nowcast of 3.8% PCE. Markets are positioned for cuts. If November inflation confirms the nowcast, the repricing hits long-duration growth equities hardest — including every name above.

**2. AI capex financing stress — HIGH.** The buildout has outgrown operating cash flow. Oracle's $10B equity raise, Alphabet's negative FCF, stalled buybacks, and $1.3T+ of aggregate debt (unverified) mean the marginal data center is now funded by capital markets. Combine with risk 1 and this becomes the sector's primary vulnerability. **Watch CoreWeave (Nov 10) and Nvidia (Nov 17).**

**3. Data center political contagion — MEDIUM-HIGH, and newly re-rated.** Twenty-nine-plus local measures passed, newly elected governors and senators pledging tougher regulation, Abbott's Texas moratorium, and 11% public support. The risk is escalation from municipal to state and federal level under a Democratic Congress. This mostly impairs greenfield developers rather than the equipment layer — the explicit basis for today's pick construction — but a severe enough slowdown eventually reaches everyone.

**4. Oil supply shock — MEDIUM.** Brent near $103 with a fragile risk premium: four vessel attacks in early November, the Qeshm strike, Hormuz exposure, and structurally elevated insurance costs keeping a floor above $100. Abu Dhabi talks November 10–11 are a two-sided catalyst. Sustained $110+ would feed directly into risk 1.

**5. AI-disruption de-rating in software — MEDIUM.** ServiceNow's selloff showed the market will punish seat-based SaaS on the *theory* of AI substitution regardless of reported results. NICE's 46% AI ARR growth argues the fear is overdone, but sentiment is driving multiples. Cloudflare on November 5 is the next read.

## Bottom Line

The midterm relief rally is real but narrow — the market got the outcome-certainty it wanted, and the index-level bounce largely reflects the removal of a contested-election tail risk rather than any improvement in fundamentals. The more durable development is that **the AI buildout acquired a political cost curve overnight**, and the market has not yet worked out that this makes already-permitted capacity and the equipment that densifies it *more* valuable, while making greenfield construction exposure *less* so. Own the picks and shovels one layer below the obvious ones — distribution, grid, interconnect — where multiples are a third of the construction names and the ballot box cannot easily reach. With the Fed leaning toward a hike rather than a cut, let valuation, not narrative, set your entry: **WCC at roughly 18–20x forward is the highest-conviction expression of this view, and HUBB is the one that works regardless of how the siting fights resolve.**
