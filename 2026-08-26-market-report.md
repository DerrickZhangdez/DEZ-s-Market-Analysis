## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The July PCE report was the story of the morning, and it was not a good one.** Core PCE came in at 3.3% year over year, unchanged from the prior month and *above* the 3.2% consensus. Headline PCE ran 3.7% versus 3.6% expected. Inflation is not merely above target — it has stopped improving, and it has stopped improving at a level nearly double the Fed's mandate.

What makes this cycle genuinely unusual is the combination on the other side of the ledger. Both **nonfarm payrolls and retail sales growth were negative in July**. That is a stagflationary configuration, and it is producing a Fed that is visibly split: the last FOMC meeting produced a rare **9–3 vote**, and Chair Warsh — in office only since May — has said the Fed "won't hesitate" to stop inflation while the bond market openly doubts he can.

**Rate expectations have flipped from cuts to hikes.** Markets now price **40.4% odds of a hike at the September meeting**, rising to **72.7% odds of at least one hike by December**. Read that against equity indices sitting within a few percent of record highs and the tension in this market becomes obvious: multiples are being paid on an assumption of falling discount rates while the futures market prices the opposite.

**What to watch this week:**
- **Friday, August 28 — Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote.** The consensus expectation is that he reaffirms commitment to bringing inflation down while declining to pre-commit on September. Any explicit signal in either direction moves every asset class. Given the 9–3 split, he has a communication problem, not just a policy problem.
- **Tonight — Nvidia.** Roughly 7-8% of S&P 500 market cap reporting into a market that has priced perfection.
- The rates-equities disconnect itself. Bond yields have been the transmission mechanism all month; the August 19 session saw the Dow drop 700 points specifically because a Treasury plan to subdue yields failed.

## Geopolitical Factors

**The Iran file moved from military to economic escalation this week, and oil told you the market prefers it that way.** Washington announced a comprehensive sanctions package on Iran dubbed "Operation Economic Outcast" on August 24. The response was a *decline*: Brent fell to **$91.82** (−0.38%) and WTI to **$84.60** (−0.48%), Brent's lowest since August 19. As Saxo's Ole Hansen framed it, the shift from military escalation toward economic pressure has drained anxiety from crude, and the sanctions package was less aggressive than traders feared.

This is a market pricing a *contained* Iran risk, not an absent one. Brent near $92 still carries a persistent geopolitical premium — the pre-conflict fair value on supply-demand alone is materially lower. The tail has not gone away: any escalation threatening **Strait of Hormuz** transit, through which roughly 20% of global oil supply passes, reprices energy and equities instantly and violently. Discussions of a US-Iran deal that gained traction in June remain unresolved.

**The second geopolitical channel is technology, and it hit the tape harder than Iran did.** Weekend reports that Washington may permit **Apple to source memory chips from Chinese suppliers** helped drive Monday's semiconductor rout — SanDisk −9%, Micron −7%, SOXX −2.7%. This is the more consequential story for equity investors: a policy shift that would land directly on the US and Korean players who dominate memory, in the middle of the tightest memory market in years. Governments and companies continue to prioritize security, resilience and sovereignty over cost, which raises structural investment *and* structural cost simultaneously.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: Healthcare Tech, Medical Devices & Biotech With Real Revenue

**The setup.** Healthcare stocks lagged the S&P 500 for most of 2026 and have now turned — XLV has returned **28.65% over the trailing year**, and State Street's Q3 2026 sector outlook upgraded healthcare from neutral to **positive** after nearly a year of caution. Barclays' US biopharma team points to M&A returning, capital markets reopening, and oncology breakthroughs pulling capital back in. Notably, **equal-weighted XBI is outperforming cap-weighted IBB in 2026** — the money is going to small and mid-cap innovators, not the established giants. That is the single most useful fact for a stock-picker in this sector today: the alpha is below the megacap line.

**The M&A wave is real and it is repricing the mid-cap universe.** In the course of researching this sector today, three separate names on my initial screen turned out to be *already acquired or under agreement*:

- **Penumbra (PEN)** — merger agreement with Boston Scientific at **$374/share**, valuing it at **$14.5 billion**, closing expected by year-end 2026.
- **Masimo (MASI)** — shareholders approved acquisition by **Danaher at $180.00 cash** in May 2026, and the stock closed at $179.95, i.e. fully arbitraged.
- **Exact Sciences** — absorbed into Abbott.

Large pharma is flush with cash and facing patent cliffs; the priority areas for deals are oncology, immunology, neuroscience and cardiometabolic. Goldman just raised its 2030 global obesity-drug sales forecast 15% to **$114 billion**, with oral formulations expected to be 40% of the market. For an investor, the takeaway is double-edged: the takeout bid supports mid-cap medtech valuations, but three of the most obvious "cheap quality" screens are no longer investable at a fundamental price. **You now have to underwrite the businesses that are left, on their own cash flows.**

**Where the real revenue actually is.** Sorting the spotlight universe by whether the fundamentals justify the price:

*Genuine growth at a defensible price.* **argenx (ARGX)** is the sector's standout operating story — Q1 2026 product net sales of **$1.3 billion, up 63% year over year**, with operating profit more than doubling to **$394 million**, on top of $4.2 billion of 2025 VYVGART sales. Today UBS upgraded it to Buy with a **$1,400 price target** (from $960), lifting its 2026 VYVGART forecast to **$6.1 billion** ($4.6B myasthenia gravis, $1.5B CIDP) and arguing toward ~$20 billion peak sales, citing better-than-expected Phase 3 myositis data and an underappreciated pipeline. It is a magnificent business. It is also a **~$60 billion market cap on ~$6 billion of sales that just jumped 15.25% on Monday's ALKIVIA readout** and trades near $1,022. I am covering it, not buying it here — I do not chase a 10x-sales biotech three days after a 15% gap. That discipline is the difference between a research note and a pick.

*Reset stories to avoid.* **Inspire Medical (INSP)** is the cautionary tale of the cohort: revenue *fell 7.6%* in Q2 to $200.6 million, 2026 guidance implies just 4–10% growth against a disclosed five-year 50% revenue CAGR, adjusted operating margin guides to 4–6%, and the Category 1 CPT code that underpins the reimbursement thesis was not approved in April with implementation potentially slipping to 2028. Management guides Q3 revenue *down* 8–10%. An 85.5% gross margin does not save a business whose reimbursement pathway is stalled. **United Therapeutics (UTHR)** similarly declined to reaffirm 2026 revenue growth expectations with Q2 revenue of $783 million essentially flat sequentially and nebulized Tyvaso under competitive pressure — the path to the $4 billion 2027 run-rate now depends entirely on 2027 approvals for ralinepag and Tyvaso-IPF. **Lantheus (LNTH)** is fighting PYLARIFY erosion (−6.5% YoY to $240.9 million in Q1) with a TruVu launch that does not arrive commercially until Q4. **Qiagen (QGEN)** cut 2026 growth from "at least 5%" CER to **1–2% CER**. And **ADMA Biologics**, which screens at roughly 12x forward earnings on $530–560M revenue and $265–300M adjusted EBITDA, remains uninvestable for the same reason I flagged on August 14: it is defending multiple securities-fraud class actions alleging inflation of 2025 revenue via channel-stuffing, and when the *integrity of the revenue line* is the allegation, every multiple built on that line is unusable. It is also down roughly 49% YTD, with Q2 revenue growth of just 2% as Bivigam declined.

*The survivors — where today's picks come from.* What is left after that filtering is a specific and interesting cohort: businesses with real cash flow, mid-single to low-double-digit organic growth, and multiples that de-rated with the sector rather than with their own fundamentals. **Globus Medical** raised 2026 EPS guidance to $4.95–5.05 and generated $412.1 million of operating cash flow in the first half. **Jazz Pharmaceuticals** raised full-year revenue guidance to $4.60–4.75 billion with Xywav +13% and Epidiolex +16% — and trades under 10x forward earnings. **Omnicell** beat Q2 non-GAAP EPS by more than double ($0.94 vs $0.42 expected) and raised full-year EPS guidance to $2.15–2.30, with $636 million of ARR and a $640 million backlog, on a $1.7 billion market cap. **Progyny** is compounding EBITDA on a shrinking share count with an enterprise value *below* its market cap. **CONMED** trades at roughly 11x forward earnings after a drawdown that took the stock down 74% from its highs, while guiding to 5.0–6.5% organic growth and $4.30–4.45 adjusted EPS.

That is the spotlight thesis in one line: **the sector's re-rating is being led by biotech beta and takeout premiums, while the profitable, boring, cash-generative middle of healthcare is still priced for the sector's bad year.** That is where I want to be.

### Artificial Intelligence

Everything in AI today was positioning ahead of tonight's Nvidia print, so start with the number that will actually matter. Consensus for fiscal Q2 (reported tonight) is **$91.85 billion of revenue and $2.08 of EPS**, against management's guide of ~$91 billion ±2% — a consensus sitting only 0.9% above the midpoint, which leaves almost no room for a headline beat to impress. Jefferies is at **$95 billion**. But the trade will be set by **Q3 guidance, where consensus is roughly $104 billion**, plus gross-margin trajectory and Blackwell Ultra execution commentary. The historical pattern is the risk: Nvidia has beaten estimates **five consecutive quarters and the stock has fallen after four of them**. Year-over-year revenue growth of 97% is a bar the company has to clear *and* explain the durability of.

The capex backdrop remains extraordinary and is the strongest fundamental argument for the AI complex. The five largest US cloud and AI infrastructure providers — Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Oracle — have committed to roughly **$660–690 billion of 2026 capital expenditure, nearly double 2025**. Aggregate estimates including Stargate ($500B, OpenAI/SoftBank/Oracle), Oracle's own buildout and Anthropic's **$50 billion** US data center program with Fluidstack in Texas and New York put total 2026 AI infrastructure investment **above $1 trillion**.

The new and underappreciated wrinkle is **input-cost inflation inside AI**. Reuters reported that Nvidia has notified customers of possible price increases **above 15%** on some AI-server systems shipping in early 2027, driven by memory and component costs. That is the first hard evidence that the AI buildout's bottleneck has migrated from compute allocation to the bill of materials — and it is simultaneously bullish for memory suppliers and margin-negative for everyone downstream of them.

### Hardware & Semiconductors

Semis were the week's volatility engine. Monday's session saw the S&P and Nasdaq fall as chip stocks sank on two catalysts: weekend reports that **Nvidia plans to raise prices on some AI servers**, and reports that **Washington may allow Apple to source memory from Chinese suppliers**. Position sizing, not conviction, is the operative discipline here.

### Software & SaaS

The "SaaSpocalypse" — which erased more than $1 trillion of software value earlier in 2026, with the sector down more than 20% YTD by February — has been substantially reversed for the right names, and the market has now sorted the cohort with some precision.

**What worked:** **Snowflake** grew revenue **33% to $1.39 billion** with **126% net revenue retention**, announced an expanded **$6 billion collaboration with AWS**, and acquired Natoma. **Atlassian** popped more than 20% on results and delivered its most profitable quarter since 2021. The winners' common trait, as the survivors' framing goes, is being the *platform that makes AI safe, governed or actionable* — CrowdStrike, Palo Alto, Axon, Snowflake, Rubrik — so that seat count matters less than platform position.

**What did not:** the seat-based, workflow-only vendors where agents genuinely substitute for licensed users. The de-rating there has been severe enough to invite private capital — the takeaway from recent take-private interest across the software complex is that private markets will underwrite those cash flows at prices public markets currently refuse.

The honest read is that the agent-disruption thesis was applied indiscriminately in the first half and is now being applied selectively. Selective is correct. That does not make the survivors cheap — it makes them *sorted*.

## Risk Factors

**1. A Fed that hikes into a weakening economy (severity: highest).** Core PCE stuck at 3.3% with negative July payrolls and retail sales is the configuration that has no good policy answer. Markets price 40.4% odds of a September hike and 72.7% by December while equities sit near records on falling-discount-rate assumptions. The 9–3 FOMC split means the reaction function itself is uncertain, and Warsh's Friday keynote is the first real test of whether he can communicate a hawkish stance without breaking the bond market. **This is the risk that reprices everything at once.**

**2. Nvidia's guidance, not its quarter (severity: very high, imminent).** Consensus of $91.85B/$2.08 sits only 0.9% above the guided midpoint, Q3 consensus is ~$104B, and the stock has fallen after four of its last five beats. With Nvidia at roughly 7–8% of S&P market cap and the VIX at 15.72, the index has no volatility cushion for a soft Q3 guide or a gross-margin miss driven by memory-cost inflation. A disappointment tonight propagates through the entire ~$1 trillion AI capex complex.

**3. Memory-market policy shock and AI input-cost inflation (severity: high).** The prospect of Apple sourcing Chinese memory already knocked 7–9% off Micron and SanDisk in a session and dragged the memory group toward bear-market territory. Simultaneously, Nvidia's notified **>15% price increases** on early-2027 AI servers show component costs migrating into the AI supply chain's margins. Both risks are policy- and pricing-driven rather than demand-driven, which makes them poorly hedged by an "AI demand is strong" thesis.

**4. Iran escalation reversing from economic to military (severity: high, low probability near-term).** The market's benign read of "Operation Economic Outcast" — oil *falling* to a one-week low on sanctions news — is precisely what makes the tail dangerous. Brent at $91.82 already embeds a risk premium; any Strait of Hormuz threat, through which ~20% of global oil transits, reprices energy and equities instantly and would hand the Fed an inflation shock in the middle of an already-split policy debate.

**5. The consumer, showing up in single stocks first (severity: moderate but rising).** Dick's Sporting Goods **−30.7%** in a session the S&P closed higher, with Skechers alongside it, on the back of negative July retail sales. Consumer weakness has so far been an idiosyncratic-blowup phenomenon rather than an index phenomenon. That is how it usually starts.

## Bottom Line

The index closed up 0.3% and told you almost nothing; the VIX at 15.72 in front of Nvidia's guidance and Warsh's first Jackson Hole is the real position the market has taken, and it is an expensive one to be wrong about. I would not add AI-complex beta into tonight's print — not because the $1 trillion capex cycle is fake, but because the risk-reward on a stock priced for 97% growth with input costs rising and consensus 0.9% above the guide is unattractive at a 15-handle VIX. The better use of capital right now is the part of the market that has already had its bad year: profitable, cash-generative mid-cap healthcare, where State Street just turned positive, XBI is beating IBB, three of my screen's names got acquired out from under me, and businesses like Jazz at under 10x forward earnings and Omnicell at 16x with $636 million of ARR are still priced as though the sector's 2026 de-rating were the terminal state. **Own cash flows the Fed cannot reprice much and the AI narrative cannot inflate — and let tonight's tape happen to someone else's book.**
