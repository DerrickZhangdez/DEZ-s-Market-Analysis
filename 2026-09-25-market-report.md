## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The Fed has pivoted to tightening.** On September 16 the FOMC voted **12–0** to raise the target range by 25bp to **3.75%–4.00%**, the first increase since 2023. The unanimity matters as much as the move: there was no dissent from the doves, which tells you the committee viewed the inflation data as leaving no room for debate.

Key projections from the September Summary of Economic Projections:

| Metric | 2026 Projection |
|---|---|
| Median PCE inflation | 3.7% |
| Core PCE inflation | 3.4% |
| Median year-end fed funds | 4.1% (implies 4.00%–4.25%) |

The 4.1% year-end median is the number to trade off. It is consistent with **one more 25bp hike before December**, and a strong majority of officials indicated another hike is possible this year. Meanwhile the dot plot points toward low-4% fed funds persisting into 2027 — this is not a "hike once and cut" path; it is a plateau.

**Why the Fed is hiking into a 3.7% PCE print:** the proximate driver is energy. Middle East tension and the Hormuz disruption have pushed crude into triple digits, and that is feeding through goods and services with a lag. The labor market has remained steady, removing the usual constraint on tightening. The uncomfortable implication is that the Fed is tightening against a **supply shock** — a blunt instrument for the problem at hand, and one that raises the odds of a policy error in either direction.

**Rates:** The 10-year slipped 3bp to **5.17%**. A 5%+ long end with a 4% front end is a meaningfully positive-carry environment for the first time in years, and it is the single most underappreciated input into equity sector selection right now. It is very good for businesses that hold custodial float (see HealthEquity below) and very bad for unprofitable long-duration equities.

**What to watch next week:** Micron's fiscal Q4 report on **September 30 after the close** is the most consequential single print on the near calendar given memory's centrality to the AI trade. Beyond that, PCE inflation and any follow-through on the US–Iran negotiating track.

## Geopolitical Factors

Geopolitics is not background noise this quarter — it is the primary inflation input and therefore the primary monetary-policy input.

**Strait of Hormuz.** Transit volumes have fallen to **roughly half their normal rate**; only six commodity vessels passed on September 9 against a typical twelve. This is the critical detail for investors: the risk premium in crude is **structural, not sentiment-driven**. It is backed by observable throughput data, not by fear. That makes it stickier than the usual geopolitical spike.

**Bab el-Mandeb compromised.** Houthi forces captured Mayun Island, Zuqar and the Hanish islands between September 11 and 15, degrading the main alternative routing option. Losing the workaround at the same time as the primary chokepoint is what converts a disruption into a sustained repricing.

**The negotiation track.** Reports that US and Iranian negotiators are exploring a phased deal — lifting naval blockades on Hormuz tankers in exchange for sanctions easing — pulled Brent from above $108 back to roughly **$105–107**. This is the key two-way risk on the tape: a credible deal is worth a fast $15–20/bbl downside in crude and an immediate relief rally in equities and duration; a collapse in talks re-tests the highs.

**Why the downside tail is fat.** The US Strategic Petroleum Reserve has fallen to roughly **285 million barrels, its lowest since 1982**. The institutional capacity to repeat a 400-million-barrel coordinated release simply is not there. With that buffer gone, Citi assigns a **30% probability to Brent spiking to $150**, and Wood Mackenzie argues $200 is not impossible if Gulf supply losses approach 15 mb/d. Against that, the EIA's base case still has Brent averaging about $90/bbl in 2H 2026. That is an extraordinarily wide distribution, and it is the main reason to own cash-generative defensives rather than to press beta here.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: Healthcare Technology, Medical Devices & Revenue-Generating Biotech

*(Rotation: day-of-year 268 → 268 mod 6 = 4 → Healthcare tech, medical devices & biotech with real revenue.)*

Healthcare is the most interesting place in this market precisely because it has been the least interesting for three years. While capital chased AI infrastructure, the sector de-rated to the point where a number of durable, cash-generative franchises now trade at multiples that assume permanent impairment. Several of those businesses are not impaired.

**The structural demand case is not in question.** McKinsey has health services and technology (HST) as the **fastest-growing segment within healthcare in 2026**, driven by AI-enabled software, data analytics, workflow automation and technology outsourcing. The underlying drivers are demographic and therefore non-cyclical: an aging US population, provider workforce shortages and rising labor costs, reimbursement pressure compressing hospital and physician-group margins. Providers under margin pressure buy exactly one category of thing — technology that automates administrative work, improves clinical documentation, optimizes revenue-cycle management, and produces actionable insight. That is a demand curve that steepens when the customer is struggling, which is a rare and valuable property.

**The valuation case.** Much of medtech continues to trade below historical averages on lingering macro uncertainty, execution stumbles and near-term margin pressure. The opportunity is specifically in names **where those pressures are demonstrably easing but the multiple has not yet recovered** — the gap between fundamentals and price is where the return is. Screening on price-to-sales relative to a company's own historical range surfaces Integra LifeSciences (IART) and STERIS (STE) alongside Fresenius Medical Care (FMS) as candidates trading below their own ranges despite intact or improving cash generation.

**M&A is validating the thesis in real time — and this is the most important signal in the sector.** Strategic acquirers with the best information are paying up for mid-cap medtech assets:

- **Boston Scientific is acquiring Penumbra (PEN)** at **$374 per share**, an enterprise value of roughly **$14.5 billion** (announced January 15, 2026). Penumbra — the world's leading thrombectomy franchise in ischemic stroke, pulmonary embolism and acute limb ischemia — was compounding revenue at 15–17% (Q1 2026 revenue $374.8M, +15.6%; Q2 $390.0M, +14.9%). A strategic paid $14.5B for that growth profile.
- **Repligen (RGEN) is acquiring BioLife Solutions** for an enterprise value of approximately **$1.5 billion**, 64% stock / 36% cash — consolidation in bioprocessing tools.
- **InMode (INMD)** received a take-private proposal from M.N. Business Strategy at **$16.20 per share** (June 17, 2026).

When trade buyers and sponsors are this active, the public market's discount is the anomaly, not the private valuations.

**Sub-sector by sub-sector:**

**Surgical devices & orthopedics.** The steadiest fundamental performer and the cheapest corner of the group. CONMED delivered $1.375B of 2025 revenue (+5.2% reported, +5.1% cc), with Orthopedics +5.5% cc, and has since strung together **three consecutive quarters of at least mid-single-digit growth**, prompting management to raise full-year organic guidance to 5.0%–6.5% and adjusted EPS to roughly $4.54 at the midpoint. This is a business that is executing while priced as though it is not.

**Bioprocessing & life-science tools — improving, but the multiple has run.** Repligen is the cleanest recovery story in the group: Q1 2026 revenue $194M (+15% reported, +11% organic), Q2 $204M (+12% reported, **+13% organic**), with growth broad-based across filtration, chromatography, proteins and process analytics. Full-year guidance is $810–840M (+10–14% reported) with adjusted EPS of $2.03–2.09, and analysts have pushed targets into the $160–185 range. The fundamentals have genuinely inflected after a difficult 2024. **But the stock has already priced it and then some** — at roughly $186.75 (September 22), RGEN trades at a **forward P/E near 77–86x, a trailing P/E of 254x against a 5-year median of 91x, and a P/S of 11.8x** versus a life-sciences industry average around 3.7–4.8x. Paying ~80x forward for low-teens growth is a PEG north of 5. Excellent company; we are not buyers at this price, and it is excluded from today's picks on valuation discipline.

**Diagnostics & lab services.** The defensive anchor of the sector. Labcorp guided 2026 enterprise revenue to **$14.71–14.83B (+5.4%–6.3%)** with adjusted EPS of $18.10–18.55 and free cash flow of $1.24–1.36B. At its **September 10 Investor Day** management laid out a 2026–2029 framework of 5–8% compound revenue growth, 75–150bp of adjusted operating margin expansion by 2029, and **8.5%–11.5% compound adjusted EPS growth**. Both segments are contributing — Diagnostics Laboratories +5.3–6.0%, Biopharma Laboratory Services +5.5–6.5%.

**Pharma services / CROs — a genuine trough.** ICON reaffirmed FY26 revenue of $7.85–8.15B and EPS of $10–11, which is a **down year** against 2025's $8.25B and $12.53. The market has duly de-rated it to **14.7x forward**, below the 15.0x sub-industry, 19.5x medical sector and 22.0x S&P 500 multiples, and below its own **five-year median of 18.3x**. The reason to be interested rather than dismissive: the demand environment is improving on strengthening biotech funding and sustained large-pharma investment, even as 2026 margin and pricing headwinds persist. You are buying a franchise at a discount on trough earnings — which is the only time quality CROs are ever cheap.

**Value-based care & healthcare services — the violent turnaround.** Evolent Health has gone from annualized revenue *declines* of 5.6% over two years to **+46.9% year-over-year in Q2 2026**, with $652.5M of revenue beating estimates by 9.4%. Guidance was raised to **$2.6–2.7B**. Performance Suite revenue hit $485M, up 50% sequentially, and a new oncology Performance Suite partnership will cover roughly **1.5 million lives across Medicaid and Medicare in 11 states**, targeted to launch by December 2026 and generate about **$300M in annualized revenue**. Management renewed three of its largest customers, giving visibility into 2027 growth above 25%.

**Commercial-stage biotech with real revenue.** Corcept Therapeutics posted Q2 2026 revenue of **$256.1M, +32% year-over-year**, and raised 2026 guidance to **$1.1–1.2B**. Lifyorli (relacorilant), FDA-approved in March 2026 for platinum-resistant ovarian cancer, contributed $47.6M in its first full quarters and management projects it can exceed $1B annually. A **PDUFA date of December 17, 2026** for relacorilant in Cushing's syndrome is a hard near-term catalyst. The company is profitable and debt-free with Korlym generating over $700M annually. **The problem is price**: CORT trades around **172x trailing earnings** with a market cap of roughly $12.39B against $1.1–1.2B of revenue — about **10.6x sales**. Outstanding business, real catalyst, but this is not a valuation-based entry and we are excluding it from today's picks on the same discipline applied to RGEN.

Elsewhere in commercial-stage biotech: Adaptive Biotechnologies (ADPT) reported Q1 2026 revenue of $70.9M with the MRD business contributing 95% and growing 53%; Twist Bioscience (TWST) posted record fiscal Q3 revenue of $118.4M, +23%.

**Healthcare fintech — the rate-hike winner nobody is discussing.** HealthEquity closed fiscal 2026 with revenue of **$1.313B (+9%)**, **17.8 million total accounts**, HSA assets surpassing $36B and since growing **14% to $37.9B**, and adjusted EBITDA margin above **43%** (Q4 adjusted EBITDA +23% to $132.9M). Q2 FY27 delivered EPS of $0.92 in line and revenue of $325.8M ahead of the $320.68M consensus. The critical insight: **HealthEquity earns custodial yield on member cash balances**, so a Fed that is hiking to 4%+ and holding there mechanically expands its highest-margin revenue line. Almost every other equity in the market is fighting the rate environment; this one is paid by it. And yet it trades at a **forward P/E of 18.1 against a historical 53.1**.

**Policy is the sector's principal risk, and it is real.** The "big, beautiful bill" cut more than **$1 trillion from health insurance spending over a decade**, mostly from Medicaid, per CBO. Enhanced ACA premium tax credits **expired December 31**, leaving millions of marketplace enrollees facing sharply higher net premiums in 2026, with pressure building on Congress to act. CMS is also winding down the Medicare Part D premium subsidy program implemented in 2024, worth an average $16 per month in 2026 per MedPAC. Most favored nation drug pricing and PBM reform remain live issues. **This cuts directly against volume-exposed and Medicaid-exposed models** — which is precisely why our picks skew toward diagnostics, devices, custodial fee streams and outsourced R&D rather than toward payers and Medicaid-dependent providers, and why the one Medicaid-exposed name we do recommend is sized and rated as speculative.

### Artificial Intelligence

The AI complex is showing its first real cracks — not in demand, but in **physical execution and financing**.

**Oracle triggered a force majeure notice on the OpenAI-linked New Mexico data center**, allowing it to defer payments if the site misses its 2028 launch. The proximate cause is mundane and instructive: a gas pipeline needed to power the site's Bloom Energy fuel cells slipped from September 2026 to **February 2027**. The site belongs to the **$400B five-data-center Oracle–OpenAI–SoftBank program**, funded in part by an $18B loan from roughly 20 banks. When a five-month pipeline delay can trigger contractual force majeure on a project of that scale, the binding constraint on AI buildout is confirmed to be **power and physical infrastructure, not chips or capital**.

Financing terms are also being renegotiated in ways that deserve attention. Nvidia is backing **$105B in financing for an OpenAI data center in Ohio**, and announced a **$100B strategic partnership with OpenAI to build 10GW of AI data centers** — but reporting indicates the final OpenAI data center deal came in **$145B lower than initially reported**, which some read as a signal of artificial demand in the chip channel. Separately, one previously-reported $100B OpenAI arrangement is now described as dead. Vendor financing of one's own customers is a pattern worth watching closely; it flatters near-term revenue and concentrates credit risk.

Elsewhere: **Google will fly an experimental Project Suncatcher satellite carrying four TPUs** on a SpaceX launch October 1, intended to operate about a year in low Earth orbit — an early move toward orbital compute. Google, OpenAI and Anthropic are forming a voluntary industry safety standards body, tentatively the **Frontier AI Standards Agency**, targeted for late 2026 or 2027 without government oversight. Amazon committed **over $100M to a 585,000 sq ft advanced manufacturing plant in Greenwood, Indiana**, opening by 2028.

### Technology (Broad)

The broad tech tape is being governed by a single dynamic: **the bar for rewarding good news has risen sharply**. Thursday's after-hours earnings leaned positive with more beats than misses, yet stock reactions were conspicuously mixed, with several companies beating and still trading down. The "beat but fall" pattern in TD Synnex, Verbio and CVS Group is the signal — markets are demanding not just bottom-line beats but **revenue growth and forward guidance upgrades**. Delivering on EPS alone is no longer sufficient.

This is what late-cycle multiple compression looks like before it shows up in the index. When beats stop being rewarded, forward returns come from entry multiple rather than from momentum — which is the entire basis of today's pick construction.

Costco offered a useful read on the consumer, beating with **EPS of $6.75 against a $6.55 estimate** and topping on revenue, with demand described as strong. Read alongside Darden's revenue shortfall and Scholastic's double-miss collapse, the picture is a **value-seeking, trading-down consumer** — resilient in aggregate, selectively weak in discretionary categories like dining out.

### Hardware & Semiconductors

Memory is the epicenter. **Micron trades at $1,066.01, up roughly 17% over the past month and approximately 275% in 2026** — one of the great runs of the cycle. Micron, SanDisk (SNDK) and SK Hynix were each down more than 2% in Thursday pre-market, extending Wednesday's declines, which looks like positioning ahead of the print rather than a thesis change.

The debate is not about demand but about **whether Micron can sustain peak margins as new supply arrives** — the classic memory-cycle question, and the reason a stock up 275% is a high-variance event into a print.

### Software & SaaS

Enterprise software remains a story of bifurcation between companies with demonstrable AI monetization and everyone else. The read-through from the healthcare spotlight is directly relevant: **HST is the fastest-growing healthcare segment precisely because of AI-enabled software, analytics, workflow automation and outsourcing** — vertical SaaS aimed at margin-pressured customers is outgrowing horizontal SaaS aimed at discretionary IT budgets. Evolent's AI automation contribution to its Performance Suite margins is a concrete instance of AI showing up in reported numbers rather than in a roadmap.

With the 10-year at 5.17%, the discount-rate math remains punishing for unprofitable, long-duration software. Rule-of-40 companies with real free cash flow continue to separate from growth-at-any-cost names, and we expect that spread to widen if the Fed delivers the additional hike its dot plot implies.

**Thesis.** The rare equity that is **structurally paid by the Fed's hiking cycle** — HealthEquity earns custodial yield on member HSA cash, so a 3.75%–4.00% funds rate heading toward 4.00%–4.25% mechanically expands its highest-margin revenue — yet it trades at a forward P/E of 18.1 against a historical 53.1. ## Risk Factors

**1. Hormuz escalation and a crude spike — severity: critical.** Transit volumes are already at half normal rates and the Bab el-Mandeb alternative is compromised. With the SPR at 285 million barrels — the lowest since 1982 — the policy buffer is gone. Citi puts 30% odds on Brent at $150; Wood Mackenzie will not rule out $200 if Gulf losses approach 15 mb/d. A move of that magnitude forces the Fed to keep hiking into a demand shock and is the fastest path to a broad equity de-rating.

**2. Fed tightening into a supply shock — severity: high.** A 12–0 vote, 3.7% headline and 3.4% core PCE projections, and a 4.1% year-end median dot all point to at least one more hike. Monetary policy cannot produce oil, so the committee is using a demand tool on a supply problem. The risk is over-tightening into a slowdown that the inflation prints will not reveal until it is too late.

**3. Complacent volatility against fat tails — severity: high.** A VIX at 15.61 alongside an active Middle East conflict, a hiking Fed, a 5.17% 10-year and a genuinely bimodal oil outcome is a mispricing of risk. Low implied vol makes hedges cheap and makes the market fragile to a gap move in either direction.

**4. AI infrastructure execution and financing risk — severity: moderate-to-high.** Oracle's force majeure on the OpenAI New Mexico site over a five-month pipeline delay shows that power, not silicon, is the binding constraint on a $400B program. Combined with an OpenAI–Nvidia arrangement coming in $145B below reported figures, a dead $100B deal, and Nvidia backing $105B of financing for its own customer's buildout, the AI capex cycle carries more credit and timing risk than the equity multiples imply.

**5. Healthcare policy — severity: moderate, but sector-specific and severe.** Over $1 trillion cut from health insurance spending over a decade (mostly Medicaid), expired enhanced ACA premium tax credits, the wind-down of the Medicare Part D premium subsidy, and live most-favored-nation drug pricing and PBM reform. For the spotlight sector this is the dominant idiosyncratic risk and it directly shapes today's pick construction.

## Bottom Line

The index level is doing nothing while the composition of the market changes underneath it — a flat S&P with a hiking Fed, a 5.17% 10-year and $105 crude is not stability, it is a standoff. In that environment the return comes from entry multiple and cash generation, not from momentum, and the evidence for that is the "beat but fall" pattern now punishing companies that deliver EPS without guidance upgrades. Healthcare is where the discount is widest relative to fundamentals, and the strategic acquirers — Boston Scientific at $14.5B for Penumbra, Repligen at $1.5B for BioLife — are already voting with their balance sheets; we would rather own CONMED at 10x forward and HealthEquity being paid by the very rate regime everyone else is fighting than pay 80x for the same sector's consensus names.
