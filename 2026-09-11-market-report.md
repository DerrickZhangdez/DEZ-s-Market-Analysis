## Executive Summary

Inflation, not AI, ran the tape today. The August CPI printed 3.4% year-over-year against a 3.3% consensus, with core up 0.3% month-over-month — a tenth hotter than forecast — and the market's response was immediate and unambiguous: CME FedWatch odds of a quarter-point **hike** at next week's FOMC leapt to roughly 90%, up from ~60-63% before the print. Equities faded into the close, with the S&P 500 off 0.38% to 7,718.60, the Dow down 0.51% to 53,414.25, and the Nasdaq Composite down 0.29% to 26,506.99 — the fifth consecutive down session for the major averages. Beneath the index-level damage sits the real story of this market: a triple-digit oil price driven by the Iran conflict is feeding straight into producer prices (PPI +5.4% y/y, energy +4.2%), which means the Fed is now tightening into an energy shock rather than easing into a soft landing.

The dispersion underneath is what matters for capital allocation. The AI infrastructure complex — power, cooling, interconnect, electrical construction — continues to compound backlog at rates the broad industrial economy cannot match, and it is doing so for reasons (hyperscaler capex contracts already signed) that are largely insulated from a 25bp move in the funds rate. That is today's spotlight, and it is where today's picks are concentrated.

## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The hiking cycle is back on the table.** The Fed currently sits at **3.50%–3.75%** after a 9–3 vote to hold at the July meeting — a vote whose three dissents were themselves the tell. Chair Warsh and several colleagues had conditioned continued patience on evidence of ongoing disinflation. Today's report did not deliver it.

**August CPI, released 8:30am ET today:**
- Headline: **+0.4% m/m, +3.4% y/y** (consensus 3.3%) — matching July's annual pace, with gasoline the primary culprit
- Core: **+0.3% m/m, +2.4% y/y** — the monthly figure a tenth above forecast, the annual in line

**August PPI, released yesterday:**
- Headline final demand: **+0.4% m/m, +5.4% y/y**, with final demand goods **+1.1%** and energy **+4.2%**
- Core PPI: **+0.2% m/m**

The PPI/CPI gap is the most important number on this page. Producer prices running at 5.4% against consumer prices at 3.4% means either margin compression is coming for goods producers, or a second wave of consumer inflation is in the pipeline. Neither outcome is friendly to multiples.

**Fed path.** Rate-hike odds for the September 16–17 FOMC moved from roughly 60% (post-August payrolls, +162k) to ~63% (post-PPI) to **~90%** after today's CPI. Nationwide has moved to an explicit call for a quarter-point hike. The Fed's preferred PCE gauge sits just above 3%.

**What to watch next week:** the FOMC decision and, more importantly, the dot plot and Warsh's press conference. A hike that is framed as a one-off insurance move against energy pass-through is survivable for equities; a hike accompanied by dots showing a sustained path above 4% is not. Note also that the September CPI report does not land until October 24 — the Fed will act next week without another inflation reading.

## Geopolitical Factors

The Iran conflict remains the dominant exogenous variable and has now fully transmitted into the inflation data, which is what makes it a monetary problem rather than merely a headline risk.

- **Supply disruption at scale.** The IEA estimates that nearly **20 million barrels per day** of crude and petroleum product exports have been disrupted — among the largest oil supply shocks on record.
- **Price.** US crude breached **$100/bbl** earlier in the week before dipping back below; Brent has traded around the **$89–$100** range depending on session, with fresh attacks on shipping near Yemen re-pricing the Red Sea and Bab el-Mandeb risk premium. **Diesel passed $6/gallon — a fresh record**, which matters disproportionately because diesel is the freight and agriculture input cost that propagates through the entire goods CPI basket with a lag.
- **Chokepoint risk.** The tail risk remains Strait of Hormuz interdiction or damage to Gulf production infrastructure. BlackRock and the Bank of England have both flagged geopolitical risk as the primary threat to global markets, with the transmission channel running through energy prices into stagflation.
- **Second front: US–China technology competition.** Export controls and trade friction continue to fragment semiconductor and equipment supply chains — a slower-burning risk than the Gulf, but one that directly touches the spotlight sector's component sourcing.

The IEA has stood up a 2026 Energy Crisis Policy Response Tracker cataloguing demand-curbing and consumer-support measures — a useful tell that policymakers expect this to persist rather than resolve.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: AI Infrastructure & Data Centers — Power, Cooling, Networking, Interconnect, Memory

*(Rotation: day-of-year 254 mod 6 = 2)*

This is the part of the AI trade that has quietly become more investable than the semiconductors themselves, and the reason is arithmetic. Hyperscaler capex — Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft and Meta — is running at a projected **$720–745 billion in 2026**. Of the roughly **$330 billion** flowing specifically to data center construction, approximately **60% goes to silicon and 40% to physical infrastructure**: real estate, power, cooling, networking. That 40% slice is a ~$130 billion annual market growing at rates that dwarf its historical baseline, and it is served by companies that trade at **18–35x forward earnings** versus NVIDIA near 47x.

**The binding constraint has moved from chips to electrons.** This is the single most important structural shift in the AI buildout this year. Grid interconnection queues in Northern Virginia and Dublin now stretch to **2028 and beyond**. "Speed to power" has replaced chip allocation as the determinant of whether a data center project is viable at all. The companies that can compress the interval between a signed lease and an energized facility — electrical contractors, switchgear manufacturers, utility-scale equipment suppliers — hold genuine pricing power for the first time in decades.

**Cooling is undergoing a step-change, not a cycle.** Liquid cooling adoption is moving from **under 5% of new builds in 2023 to a projected 35–40% in 2026**, because Blackwell-class rack densities leave no thermal alternative. This is a forced architectural migration on a fixed timetable, which is a far better setup than a demand cycle: the customer does not get to defer. Vertiv is the integrated incumbent (CRAH units, coolant distribution units, immersion); Modine has repositioned from legacy automotive heat exchange into near-pure-play data center cooling; Eaton bought Boyd Thermal to buy its way into liquid cooling; Trane reported Americas commercial HVAC bookings up over 120% with a record $7.8 billion backlog.

**The backlog evidence is extraordinary and worth stating precisely**, because backlog — unlike a demand forecast — is contracted revenue:
- **Quanta Services (PWR):** record **$48.5 billion** backlog; Q2 revenue +41.1% y/y to $9.56 billion, beating by 12.1%; adjusted EPS +71% y/y to $4.24, a ~29% beat; 2026 guidance raised to $34.7–35.2B revenue.
- **EMCOR (EME):** record **$15.6 billion** backlog, **+32.9% y/y**; FY2026 guidance raised to $20.0–20.5B revenue and $32.00–33.25 diluted EPS.
- **Powell Industries (POWL):** backlog to **$2.4 billion** with $934M in new orders, including a single **>$400 million** greenfield data center award — the largest order in company history — after Q2 orders rose 97% y/y.
- **Vertiv (VRT):** backlog **above $15 billion**, extending visibility into 2027; Q2 net sales $3,274M, **+24% y/y** (+18% organic), after +23% organic in Q1 with the Americas +44% organic.

**Interconnect and optics are the highest-beta expression** — and the most dangerous on valuation. Ciena is up **113%** YTD (FQ3: $1.67B revenue, +37% y/y; adjusted EPS +215%; direct cloud provider revenue +76%), driven by WaveLogic 6 into data center interconnect and the architectural shift from centralized training to distributed inference, which is fundamentally a networking problem. Fabrinet is up ~52% with record quarterly revenue and data center now **51% of total revenue, growing 68% y/y**. Credo posted 201.5% y/y revenue growth at a 68.5% GAAP gross margin. The fundamentals here are real; the entry prices on several of these names are not.

**Storage is the under-discussed leg.** Seagate's nearline capacity is **nearly fully allocated through 2027**, with Mozaic HAMR drives at 44TB shipping to five major cloud customers — annual revenue +34% to $12.2B and EPS +92% to $15.58. The HDD market is forecast to grow from $51.8B in 2026 to $69.7B by 2031. AI data lakes need cold capacity at a scale flash cannot economically serve, and this is a genuine oligopoly.

**Where the risk sits.** Much of this complex has already re-rated violently — Seagate +209% YTD, Ciena +113%, Modine +173% over 52 weeks, Powell +87% YTD. The infrastructure trade is no longer undiscovered. Today's picks therefore deliberately weight toward the names where backlog coverage and forward multiples still line up, and explicitly down-rate the ones where they do not.

Six names, all drawn from today's AI infrastructure spotlight, spanning **six distinct sub-sectors**: electrical/mechanical construction services, integrated power & thermal systems, photonics contract manufacturing, grid & utility electrical components, enterprise data management, and HVAC/liquid cooling. ### 1. EME — EMCOR Group, Inc. *(Electrical & mechanical construction and facilities services)*

- **Thesis:** The cheapest credible way to own the data center power bottleneck. EMCOR converts the "speed to power" constraint directly into revenue — it is the firm physically installing the electrical and mechanical systems — and it is doing so at a multiple below both its sector and its own growth rate.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Trailing P/E **23.4x** (on EPS of $32.24 at $754.16, as of Sept 4). Forward P/E **20.3x**. That forward multiple sits **9% below the Industrials sector average of 25.7x** and far below the construction industry average near 48x. A DCF screen puts the shares ~43% undervalued; I would not underwrite that figure, but the direction is right. EV/EBITDA and P/S are modest by construction-sector standards given the asset-light services model. *PEG: approximately 1.0–1.2 on management's own guidance — estimated.*
- **Growth Profile:** FY2026 guidance raised to **$20.0–20.5B revenue** with **$32.00–33.25 diluted EPS** and a 9.5–9.8% operating margin. Backlog at a record **$15.6B, +32.9% y/y** — and critically, management has stated the backlog growth is concentrated in high-margin, technically demanding work, which is why margins are expanding alongside volume rather than compressing.
- **Competitive Moat:** Skilled-trade labor scarcity is the moat, and it is a genuinely hard one to breach. There is no capital solution to a shortage of licensed electricians and mechanical engineers with data-center-grade certifications. EMCOR's scale, multi-region union relationships, and safety/execution record make it one of a very short list of contractors a hyperscaler will trust with a billion-dollar critical-facility program on a fixed timeline.
- **Key Risk:** This is a project-driven business with real cyclicality. A hyperscaler capex digestion pause — the risk the Oracle reaction is arguably pricing — would hit new-order intake well before it hits the backlog, and the stock would de-rate on the leading indicator. Labor cost inflation also compresses margin on fixed-price work.
- **Rating:** **Strong Buy**

### 2. VRT — Vertiv Holdings Co *(Integrated data center power & thermal management)*

- **Thesis:** The category-defining incumbent in the one part of the AI buildout undergoing a forced architectural change. Liquid cooling going from <5% to 35–40% of new builds is not a demand forecast — it is a physics requirement of Blackwell-class rack density, and Vertiv sells the integrated power-and-cooling stack rather than a component.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Forward P/E **38.4x** (as of Aug 31), with sources ranging **33x to 47.7x NTM** depending on the price snapshot — the stock has been volatile, including a 15% drawdown that moved the forward multiple across that band. Against ~43% EPS growth this implies a **PEG near 1.0–1.1**, which is cheaper than the S&P on a growth-adjusted basis despite the optically high headline multiple. The sector comparison is stark: the average forward P/E in US Electrical is ~17x, so this is a premium that must be earned every quarter.
- **Growth Profile:** Q2 net sales **$3,274M, +24% y/y** (+18% organic). Q1 organic growth **+23%**, Americas **+44% organic**. Backlog **above $15B**, extending visibility into 2027. Stock up ~73% in 2026.
- **Competitive Moat:** Installed base and service. Vertiv's thermal and power equipment sits inside a large share of the world's existing critical facilities, and the service/maintenance annuity on that base both funds R&D and creates enormous switching friction — an operator does not re-platform cooling vendors mid-fleet. The integrated power+thermal offering is also genuinely difficult to replicate: competitors tend to hold one leg or the other.
- **Key Risk:** Multiple compression. At ~38x forward, the stock is priced for sustained high-30s/40s EPS growth; the modeled path to a 32x multiple assumes it matures into that valuation gracefully rather than abruptly. Eaton's Boyd Thermal acquisition signals that well-capitalized incumbents are buying their way into liquid cooling, and competitive intensity in CDUs will rise from here.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

### 3. FN — Fabrinet *(Optical & photonic contract manufacturing)*

- **Thesis:** The lowest-drama way to own optical interconnect. Fabrinet takes a manufacturing-services margin on the entire 800G/1.6T optics transition without having to win the technology bet on any single transceiver architecture — and it trades at roughly half the multiple of the component designers whose products it builds.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Forward P/E **26.5x** (as of Aug 20), against a trailing P/E of **51.4x** — that spread is the market pricing in the earnings ramp already visible in the order book. The forward figure is ~23% above the Hardware industry median, a defensible premium given the growth differential. *Against ~36% revenue growth, PEG is comfortably below 1.0 — estimated.* For contrast: Credo and Astera Labs carry substantial forward EV/Sales premiums, while Ciena's forward P/E is reported anywhere from 32x to 48x after a 113% YTD run.
- **Growth Profile:** FY2026 revenue **$4.64B, +35.7% y/y** (from $3.42B). Q4 FY2026 revenue **$1,316M, +45% y/y** — the **12th consecutive quarter of record sales**. Data center revenue **$669M, or 51% of total, +68% y/y**. Stock up ~52%.
- **Competitive Moat:** Precision optical assembly at scale is a genuinely specialized capability with high qualification barriers — customers spend 12–24 months qualifying a line, and requalifying elsewhere is expensive and slow. Fabrinet's Thailand manufacturing base offers cost and geopolitical positioning advantages relative to both Chinese and domestic US alternatives, which is increasingly a procurement criterion rather than a cost criterion.
- **Key Risk:** Customer concentration is the structural weakness — a large share of revenue flows through a handful of optical module and networking OEMs, and the loss or in-sourcing of a single major program would be material. Contract manufacturing margins are also structurally thin, so volume declines de-lever quickly.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

### 4. HUBB — Hubbell Incorporated *(Grid, utility & electrical infrastructure components)*

- **Thesis:** The picks-and-shovels play one layer upstream of the data center fence line. Every megawatt a hyperscaler contracts for requires utility-side transmission and distribution hardware that must be built regardless of which operator ultimately wins the AI workload — and unlike the contractors, Hubbell's utility business carries a regulated-spending floor underneath the AI upside.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Approximately **27.3x 2026 EPS**, with sell-side targets around $559 implying roughly **24x** next year's earnings. Recent price near **$483.66** against a commonly cited fair value near **$550.77** — a ~5–12% discount depending on the model. Notably, **EV/Sales remains below its own long-term average** even as the P/E sits above market, which suggests the premium is a margin story rather than a revenue-hype story. *PEG: approximately 2.0–2.5 on high-single to low-double-digit EPS growth — estimated, and the least attractive on this list.*
- **Growth Profile:** FY2026 guidance raised to **$20.25–20.55 adjusted EPS**. Segment margins are strong and rising: Utility Solutions **24.1%**, Electrical Solutions **20.2%**. The **$825 million DMC Power acquisition** pushes directly into AI data-center power infrastructure. Management and analysts see high-single to low-double-digit EPS growth sustaining beyond 2026 on grid modernization and T&D spending.
- **Competitive Moat:** Utility specification lock-in. Hubbell's products are written into utility engineering standards and approved-vendor lists, a position accumulated over decades that a new entrant cannot buy. Switching costs are asymmetric — a utility risks reliability penalties to save a few basis points on connectors, so it doesn't.
- **Key Risk:** This is the most fully-valued name on the list relative to its growth rate, and the PEG shows it. If grid capex normalizes from supercycle levels, or if utility rate cases start getting rejected on affordability grounds — a live political risk with retail electricity prices rising — the multiple has meaningful downside. Buy the business, not the entry price; size accordingly.
- **Rating:** **Accumulate**

### 5. NTAP — NetApp, Inc. *(Enterprise data management & hybrid cloud storage)*

- **Thesis:** The contrarian entry in a sub-sector where everything else has already run. Storage is indispensable to AI — models are useless without governed, accessible data — yet NTAP is **down ~19.8% over the past year while Seagate is up ~295%**. That divergence reflects a real concern about NetApp's positioning, but the valuation now compensates for it in a way the rest of the AI infrastructure complex simply does not.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Forward P/E **18.9x** (as of Sept 7), trailing **20.1x**; one dataset reports forward as low as **14.25x**, and I would treat 15–19x as the honest range. That is roughly in line with the Software industry median of 17.2x — for a company with direct AI infrastructure exposure. Free cash flow **$1.34B** (FY2025) on operating cash flow of $1.51B. Consensus target **$195.13**, ~5.6% above the recent price, with an average rating of Buy across 20 analysts. **This is the only pick on the list trading at a market-or-below multiple.**
- **Growth Profile:** Modest by AI-complex standards, and that is the point — the stock is not priced for heroics. Q3 exceeded earnings estimates on AI-ready infrastructure and all-flash array demand; management raised full-year guidance on broad-based strength. Growth is high-single-digit rather than the 30–40% of the hardware names.
- **Competitive Moat:** ONTAP's entrenchment in enterprise data estates. Data gravity is the most durable moat in infrastructure — petabytes of governed, compliance-tagged enterprise data with established snapshot, replication and retention policies do not migrate cheaply or quickly. NetApp's hybrid-cloud position (first-party integrations with the major clouds) is a genuine differentiator versus pure on-premise vendors.
- **Key Risk:** The bear case is real and the price reflects it: NetApp may be structurally disintermediated as workloads move to cloud-native object storage and as the highest-growth AI storage spend flows to hyperscaler-internal systems and to flash/HDD suppliers rather than to enterprise array vendors. A year of share-price underperformance is evidence, not noise. This is a value pick with a live thesis risk, which is why it is sized as an Accumulate rather than a Buy.
- **Rating:** **Accumulate**

### 6. MOD — Modine Manufacturing Company *(Thermal management / data center liquid cooling)*

- **Thesis:** The purest mid-cap expression of the liquid cooling transition, executing a genuine business transformation from legacy automotive heat exchange into data center thermal management. Included for the quality of the operating trajectory — flagged explicitly for the price.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Market capitalization **~$14.65B** at **$277.46** (June 23 reference), after a **+173% 52-week move**. Analyst targets have been raised to roughly **$325**, though other published targets sit near $183 — an unusually wide dispersion that itself signals how little consensus exists on fair value. **This is the most valuation-stretched name in this report and I am not going to pretend otherwise.** The multiple embeds the successful execution of a transformation that is real but incomplete.
- **Growth Profile:** The operating numbers are outstanding. Data center revenue **+90% in Q1 FY2027**, with a third consecutive record order quarter and **backlog more than doubling**. Management targets doubling data center revenue from ~$1B in FY2026 to **$2B by FY2028**, guiding **50–70% annual data center growth** through FY2028 and **20–35% total net sales growth** in FY2027. A **$4B data center cooling agreement** anchors the visibility.
- **Competitive Moat:** Decades of thermal engineering IP transferred from a brutally demanding automotive customer base into a market that is only now discovering it needs that expertise. Coolant distribution units and rear-door heat exchangers are engineering-qualified into rack designs — once specified into a hyperscaler's reference architecture, displacement is slow.
- **Key Risk:** Valuation first, execution second. At this multiple after this run, any quarter that shows data center growth decelerating below the 50% guidance floor — or the margin pressure already visible in recent quarters worsening — would produce a severe drawdown. Modine also faces well-capitalized competition from Vertiv and from Eaton's acquired Boyd Thermal capability, and the legacy automotive segment remains a cyclical drag on consolidated results.
- **Rating:** **Accumulate** *(on weakness only — I would not initiate a full position at current levels)*

### Portfolio Construction Notes

**Sub-sector distribution:** EME (construction services) / VRT (integrated power & thermal systems) / FN (photonics contract manufacturing) / HUBB (grid & utility components) / NTAP (data management software & systems) / MOD (HVAC & liquid cooling) — six sub-sectors, satisfying the three-sub-sector minimum with margin. VRT and MOD both touch cooling; VRT is an integrated power-and-thermal platform while MOD is a thermal pure-play, and they are sized as complements rather than duplicates. Investors wanting no overlap should hold one.

**Valuation discipline:** The forward multiples span **~15–19x (NTAP)** through **20x (EME)**, **26x (FN)**, **27x (HUBB)** to **38x+ (VRT)** and a stretched MOD. That ladder is deliberate. This report deliberately **declined to pick** several of the most-discussed spotlight names on valuation grounds: **CIEN** (+113% YTD, forward P/E reported 32–48x), **STX** (+209% YTD, though 24x forward is defensible), **POWL** (33x forward, +87% YTD, and a project-concentration profile that makes the multiple hard to justify), and **CRDO** (extreme forward EV/Sales). Their fundamentals are strong; their entry prices are not, and a report that recommended all of them would be a momentum list wearing a valuation costume.

**Coverage-only names.** The following were discussed above and are **explicitly not recommendations**: NVIDIA, Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta, Amazon, Oracle, Adobe, AMD, SK hynix, Dell, Trane, Eaton, Quanta Services, Powell Industries, Ciena, Seagate, Credo, Astera Labs, Lumentum, Applied Optoelectronics, Celestica, Constellation Energy, Digital Realty, Equinix, Tesla, Apple, RH, Check Point, Element Solutions, International Paper, Atmos Energy, Shopify, HP, Fortinet, Global Payments.

**Ticker-collision checks.** **MOD** (Modine Manufacturing, NYSE) is the most dangerous symbol in this report — it is distinct from **MODG** (Topgolf Callaway) and from any "MOD"-prefixed fund share class; verify the CUSIP before entry. **EME** (EMCOR Group) is distinct from **EMR** (Emerson Electric) and from **EMN** (Eastman Chemical). **FN** (Fabrinet) is a two-letter symbol and is distinct from **FNF**, **FN**-prefixed Fannie Mae instruments, and from **FNV** (Franco-Nevada) — note also that Fabrinet is Cayman-domiciled with Thailand operations. **HUBB** (Hubbell) is distinct from **HUB** and from **HUBS** (HubSpot) — a one-character difference between an electrical-equipment industrial and a SaaS company, and the second most dangerous collision here. **NTAP** (NetApp) is distinct from **NTAB** and from **NTNX** (Nutanix, a frequently-confused adjacent infrastructure name not picked here). **VRT** (Vertiv) is distinct from **VRTX** (Vertex Pharmaceuticals), from **VRTV**, and from **VIRT** (Virtu Financial, picked 08-31 and on today's exclusion list) — the VRT/VIRT pair is a live transposition risk given the recent report history.

**Sizing guidance.** Given a ~90% probability of a rate hike next week and an unresolved energy shock, these are positions to build in tranches rather than at once. The AI infrastructure thesis is measured in years of contracted backlog; it does not require being fully invested before Wednesday.

## Risk Factors

**1. A hawkish Fed hiking into an energy shock — SEVERE.** The highest-severity risk on the board. ~90% odds of a hike next week, and the Fed will not see another CPI print until October 24. The specific danger is not the 25bp itself but the framing: if the dots show a sustained path above 4%, the entire equity complex re-prices on a higher discount rate simultaneously. Long-duration growth equities — which describes most of the AI infrastructure trade — carry the largest sensitivity.

**2. Energy price escalation and the stagflation channel — SEVERE.** Crude near $100, diesel at a record above $6, roughly 20 mb/d of export capacity disrupted, and an unresolved Iran conflict. A Strait of Hormuz interdiction or a strike on Gulf production infrastructure would push oil substantially higher into an economy already running 3.4% CPI — a stagflationary combination in which the Fed has no good option and equities and bonds sell off together.

**3. Hyperscaler capex digestion — HIGH.** The entire spotlight thesis rests on $720–745B of 2026 capex converting into orders. The Oracle reaction is the tell worth watching: the market is now scrutinizing how AI buildout is *funded*, not merely how fast it grows. Any hyperscaler guiding to capex moderation, or any credit-market event that raises the cost of financing data center construction, would hit the infrastructure names harder than the semiconductors because their multiples are supported by backlog visibility rather than by installed-base cash flow.

**4. Valuation compression across the AI infrastructure complex — HIGH.** Seagate +209%, Modine +173%, Ciena +113%, Powell +87%. This is no longer an undiscovered trade. Crowded positioning in a narrow set of names means the unwinds are violent and correlated, and a rate-driven de-rating would not discriminate between the names with 20x forward multiples and the ones with 45x.

**5. PPI-to-CPI margin squeeze — MODERATE.** Producer prices at 5.4% against consumer prices at 3.4% resolves one of two ways: either producers absorb it, which compresses goods-sector margins into Q4, or they pass it through, which keeps the Fed hiking. Watch Q3 gross margin guidance across industrials and consumer goods for which path the economy takes. Fixed-price contractors — EMCOR among them — sit directly in this channel.

## Bottom Line

Today the market learned that the Fed is likely hiking next week into an oil-driven inflation impulse it cannot control with rates, and it de-rated accordingly — but the selling was indiscriminate in a way that creates opportunity. The AI infrastructure buildout is governed by contracted backlog measured in years and by a power constraint measured in grid interconnection queues stretching to 2028; neither is meaningfully altered by 25 basis points. Own the suppliers to the buildout rather than its principals, favor the names where backlog coverage and forward multiples still reconcile — EMCOR at 20x forward with a record backlog up 33% is the cleanest expression of that — and build positions in tranches, because a hawkish dot plot on Wednesday will likely offer better entries than today's close.
