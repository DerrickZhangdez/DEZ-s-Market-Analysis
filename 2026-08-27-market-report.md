## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The Fed is not easing — and the hawks are getting louder.** The FOMC left the funds rate unchanged at **3.50%–3.75%** in July, a fifth consecutive hold. The critical detail is the dissent pattern: **three members dissented in favor of a 25bp *increase***, and markets had assigned roughly a one-in-three probability to a hike at that meeting. That is not a committee looking for an excuse to cut.

**Inflation is the reason.** Core PCE has been moving the wrong way for three quarters — from 3.0% in December 2025 to 3.3% by June 2026. July's reading, released 8/26, came in at **+0.2% m/m and +3.3% y/y**, in line with expectations; Bloomberg characterized it as a "muted advance" with **consumer spending stalling**. The June SEP projects PCE inflation at 3.6% for 2026 and core at 3.3%, falling only to 2.5% in 2027 — i.e., the Fed's own forecast concedes roughly two more years above target.

**What that means for positioning.** The stalling-spending / sticky-inflation combination is the least comfortable macro mix for equities: it caps the Fed's willingness to support growth while leaving the door open to tightening. The 10-year at **4.74%** (+0.89% today) is the transmission channel to watch — long-duration equity multiples and rate-sensitive small caps are both exposed. Part of the inflation persistence reflects genuine supply shocks, energy prominently among them, which links directly to the geopolitical section below.

**This week/next:** September FOMC positioning dominates. Any upside surprise in the next CPI or employment cost print materially raises hike odds from here.

## Geopolitical Factors

Energy is the live geopolitical transmission mechanism, and 2026 has been violently two-sided:

- **The Strait of Hormuz remains the central issue.** Crude and petroleum liquids transiting Hormuz averaged **4.9 million b/d in Q2 2026, down from 21.6 million b/d in Q4 2025** before the conflict began. That is a structural re-routing of global crude logistics, not a headline.
- **Price path has been whipsawed by diplomacy.** Following a June US–Iran memorandum of understanding, Brent fell to **$69** on July 2. Renewed tanker attacks in Hormuz then drove Brent as high as **$105 on July 23**. It fell back below $80 on August 4 (**$79.36**) as US–Iran talks resumed, then reversed again to close above **$90 on August 17 ($90.87)**.
- **The security architecture is shifting.** On August 7, Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan signed the **Mecca Mutual Defense Agreement**, combining Saudi financial weight, Turkish defense manufacturing and Pakistani nuclear/military experience — a visible move away from dependence on US security guarantees. The second-order implication is a more autonomous, less predictable Gulf posture.

**Investment read-through:** Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb disruptions establish a firm *floor* under crude while diplomatic progress caps the upside — a wide, headline-driven range rather than a directional trend. This argues for exposure to energy **services and equipment** (paid on activity and international/offshore capex cycles) over commodity price beta, which is reflected in today's picks.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: Industrials, Energy, Materials & "Picks-and-Shovels" Plays

*(Day-of-year 239 → 239 mod 6 = 5)*

This is the sector where the AI story stops being software and becomes steel, copper, concrete and turbines — and where the valuation dispersion is widest.

**The demand case is now measurable, not thematic.** Roughly **80% of the growth in private domestic demand in H1 2025 came from data-center and high-tech investment**. Industrials have been one of 2026's stronger sectors, supported by four simultaneous tailwinds: AI infrastructure buildout, defense demand, reshoring, and improving manufacturing activity.

**But the obvious names are fully priced.** This is the crux of today's analysis. The industrials sector trades at roughly a **24x P/E — above the S&P average** — and the most direct AI-infrastructure beneficiaries have already re-rated:

- **Comfort Systems (FIX)** has returned ~80% YTD on record backlog, expanding margins and modular-construction exposure (Zacks #1).
- **Sterling Infrastructure (STRL)** posted a Q1 blowout — $825M revenue vs. $603M expected, EPS $3.59 vs. $2.29, sales up >90% y/y — and raised FY26 guidance to $3.7–3.8B (+20% at the midpoint) on $3.8B signed backlog and a $6.5B pipeline.
- **MYR Group (MYRG)** carries record backlog of **$3.16B (+20% y/y)** including large Xcel transmission awards extending into 2027, delivered record Q2 revenue of $1.08B and record EBITDA of $85M, and is acquiring Valley Electric and Comet Electric for ~$328M. It also trades at roughly **31–41x forward earnings**, which one analysis frames as fully pricing both the AI data-center and grid-electrification super-cycles with minimal margin of safety.
- **EMCOR** has identified electrical construction as its primary M&A focus, competing directly for the same assets.

Together, Sterling, Quanta and Comfort Systems each own a distinct stage of data-center construction — site preparation, grid connection, HVAC installation. Excellent businesses; the market knows.

**Two names screened well and were rejected on structure, not quality:**

- **Arcosa (ACA)** is being acquired by **CRH for $150/share all-cash** ($8.5B, ~11.5x 2026E adjusted EBITDA, a 25% premium to the 60-day VWAP). With shares near $145, this is a merger-arbitrage spread, not a fundamental investment. **Not a pick.**
- **Materion (MTRN)** and **Carpenter Technology (CRS)** both have genuinely excellent operating momentum — Materion raised FY26 adjusted EPS guidance to $6.80–$7.20 on record 23.3% EBITDA margins with aerospace/defense +40% and semiconductor +23% y/y; Carpenter is guiding FY26 operating income up ~32% with record 35.6% segment margins on the Boeing/Airbus engine ramp. But after Materion's 25% single-day surge to ~$284 (≈40x forward) and with Carpenter at 42–50x, neither offers a valuation-based entry today. **Coverage only.**

**Where the value actually is.** The mid-cap discount is the opportunity: mid-caps have delivered earnings growth **nearly matching large-cap peers while their stocks have not been rewarded**, producing one of the widest mid-vs-large valuation gaps of this cycle. The value sits one layer removed from the data-center headline — in **energy services, utility-grade infrastructure products, construction materials and specialty chemicals**, where the same electrification and reshoring demand exists at 13–22x rather than 30–50x.

**Energy picks-and-shovels specifics.** Gas compression is the clearest LNG derivative: **Kodiak Gas (KGS), Archrock (AROC) and USA Compression (USAC)** are the primary plays, yielding 8–10% against a projected **15% increase in LNG export demand**. Archrock raised its Q2 2026 dividend to $0.23 ($0.92 annualized, +10% y/y), targets returning 25–35% of operating cash flow, and management sees LNG-linked gas demand rising from ~20 Bcf/d in 2026 to 35 Bcf/d by 2030 — but at a **9.85x TTM EV/EBITDA and 3.8x forward P/S versus 1.5x for the sub-industry**, with current-year EPS growth projected to *decline*, it is a yield vehicle at a premium multiple rather than a valuation pick. I preferred the oilfield equipment and international-services names below.

On the broader energy sector: it outperformed the market in H1 2026, and there are consequently **few genuinely undervalued names left** — which makes the two exceptions below worth isolating.

### Artificial Intelligence

**Nvidia's print was the event, and the reaction was the signal.** Q2 FY2027 delivered **Data Center revenue of $89.02B, up 117% y/y**, on a ~$96B total quarter, with both revenue and adjusted EPS beating — a fifth consecutive quarter above expectations. Guidance was arguably the stronger half: **Q3 revenue of $108B ±2%** at ~74% non-GAAP gross margin, and critically, **that guide assumes zero China compute**. Blackwell Ultra drove the acceleration, with hyperscale deployment ramping fast enough to more than double the segment y/y.

The number that should reframe how investors think about this cycle: **supply commitments have grown to $279B, largely tied to memory procurement for the Vera Rubin platform**, already in full production at Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure. Nvidia has pre-committed a quarter-trillion dollars of purchase obligations — an enormous vote of confidence and an enormous fixed obligation, and the single best argument for owning the suppliers of power, cooling, materials and infrastructure rather than only the chip.

Yet the stock fell 0.98%. Four straight quarters of post-earnings declines suggest expectations, not results, are the binding constraint.

### Hardware & Semiconductors

**This is the market's genuine fault line.** The semiconductor sector is in its **sharpest correction since 2022**, memory-led, with **chip stocks having shed more than $1 trillion** in the late-July drawdown. Micron and SK Hynix ADRs fell more than 8% and **Sandisk dropped 13%** at the worst of it.

Four drivers compounded:
1. **Broadcom's cautious AI outlook** — notable because its own guidance still calls for AI semiconductor revenue to grow **over 200% y/y to $16.0B**;
2. a deepening **memory downcycle** in commodity DRAM/NAND;
3. a projected **collapse in global smartphone demand**;
4. **CXMT's blockbuster Shanghai listing**, renewing fears that Chinese memory capacity expansion compresses pricing.

The resulting structure is a **bifurcated memory market**: AI-centric HBM and high-spec products command premiums while commodity DRAM faces margin compression as mobile and notebook end-markets stay tepid. Set against **record global semiconductor sales of $120.6B in May 2026 (+104.1% y/y)**, the selloff looks driven by valuation and near-term margin pressure rather than fundamental AI demand deterioration — but "valuation-driven" corrections in a sector this crowded can run further than fundamentals justify. This is precisely why today's picks avoid semis entirely.

### 1. WFRD — Weatherford International plc

**Thesis:** The cheapest quality asset in oilfield services, with ~80% international revenue exposure — precisely the capex cycle that firms when Brent holds a $80–90 floor, and precisely the geography insulated from North American shale discipline. Management has guided to a softer H1 followed by an H2 rebound as international contract awards convert into active work, which means the setup is a second-half re-rating into an already-low multiple.

**Valuation Metrics:** P/E ~**16.5x** trailing — a significant discount to both its industry and the broader market. Market cap ~$5.81B. Profitability rated 9/10 by screening services, with an improving balance sheet.

**Growth Profile:** FY2026 revenue guided to **$4.6–5.05B**, flat to slightly down — this is explicitly *not* a revenue-growth story. The earnings case rests on margin improvement via cost control and productivity, plus cash conversion: **2025 adjusted FCF of $466M at a 43.7% conversion rate**, with 2026 conversion guided to the low-to-mid 40s. Revenue is diversified across Well Construction & Completions (~$1.9B), Drilling & Evaluation (~$1.3B) and Production & Intervention (~$1.3B).

**Competitive Moat:** Entrenched international and national-oil-company relationships with high switching costs on multi-year integrated contracts; a global service footprint that only three or four companies can replicate; and technical depth in managed pressure drilling and well intervention where price is not the primary award criterion.

**Key Risk:** The flat-to-down revenue guide leaves no room for execution error — if H2 international activity slips, the thesis becomes a value trap rather than a re-rating. Tariff impacts and offshore project timing are the named near-term pressures.

**Rating: Buy**

### 2. WHD — Cactus, Inc.

**Thesis:** A fortress balance sheet attached to genuine hypergrowth — Q2 CY2026 revenue grew **64.3% y/y** while the company sits on net cash, a combination almost nonexistent in energy equipment. The Spoolable Technologies segment gives it a consumable-products profile inside a cyclical industry.

**Valuation Metrics:** **EV/EBITDA of 11.29x** (mid-July), with a later August reading of 14.84x — versus an oil & gas industry median of 7.45x. **Net cash of $405.96M, or $5.08/share** ($445.61M cash against just $39.65M debt) on a ~$3.24B market cap, meaning roughly 12% of the market cap is net cash. Sell-side Buy rating with an $82 target; one DCF-based analysis frames the shares as ~49% undervalued.

**Growth Profile:** Q2 CY2026 revenue of **$449.5M (+64.3% y/y)** with adjusted EBITDA of **$132.8M — a 29.5% margin**. Those are structurally high margins for equipment manufacturing, reflecting the rental and aftermarket mix rather than one-time pricing.

**Competitive Moat:** Proprietary wellhead and pressure-control designs with a large installed base that pulls through high-margin rental and repair revenue; spoolable pipe is a genuine consumable with switching costs at the operator level; and the net cash position lets it take share through downturns when leveraged competitors cannot bid.

**Key Risk:** It trades at a clear premium to the industry median multiple, so the valuation depends on that 60%+ growth rate persisting. North American completions activity is the most volatile spending line in energy — a US shale slowdown hits Cactus faster and harder than it hits Weatherford. I am deliberately holding both, as they are exposed to different halves of the world.

**Rating: Buy**

### 3. VMI — Valmont Industries, Inc.

**Thesis:** The purest reasonably-priced way to own grid electrification. Valmont makes the transmission structures and utility steel poles that every data center interconnection and grid-modernization project physically requires, and management describes demand as **supply-constrained, not demand-constrained** — the strongest possible pricing environment. It trades at roughly half the multiple of the electrical contractors chasing the same demand.

**Valuation Metrics:** At roughly **$498** against FY2026 EPS guidance of **$22.25–$23.50**, the forward P/E is **~21.6x** — versus 31–41x for MYR Group and ~24x for the industrials sector. With ~20% EPS growth at the guidance midpoint, **PEG ≈ 1.1**. The Street's mean target sits near $527.

**Growth Profile:** FY2026 sales guided to **$4.3–4.45B (+6.7%)** with EPS up nearly **20%** at the midpoint — margin expansion, not just volume. Infrastructure sales grew **14.8% y/y** with **North America Utility up 33.9%** on both price and volume. Long-term guidance targets a **17% operating margin and $35 EPS by 2029**, implying a mid-teens EPS CAGR, and investments in capacity, automation and AI are expected to unlock **$350–400M of incremental annual revenue**.

**Competitive Moat:** Utility-qualified supplier status is a multi-year approval process that effectively locks out new entrants; galvanizing and large-structure fabrication capacity is capital-intensive and currently industry-wide constrained; and freight economics on very large steel structures create natural regional monopolies.

**Key Risk:** The agriculture/irrigation segment is a genuine cyclical drag with its own commodity-price cycle that has nothing to do with the grid thesis, and can mask infrastructure strength in consolidated results. Steel input costs are passed through with a lag, compressing margins in a rising-price environment — a live risk given the inflation backdrop above.

**Rating: Strong Buy**

### 4. EXP — Eagle Materials Inc.

**Thesis:** High-return-on-capital cement and aggregates assets at 13x forward earnings, with volumes accelerating sharply. Cement is the most structurally attractive materials business in North America — regional near-monopolies, no import substitution economics, and infrastructure demand independent of the housing cycle.

**Valuation Metrics:** **Forward P/E (non-GAAP) of 13.38x** and a current P/E of **16.1x** — above the global basic-materials average of 14.1x but far below a 30.2x peer average. Fair value estimated at **$223.56** against a recent close of **$211.34**. JPMorgan upgraded the shares from Underweight to Neutral on June 2, 2026 with a $225 target.

**Growth Profile:** Fiscal Q4 2026 delivered **cement sales volume +15%** and **organic aggregates sales volume +24%** — volume acceleration of that magnitude in heavy materials indicates real end-market demand, not price-driven optics, and it flows through a high-fixed-cost base with substantial operating leverage.

**Competitive Moat:** Cement plants require permits that are effectively unobtainable for new entrants, and the weight-to-value ratio makes shipping cement more than ~150 miles uneconomic — producing durable regional pricing power. Aggregates reserves are a finite, permitted, appreciating asset base.

**Key Risk:** Residential construction exposure makes it directly rate-sensitive, and with the Fed potentially *hiking* in September, that is a real and immediate threat to the volume trajectory. Cement is also cyclical and regionally concentrated, so a slowdown in its core geographies hits disproportionately.

**Rating: Buy**

### 5. CBT — Cabot Corporation

**Thesis:** A specialty chemicals franchise generating a **~9.4% free cash flow yield** while trading below its own historical multiple and at a discount to inferior peers — with battery materials as unpriced optionality. This is the classic setup where the market extrapolates chemicals-sector malaise onto a company whose margins say otherwise.

**Valuation Metrics:** Trading **below its 5-year average EV/EBITDA of 7.6x**, and at a discount to peers including Orion Engineered Carbons **despite superior margins and growth prospects**. Free cash flow yield **~9.4%**. DCF analysis implies fair value of **$88–105**, with peer-multiple cross-checks implying above $100.

**Growth Profile:** **EPS expected to grow ~20% over the next year**, which comfortably covers the dividend. The quarterly dividend of **$0.4725** yields ~2.1%, has grown at a **7.9% average annual rate over ten years**, and has never been materially cut — a rare record in specialty chemicals.

**Competitive Moat:** Carbon black is a scale-and-logistics business with high barriers: plants are capital-intensive, environmentally permitted, and located adjacent to customers. Cabot is qualified into tire manufacturers' specifications, where requalification is slow and costly. Its battery-materials position extends the same conductive-additives technology into a structurally growing end market.

**Key Risk:** Carbon black demand is tied to tire and automotive production, so a consumer slowdown — and July's *stalling* consumer spending is a warning — hits volumes directly. The broader chemicals sector remains plagued by stagnant demand and overcapacity, which can compress multiples regardless of company-specific execution.

**Rating: Accumulate**

### 6. ACM — AECOM

**Thesis:** Record backlog at a 14x multiple, in an asset-light professional services model that captures infrastructure spending without taking construction balance-sheet risk. AECOM gets paid to design the projects the other five picks supply, and it trades at a discount to peers because of a one-time charge that I believe is separable from the earnings power.

**Valuation Metrics:** **Forward 12-month P/E of 14.01x**, an explicit discount to industry peers — and against **~50x for Tutor Perini** and 31–41x for MYR Group, it is the cheapest way to own the same infrastructure backlog.

**Growth Profile:** Q3 FY2026 produced **$4.2B of quarterly wins** and backlog growth of **13% to an all-time high of $27.8B** — roughly two years of visible revenue. Consensus implies EPS growth of **13.5% in FY2026 and 12% in FY2027**, putting **PEG near 1.1**.

**Competitive Moat:** Technical pre-qualification for large public infrastructure programs is a genuine barrier — agencies award to a short list of firms with the requisite credentials and past performance. The asset-light model produces high returns on invested capital, and the design position at the front of the project lifecycle creates a pipeline into downstream program-management revenue.

**Key Risk:** **The $337M project charge taken in Q3 FY2026 is the central concern, and I want to be direct about it** — it suggests legacy project risk that may not be fully contained, and analysts note that margin expansion has remained elusive despite backlog growth. This is why ACM is rated Accumulate rather than Buy: the valuation discount is real but so is the reason for it. Position sizing should reflect that the charge could prove to be the first of several rather than the last.

**Rating: Accumulate**

## Risk Factors

**1. A September Fed hike — SEVERE, and underpriced.** This is the top risk precisely because the market is not positioned for it. Three FOMC members already dissented in favor of a hike; core PCE is at 3.3% and has *risen* 30bp over three quarters; the Fed's own SEP concedes above-target inflation into 2028. With the 10-year at 4.74% and the Russell 2000 up 20.76% YTD, a hike would hit the year's leadership hardest. Small and mid-caps, and rate-sensitive construction demand, are the direct casualties — a risk my EXP position carries explicitly.

**2. The semiconductor correction broadening into the whole AI complex — HIGH.** Chip stocks have already shed over $1 trillion in the sharpest correction since 2022. Commodity DRAM/NAND pricing is still falling, CXMT's capacity expansion is a structural overhang, and smartphone demand is projected to collapse. The transmission risk is sentiment: if memory weakness gets re-read as *AI* weakness, the correction spreads to the infrastructure names regardless of their order books.

**3. Energy supply shock via Hormuz — HIGH.** Hormuz throughput at 4.9M b/d against 21.6M b/d pre-conflict means the system has no spare buffer. Brent has traversed $69 → $105 → $79 → $90 within two months on headlines alone. A renewed tanker attack sends crude sharply higher, feeding directly into the core-inflation problem in Risk 1 and compounding it — these two risks are correlated, not independent.

**4. AI capex concentration and circularity — MODERATE but structurally important.** Nvidia's **$279B of supply commitments** is a quarter-trillion-dollar bet that hyperscaler budgets hold. Its Q3 guide assumes zero China compute — prudent, but it means the entire $108B rests on a narrow set of Western buyers. Any single large hyperscaler trimming capex cascades through the whole physical-infrastructure chain, today's spotlight sector included.

**5. Stalling consumer spending — MODERATE.** July's PCE report showed spending *stalled* alongside 3.3% core inflation. That is the stagflationary corner: it caps Fed support while squeezing demand. My CBT position (tire/auto end markets) carries this risk most directly.

## Bottom Line

The market's message today was not Nvidia's $89B — it was that Nvidia delivered $89B, guided to $108B, and *fell anyway* while the Dow outperformed. Leadership is rotating from the companies making AI chips to the companies supplying the physical world those chips need, and with the industrials sector's marquee names at 30–50x while equally-exposed mid-caps trade at 13–22x, the rotation has not yet reached the valuation. I would own that gap through infrastructure products, construction materials, specialty chemicals and energy services — and I would own it with a hawkish-Fed hedge in mind, because the single largest risk to every long position above is that September brings a hike the market has not priced.
