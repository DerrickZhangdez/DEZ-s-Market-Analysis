## Executive Summary

The market is in the eighth day of a semiconductor de-rating that has nothing to do with semiconductor fundamentals and everything to do with the cost of money. Wednesday's hot September CPI — headline **3.7%** against 3.6% expected, core **2.6%** against 2.5% — landed on a Fed that has already begun *hiking*, and it pushed the S&P 500 down 1.08% to 7,721 and the Nasdaq Composite down 1.88% to 26,693. Overnight the damage went global and got worse: Korea's KOSPI fell **7.57%** and tripped a 20-minute circuit breaker, with **Samsung down ~9.5%** and **SK hynix down ~11.6%**, on a combination of the US rate shock, fresh evidence that China's CXMT is closing the DRAM technology gap, and a visible crack in AI credit — CoreWeave fell 15.46% Wednesday to $65.78 with its bonds yielding 15–16%.

The dissonance is the opportunity. In the same 48 hours, **ASML** posted a record quarter (€11.7B) and raised full-year guidance to €44–45B, and **TSMC this morning** reported Q3 revenue of **$46.7B** above its own guidance range, profit up ~69%, Q4 guidance of **$52.3–54.3B** well ahead of the ~$51B consensus, and a reiterated 2026 capex budget of **$60–64B** with 2026–2028 spending "significantly higher" than the prior three years. Both stocks fell anyway. When the two companies with the best visibility into 2027–2030 silicon demand beat and raise into a selloff, the selloff is about discount rates and leverage, not demand.

Today's spotlight is **semiconductors and semicap equipment** (day-of-year 288 mod 6 = 0), and the slate below is built on a single discrimination: **own the parts of the chain paid in cash out of TSMC's capex budget and out of wafer starts; avoid the parts financed with high-yield debt and the parts whose economics depend on DRAM spot pricing.** That rules out the neoclouds, and it rules out owning memory directly into a Chinese capacity threat. It rules in packaging, test, process control, photonics, and the unglamorous legacy-node analog business that nobody is talking about.

## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The regime has changed and much of the market is still positioned for the old one.** The FOMC raised the federal funds rate by 25bp on **September 16** to **3.75%–4.00%** — the first hike in three years. The September dot plot showed most policymakers expecting another increase before year-end, projected 2026 headline PCE at **3.7%**, and did not show inflation returning to 2% until **2029**.

**Wednesday's CPI made that path harder, not easier:**

| Metric | Actual | Expected | Prior |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline CPI (YoY) | **3.7%** | 3.6% | 3.4% |
| Core CPI (YoY) | **2.6%** | 2.5% | — |

Headline inflation *accelerated* 30bp month-over-month. That is the number that matters: not the small beat against consensus, but the direction. CME FedWatch odds of an October hike jumped to **35%** from 22% a day earlier.

**The October 27–28 FOMC is the event.** Two structural features make it unusually fraught:

1. **The Fed is flying without an update.** The next CPI release is **November 10** — *after* the meeting. The FOMC will decide on September data, with October's inflation an unknown.
2. **Officials are not unified.** NY Fed President Williams has said there is "no need for urgency." Governor Barr's comments in September were read hawkishly enough to move the market's hike expectations. J.P. Morgan Wealth Management's base case is a **hold** in October with perhaps 50bp of tightening later; fixed-income markets have at times priced hikes at **both** October and December, which would take the funds rate to 4.00%–4.25%. A 35% implied probability is precisely the uncomfortable zone where the meeting is a genuine coin-flip risk event rather than a formality.

**What this does to equity valuation.** A rising front end compresses the multiple on long-duration cash flows and raises the hurdle rate on every capex-funded growth story. This is the mechanical reason a record ASML quarter and a beat-and-raise TSMC print can be met with selling. It is also why the valuation discipline in the Stock Picks section below is tightened rather than relaxed: in a hiking regime, paying 40x forward for 25% growth is a materially worse trade than it was in a cutting regime, and several otherwise-excellent semicap names are rejected below on exactly that basis.

**What to watch this week and next:** October 27–28 FOMC; the earnings cluster of **October 22 (Besi), October 26 (Cadence), October 28 (KLA, FormFactor), October 29 (ASE)**; and — more important than any single print — the direction of high-yield spreads on AI infrastructure credit.

## Geopolitical Factors

**Energy is the inflation problem.** Brent has risen roughly **13% since the start of September** to around **$107/bbl**, and the EIA's October Short-Term Energy Outlook raised its 4Q26 Brent forecast to **$105/bbl — a $14 increase** over the prior month's estimate. The EIA explicitly noted that its forecast does not reflect market events after October 1, which means the published number likely understates the current risk premium.

**Drivers, in order of market impact:**

- **Middle East / Iran.** BlackRock's October geopolitical dashboard flags the Iran conflict as applying persistent pressure to energy prices. The IEA warns that escalating attacks on regional energy infrastructure and tanker traffic threaten further price gains.
- **The September attack on Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline**, combined with a sharp rise in tanker freight rates, drove much of the September–October move. Freight is the quiet variable: when rates spike, landed crude costs rise even if the marker price does not.
- **Russia–Ukraine.** An energy ceasefire did not hold the market down for long; traders rotated attention back to Middle East supply risk.

**The feedback loop that matters for equities:** higher oil → higher headline CPI → higher odds of an October hike → a lower multiple on exactly the long-duration growth names in the spotlight sector. The semiconductor selloff and the Strait-of-Hormuz risk premium are not separate stories. They are the same story transmitted through the front end of the curve.

**One nuance worth holding onto:** futures curves remain **downward-sloping**, meaning the market prices this as a risk premium that decays, not a permanent supply shift. That argues against repositioning an entire portfolio around $107 Brent — but it also means any *disappointment* in the de-escalation timeline is not priced.

### Today's Spotlight: Semiconductors & Semicap Equipment

**The central fact: demand guidance went up this week while the stocks went down.**

| Company | Event | Result | Stock reaction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ASML** (Oct 14) | Q3 results | Record **€11.7B** sales, record gross margin; FY26 guidance **raised to €44–45B**; CEO cites customer discussions for **2029–2030** equipment orders | −1.86% regular, −2.26% after hours |
| **TSMC** (Oct 15) | Q3 results | Revenue **$46.7B** vs. $44.6–45.8B guided; net profit **+69%**; GM 66.4%; Q4 guided **$52.3–54.3B** vs. ~$51B consensus; 2026 capex reiterated **$60–64B** | Shares fell |
| **Samsung / SK hynix** (Oct 15) | — | — | **−9.5% / −11.6%**; KOSPI −7.57%, circuit breaker |

TSMC's Q3 capex alone was **$13.8B**, implying roughly **$19.3–23.4B in Q4** to hit the full-year range. Management guided that capex from **2026 through 2028 will be significantly higher** than the preceding three years, and sell-side estimates for 2027 cluster around **$83–85B**. That is a ~30%+ step-up in the single largest pool of equipment spending on earth, stated on the record this morning, into a tape that sold it.

**So why are the stocks down? Four distinct forces, which deserve to be separated because they do not carry equal weight:**

1. **Rates (dominant, and transient in character).** Hot CPI → higher discount rate → multiple compression on the longest-duration equities in the index. This is real and it is *mechanical*, but it is a valuation event, not an earnings event.
2. **AI credit stress (serious, and the one to actually monitor).** CoreWeave −15.46% Wednesday with bond yields at **15–16%** and a Gartner warning on the neocloud model. Earlier in the month, a report that OpenAI's annualized revenue was ~**$50B** rather than the $68–70B circulating among investors knocked Nvidia −3%, Oracle −6%, CoreWeave −8% and Broadcom −4% in a single October 8 session. Oracle's CDS have widened on data-center funding concerns, and Nvidia has agreed to backstop up to **$105B** of OpenAI's lease obligations. **This is the genuine risk**, and it is a risk about *who pays for the buildout*, not whether the silicon works.
3. **Chinese competition in memory (specific, and correctly priced into memory only).** CXMT has made progress on a next-generation DRAM design and is targeting server DDR5 launch by end-2026. This is a legitimate, durable threat to DRAM pricing. It is **not** a threat to lithography, process control, advanced packaging or test intensity — if anything, more competitors building more DRAM is more wafer starts and more test.
4. **Positioning (mechanical, self-correcting).** Samsung and SK hynix buyback programs have run their course, removing a price-insensitive bid, and leveraged retail ETFs in Korea amplified the downside.

**The analytical seam this creates.** Forces 1 and 4 are indiscriminate — they sell the whole SOX. Force 3 is specific to memory pricing. Force 2 is specific to debt-financed capacity. The names that get sold by 1 and 4, are *untouched* by 3, and have *no exposure* to 2, are mispriced. That is the entire basis of today's slate.

**The distinction that does the work: *pricing* exposure vs. *volume and complexity* exposure.** A DRAM maker's earnings are a function of bit prices. A probe-card maker's earnings are a function of how many die get tested and how hard they are to test — HBM stacks require vastly more test insertions per bit than commodity DRAM, and that intensity rises with every stack layer regardless of what a DDR5 module sells for. CXMT adding DRAM capacity is *bearish* for the first and *bullish* for the second. Thursday's tape sold both identically.

### Artificial Intelligence

**The AI trade has split into two assets that were, until three weeks ago, priced as one.**

**Asset A — the silicon and the tools.** Demand confirmed this week by ASML and TSMC in the strongest possible terms: record quarters, raised guidance, capex budgets extended through 2028, customers negotiating equipment for the end of the decade.

**Asset B — the leveraged capacity layer.** CoreWeave at a 15–16% cost of debt, Oracle CDS widening, Nvidia backstopping $105B of OpenAI leases, and an OpenAI revenue figure that turned out to be ~$50B rather than the $68–70B investors had been working from (the gap is largely an accounting artifact — the larger number included gross sales through cloud partners that OpenAI does not book in full — but the market repriced the *disclosure quality* as much as the number). Bloomberg subsequently reported OpenAI still expects a **$70B+ run rate by year-end**.

The distinction matters because Asset B's problems **transmit to Asset A only if financing costs actually force capex cancellation**. Nothing in TSMC's guidance this morning suggests that has begun — a company that just raised Q4 revenue guidance ~$2B above consensus and reiterated a $60–64B capex budget is not seeing its customers retrench. But TSMC's visibility is into orders *already placed*. The honest position is that Asset A is currently pricing a risk that Asset B has not yet transmitted, and the monitoring variable is high-yield spreads on AI infrastructure paper, not the SOX chart.

Dan Ives called the CoreWeave selloff "way overdone," arguing hyperscaler earnings will support AI spending; CoreWeave's own CEO told staff the market was acting "irrationally." Both may be right about the medium term and still wrong about the next eight weeks — a borrower paying 15% does not get to decide when the market stops asking questions.

### 1. ASX — ASE Technology Holding *(OSAT — assembly, test & advanced packaging)*

- **Thesis:** TSMC this morning reiterated a $60–64B capex budget with 2027 estimates at $83–85B, and advanced packaging remains the industry's tightest bottleneck — ASE is the largest independent beneficiary and is widely expected to absorb wafer-on-substrate overflow from TSMC's constrained CoWoS lines. The ADR fell **6.87% on Wednesday to $44.33** on rate fear, while the Taiwan listing (3711.TW) closed **+2.42%** the same day — a dislocation between the same equity in two venues.
- **Valuation metrics:** Forward P/E ~**26.3x** against a Zacks industry average near 14.2x; 2026 EPS consensus ~**$1.05**, implying **+84.2%** growth, for a **PEG near 0.3**. P/S elevated at ~4.7x versus a historical median near 1.0x.
- **Growth profile:** Q3 revenue **NT$244.16B (~$7.64B), +44.8% YoY**. 1H26 revenue +24%, with the ATM segment +35% and leading-edge advanced packaging growing faster still. LEAP services revenue tracking **above $3.5B** for 2026, with management targeting a **doubling in 2027**. Q2 net income **+180%** to NT$21.85B.
- **Competitive moat:** Scale. ASE is the world's largest OSAT, and advanced packaging qualification is a multi-year, customer-specific process — once a package is qualified into a part, it does not move. The 2026 capex plan of **$10.5B** entrenches that lead.
- **Key risk:** That same $10.5B capex is the bear case — it is a large fixed commitment into a cycle that could digest. Secondary risks: Taiwan concentration, ADR/local price divergence, and a GuruFocus data record describing the company as unprofitable that **conflicts** with reported Q2 net income of NT$21.85B and appears stale or erroneous — I am relying on the filings and Zacks estimates, and flagging the discrepancy rather than ignoring it. Earnings **October 29**.
- **Rating: Buy**

### 2. TSEM — Tower Semiconductor *(Specialty foundry — silicon photonics & RF)*

- **Thesis:** Optical interconnect is the one AI bottleneck that is neither a memory-pricing story nor a debt-financed capacity story, and Tower is the merchant foundry for it. Silicon photonics revenue grew **over 270% YoY** in Q2 to a **$680M annualized run rate**, targeting **$1B by Q4 2026**, with **$1.3B of contracted silicon-photonics revenue already booked for 2027**. The stock fell **~14% last week** to about **$206.86** alongside memory names with which it shares no economics.
- **Valuation metrics:** Q2 GAAP diluted EPS **$0.79** (adjusted **$0.88** per a second source — the two bases differ and I have not reconciled them). Management's model targets **$2.8B revenue / $750M net profit in 2028**, raised at Q2 to a **$3.6B revenue** ambition. Against a roughly $23–25B market capitalization, the 2028 target implies a materially lower forward multiple *if delivered*. **I found no reliable current forward P/E**, and one screen showed a PEG of 8.165 that appears to be an unverified automated figure I am explicitly not relying on. This is the weakest valuation evidence in today's slate and the rating reflects that.
- **Growth profile:** Q2 revenue **$460M, +24% YoY**; net profit **$91M, +95% YoY**; 20% net margin. RF infrastructure growing strongly. RFSOI revenue **−14% YoY** on a deliberate strategic transition — a genuine mix headwind, not hidden.
- **Competitive moat:** Specialty process IP that scaled logic foundries do not prioritize. Silicon photonics requires co-developed PDKs and years of customer-specific qualification; the $1.3B of 2027 contracts is the moat made visible.
- **Key risk:** Execution timing. The entire thesis is that 2027–2028 contracted revenue converts on schedule; analysts' principal concern is that revenue and margin ambitions take longer than projected. Tower is also a single-country-risk Israeli issuer, and the RFSOI decline must be outrun by photonics growth, not merely offset.
- **Rating: Buy**

### 3. FORM — FormFactor *(Test & measurement — probe cards, HBM)*

- **Thesis:** **This is the cleanest mispricing on the board today.** FormFactor sold off with memory, but it does not sell memory — it sells the probe cards used to test it, and it ships in volume to **all three** major HBM manufacturers. Probe-card demand scales with die count and **test intensity**, not with DRAM spot price. HBM stacks require far more test insertions per bit than commodity DRAM, and that intensity rises with every added layer. **CXMT adding DRAM capacity is bearish for bit prices and bullish for probe cards.** Thursday's tape sold both as one.
- **Valuation metrics:** Close **$128.09 (October 13)**, market capitalization ~**$10.0B**. *I was unable to source a current forward P/E, EV/EBITDA or PEG from a provider I trust, and I am not going to invent them* — the market cap moved from ~$8.48B (August 3, at $108.80) to ~$11.0B in June to ~$10.0B now across the sources I found, which tells you how much the multiple has been re-rating in both directions. Size the position accordingly.
- **Growth profile:** Q2 FY26 revenue **$258.2M, +31.9% YoY**, beating ~$240M consensus; **DRAM probe-card revenue $85.0M, +49.0% YoY**. Q1 revenue $226.1M was an all-time record with record DRAM revenue. The company's filings tie that growth directly to HBM designs for generative AI.
- **Competitive moat:** Probe cards are custom-engineered per device design and must be qualified alongside the chip. Switching mid-program is impractical. Being designed in at all three HBM vendors simultaneously is a structural position no new entrant replicates quickly.
- **Key risk:** Memory capex is lumpy, and if the DRAM makers genuinely cut — as opposed to merely seeing their shares fall — probe-card orders follow with a lag. Freedom Capital Markets raised its target from $64 to $140 on October 5, a move so large it signals how unsettled the consensus view is. **Disclosure: FORM was recommended in this series on August 4, 2026** — roughly ten weeks ago, outside every exclusion window, and detailed in the Rotation Check. Earnings **October 28**.
- **Rating: Buy**

### 4. KLAC — KLA Corporation *(Process control — inspection & metrology)*

- **Thesis:** The quality anchor of the slate and the one name here with a near-monopoly. Process control is the only semicap category whose revenue scales with **yield-ramp difficulty** rather than unit tool count — and TSMC's Q3 gross margin dipped 1.3 points precisely because the **2nm ramp** is underway. Harder nodes mean more inspection steps per wafer. KLA is paid for the industry's difficulty, which is rising.
- **Valuation metrics:** Forward P/E ~**33.5x** (October 13), PEG ~**1.80** on 5-year expected growth. One provider showed 53.57x from a July 28 snapshot and another 34.84x — I am using the ~33–35x range and treating the 53x outlier as stale. **This is the richest multiple I am willing to underwrite today**, and it is why the rating is Accumulate rather than Buy: at a 4% funds rate, 33x requires the monopoly to be real, and I believe it is, but it does not leave room for error.
- **Growth profile:** June-quarter revenue **$3.658B** with GAAP net income **$1.363B** (~37% net margin). September-quarter guidance **$4.0B ± $200M** — roughly +9% sequentially — with non-GAAP gross margin guided to **62.5% ± 1.0%**, the best in semicap. Management describes momentum as **accelerating through 2H26 and into 2027**. *(Note: one 8-K extract returned diluted EPS guidance of $1.14–1.16, which is irreconcilable with $4.0B of revenue at 62.5% gross margin and is almost certainly from an older filing or a different basis. I am not relying on it.)*
- **Competitive moat:** The widest in the sector. KLA holds a dominant share of optical inspection and metrology; its installed base generates high-margin service revenue that cushions cyclicality, and the gross margin above 62% is itself proof of pricing power no competitor contests.
- **Key risk:** The multiple, first. Then: renewed China equipment restrictions or softness, tariffs, and elevated memory input costs — all flagged in recent analyst commentary. A genuine capex digestion in 2027 would compress a 33x multiple fast. Earnings **October 28**.
- **Rating: Accumulate**

**Correlation I am disclosing rather than hiding.** ASX, TSEM, FORM and KLAC are all, at root, long the semiconductor capital-spending cycle. They are diversified by *position in the chain* — packaging, specialty foundry, test, process control — and by *demand driver* — capex dollars, contracted photonics revenue, test intensity, yield difficulty. ## Risk Factors

**Ranked by severity.**

**1. AI credit contagion — the risk that actually breaks the thesis.** CoreWeave's bonds at **15–16%**, Oracle's CDS widening on data-center funding, Nvidia backstopping **$105B** of OpenAI lease obligations, and a Gartner warning on the neocloud model. The buildout is increasingly debt-financed at a moment when the Fed is *raising* the cost of that debt. **Transmission mechanism to watch:** if neoclouds cannot roll financing, they cancel capacity; hyperscalers absorb some but not all; equipment orders fall 2–3 quarters later. Nothing in TSMC's guidance this morning indicates this has started — but TSMC's visibility is into orders already placed. **Monitor high-yield spreads on AI infrastructure paper, not the SOX chart.**

**2. The October 27–28 FOMC, decided without fresh data.** Headline CPI accelerated to **3.7%**, hike odds are **35%** — the genuinely uncomfortable zone — and the next CPI is **November 10**, *after* the meeting. The committee is visibly split (Williams: "no need for urgency"; Barr read hawkishly). A hike, or a hold with hawkish guidance, compresses multiples across every long-duration name in this report. The Fed's own projections do not see 2% inflation until **2029**.

**3. Chinese memory competition becoming structural.** CXMT's next-generation DRAM progress with server DDR5 targeted by **end-2026** is a durable threat to DRAM pricing, and Thursday's KOSPI circuit breaker shows the market now treats it as such. **Contained to memory pricing** — but if it triggers a Korean capex response (cutting investment to defend price), it reaches the equipment names, including two in today's slate.

**4. Energy-driven inflation persistence.** Brent at **$105–107**, up 13% since September, with the EIA's 4Q26 forecast raised $14 and explicitly excluding post-October-1 events. Attacks on regional energy infrastructure and tanker traffic continue. Each dollar of crude feeds headline CPI, which feeds the Fed, which feeds risk #2. Downward-sloping futures curves suggest the market expects decay — leaving any *delay* in de-escalation unpriced.

**5. Breadth deterioration and a credit-sensitive consumer.** The Russell 2000 fell **1.93%** Wednesday, underperforming large caps, and all six major banks fell despite Goldman beating EPS by ~30% and Citi beating as well. When strong bank results cannot lift bank stocks — and Bank of America absorbs a $1.2B regulatory penalty on top — the market is pricing forward credit conditions rather than realized earnings. That is a late-cycle signature.

## Bottom Line

The two companies with the clearest view of silicon demand through 2030 both beat and raised this week, and both stocks fell — which tells you this is a **discount-rate and leverage event, not a demand event**. The right response is neither to buy the dip indiscriminately nor to abandon the sector, but to separate the links of the chain that get paid in **cash out of TSMC's $60–64B capex budget and out of rising wafer starts** from those financed at **15% in the high-yield market** or priced off **DRAM spot**. Today's slate — ASE, Tower, FormFactor, KLA and Diodes — is that separation, bought into the seventh and eighth day of indiscriminate selling, with one deliberately uncorrelated legacy-analog name as ballast.

The single variable that would invalidate it is not the SOX chart and not the next CPI print: it is **AI infrastructure credit spreads**. Watch those, size positions for a 35% chance the Fed hikes into this on October 28, and remember that today's data was messy enough that every number above deserves a second source before it becomes a trade.
