# Daily Market Report — Tuesday, July 28, 2026

## Executive Summary

Markets are fracturing along a single fault line today: the AI trade is cracking while the rest of the market quietly works. A deepening semiconductor rout — now roughly $1.3 trillion of destroyed market value — pushed Nasdaq 100 futures down as much as 1% and put the index on track for a five-day losing streak, only the second of 2026, while Dow futures rose 0.6% on a genuinely strong cyclical earnings slate (UPS, Sherwin-Williams, IQVIA, Polaris all raised or beat). The proximate triggers are supply-side and structural rather than macro: reports that SK Hynix is slowing HBM capacity expansion, visible progress in China's advanced chipmaking, "circular funding" concerns across the AI capex complex, and signs of excess compute capacity at Meta. All of this lands on the first day of a two-day FOMC meeting where the risk skew has flipped from cuts to hikes — nine of eighteen policymakers now expect at least one increase this year.

The tell for investors is rotation, not de-risking: bonds rallied, oil fell, and money moved into earnings-backed cyclicals. That is the opportunity set today's spotlight targets.

## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The Fed is the week's main event, and the direction of risk has reversed.** The FOMC is mid-meeting as of today, July 28, with the decision landing tomorrow, July 29. The federal funds target range sits at **3.50%–3.75%**, unchanged since June. Futures assign a **74.9% probability of a hold** and a **25.1% chance of a 25bp hike** — a distribution that would have been unthinkable a year ago.

**Inflation is the reason.** The most recent readings are uncomfortably far from target:

| Measure | Latest | Fed Target |
|---|---|---|
| Headline CPI (May, y/y) | 4.2% | 2% |
| Core CPI (May, y/y) | 2.9% | 2% |
| Headline PCE | 4.1% | 2% |
| Core PCE | 3.4% | 2% |

The June FOMC minutes framed the balance of risks as tilted "more toward high inflation than labor market weakness" — a complete inversion of the 2025 posture. The median year-end 2026 funds rate projection was revised **up to 3.8% from 3.4%** in March, and **nine of eighteen members now pencil in at least one hike this year**. Under new Chair Kevin Warsh, the hawkish drift has itself become a named driver of the semiconductor de-rating, as higher-for-longer discount rates compress the terminal-value-heavy AI complex hardest.

**What to watch this week:** the July 29 FOMC statement and press conference (language on the hiking bias matters more than the hold itself), then the megacap earnings gauntlet — Microsoft and Meta, followed by Amazon on July 30 and Apple, with Alphabet having reported after the close on July 27. Boeing, Coca-Cola and Ford round out the week. Q2 2026 S&P 500 earnings growth is tracking roughly **22% year-over-year, with close to half of that tied to AI infrastructure spending** — a concentration that cuts both ways. The beat rate is running at 82%, but the market has been punishing misses more severely than its historical average this year (see Corning below).

## Geopolitical Factors

**Oil has de-risked substantially, but the calm is structurally fragile.** Brent trades near **$72** and WTI near **$69**, well off conflict highs. The repricing follows the **June 18 US–Iran memorandum of understanding** that ended active hostilities and reopened the **Strait of Hormuz**, which had been effectively closed since February 28.

The risk has not disappeared — it has changed shape. Analysts characterize the US and Iran as entering a period of **sustained confrontation calibrated to stay below the threshold of full-scale war**, which keeps a persistent risk premium on shipping and energy transiting Hormuz. The ceasefire is explicitly described as fragile, and any re-escalation is the single largest upside risk to crude, chemicals and industrial materials pricing. Lasting infrastructure damage from the conflict is expected to hold prices above pre-conflict levels through 2028.

**Second-order effect worth owning:** structurally tight nitrogen fertilizer and industrial gas markets are a direct consequence of these disruptions plus limited new capacity — a theme that shows up in today's picks.

**Trade and technology policy** remain live: China's demonstrated progress in advanced chipmaking is simultaneously a competitive threat to Western semis and a signal that export controls are producing substitution rather than containment. Tariffs continue to flow through industrial P&Ls in both directions — Polaris booked a tariff *refund* benefit this quarter, while US steel tariffs remain the primary support for domestic steel pricing.

**The structural thesis.** The AI buildout's economics are being questioned at the chip layer, but the *physical* layer is contracted, backlogged and largely reimbursable. The top five cloud/AI infrastructure providers have committed **$660–690 billion of 2026 capex** (some trackers put the four main hyperscalers near **$725 billion — Amazon ~$200B, Google ~$185B, Meta ~$125B, Microsoft ~$120B — up 77% from ~$410B in 2025**). Even a meaningful disappointment against those numbers leaves the electrical, mechanical, flow-control and site-development supply chain with multi-year visibility. Critically, **that visibility is contracted in backlog, not extrapolated from a P/E multiple** — which is precisely why the chip rout has not propagated into E&C order books.

**The discipline problem.** The market has already figured this out for the obvious names, and the "AI power" trade is now where the froth lives. Names I researched today and am explicitly **not** recommending on valuation grounds:

The value in this spotlight is one layer removed from the data center — in **feedstock-advantaged chemicals, midstream energy infrastructure, flow control, and metals** — where cyclical earnings power is being priced at single-digit to low-20s multiples. That is where today's picks sit.

### Artificial Intelligence

The narrative shifted today from "how fast can we build" to "who pays for it." Three developments matter:

**Capacity signals are turning ambiguous.** Reports of **excess computing capacity at Meta** are the most important datapoint of the week, because Meta has been the most aggressive spender — guiding capex as high as **$145 billion for 2026**. Simultaneously, **Anthropic is in very preliminary talks to lease AI compute from Meta** (CNBC, July 17), a striking role reversal that reads as Meta monetizing surplus capacity while compute-constrained labs — Anthropic still imposes usage limits on its most advanced models — rent rather than build.

**"Circular funding" is now an explicit market concern.** The pattern of model labs, cloud providers and chipmakers financing each other's demand is drawing scrutiny. The **$500B Stargate** joint venture (OpenAI/SoftBank/Oracle — SoftBank financing, Oracle supplying cloud, OpenAI consuming) is the archetype: impressive in scale, but the end-demand chain is short on independent, cash-paying enterprise customers.

**The read-through:** if roughly half of the S&P's ~22% Q2 earnings growth is AI-infrastructure-linked, then the sustainability question is not a sector question — it is an index-level question. Investors should want AI exposure where revenue is contracted and cash-converting, not where it is capitalized on faith.

### 1. CF — CF Industries Holdings, Inc.
*Sub-sector: Nitrogen fertilizer / agricultural chemicals*

- **Thesis:** A structurally tight global nitrogen market — driven by geopolitical supply disruptions and effectively no new capacity until 2030 — is colliding with an 11% year-over-year decline in North American natural gas feedstock costs. CF is the low-cost producer positioned exactly at that spread, and the market is paying a single-digit multiple for a 69% earnings inflection.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Forward P/E **~8.6x** (some screens as low as 6.6x) versus a chemicals industry average near 12.7x on the same basis — roughly a 32% discount. Trailing P/E **~10.2x** against a chemicals industry average of ~25.7x and a peer group average of ~58.7x. PEG is **~0.12 implied** on consensus growth, though I would discount that heavily: this is a cyclical earnings rebound, not a secular compounding rate, and PEG flatters cyclical troughs. P/S and EV/EBITDA not confirmed in today's research; Q1 2026 EBITDA of $1.01B annualizes toward a mid-single-digit EV/EBITDA.
- **Growth Profile:** FY2026 consensus EPS **$15.86, up 69.3% from $9.37 in 2025**. Q2 EPS projected at $5.83 versus a $5.61 Street estimate. Q1 2026 net income $615M on EBITDA of $1.01B. One valuation screen flags the shares as ~36% undervalued; Scotiabank has upgraded on fertilizer names approaching cyclical bottoms, and CF raised its dividend 20% with the stock up 55% in 2026.
- **Competitive Moat:** Cost curve position. A North American asset base tied to cheap domestic gas versus European producers on marginal-cost LNG is a durable, structural advantage, not a cyclical one — and it widens precisely when energy markets are disrupted. Nitrogen capacity additions require multi-year permitting and capital, capping supply response through 2030.
- **Key Risk:** Pure commodity price exposure. A natural gas spike, a demand air-pocket from farmer economics, or restarts of idled global capacity would compress the spread fast. The 55% YTD run also means this is no longer an untouched contrarian entry.
- **Rating:** **Strong Buy**

### 2. TRGP — Targa Resources Corp.
*Sub-sector: Midstream energy infrastructure / NGL logistics*

- **Thesis:** Volume-driven, fee-based Permian gathering and NGL export growth with contracted acreage dedications — the closest thing in energy to a toll road — priced at a PEG of 0.35 while management raises guidance. This is energy exposure that does not require a bullish crude call.
- **Valuation Metrics:** EV/EBITDA **14.94x** (a premium to the midstream industry average, and the one metric that gives me pause). **PEG 0.35** — a low P/E relative to growth; against 26.6% expected EPS growth that implies a forward P/E in the high-single to low-double digits. Trailing P/E and P/S not confirmed in today's research.
- **Growth Profile:** FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance **raised to $5.7–5.9B, a $300M increase at the midpoint**. Consensus 2026 EPS **$10.75, +26.6% y/y**; revenue ~$19.3B, **+13.1% y/y**. Record Q1 2026 EBITDA of $1.4B. Goldman Sachs raised its price target on Permian growth and models a **9% EBITDA CAGR for 2026–2030**. Drivers include stronger Permian inlet volumes, higher implied NGL production, completed export facility maintenance, and Waha spread plus spot NGL export marketing upside.
- **Competitive Moat:** Integrated, hard-to-replicate Permian footprint — gathering and processing connected through fractionation to Gulf Coast export docks. Acreage dedications and long-term fee-based contracts convert producer drilling activity into contracted volume growth, and permitting plus capital intensity makes duplication impractical. Low double-digit volumetric growth is guided "in 2026 and beyond."
- **Key Risk:** The 14.9x EV/EBITDA premium is the thesis's weak point — it leaves less room for error than the multiple-driven names I passed on. Beyond valuation: a Permian activity slowdown if crude breaks below producer economics, and NGL price spreads narrowing on new fractionation supply.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

### 3. FLS — Flowserve Corporation
*Sub-sector: Flow control machinery (pumps & valves) — nuclear, LNG, power*

- **Thesis:** The purest "picks-and-shovels" exposure to nuclear restart, LNG expansion and data-center power that is still trading *below* its industry multiple, with a high-margin aftermarket annuity attached to a growing installed base. Management hit its long-term margin targets ahead of schedule and is consolidating the nuclear valve niche.
- **Valuation Metrics:** P/E **~24.7x**, below the Machinery industry average of ~26.7x and well below a peer group average of ~37.4x. This is the rare name in the AI-power supply chain not carrying a premium — compare POWL at ~53x or CW at ~53x for overlapping end-markets. Analysts recently trimmed the price target by $5 to **$83** on discount-rate and margin assumptions. P/S, EV/EBITDA and PEG not confirmed in today's research.
- **Growth Profile:** 2026 guidance reaffirmed, with analysts **raising 2026 and 2027 earnings forecasts** on a solid backlog. Nuclear bookings are the standout: **~$400M of nuclear awards in FY2025** and over $110M in a single recent quarter. Demand cited across power, nuclear, data centers and electrification. The **$490M acquisition of Trillium Flow Technologies' Valves Division** (closing mid-2026) deepens nuclear and traditional power exposure; shares rose 9.4% on the margin beat and deal announcement.
- **Competitive Moat:** Installed-base aftermarket. Pumps and valves in nuclear and LNG service are safety-qualified, spec'd into plant designs, and carry decades-long replacement and service cycles with high switching costs — qualification barriers in nuclear flow control are among the highest in industrials. Aftermarket revenue is recurring and structurally higher-margin than original equipment.
- **Key Risk:** Project timing. Nuclear and LNG awards are lumpy and slip; a soft bookings quarter reads as a broken thesis even when the pipeline is intact. Trillium integration and the cash outlay add execution risk, and one valuation screen already frames the shares as only modestly discounted post-deal.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

### 4. FLR — Fluor Corporation
*Sub-sector: Engineering & construction services (nuclear/SMR, data centers, mining)*

- **Thesis:** A deeply out-of-favor E&C name with a de-risked, 82%-reimbursable backlog, a balance sheet transformed by $2.5B of divestiture proceeds, and a buyback set to retire 14% of the share count in a single year. You are buying the same nuclear-and-data-center order book the market pays 50x for elsewhere, at a fraction of the multiple, because of a bad quarter.
- **Valuation Metrics:** One analyst base case values FLR at **$58–62 per share, 30–40% upside**, contingent on normalized FCF of **$400–450M** and buyback execution. The stock is down 26% from recent highs. Explicit trailing/forward P/E, P/S, EV/EBITDA and PEG were not confirmed in today's research — the honest framing here is that this is an FCF-and-buyback story, and I would underwrite it on normalized free cash flow rather than a reported earnings multiple, given the charges distorting trailing EPS.
- **Growth Profile:** Backlog rose to **$25.7B in Q1 2026 and is 82% reimbursable** — the key quality metric, since reimbursable contracts transfer cost-overrun risk to clients. Roughly **half of ending 2025 backlog converts to 2026 revenue**. Front-end work in hand represents **over $60B of potential backlog** if clients proceed, with another **$40B under evaluation over three years**, concentrated in nuclear power, mining, gas-fired energy and data centers. Recent wins include a **TeraWulf** data center campus master-planning agreement and an **X-energy** contract supporting four small modular reactors. NuScale and China fabrication yard divestitures generated **over $2.5B in cash**, funding a **$1.4B buyback targeting a 14% share count reduction in 2026**.
- **Competitive Moat:** Scarcity of qualified large-scale EPC capacity. There are very few firms globally that can execute nuclear, LNG and gigawatt-scale industrial projects, and the credential set — nuclear qualification, bonding capacity, licensed engineering depth — takes decades to assemble. As reshoring, SMRs and data-center power converge, that scarcity is the asset.
- **Key Risk:** Execution, and it is not hypothetical. **Q1 2026 adjusted EPS came in well below expectations** with revenue down 8–9.5% y/y, hit by cost overruns on a mining project and a legal charge tied to an Afghanistan lawsuit. Fixed-price legacy work has repeatedly burned Fluor shareholders; the 82% reimbursable mix mitigates but does not eliminate this. Sizing should reflect that — this is my highest-variance pick.
- **Rating:** **Accumulate**

### 5. STLD — Steel Dynamics, Inc.
*Sub-sector: Steel production & metals recycling*

- **Thesis:** Best-in-class EAF steelmaker with expanding metal margins, priced at 21x with a PEG of 0.34, benefiting from tariff-supported domestic pricing and demand from the same non-residential construction and industrial buildout driving everything else in this report.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Price **$239.47**, P/E **21.19**, **PEG 0.34** — attractive relative to near-term growth. Trailing metrics reflect a five-year EPS decline, so the PEG deserves the same cyclical skepticism I applied to CF. P/S and EV/EBITDA not confirmed in today's research.
- **Growth Profile:** **Q2 2026 EPS jumped to $3.71**, against Q1 guidance of $2.73–2.77. Q1 profitability from steel operations was guided meaningfully higher sequentially on increased shipments and **metal margin expansion as average realized selling values rose faster than scrap raw material costs**. Demand is described as strong across non-residential construction, energy, automotive and industrial end markets. Shares are up ~80% over the past year on tariffs and pricing momentum. KeyBanc has reiterated its rating on steel spreads.
- **Competitive Moat:** Low-cost electric-arc-furnace operating model with vertically integrated metals recycling, which structurally lowers scrap input costs and shortens the cycle between order and shipment versus integrated blast-furnace competitors. Domestic mini-mill footprint is also the direct beneficiary of trade protection on imported steel.
- **Key Risk:** This is a cyclical trading at 80% above year-ago levels on policy-supported pricing. Any tariff relaxation or steel price normalization compresses metal margins quickly, and **early losses in the aluminum ramp** plus a five-year EPS decline raise legitimate questions about through-cycle earnings consistency. Lowest conviction of the five on durability; the valuation is what earns it a place.
- **Rating:** **Accumulate**

## Risk Factors

**1. A hawkish Fed surprise — severity: high, probability: moderate.** Markets price a 74.9% hold, but nine of eighteen FOMC members expect at least one hike in 2026 and the median dot moved up to 3.8%. With headline CPI at 4.2% and core PCE at 3.4%, a hawkish statement tomorrow — even without a hike — would hit long-duration equities hardest, precisely where the market is already breaking. This is the largest single-day risk on the calendar.

**2. AI capex disillusionment broadening from chips into the index — severity: high, probability: moderate.** SK Hynix's HBM slowdown, Meta's excess capacity, and "circular funding" concerns have already erased ~$1.3T from semiconductors. If roughly half of the S&P's ~22% Q2 earnings growth is AI-infrastructure-linked, a genuine capex deceleration is not a sector rotation — it is an index-level earnings problem. Watch Microsoft and Meta capex commentary this week as the swing factor.

**3. Punitive reaction function to in-line results — severity: moderate-to-high, probability: high.** Corning fell 16.4% for guiding *in line*. With an 82% beat rate already priced, the asymmetry into the remaining megacap reports is poor: beats are expected, and anything merely adequate is being sold. This is the most likely source of single-name damage this week.

**4. Hormuz re-escalation — severity: high, probability: low-to-moderate.** The June 18 MOU reopened the Strait, but analysts describe sustained sub-war confrontation as the base case. Renewed closure would spike oil, chemicals and industrial materials, reigniting exactly the inflation the Fed is already leaning against — a genuine stagflationary tail. Note this cuts *for* several of today's picks (CF, TRGP) and against the broad market.

**5. China semiconductor competition as a structural de-rating — severity: moderate, probability: moderate-to-high.** Unlike the other risks, this one does not mean-revert. Evidence that export controls are producing domestic substitution rather than containment permanently compresses the addressable market and pricing power of Western chipmakers and equipment suppliers.

## Bottom Line

The market is not selling off — it is re-sorting, and the sort is on cash-backed earnings versus capitalized expectations. With chips shedding $1.3T on real supply-side signals while UPS, Sherwin-Williams, IQVIA and Polaris raise guidance, the correct posture is to keep AI exposure but move it one layer down the stack into contracted backlogs and feedstock advantages priced in the single digits to low twenties — CF, TRGP, FLS, FLR and STLD over the 50x "AI power" names the crowd has already bid up. Respect tomorrow's FOMC: with nine of eighteen members leaning toward a hike, this is a session to have positioning right beforehand rather than react afterward.
