## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The December meeting is live, and it is live in the hawkish direction.** This is the single most under-appreciated feature of the current setup. Following an October jobs report showing **165,000 jobs added and unemployment at 4.1%**, CME FedWatch-implied odds of a **25bp rate hike** at the **December 8–9 FOMC** have pushed above 80%, with one source putting it near 85%. Investors who spent 2024–2025 trained to buy every dip on the assumption that the Fed put sits beneath them are positioned against the current reaction function.

**Today's CPI is the hinge.** Consensus (Dow Jones) looks for **headline +0.3% m/m and core +0.2% m/m**, against September's hot **+0.6%** headline. Kiplinger modeled +0.28% headline. A quirk worth flagging for anyone reading the tape today: because of the 2025 government shutdown, **this release will not carry an official year-over-year headline inflation rate** — expect confused and contradictory headlines in the first minutes, and expect some algorithmic feeds to mis-handle the missing series.

The asymmetry around the print is well defined. MUFG's framing is the most useful: a **core print of 0.1%** would likely pull December hike odds back toward **55–60%**, while anything at or above consensus leaves the hike essentially intact. One strategist noted that absent an indisputably soft 0.1% reading, the data is unlikely to dissuade the Fed. Note also that a hotter print may move markets *less* than a cooler one, simply because a hike is already the base case — the pain trade is dovish.

**What to watch this week:** the CPI reaction function in long-end Treasuries (a soft print that eases yield pressure is the cleanest bullish catalyst available), Fed speakers repositioning into the December blackout, and the **December 11 government funding deadline**, which has now acquired political dimensions described below. **Nvidia reports November 17** and is the sector's single largest scheduled risk event.

## Geopolitical Factors

**Iran de-escalation is the dominant macro input right now, and it is transmitting through oil.** Brent fell below $90 Tuesday morning for the first time since August after President Trump signaled that further military action against Iran may not be necessary. The scale of the unwind is remarkable: **Brent fell roughly 17% last week alone, the largest weekly decline since 2020**. For context on how violent 2026 has been in energy, Brent began the year near **$60**, spiked as high as **$130** during the March–April escalation, and now sits near $90.

ING characterized the move as the market continuing to unwind the geopolitical risk premium. Flows through the **Strait of Hormuz have largely recovered** — but this is where caution is warranted: the **US naval blockade in the Strait remains in place**. The risk premium is being priced out faster than the underlying military posture is actually being dismantled. That is a reversible condition. Analysts quoted across sources agree renewed escalation could reverse the decline quickly, though one argued a further price collapse is now more likely than a spike.

**Two other live threads:**

- **December 11 funding deadline.** Per Bloomberg, congressional Democrats are weighing whether to use the shutdown deadline as leverage to pressure the administration into ending the Iran conflict. This fuses the fiscal and geopolitical calendars into a single event risk a month out — and it lands two days after the FOMC.
- **China trade truce extended.** China officially announced an extension of the trade truce with Washington that had been due to expire **today, November 10**. This removes a tail risk that had been quietly weighing on semiconductor and hardware supply chains.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: AI Infrastructure & Data Centers

*(Rotation: day-of-year 314 mod 6 = 2 → AI infrastructure & data centers — power, cooling, networking, interconnect, memory.)*

The spotlight lands on this bucket at an unusually interesting moment, because the sector has just been through a textbook de-rating and the fundamental data published *during* that de-rating was almost uniformly strong. That divergence is the entire opportunity set today.

**The scale of the drawdown.** Over the month ending November 6: **Nvidia −21.4%, Broadcom −22%, Arista −20.86%, Vertiv −25.81%**. Nvidia sits roughly **24% below its October high**, with approximately **$1.4 trillion of market value erased**, including a **−9.26%** single session on November 5 that took it to $181.60. The S&P fell 4.4% that day, its worst since the April 2025 tariff episode.

**What did *not* sell off is the tell.** **Equinix and Digital Realty each fell under 4%** over the same month in which chip and cooling names dropped more than 20%. The market did not sell "data centers." It sold **the equity duration attached to the 2028–2030 leg of the capex curve** while leaving contracted, cash-generating, already-leased infrastructure essentially untouched. That is a discriminating selloff, not a panic — and it tells you precisely where the market thinks the risk is.

**The bear case has three legs, and they are not equal:**

1. **Financing structure.** Free cash flow at several hyperscalers has turned negative, and the buildout is increasingly debt-funded. Hyperscaler CDS has reportedly widened to record levels. This is the leg with real substance.
2. **Circularity.** Nvidia's backstop arrangements — guaranteeing portions of OpenAI's data center debt — are the specific target of "circular financing" criticism. Legitimate concern, though the counter-argument that these deals ultimately rest on real end-revenue is also defensible.
3. **Pipeline duration.** David Sacks warned the AI data center pipeline could dry up or shrink by 2028. This is the softest leg: a 2028 concern being priced into 2026 equities with 2027 order books already substantially committed.

**The sell-side pushback is coordinated and, I think, mostly right on the near term.** Morgan Stanley and UBS both argue the selloff is overdone, with Morgan Stanley framing the realistic downside as **a push-out of projects from 2028 to 2029 rather than a derailment of the capex story**. Nvidia has put top-five hyperscaler capex at **~$800B in 2026 rising to ~$1.3T in 2027**; independent analysts land at $1.2–1.3T for 2027. Even heavily discounted, the 2027 order book is largely spoken for.

**Where this leaves the investable set.** The names that matter today are the ones where **2026 reported results and 2027 guidance both accelerated while the stock de-rated**. That filter is unusually productive right now:

- **Advanced Energy** posted record Q3 revenue of **$696M, +50% YoY**, with data center computing revenue at a record **$256M, +49%**, and raised full-year data center growth guidance to the **high-50% range**.
- **TTM Technologies** reported record Q3 revenue of **$1,203.9M** with **data center and networking up 115% YoY** to 41% of revenue, a record 20.1% adjusted EBITDA margin — **and the stock fell on the print**.
- **Arista** grew revenue **58% YoY** to ~$3.66B against $3.3B guidance, at a **49.1% non-GAAP operating margin**, and guided Q4 to $3.9–4.0B.
- **Credo** grew fiscal Q1 2027 revenue **115% YoY** and guided to **>85% full-year growth**.

Four companies, four different layers of the stack — power conversion, PCB/substrate, switching, interconnect — all accelerating, all de-rated. The thermal and power layer in particular (Vertiv −25.8%) now trades at a meaningfully lower multiple against order books that lengthened rather than shortened during the quarter.

**The discipline to apply:** this is a sector where "cheap" and "value trap" are separated almost entirely by **customer concentration** and **balance-sheet dependence on hyperscaler debt issuance**. Credo derives ~90% of revenue from ten customers. Arista's two largest customers were ~26% and ~16% of 2025 revenue. These are not disqualifying, but they are the correct variable to underwrite, and they are why the picks below are spread across six distinct layers rather than concentrated in the highest-growth two.

### Artificial Intelligence

The AI complex enters Tuesday in a repair phase rather than a recovery. Monday's bounce was led by exactly the names that had been hit hardest, which is characteristic of short-covering and systematic re-leveraging rather than fundamental re-rating. **Nvidia's November 17 earnings** — where consensus looks for a **12%+ sequential rise in data center revenue** — is now the sector's referendum. Morgan Stanley and UBS have both flagged a prospective **Anthropic IPO** as a meaningful near-term sentiment catalyst, which would mark the first major pure-play model-layer listing and would give the market a live comparable for private AI valuations that have so far been marked only in private rounds.

The regulatory overhang that briefly spooked the market post-midterms has been downplayed by Wall Street brokerages, who argue Democratic regulation risk to AI is being overstated. On current evidence that looks right — no concrete legislative vehicle has emerged — but it is a watch item rather than a closed one.

Monday's tech comeback was broad but low-conviction. The clearest single-name signal came Tuesday pre-market from **Take-Two Interactive**, up ~4% after fiscal Q2 adjusted EPS of **$1.00 vs. $0.94** consensus on revenue of **$1.60B vs. $1.58B** — a clean beat in a consumer-discretionary-adjacent software name, which is modestly encouraging for the consumer read-through. **Hims & Hers** fell on disappointing guidance, and **Casella Waste** dropped more than 5% on a miss, both reminders that the market is still punishing guidance disappointments severely regardless of sector.

On the analyst tape: **Cloudflare** was upgraded two notches to **Buy from Sell at DZ Bank** with a **$300 target** — an unusually large revision that speaks to how far security/edge infrastructure names had fallen. **DraftKings** was cut to Equal Weight at Wells Fargo with the target slashed to **$21 from $30**. Monday saw **AMD upgraded to Outperform at Oppenheimer** and **Airbnb** raised from Sell to Neutral at Rothschild & Co Redburn.

### Hardware & Semiconductors

The semiconductor tape is where the AI de-rating was most violent and where the fundamental dislocation is therefore widest. The pre-market standout is **Sanmina, up ~19%** on an outlook that came in ahead of Street expectations — a contract manufacturer guiding above consensus is a genuinely useful leading indicator for hardware build rates, and it is difficult to reconcile with a thesis that the buildout is stalling.

Supply-chain conditions improved on the margin with China's extension of the trade truce. The back-end and substrate layers — advanced packaging, high-layer-count PCBs, optical interconnect — continue to run tighter than front-end logic, which is the structural reason TTM is adding **$400M of capacity through early 2028** specifically for advanced PCB products serving AI applications. Capacity additions of that duration are not made against order books management believes are fragile.

### Software & SaaS

Enterprise software remains the quiet underperformer of the AI trade — the layer that is supposed to monetize AI rather than build it, and which has so far been valued as though it will capture little of the value. The DZ Bank upgrade of Cloudflare is one of the first meaningful sell-side attempts to call a bottom in the group. MongoDB, Wix, and other SaaS names appeared across Tuesday's ratings tape without a unified direction. The group's problem is not fundamental deceleration so much as the absence of a clean narrative: it is neither a beneficiary of the capex boom nor insulated from it.

## Stock Picks — Valuation-Based

**Construction logic:** Six names, six distinct layers of the AI infrastructure stack and its adjacencies, deliberately avoiding concentration in the highest-multiple interconnect/networking names alone. One large-cap anchor (ANET), one regulated-adjacent cash generator (CEG), two mid-cap industrial/electronics compounders (AEIS, TTMI), one high-growth interconnect position sized as the speculative sleeve (CRDO), and one deliberate **non-AI-capex diversifier** (UI) to hedge the scenario in which the capex thesis does break. **No company here exceeds $1T market cap; none is a Magnificent Seven constituent.**

### 1. ANET — Arista Networks *(Hyperscale AI Ethernet switching)*

- **Thesis:** The purest listed expression of AI networking just grew revenue 58% YoY at a 49% operating margin and guided Q4 above, yet the stock fell ~21% in the month to November 6 alongside names with far weaker results. You are buying a de-rated best-in-class franchise, not a broken one.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Trailing P/E ~**53.6x** (vs. 5-year median 43.2x); forward P/E ~**35–40x** depending on source and date; EV/EBITDA elevated but supported by a net-cash balance sheet and minimal capital intensity. PEG approximately **0.9–1.0** against ~40% FY26 revenue growth.
- **Growth Profile:** Q3 revenue ~**$3.66B, +58% YoY**, against $3.3B guidance. Non-GAAP EPS **$1.20 vs. $1.08** consensus. Q3 non-GAAP operating margin **49.1%**; Q4 guided to **$3.9–4.0B** at 48–49% margin. FY26 revenue ~**$12.6B, +40%**, with AI-specific revenue **$3.5B+**.
- **Competitive Moat:** EOS software as a single consistent operating system across the portfolio creates real switching costs at the network-operations layer; deep merchant-silicon partnerships; and credible leadership in open Ethernet rack-scale architectures as the industry moves away from proprietary AI fabrics.
- **Key Risk:** **Customer concentration** — the two largest customers were ~26% and ~16% of 2025 revenue. A single hyperscaler deferring a build cycle is a double-digit revenue event. This is the position's dominant risk and the reason it is an anchor rather than an oversized holding.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

### 2. CEG — Constellation Energy *(Nuclear power generation / data center PPAs)*

- **Thesis:** The cheapest credible way to own AI electricity demand. A 36% Q3 earnings beat and a forward multiple under 20x sit against a stock still **down ~23% YTD** — the market has repriced the AI-power trade far more aggressively than it has repriced the underlying contracted cash flows.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Forward P/E ~**19.4x** (as of Nov 2) to **20.3x**, against an industry median of ~13.0x — a premium, but a narrow one for this growth profile. Consensus EPS **$12.17 (FY26, below company guidance), $13.31 (FY27), $16.95 (FY28)** — implying ~18% EPS CAGR, putting PEG near **1.1**.
- **Growth Profile:** Q3 adjusted EPS **$5.06 vs. $3.72** expected, a **36% beat**; revenue **$9.55B vs. $9.21B**. Q1 2026 adjusted operating earnings rose to **$2.74 from $2.14**, aided by the Calpine acquisition. A **20-year agreement for 890 MW of upgraded nuclear capacity** is in place.
- **Competitive Moat:** The largest US carbon-free generation fleet. Nuclear capacity is effectively non-replicable on any timeline relevant to current AI demand — permitting and construction lead times are measured in a decade-plus. Hyperscalers requiring 24/7 carbon-free power have a very short list of counterparties, and Constellation is at the top of it.
- **Key Risk:** **Regulatory and political**, specifically PJM co-location policy and the upcoming backstop auction. If regulators restrict behind-the-meter data center arrangements or cap how many private bilateral deals can be struck, the premium multiple compresses quickly. This is a policy-dependent thesis and should be sized accordingly.
- **Rating:** **Strong Buy**

### 3. AEIS — Advanced Energy Industries *(Precision power conversion)*

- **Thesis:** Record quarter, raised guidance, 50% revenue growth, and a data center business that grew 49% to a record — in a layer of the stack (high-efficiency power conversion) where the engineering barrier is genuinely high and the customer qualification cycle is multi-year.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Q3 non-GAAP EPS **$3.65**; annualizing Q3–Q4 run-rate implies roughly **$14–15 of non-GAAP EPS power** entering 2027. I was unable to verify a current share price from available sources, so **confirm the live multiple before entry** — the valuation case rests on the earnings trajectory below, which is company-reported, rather than on a multiple I can independently source today.
- **Growth Profile:** Q3 revenue **$696M, +50% YoY**, above the high end of guidance. GAAP EPS from continuing operations **$2.80**; non-GAAP **$3.65**, above the high end. **Data center computing revenue a record $256M, +49% YoY.** Q4 guided to **~$735M ±$25M**. FY26 revenue growth raised to the **low-to-mid-30% range** from low-to-mid-20%; **data center growth guidance raised to the high-50% range**.
- **Competitive Moat:** Precision power conversion is a qualification-gated business — designs are locked into customer platforms for years and switching mid-cycle is prohibitively risky for the customer. The new flagship **Thailand facility is ramping with multiple data center customers already qualified**, and capacity is being expanded to support **over $5B of revenue by 2028**.
- **Key Risk:** The **800-volt data center product cycle is not a near-term earnings driver** — management expects high-volume production only in **2028**. The stock is therefore exposed to a multi-year air pocket between today's semiconductor-equipment-driven results and the next architectural step-up. Semi-cap equipment cyclicality remains the other half of this business and can offset data center strength.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

### 4. TTMI — TTM Technologies *(Advanced PCBs & substrates / A&D)*

- **Thesis:** Beat on every line, posted a record EBITDA margin, guided FY27 revenue up ~46%, **and the stock sold off on the print**. That reaction is the mispricing. A third of the business is aerospace and defense, which provides a genuine non-AI earnings floor that pure data center names lack.
- **Valuation Metrics:** FY26 guided non-GAAP EPS **$4.97–5.03** on revenue of **$4.47–4.49B**. Market cap data conflicted sharply across sources (**~$7.0B vs. ~$14.3B**), making a reliable P/E impossible to state — the implied multiple is somewhere between **~14x and ~28x** FY26 earnings. **Verify share count and live price before entry.** The FY27 revenue guide implies continued strong EPS leverage off the 20.1% EBITDA margin base.
- **Growth Profile:** Q3 revenue a record **$1,203.9M** (vs. $1.10–1.14B guidance), up ~**60% YoY** from $752.7M. Non-GAAP EPS **$1.46 vs. $1.26** consensus. **Data center & networking +115% YoY, now 41% of revenue**; aerospace & defense **+21%, 34% of revenue**. Record adjusted EBITDA margin **20.1%**. Q4 guided to **$1.42–1.44B** and EPS **$1.77–1.83**. Preliminary **FY27 revenue $6.5–6.75B**.
- **Competitive Moat:** High-layer-count and N+M advanced PCB capacity is scarce, capital-intensive, and qualification-gated — and a US-domiciled supplier with security clearances for defense work is scarcer still. The **$400M capacity investment through early 2028** extends that lead. The A&D backlog provides genuine diversification, not cosmetic diversification.
- **Key Risk:** **Capital intensity colliding with a capex pause.** The company is committing $400M against a 2027–2028 demand picture. If hyperscaler orders push out as the bears argue, TTM carries the fixed cost through the trough — and the negative stock reaction to a strong print suggests the market is already underwriting some of this.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

### 5. CRDO — Credo Technology *(High-speed interconnect / AECs)*

- **Thesis:** Revenue tripled in FY26 and grew another 115% YoY last quarter, management guides to >85% growth this fiscal year, and the stock has de-rated roughly 34% in a month. The growth-adjusted valuation is now defensible in a way it has not been for two years.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Forward P/E cited between **~21x** (per 24/7 Wall St, after the ~34% monthly drawdown) and **~34x** depending on source and date — **a wide and unresolved spread; verify before entry.** Normalized EPS is projected to rise from **$3.46 (FY26) to $9.71 (FY28)**. Even on the higher 34x figure, PEG sits well below 1 against >85% guided growth. Median analyst target cited at **$289**.
- **Growth Profile:** FY2026 revenue **$1.3B vs. $436.8M** in FY25; net income **$472.3M vs. $52.2M**. Fiscal Q1 2027 revenue **$479M, +115% YoY**; adjusted EPS **$1.20 vs. $1.17** consensus. CFO guided to **>85% full-year revenue growth** for FY27. AEC products drove **more than 90%** of the Q1 YoY revenue increase.
- **Competitive Moat:** Active Electrical Cables solve a specific physical problem — reliable high-bandwidth copper interconnect at rack scale — where Credo's SerDes IP is genuinely differentiated. Its AECs and 1.6T interconnects are designed into training clusters at **five of the six major hyperscalers**, and interconnect qualification is sticky within a cluster generation.
- **Key Risk:** **Severe customer concentration plus a credible competitive threat.** Top-10 customers were **~90% of FY26 revenue**. Meanwhile **MaxLinear is explicitly targeting 20–30% of the next-generation 1.6T AEC market**. Sequential growth has already decelerated sharply in one recent stretch (51.9% to 7.4%). This is the highest-variance name in the slate and should be sized as a speculative sleeve, not a core position.
- **Rating:** **Accumulate**

### 6. UI — Ubiquiti *(Enterprise & edge networking — non-AI-capex diversifier)*

- **Thesis:** 40% revenue growth and 54% EPS growth at a **28.6x forward multiple**, from a business with almost no hyperscaler dependence. This is deliberately the hedge in the slate: if the AI capex thesis breaks, this is the position that should not break with it.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Market cap **$40.55B**. Trailing P/E **~33.9x** (MarketBeat) to **~37.8x** (Simply Wall St); **forward P/E ~28.6x**; P/S ~**11.2x**; beta **1.34**. Against ~40% revenue and ~54% EPS growth, PEG is comfortably **below 0.7** on the forward multiple — the most attractive growth-adjusted valuation in this slate.
- **Growth Profile:** Fiscal Q1 2027 (quarter ended Sept 30, 2026) revenue **$1,026.2M vs. $733.8M** a year prior (**~+40%**); GAAP diluted EPS **$5.29 vs. $3.43** (**~+54%**). Enterprise Technology contributed **$941.3M**. FY2026 revenue **$3.27B, +27.2%**, with earnings of **$960.3M, +34.9%**. Gross margin steady at **46–47%**. Shares rose **12.7%** Monday on the print; a **$1.00/share dividend** is payable November 23.
- **Competitive Moat:** A direct-to-community distribution model with near-zero traditional sales force, producing structurally lower opex than Cisco or HPE/Aruba; an installed base of WISPs, integrators and prosumers with high switching costs at the management-software layer (UniFi); and brand loyalty in a segment incumbents have historically underserved.
- **Key Risk:** **Valuation leaves no margin for a growth stumble, and the stock has a history of violent drawdowns** — it fell roughly 50% from $1,099.99 to $547.67 over one stretch earlier this cycle. Governance is also concentrated under a controlling founder with limited analyst coverage and sparse disclosure relative to peers. Buying immediately after a 12.7% earnings pop adds timing risk; scaling in is appropriate.
- **Rating:** **Accumulate**

## Risk Factors

**1. A hot October CPI reviving an aggressive December hike path — SEVERE, and it resolves today.**
With hike odds already above 80%, a core print at or above 0.2% effectively locks in the December 9 move and puts the January meeting in play. Long-duration growth equities — which is to say most of this report's spotlight sector — carry the entire adjustment. This is the highest-probability, highest-impact risk on the board and it is hours away.

**2. Nvidia's November 17 print as a single-point-of-failure event — SEVERE.**
The entire AI infrastructure complex has been reduced to one earnings report. Consensus expects a 12%+ sequential data center revenue increase. Given that Nvidia is ~24% off its highs with $1.4T of value already erased, the asymmetry is poor in both directions: a beat may be partially priced after Monday's bounce, while a guide-down transmits instantly to every name in the spotlight section above.

**3. AI capex financing conditions tightening — HIGH, and structural rather than event-driven.**
Negative hyperscaler free cash flow, record-wide hyperscaler CDS, and debt-funded buildouts form a genuine fragility. This is the one bear argument that does not depend on demand weakening — it depends only on credit markets repricing. Monitor CDS spreads and investment-grade tech issuance rather than equity prices for early warning.

**4. Reversal of the oil-driven relief rally — MODERATE-HIGH.**
Brent's 17% weekly collapse is doing enormous work for both equity multiples and the inflation outlook, yet it rests on a political signal, not a settlement. The **US naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz remains in place**. ## Bottom Line

Monday's 2.35% advance was a volatility-crush rally on collapsing oil, not a resolution of anything — the VIX's 22% drop tells you positioning unwound, not that conviction returned. The actual opportunity is narrower and more specific: within AI infrastructure, companies reporting 50–115% growth with raised guidance de-rated 20–26% alongside the complex, while contracted assets like Equinix and Digital Realty barely moved, which means the market sold capex *duration* rather than capex *demand*. I would be a buyer of that dislocation across the stack — power, substrate, switching, interconnect — while explicitly hedging the thesis with a non-AI-capex position, and I would size everything with the understanding that this morning's CPI and Nvidia's November 17 print are two unresolved binary events standing between here and year-end.
