## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The Fed is in a tightening cycle, not an easing one.** This is the single most mis-framed fact in the current market narrative. In September 2026 the FOMC raised the federal funds target range by 25bp to **3.75%–4.00%** in a unanimous vote — the first hike in more than three years — explicitly to bring down elevated inflation.

**Inflation remains the binding constraint.** Core PCE rose from 3.0% in December 2025 to 3.3% in July 2026, and has printed above 3% in every month of 2026. That is not a rounding error above target; it is a persistent overshoot that has already forced one hike.

**The labor market just cracked.** September payrolls rose only 29,000. Unemployment ticked up to **4.2%** from 4.1%. July and August were revised down by a combined 60,000. Average hourly earnings growth cooled to 3.0% YoY from 3.1%.

**The market's repricing was immediate and aggressive.** Before Friday, fixed-income markets were "firmly pricing in back-to-back hikes" for October and December, with futures implying ~4.1% by January and ~4.8% by October 2027. After the jobs report, CME FedWatch put the odds of an October hike at just **17%**. The next FOMC meeting is **October 27–28**.

**This is the stagflationary squeeze in its purest form.** Inflation above 3% argues for hiking; payrolls at 29,000 argues for cutting. The Fed is boxed in, and the most likely outcome — a hold — satisfies neither mandate. Markets have resolved this tension optimistically. They may be right. But the resolution is an assumption, not a fact.

**What to watch this week:** ISM Services PMI Monday at 10:00 ET is the principal confirmed US macro event of the day. Given that the service sector is where both the inflation persistence and the labor softness are concentrated, this print carries more weight than its usual billing.

### Today's Spotlight: AI Infrastructure & Data Centers

*(Rotation: day-of-year 278; 278 mod 6 = 2 → AI infrastructure & data centers.)*

**The demand side is not in question.** Wall Street consensus pegs 2026 hyperscaler capex at roughly **$527 billion**. These are genuinely excellent businesses. AAON's backlog more than doubled to a record $2.13B with liquid-cooling sales up 186.8% TTM and FY26 revenue guidance raised to 40–45% growth. **Friday's HDD episode is the cautionary tale made concrete.** Seagate entered Friday **up 210% YTD** and Western Digital **up 150% YTD**. Then Toshiba announced a ¥60 billion (~$380M) investment to double hard-drive capacity, and both fell ~10% in a session — WDC closing at $415.29, down $47.27. Nothing changed about AI storage demand. What changed was a single assumption: that constrained supply would persist. When a trade is priced for a supply assumption rather than a demand assumption, it carries hidden fragility that no demand forecast will reveal. **This is precisely the risk embedded in a 49x EV/EBITDA multiple.**

**Where value still exists in this buildout — the four underpriced layers:**

1. **Interconnect and passive content.** Connector and cable businesses attach to every rack but are valued as industrial cyclicals rather than AI pure-plays. TE Connectivity's data center networking business is ~$3B of ~$20B revenue — large enough to move the needle, small enough that the whole company hasn't been re-rated.
2. **Distribution.** The unglamorous middle layer. WESCO moved $4.3B of data center product in FY25 (+50%) and ~$1.4B in Q1 2026 alone (+~70%), yet trades at a market multiple.
3. **Power generation.** IPPs with dispatchable capacity are signing 15-year contracts with investment-grade hyperscalers, and several still trade at single-digit forward earnings.
4. **Engineering and construction.** Gas-plant EPC capability is genuinely scarce — there are only a handful of firms in North America that can build a 1.4 GW combined-cycle plant.

Our picks today are drawn deliberately from these four layers rather than from the semiconductor and thermal names the market has already repriced.

### Artificial Intelligence

**The cost curve is collapsing, and that is a margin story, not just a capability story.** OpenAI launched **GPT-6.1 Sol** at **$2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output** — roughly **one-fifth** the price of GPT-6 Astra, while delivering near-Astra performance on agentic coding and computer use. At DevDay 2026 it also introduced "dots," always-on GPT-6 Astra agents.

A five-fold price cut at constant capability is the most important datapoint in AI this week. It expands the addressable market and it compresses revenue per token simultaneously. For infrastructure suppliers, falling inference cost drives volume — favorable. For model developers monetizing per-token, it is a deflationary treadmill.

**Anthropic's IPO filing is the first full look at AI-lab economics, and it is sobering.** The prospectus targets a valuation **above $2 trillion** and discloses **$518 billion in decade-long cloud, compute and infrastructure obligations, roughly 80% of which are non-cancelable**, against 2025 revenue of **$4.6 billion**, operating losses of about **$8 billion**, and compute costs of **$7.3 billion**. The company is targeting an offering as soon as mid-November, with formal marketing potentially beginning the week of November 9.

**Investors should sit with that ratio.** Non-cancelable obligations of $518B against $4.6B of revenue is a bet that revenue compounds into those commitments. If it does, the infrastructure complex has a decade of contracted demand. If it does not, those contracts are the transmission mechanism by which an AI disappointment reaches the power, cooling and networking suppliers — the same companies trading at 50x EBITDA. **The non-cancelable nature cuts both ways: it protects suppliers' near-term backlog and concentrates the eventual credit risk.** A November IPO at a $2T valuation would also be the most direct public market test of AI sentiment yet staged.

### Hardware & Semiconductors

**Foundry pricing power remains extraordinary.** TSMC holds roughly **73% of Q2 global foundry revenue**, guided Q3 2026 revenue to **$44.6–45.8B** (double-digit sequential growth), is up **50% YTD**, and is reportedly preparing to **raise prices 3–10% on sub-5nm**. A company raising prices into a 73% share position is the clearest demonstration of structural scarcity in the industry.

**Memory is where the cycle risk sits.** Server DRAM contract prices spiked **90–95% QoQ in Q1 2026**, then **58–63% in Q2**, then **13–18% in Q3** — a decelerating sequence that is the classic signature of a pricing cycle approaching its peak.

> **Data conflict disclosed:** One source projects server DRAM prices *falling 14.3% by Q3 2026* while another reports prices *rising 13–18% in Q3 2026*. These cannot both be true. The disagreement likely reflects spot-versus-contract and fiscal-versus-calendar framing, but we cannot reconcile it from today's research and will not pretend otherwise. **The direction of memory pricing into 2027 is genuinely uncertain, and position sizing in memory names should reflect that.** HBM prices are expected to rise through HBM4 ramp before easing in 2H26 as Samsung and Micron add supply.

Friday's gainers skewed toward semis — **MaxLinear +15.0%, Synaptics +14.1%, Wolfspeed +13.2%** — against losses in storage. Capital is rotating *within* the complex, not out of it.

### 1. TEL — TE Connectivity plc *(Interconnect & connector components)*

- **Thesis:** TE's data center networking business is growing ~33% YoY and sits inside a ~$20B industrial revenue base that the market still values as a connector cyclical — you get AI attach-rate growth at an industrial multiple. At a **16.6x forward P/E** it is the cheapest quality expression of AI infrastructure we found today.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Forward P/E **16.56x**; trailing P/E 20.91x. One source reports a PEG of 0.18 — *we regard that figure as distorted* (it likely annualizes a one-off growth spike) and would not underwrite the thesis on it; the forward P/E against ~20% EPS growth implies a sane PEG near 0.8. EV/EBITDA not confirmed in today's research.
- **Growth Profile:** Q3 FY26 net sales a record **$5.16B, +14% reported / +12% organic**; adjusted EPS a record **$2.94, +22% YoY**. Orders rose more than $1B YoY to $5.7B. FY26 guidance: **~15% sales growth, EPS growth above 20%**, adding more than $2.5B of revenue.
- **Competitive Moat:** Connectors are designed in, then qualified — a socket won is a socket held for the product's life. TE's breadth across data center, auto and energy infrastructure means no single end-market loss is existential.
- **Key Risk:** **AI growth has already decelerated from ~70% to ~33%**, attributed to program timing and capacity ramp. If that is cyclical, the valuation is a gift; if it is the AI connector content opportunity maturing, the re-rating case weakens materially. Automotive exposure also leaves TE levered to a consumer that just produced a 29,000 payroll print.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

### 2. NRG — NRG Energy, Inc. *(Independent power producer & retail energy)*

- **Thesis:** NRG is contracting dispatchable generation to data centers on 15-year investment-grade terms while trading at a high-single-digit forward multiple — the market is paying for the megawatts but not for the contracts.
- **Valuation Metrics:** **Forward P/E 9.03x** (current). ⚠️ **Disclosed conflict:** the same source shows forward P/E of **13.83x** as of June 30, 2026. A move from 13.8x to 9.0x in one quarter implies either a large guidance raise or a data inconsistency; we could not resolve which. Trailing P/E 26.23x. **Underwrite this name on the 13.8x figure, not the 9.0x figure** — it is still inexpensive there, and the thesis should not depend on the more flattering number.
- **Growth Profile:** FCF before growth investments **+12% YoY to $1.025B** in Q2 2026. The flagship data center agreement carries a **15-year minimum term with an investment-grade counterparty**, scalable to **2.4 GW**, projected at full operation in late 2029 to generate **$500M annual adjusted EBITDA and $375M annual FCF before growth** — about **12% accretion** to FCFbG at a 12–15% project IRR. A separate 295 MW Texas agreement is intended to scale to 500 MW and ultimately 1 GW.
- **Competitive Moat:** Firm, dispatchable capacity in ERCOT and PJM is genuinely scarce and cannot be permitted into existence quickly. The integrated retail book provides a natural hedge that merchant-only generators lack.
- **Key Risk:** **The headline economics arrive in late 2029** — five years of execution, interconnection and construction risk before the $500M EBITDA appears. Merchant power earnings are volatile in the interim, and Q2 2026 already featured an earnings miss alongside the data center announcement.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

### 3. FLEX — Flex Ltd. *(Electronics manufacturing & data center power infrastructure)*

- **Thesis:** Flex is converting itself from a low-multiple contract manufacturer into a data center power business, and has announced a spin-off that will force the market to price that transformation separately. At **20.7x forward earnings and 1.36x sales**, almost none of that is reflected.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Forward P/E **20.66x**; trailing P/E 41.66x; P/S ~**1.36x**. The trailing-to-forward gap is wide — most of this thesis is forward earnings delivery, which is a risk, not a footnote. Average analyst target $160.50 (~48.8% upside) across 11 analysts, consensus "Strong Buy."
- **Growth Profile:** FY26 revenue **$27.91B (+8.1%)**, earnings $880M (+5.0%) — modest at the consolidated level. The relevant number is the **data center segment: +35% YoY in FY26, following +50% in FY25**. Operating margin 6.2% despite tariff headwinds.
- **Competitive Moat:** Scale and vertical integration into power conversion. The **$4.4B EPC Power acquisition** (closing Q4 CY2026) adds ~$800M of 2026 revenue growing ~40% organically with **EBITDA margins approaching 30% in 2027** — dramatically above Flex's 6.2% corporate margin, which is the entire point.
- **Key Risk:** **$4.4B is a full price**, and EMS companies have a long history of destroying value through acquisition. The **Cloud and Power Infrastructure spin-off in Q1 CY2027** is the catalyst *and* the risk: separations slip, and a delayed or poorly-received spin removes the re-rating mechanism while leaving the acquisition debt.
- **Rating:** **Buy**
- *Adjacency disclosure: JBL (picked 09-17) is a direct EMS peer. FLEX is taken here as a power-infrastructure and spin-off story rather than an EMS story, but investors already holding JBL should treat these as correlated, not diversifying.*

### 4. WCC — WESCO International, Inc. *(Electrical & data center distribution)*

- **Thesis:** WESCO is a toll booth on the physical data center buildout — every switchgear, conduit and cable run passes through distribution — and it is growing data center revenue ~70% YoY while trading at a market multiple.
- **Valuation Metrics:** P/E **24.30x** on EPS of $14.30. ⚠️ **Disclosed conflict:** analyst fair value estimates were raised to **$313.25** and Street price targets sit at **$375–$440**, but the most popular independent narrative puts fair value at **$268.45 — implying the stock is 38.6% overvalued**. That is an unusually wide dispersion and the bear case deserves equal billing: this is a cyclical distributor in an unusually good cycle.
- **Growth Profile:** Q1 2026 record sales **$6.08B (+13.8%, +12.3% organic)**; **data center sales ~$1.4B, ~24% of total, up ~70% YoY**; record backlog **+22% YoY**; adjusted EPS **$3.37 (+52.5%)**. FY25 data center sales were $4.3B (+50%). FY26 guidance: adjusted EPS **$14.50–$16.50** and FCF of **$500–800M**.
- **Competitive Moat:** Distribution scale, supplier relationships and working-capital capacity. In a shortage environment — which transformers and switchgear remain — the distributor holding allocation controls the customer relationship.
- **Key Risk:** **Distribution is a cyclical, thin-margin business, and backlog is not revenue.** A 22% backlog built on data center optimism can unwind quickly if hyperscaler capex digests, and WESCO's operating leverage works in both directions. At 24x, this is no longer priced as a distributor.
- **Rating:** **Accumulate** *(not Buy — the valuation dispersion argues for scaling in)*
- *Adjacency disclosure: ARW (picked 09-15) is also a distributor, though of electronic components rather than electrical product.*

### 5. AGX — Argan, Inc. *(Power plant EPC & energy construction)*

- **Thesis:** Argan is one of a very small number of firms in North America that can actually build a gigawatt-scale gas plant, it is doing so for the data center power build, and roughly **18% of its market capitalization is net cash**.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Market cap **$5.39B**; TTM EPS $12.65; forward P/E **38.04x**; EV/EBITDA 24.91x. The headline multiple overstates the price: net cash of **$963.98M ($68.76/share)** against $9.58M of debt means the **enterprise value is roughly 18% below the market cap**, and the EV-based multiple is materially lower than the P/E suggests. Still not cheap.
- **Growth Profile:** FY26 added **$2.5B in new contract value, doubling backlog from $1.4B to $2.9B**. ⚠️ **Disclosed negative:** backlog has since **declined to ~$2.5B as of July 31, 2026**. That drawdown is burn-off on active construction rather than cancellation, but it is a real sequential decline and we flag it rather than quoting only the $2.9B peak. Currently constructing **~5.5 GW of thermal generation** across the US and Ireland, including an **860 MW ERCOT gas plant** and a **1.4 GW combined-cycle plant in Ward County, Texas**.
- **Competitive Moat:** Scarce EPC capability, bonding capacity, and a fortress balance sheet with no debt — in fixed-price construction, counterparty confidence in your solvency is itself a competitive advantage. Argan also has a direct data center hardware tie: a contract for **~2,000 horizontal pressure vessels** for thermal energy storage and chilled water buffer cooling at customer data centers.
- **Key Risk:** **Fixed-price EPC is an unforgiving business.** Percentage-of-completion accounting makes revenue lumpy, and a single project overrun on a 1.4 GW plant can erase a year of earnings. At 38x forward with backlog already declining sequentially, the margin for execution error is thin.
- **Rating:** **Accumulate**

### 6. CLS — Celestica Inc. *(AI networking & hyperscaler hardware platforms)*

- **Thesis:** Celestica has converted itself from a commodity assembler into the hardware platform partner for hyperscaler 1.6T Ethernet switching, and is guiding to 53% revenue and 68% EPS growth — the one name in this report where an elevated multiple is arguably earned by the growth rate.
- **Valuation Metrics:** ⚠️ **Disclosed conflict, and it is material.** One source reports CLS trading at **~25.0x forward P/E**; another reports **37.0x on 1-year forward earnings, a 42% premium to comps at 26.1x**. TTM P/E **40.38x**, P/S 2.71x, EV/EBITDA 27.90x. **At 25x against 68% EPS growth this is compelling; at 37x it is merely reasonable.** We could not resolve the discrepancy and investors should verify the current forward multiple before sizing. This is the least certain valuation in today's slate.
- **Growth Profile:** 2026 guidance of **$19B revenue and $10.15 adjusted EPS — +53% and +68% respectively**. Q1 2026 revenue **$4,047.0M (+53%)** with net earnings **+146%**. HPS revenue +63% to ~$1.7B in Q1 (42% of total) and +58% to ~$1.9B in Q2. CCS segment guided to **75%+ YoY growth** in FY26.
- **Competitive Moat:** Qualified, multi-year hyperscaler programs in 1.6T Ethernet switching and AI/ML compute. Requalifying a switch platform mid-cycle is expensive and slow, which creates genuine switching costs atop a historically commoditized business.
- **Key Risk:** **Customer concentration is severe** — growth is driven by a small number of hyperscaler programs, and the Enterprise segment's 101% Q1 growth came from "the ramp-up of an AI/ML compute program with one of hyperscaler customers." Losing or digesting a single program would be visible immediately. ODM margins remain structurally thin, so a volume air-pocket hits earnings hard.
- **Rating:** **Buy** *(with the explicit valuation caveat above)*

## Risk Factors

**1. The bond market — severity: highest.** The 10-year Treasury at **5.28%** alongside accelerating French OAT spreads and flight-to-quality into Bunds. Equity valuations near record highs are being supported by an assumption of falling discount rates that the bond market is actively contradicting. A sovereign-stress event in Europe transmits to US equities through credit spreads within days.

**2. Stagflationary policy trap — severity: high.** Core PCE above 3% for every month of 2026 against payrolls of 29,000. The market has priced the benign resolution (Fed holds, inflation fades, labor stabilizes). The October 27–28 FOMC is the test. **A hawkish hold with an inflation-focused statement would be a genuine repricing event**, and at a VIX of 15.31 that risk is cheaply hedged.

**3. Oil above $100 — severity: high.** Brent at $101.31 with 10+ mb/d of Gulf production shut in and the IEA cutting 2026 supply by 5.7 mb/d. This is the mechanism that converts risk #2 from a possibility into a certainty: sustained triple-digit crude feeds headline inflation and forecloses the Fed's ability to respond to labor weakness.

**4. AI capex concentration and financing structure — severity: medium-high.** Anthropic's **$518B of largely non-cancelable compute obligations against $4.6B of revenue and ~$8B of operating losses** illustrates how much infrastructure demand rests on model developers who are not yet profitable. A November IPO at a $2T target valuation will test that structure publicly. Every name in today's slate is exposed to this risk; it is the common factor and the reason for diversifying across layers rather than concentrating in the highest-multiple ones.

**5. An extraordinarily high earnings bar — severity: medium.** The S&P 500 is expected to report **+29.5% YoY Q3 earnings growth** — a third consecutive quarter above 25% and an eighth consecutive double-digit quarter. Expectations that elevated convert in-line results into disappointments. Costco (Oct 8), PepsiCo (Oct 8) and Delta (Oct 9) precede the real test: the money-center banks on October 13–14.

## Bottom Line

The market is celebrating a weak jobs report because it removed a rate hike, which is a strange thing to celebrate when core inflation remains above 3%, Brent is above $100, and the 10-year yields 5.28%. We are not bearish — earnings growth near 30% and $527B of hyperscaler capex are real — but we think the *expression* of the AI trade matters far more than the trade itself right now, and Friday's 10% single-session losses in Seagate and Western Digital on one competitor's capacity announcement are the proof. **Today's slate deliberately buys the AI buildout at 9x to 24x through power, distribution, interconnect and construction rather than at 50x to 190x through semiconductors, cooling and storage** — the same megawatts and the same racks, with considerably less of the price already paid. Hedges are cheap at a VIX of 15.31 with realized volatility at 10.51; the October 27–28 FOMC is the date to own them into.
