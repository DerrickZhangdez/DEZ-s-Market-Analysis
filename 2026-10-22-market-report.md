## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: Enterprise Software & SaaS

*(Rotation: day-of-year 295; 295 mod 6 = 1 → Enterprise Software & SaaS.)*

This is the most dislocated major sector in the market right now, and the dislocation has a specific, nameable cause that is worth separating from noise.

#### The "SaaSpocalypse": what actually happened

Early 2026 saw a violent de-rating across software. Reported figures vary by source — one puts the January–February drawdown at roughly $2 trillion in sector market capitalization, another cites $285B in a narrower cut — and these come largely from industry blogs rather than primary research, so the precise number should be held loosely. The *mechanism*, however, is consistent across every source and is corroborated by analyst behavior:

**The seat-compression thesis.** Agentic AI performing autonomous knowledge work implies enterprises need fewer paid human seats. The oft-cited illustration is a company that licensed 500 seats renewing for 50 while holding output constant. Atlassian is the most frequently named casualty, with reports of its first-ever decline in enterprise seat counts.

**Caution on the evidence.** Most of the dramatic seat-count anecdotes are exactly that — anecdotes, often published by vendors with a commercial stake in the "SaaS is dead" framing. I could not trace them to filings. What *is* verifiable is that the market has repriced on the thesis: by March 2026 public B2B software had compressed roughly 25% year-to-date, with traditional SaaS trading below 5x revenue.

**The pricing-model response.** Vendors are migrating off pure per-seat billing. Deloitte's forecast (as cited secondhand) has seat-based revenue falling from 21% to 15% of total SaaS income. Workday introduced "Flex Credits" to move toward consumption. The consensus landing zone across sources is hybrid — per-seat *plus* agent pricing at a higher blended price point — not the elimination of software spend.

#### The analytical framework for this report

If seat compression is the dominant risk, then the correct screen is not "cheap software." It is **software whose revenue is not a function of human headcount.** That reframes the sector into tiers:

| Billing basis | Seat-compression exposure | Examples of model |
|---|---|---|
| Per knowledge-worker seat | **High** | Collaboration, CRM seats, HR seats, devtools-by-user |
| Consumption / telemetry volume | **Low — potentially inverse** | Observability, infrastructure monitoring |
| Transaction / network connection | **Low** | EDI networks, customs filings, payments |
| Per-jurisdiction / per-asset / regulated module | **Low** | Government ERP, bank core systems |

The second row deserves emphasis: for consumption-priced infrastructure software, AI agents are a **tailwind, not a threat**. More autonomous workloads generate more hosts, more traces, more logs, and more telemetry to monitor. An agent that replaces a human seat still has to run somewhere, and that somewhere is billable.

This is the thesis that organizes today's picks, and it is why a sector most investors are avoiding wholesale is, selectively, where the best risk-adjusted setups currently are.

#### Rate-regime overlay

Layer the Fed on top. With policy at 3.75%–4.00% and a December hike live, the software names that work are those that convert revenue to free cash flow *today*. Unprofitable growth at 8x revenue has two separate problems in this regime — a discount-rate problem and a business-model problem — and we are avoiding that entire cohort.

## Stock Picks — Valuation-Based

**Methodology note.** All five picks are drawn from today's spotlight sector and screened on a single organizing principle: *revenue that is not billed per human seat*, combined with positive free cash flow in a 4% rate regime. None appears on the 7-report exclusion list, and none has been recommended in the last month of reports. ### 1. TYL — Tyler Technologies, Inc. *(Public-sector vertical SaaS — courts, public safety, municipal ERP)*

**Thesis:** Tyler sells mission-critical systems of record to courts, police departments, and municipal governments on multi-year contracts, billed per jurisdiction and module with an attached payments business — a revenue model with essentially zero exposure to knowledge-worker seat compression. It has de-rated with the software complex anyway, and Wells Fargo's upgrade to Overweight marks the point where the Street began separating it from seat-based peers.

**Valuation Metrics:**
- Price: ~$377 (October 20 reference; $339.94 cited earlier in the month — the stock has rallied several sessions running)
- Market cap: ~$16B
- P/E (trailing): ~50x — against a **5-year median of 94.2x**
- P/E (forward): ~23.0x–24.6x (sources: StockAnalysis 23.0x, Yahoo 23.7x, GuruFocus 24.6x)
- P/S: ~6.6x on TTM revenue of ~$2.43B
- EV/EBITDA: not reliably sourced today — treat as a gap
- PEG: ~2.0–2.5x estimated on high-single-digit growth; this is **not** a cheap-on-PEG name

**Growth Profile:** TTM revenue ~$2.43B, +8.2%. Growth and earnings growth are both decelerating — Yahoo flags this explicitly for FY26 Q2. The offsetting driver is mix: continued cloud migration and expanding integrated payments carry higher margins than the legacy on-premise base, so earnings should compound faster than revenue.

**Competitive Moat:** Among the most durable in software. Replacing a county court case-management system or a city's public-safety dispatch platform is a multi-year, politically visible, failure-intolerant project. Switching costs are extreme, contracts run long, revenue is recession-resistant (municipal budgets are not cyclical in the way enterprise IT is), and the payments attach rate grows without requiring new seats.

**Key Risk:** The valuation still embeds a quality premium — 50x trailing is not a value multiple, and growth has slowed to high single digits. If cloud-migration revenue fails to accelerate, the forward multiple compresses toward the growth rate. Municipal budget stress in a high-rate environment is a secondary risk.

**Rating: Accumulate** *(quality at a reasonable — not cheap — price; build on weakness)*

### 2. DT — Dynatrace, Inc. *(Observability & application performance monitoring)*

**Thesis:** Dynatrace bills on consumption — hosts monitored and telemetry volume — not seats, which means autonomous AI agents *increase* its billable surface rather than shrinking it. Every agentic workload an enterprise deploys generates infrastructure that must be monitored, and it has been de-rated alongside seat-based software it does not resemble.

**Valuation Metrics:**
- Market cap: ~$16.9B–$19B (sources differ)
- P/E (trailing): **wide disagreement** — 33.6x on one aggregator, 73.7x per an InvestingPro-linked summary, 117.0x GAAP on Yahoo. The spread reflects GAAP vs. adjusted treatment of stock comp; the GAAP number is the honest one and it is high.
- P/E (forward): ~29.6x
- P/S: ~8.1x
- EV/EBITDA: not reliably sourced
- PEG: ~1.9–2.0x on forward P/E against mid-teens growth

**Growth Profile:** Q2 CY2026 revenue $554.5M, **+16.2% YoY**; trailing twelve-month growth +17.9%. Management guided FY2027 revenue growth of ~14.6% — decelerating but still comfortably double-digit. EPS guidance: $2.00 (FY27E) rising to $2.35 (FY28E), implying ~17% earnings growth.

**Competitive Moat:** The Davis causal-inference AI engine and single-agent architecture provide genuinely differentiated root-cause analysis rather than correlation-based alerting. Observability is deeply embedded in incident-response workflows, and rip-and-replace means losing historical baselines. Consumption pricing aligns Dynatrace's revenue with its customers' infrastructure growth.

**Key Risk:** Competition is the real issue, not seat compression — Datadog competes hard on breadth, and OpenTelemetry is commoditizing the instrumentation layer, which could compress pricing power over time. The GAAP multiple also leaves no room for a growth stumble.

**Rating: Buy**

### 3. SPSC — SPS Commerce, Inc. *(Retail supply-chain network / EDI)*

**Thesis:** A genuine network-effect business — retailers mandate SPS connectivity, suppliers must comply, and the value compounds with each node — trading roughly 63% below its peak after an earnings miss, with an active Morgan Stanley-run sale process and reported advanced private-equity talks providing a valuation floor. Revenue is billed per trading-partner connection and transaction volume, not per seat.

**Valuation Metrics:**
- Price: ~$85.25 (October 16 close); 52-week range **$49.04–$114.87**
- Market cap: ~$3.07B–$3.22B
- P/E (trailing): **43.2x GAAP** per Yahoo — note one source cites "about 17x earnings," almost certainly on a forward non-GAAP basis. **This is a material discrepancy and the GAAP figure is the conservative one.**
- P/S: ~4x on revenue approaching ~$800M annualized
- EV/EBITDA: not reliably sourced
- PEG: unfavorable on GAAP earnings given 6–8% growth; this is a special-situation and asset-value call, not a growth-at-a-reasonable-price call

**Growth Profile:** Q2 2026 revenue $197.8M vs. $187.4M, **+6%**. Organic recurring revenue growth slowed to 6% in Q2 2026, against 18% reported 2025 growth that was heavily acquisition-assisted. 2026 organic guidance is 7–8%. The deceleration is the whole reason the stock is down — Q3 results missed revenue expectations and the stock fell 24%.

**Competitive Moat:** Network effects, which are the most durable moat type in software and the hardest to disrupt with AI. An AI agent does not remove the need for a standardized retailer-supplier data interchange; if anything it increases transaction volume. 45,000+ recurring revenue customers and retailer mandates create compulsory adoption.

**Key Risk:** Organic growth at 6% does not support a 43x GAAP multiple on fundamentals alone — a meaningful part of this thesis rests on the sale process completing. **If the GTCR talks collapse, the stock likely retests the lower half of its 52-week range.** William Blair estimates a buyout at $90–100/share ($3.3–3.6B); Citigroup raised its target to $99 on October 20, but Yahoo's consensus 1-year target sits at just $78.36 — *below* the current price, which tells you the Street does not underwrite the standalone business here.

**Catalyst calendar:** Q3 earnings October 29.

**Rating: Buy** *(special situation — size it as one, not as a core software holding)*

### 4. PRGS — Progress Software Corporation *(Infrastructure software & data platforms)*

**Thesis:** A disciplined infrastructure-software roll-up throwing off enormous free cash flow relative to its market capitalization, trading at a mid-single-digit forward earnings multiple because the market has lumped a non-seat-based, mission-critical infrastructure business in with the SaaS de-rating. The leverage is real and is the reason the opportunity exists.

**Valuation Metrics:**
- Market cap: ~$1.67B
- P/E (trailing): ~17.5x–20.2x (sources: 17.5x mid-year, 19.6x InvestingPro, 19.7x–20.2x Yahoo)
- P/E (forward): **~5.4x–6.5x** (Yahoo 6.47x; GuruFocus 5.39x as of June 3) — this is non-GAAP EPS guidance of $6.15–6.23 against a depressed share price
- P/S: ~1.7x on FY26 revenue guidance of ~$990M–$1.0B
- PEG: **0.31** per InvestingPro
- 52-week range: $23.82–$60.87

**Growth Profile:** FY2026 revenue guidance ~$990M to just over $1B; EPS guidance narrowed to **$6.15–$6.23**. Adjusted free cash flow guidance of **$275–283M** against a $1.67B market cap is roughly a **17% FCF yield on equity** — and roughly 10% on enterprise value of ~$2.77B once net debt is included. Q3 FY26 adjusted FCF was $87M (+17% YoY); year-to-date $265M (+44%).

**Competitive Moat:** OpenEdge and the core infrastructure portfolio are embedded in customer applications with very high renewal rates and minimal discretionary churn — customers do not rip out the database layer their applications are written against. The acquisition engine (ShareFile, and the $400M purchase of Domo's AI and data platform business closed in Q3) is managed for cash conversion rather than reported growth. Management expects Domo to contribute well over $100M in annual EBITDA once fully integrated by end of FY27.

**Key Risk:** **Leverage, plus a flattered cash-flow year.** The company ended Q3 with $114M cash against ~$1.24B total debt — net debt ~$1.1B, net leverage ~2.7x TTM. In a rising-rate environment that is a genuine cost. Critically, **management itself attributes the unusually strong FY26 free cash flow to resolving ShareFile billing and collection problems from FY25**, meaning FY26 includes catch-up collections and analysts expect normalization next year. Underwriting the 17% FCF yield as a run-rate would be an error. The dividend was suspended to fund ShareFile and redirect capital to debt paydown ($170M repaid year-to-date). Organic growth is minimal — this is a cash-harvest story, not a growth story.

**Rating: Buy** *(deep value, with the leverage risk explicitly priced and position-sized accordingly)*

### 5. DSGX — The Descartes Systems Group Inc. *(Global logistics, customs & trade-compliance network)*

**Thesis:** Descartes operates a transaction-priced logistics and customs-compliance network whose revenue scales with shipment volume and regulatory complexity rather than headcount. It has sold off roughly 21% over twelve months on generalized "AI will disrupt software" sentiment that BMO has explicitly called overdone, leaving a high-margin network business at a meaningful discount to its own history.

**Valuation Metrics:**
- P/E: **~26x against a one-year historical average of ~40x**
- Revenue growth assumption in third-party models: ~10.9%–11%
- Operating margins: ~32%
- 12-month price performance: approximately −21%
- P/S and EV/EBITDA: not reliably sourced today — a gap worth closing before sizing a position

**Growth Profile:** Forecast ~11% annual revenue growth at ~32% operating margins — a rare combination of double-digit top-line growth and genuine profitability in a sector where one usually comes at the expense of the other. Growth is a blend of organic network expansion and consistent tuck-in acquisitions.

**Competitive Moat:** The Global Logistics Network plus proprietary customs and trade-content data. The regulatory content library — duty rates, restricted-party lists, filing requirements across jurisdictions — is expensive to build, must be continuously maintained, and carries compliance liability if wrong. That is precisely the kind of accuracy-critical, legally-exposed workload enterprises are *least* willing to hand to a generative model. Transaction pricing means more trade volume equals more revenue.

**Key Risk:** Sensitivity to global trade volumes. A tariff shock or a contraction in cross-border shipping hits the transaction base directly, and the current geopolitical environment — sanctions shifts, Gulf shipping disruption — cuts both ways. Sentiment risk also persists: the stock fell again on September 2 purely on sector-wide caution, with no operational deterioration. Note that two sources describe the decline as sentiment-driven rather than fundamental, which supports the thesis but also means there is no catalyst forcing a re-rating.

**Rating: Accumulate**

The October 16 executive order waiving certain Russian energy sanctions is the offsetting force, but it is a political decision that could reverse. Crude at $111+ is simultaneously an equity risk, an inflation risk, and therefore a monetary-policy risk — the three channels compound rather than diversify.

**4. Software seat-compression de-rating continuing — MODERATE to HIGH (sector-specific).**
Directly relevant to today's picks. The sector has already compressed ~25% YTD, and the narrative has momentum independent of fundamentals — Descartes fell on a day with no company news at all. Even correctly-selected non-seat-based software can keep de-rating if the market continues to sell the sector indiscriminately. Our mitigation is business-model selection, not valuation alone, but mitigation is not immunity.

**5. Earnings-season dispersion and single-name gap risk — MODERATE.**
The reaction function this week has been brutal and non-linear: Netflix −4.2% on slowing growth, Chipotle −5.35%, Dexcom −4.46%, Abbott −3.48% *on a beat with reaffirmed guidance*, against SMCI +9.18% and CRL +7.93%. Beating consensus is not sufficient protection. With SPSC reporting October 29, this is a live, dated risk inside the pick list.

## Bottom Line

The market is trading capex, not earnings, and this morning it is trading it badly — Meta and Tesla both beat and both got sold, which tells you positioning in the AI complex is stretched enough that good news is no longer sufficient. With the Fed hiking into a hot CPI and Brent above $111, the path of least resistance for long-duration growth equities is lower over the next week, and the October 28 statement language on December is the binary event to manage around.

The opportunity is in the wreckage of a different trade. Enterprise software has de-rated roughly 25% this year on a seat-compression thesis that is directionally real but has been applied indiscriminately — including to businesses that do not sell seats at all. Tyler's jurisdictions, Dynatrace's telemetry, SPS Commerce's trading-partner connections, Progress's infrastructure licenses, and Descartes's customs filings are all billed on something other than human headcount, and for consumption-priced infrastructure in particular, agentic AI is a revenue tailwind rather than a threat. Buy the model, not the multiple — and keep the leveraged and deal-dependent names half-sized until the Fed's December intentions are legible.
