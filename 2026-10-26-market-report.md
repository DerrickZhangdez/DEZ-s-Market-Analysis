## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The Fed is in a tightening cycle, not an easing one.** This is the single most important framing for positioning and it is routinely mis-stated. The September FOMC statement raised the target range by 25bp **to 3.75–4.00%**, and the vote was unanimous. Some data aggregators still carry 3.50–3.75%; the Board's own statement is authoritative. The minutes recorded that "most participants" saw another increase as likely appropriate "by year end."

**Wednesday's decision (Oct 27–28 meeting, announcement 2:00 PM ET Oct 28) is priced as a hold.** Kalshi shows roughly 94% probability of no change; CME FedWatch via Forbes is nearer 80%. The live meeting is **December 9**, where a hike is widely expected. That means Wednesday's market-moving content is the *statement language and the dot plot*, not the rate itself — and it lands three to four hours before Microsoft and Alphabet report.

**Inflation remains the unresolved problem.** Headline inflation has run above the Fed's 2% target for five consecutive years. We were unable to source the September CPI print (scheduled for release October 14) from available feeds and flag that as a gap in today's analysis. The mechanical problem for the Committee is that the current impulse is a **supply shock, not a demand shock**: Brent at $114+ and diesel at $6.32/gal feed directly into goods and services costs through freight and energy inputs, and monetary tightening does nothing to restore 3 mbpd of bombed refining capacity. Tightening into an energy supply shock compresses demand without fixing the price source — the classic 1970s policy trap, and the reason the long end is pinned near 5%.

**The 10-year at ~5.0% is the key cross-asset variable.** It caps multiple expansion across the market, it is actively hostile to long-duration growth equities, and it is why the rotation into cash-generative cyclicals has legs. It also raises the hurdle rate on every rate-sensitive industrial — a point we take seriously in portfolio construction below.

**Week ahead:** FOMC decision Wednesday 2:00 PM ET; 179 S&P 500 companies report; Microsoft (FQ1 2027) and Alphabet (Q3 2026) Wednesday after the close, calls at 5:30 PM and 4:30 PM ET respectively; Amazon Thursday after the close (5:00 PM ET call); Eli Lilly Thursday pre-open; ExxonMobil Friday pre-open. Consensus clusters near $4.71–4.72 EPS for Microsoft on roughly $90.63B revenue, and $2.94–3.03 for Alphabet, with meaningful dispersion between sources.

## Geopolitical Factors

Geopolitics is not a sidebar in this report — it is the primary driver of the commodity complex and, through it, of the inflation path and the Fed's December decision.

**The escalation.** Brent settled Friday at $116.72, the highest since April 29. The drivers: reported U.S. and Israeli planning for fresh strikes on Iran **after the midterm elections**, continued Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, and a U.S. order for non-emergency government personnel and eligible family members to depart Saudi Arabia. The Strait of Hormuz was closed at one point during the conflict, halting exports and spiking product tanker rates to record levels.

**The de-escalation attempt.** Monday's 2% pullback is attributed to Qatari mediators arriving in Iran. Middle Eastern crude exports have recovered to near pre-war levels. This is the market pricing diplomacy, not resolution.

**The damage that does not reverse quickly.** Roughly **3 million barrels per day of regional refining capacity** has been knocked out by drone and missile strikes. Crude export recovery and refining capacity recovery are different timelines — crude can be re-routed in weeks, a damaged crude distillation unit takes quarters to rebuild. This asymmetry is precisely why *crude* is off its highs while *crack spreads* are at records. It is the central fact of this week's energy tape.

**What the forward curve says.** Brent futures for April 2027 trade just under $100, which tells you the market expects a prolonged geopolitical risk premium rather than a spike-and-collapse. The IEA reportedly sees supply short of demand in Q4 2026, with only a seasonal surplus in Q1 2027.

**Trading implication.** This is a two-sided risk, and that cuts against sizing energy aggressively in either direction. A strike after the midterms re-prices crude violently higher; a credible Qatari-brokered de-escalation collapses both the crude premium and the crack spread at once. Owning peak-cycle refining earnings here is a bet on the *absence* of diplomacy.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: Industrials, Energy, Materials & "Picks-and-Shovels"

*(Day-of-year 299; 299 mod 6 = 5.)*

This bucket is where the current macro regime actually transmits into earnings, and it divides cleanly into three groups with very different risk profiles. Getting that division right is the whole analytical exercise today.

#### Group 1 — Peak-cycle energy: the value trap

The numbers here are spectacular and that is exactly the problem.

**Refining.** The 3-2-1 crack spread has been estimated at roughly **$64.83/bbl** on October 6 — near the widest reading since 1986 — with other estimates above $69/bbl, against a normal band of roughly $15–25. Valero's Q3 refining margin was **$39.24 per barrel, versus $13.14** in the year-ago quarter — a 3x expansion. Marathon Petroleum and Valero shares had nearly doubled year-to-date as of July; Phillips 66 was up 66%.

The supporting newsflow is intense: the DOE authorized an emergency exchange of up to 400,000 barrels from the SPR on October 23 to cover a disruption at Phillips 66's Lake Charles refinery, and a lockout of 385 workers began at its Linden/Bayway, New Jersey refinery on October 19. Valero's COO said on the October 22 call that he expects spreads to recover from recent pressure but gave no timetable, attributing softness to SPR-release headlines and Russian sanctions news. The EIA has noted gasoline cracks declined through the second half of the quarter.

**Why we are not picking a refiner.** A trailing P/E computed on $39/bbl margins is arithmetically meaningless as a valuation anchor. Marathon Petroleum illustrates the trap precisely: sources show a trailing P/E between 13.5x and 16.0x and a **forward P/E of 5.46x on Yahoo and 7.6x for 2026 on Trefis** — multiples that look like a screaming bargain and are in fact the market correctly refusing to capitalize a windfall. HF Sinclair trades at a trailing 11.61x **against its own 5-year median of 7.85x**, i.e. it is *expensive* on its own history despite the low absolute number, and Simply Wall St's $102.33 fair value sits below the $114.60 price. Phillips 66 additionally carries a 48% debt-to-capital ratio, leaving it the least room if spreads normalize. The correct read: these equities are a levered bet on Middle East escalation, not a valuation opportunity.

**Product tankers — the same trap, more extreme.** Six U.S.-listed tanker companies earned a combined **$1.83 billion in Q2 2026 net income against $330 million a year earlier**. Scorpio Tankers alone earned $387.5M, roughly 5x. Scorpio's own 6-K describes rates spiking to record levels when Hormuz exports stopped and then trending down from those highs as the quarter progressed and the global fleet repositioned. BofA downgraded Scorpio to Underperform on the explicit thesis that **product tanker rates have moved multiple standard deviations above peak levels**. Teekay trades near 6.2x against a fair ratio of 7.4x; Scorpio traded at 6.97x at the time of the downgrade. Balance sheets are genuinely strong — Evercore cites average debt-to-capital near 25%, Teekay holds net cash at 34.5% of market value, Scorpio 21.8% — but low single-digit P/Es on peak-cycle shipping earnings are the oldest false signal in the sector. A Hormuz reopening collapses the thesis overnight.

**Oilfield equipment — better logic, poor timing.** The structural argument is real and NOV states it in its own 10-Q: reduced production capacity from years of underinvestment should spur upstream investment, with orders improving in H2 2026 and growing further in 2027. But the current numbers do not support it yet. NOV trades at a forward P/E near 16.9x with a trailing multiple around 23x, consensus is Hold, and Zacks-sourced commentary notes conservative upstream capital spending is still hurting equipment demand. Critically, **NOV reports Q3 on October 28** — buying a thesis two days ahead of the print that tests it is not analysis, it is a coin flip. Weatherford is cheaper at ~15.6x with free cash flow margin improved to 13.6%, but Q3 revenue **fell 7.2% year-on-year to $1.14 billion**; a declining top line undercuts any growth-at-a-reasonable-price framing. Chart Industries and Flowserve both carry unresolved M&A status in available sources and are uninvestable on data-integrity grounds today.

#### Group 2 — Capex recipients: where the durable money is

The structural spending story is far better documented than the reshoring narrative, and it is worth separating the two.

**The grid and power buildout is the real boom.** S&P Global's Regulatory Research Associates forecasts roughly **$1.3 trillion of U.S. energy utility capex between 2026 and 2030** — a record — driven primarily by data center demand. Schwab's October 2 sector note cites capital spending on electricity capacity, AI infrastructure construction, defense and energy as the support for industrials, while flagging higher costs and possible data center construction delays as the offset. State Street remains positive, citing AI infrastructure, defense, and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act's capex incentives as justifying current sector valuations. Fidelity highlights electrical power equipment, especially large gas turbines, as the standout.

**Reshoring, by contrast, is contested.** IoT Analytics concludes it is **too early to call a U.S. reshoring boom**, with manufacturing construction spending declining since 2024, and reframes what is actually happening as a data center boom and a possible power/grid boom. Fidelity separately flags slowing U.S. manufacturing activity and a soft housing market. Investors should underwrite the grid, not the factory.

**M&A confirms it.** PwC reports record industrial manufacturing deal value of **$173 billion** over the past year, driven by AI infrastructure, grid modernization, and defense/resilience spending. Atkore is a live example — Prysmian agreed on August 3 to acquire it for $95.00/share cash, a ~30% premium to the $72.96 July 31 close, roughly $3.8B enterprise value. That deal makes Atkore a merger-arb instrument rather than a fundamental holding, and we exclude it on that basis.

**Defense is being structurally re-rated, and today supplied the catalyst.** Morgan Stanley's Kristine Liwag upgraded Lockheed Martin to Overweight this morning with the target raised to $835 from $690, on a **10-year F-35 framework holding production at 156 aircraft per year** — her phrase was that the deal "removes significant risk from the stock." Lockheed reported a **record $292 billion backlog, up 27% from Q2**, Q3 sales near $20.7B, adjusted EPS of $7.97, and raised 2026 sales and free-cash-flow guidance. Missiles and Fire Control sales rose 24% year-on-year — the munitions-replenishment cycle showing up in reported numbers rather than in narrative. Morgan Stanley's risk/reward skew is 4.0x. Note the sell side is genuinely split: Jefferies raised its target to $615 but kept Hold, BofA to $620 at Neutral flagging cash-flow risk; of 26 analysts, 13 buy and 12 hold. We regard the split as the opportunity.

#### Group 3 — Materials: selective deep value

Morningstar reports basic materials underperformed the broad market in Q3 2026 and that **about half of its materials coverage now carries 4- or 5-star ratings**, i.e. trades below fair value. Its mid-2026 most-undervalued list named Albemarle, Amrize and Celanese. Screens surface Avient (forward P/E ~11.7) and H.B. Fuller (~11.1), though those figures are roughly 110 days stale. Cleveland-Cliffs and ICL appear on Seeking Alpha's $2–10B undervalued screen from June.

Caution is warranted on two fronts. Metals service centers have already re-rated — Reliance trades between 19.6x and 23.4x depending on source, **above** the Metals and Mining industry average of 17.8x and peer average of 17.3x, and it is not a value name despite the sector label. And in precious metals, Morningstar's broad verdict is that **its gold coverage is overvalued** — though its fair values rest on a January 2026 assumption of ~$4,700/oz gold, well above today's ~$3,996 spot, which cuts against the names less than the headline suggests. Within that group the dispersion is large and exploitable: Newmont sits at roughly **11.1x normalized earnings and 8.4x price/cash flow**, against Agnico Eagle at 15.4x and 11.2x, with Morningstar explicitly calling Agnico expensive relative to fair value.

**Today's analyst tape in this bucket.** JPMorgan upgraded Kaiser Aluminum to Neutral from Underweight while *cutting* the target to $140 from $146 — a valuation-driven upgrade, not an endorsement. JPMorgan raised Roper Technologies to Neutral from Underweight with the target up to $490 from $415. BMO initiated FirstEnergy at Outperform, $50 target. Notably, **KeyBanc downgraded Carpenter Technology (CRS) to Sector Weight from Overweight** — CRS was a pick in this series earlier in October, and the downgrade is a live mark against prior positioning that we flag rather than bury. Benzinga also lists Floor & Decor and USCB Financial among Monday's downgrades, and 24/7 Wall St. notes a Boise Cascade call.

#### Spotlight conclusion

Own the **recipients** of energy-shock and AI-driven capital spending — defense, power delivery, water infrastructure — rather than the **beneficiaries of the commodity price itself**. The former have multi-year contracted backlogs and are being valued on normalized earnings; the latter are being valued, correctly, on earnings nobody believes are repeatable.

### Hardware & Semiconductors

Semis are the clearest expression of the AI de-rating. The group has absorbed two down weeks alongside the Nasdaq, and the July episode — when chip stocks sold off hard on doubts about the sustainability of the AI infrastructure buildout — has clearly left the market quicker to sell capex-dependency risk.

Three structural pressures are worth separating from the price action. First, **customer concentration**: when a single hyperscaler trims capex guidance, as Tesla did, the entire supply chain marks down together, which tells you the market views these revenue streams as correlated rather than diversified. Second, **the memory and interconnect bottleneck** continues to shift value within the supply chain rather than across it. Third, **cost inflation and power availability** are increasingly the binding constraint on data center buildout rather than chip supply — Schwab explicitly names possible data center construction *delays* as a risk to the industrial capex thesis, and that risk lands on semis too.

We note considerable price dispersion across sources for Nvidia in available feeds (quotes ranging from roughly $201 to $222 on different pages), and have not relied on any single quote. For the specific semiconductor sub-sectors — OSAT, specialty foundry, process control, analog — this series conducted detailed deep dives on October 15 and October 21, and those names are on today's exclusion list.

### Software & SaaS

### 1. LMT — Lockheed Martin Corporation
*(Aerospace & defense — combat aircraft, missiles & fire control, space, rotary systems)*

**Thesis.** This morning's 10-year F-35 framework fixes production at 156 aircraft per year and removes the single largest source of program risk that has suppressed Lockheed's multiple for three years, while a record $292 billion backlog — up 27% in one quarter — converts the geopolitical environment described above into contracted, multi-year revenue rather than a sentiment trade. Missiles and Fire Control sales up 24% year-on-year is the munitions-replenishment cycle arriving in reported results.

**Valuation metrics.** At roughly $604 and Q3 adjusted EPS of $7.97, the stock trades near **19–20x** a run-rated adjusted earnings base. Morgan Stanley's $835 target implies ~38% upside and a risk/reward skew of 4.0x. Trailing P/E, forward P/E, P/S, EV/EBITDA and PEG from a single consistent vendor snapshot were not obtainable at writing; the multiple above is derived from the sourced price and quarterly EPS and should be refreshed before execution.

**Growth profile.** Q3 sales ~$20.7B; management raised both 2026 sales and free-cash-flow guidance. Backlog +27% quarter-on-quarter to $292B provides revenue visibility well beyond the forecast horizon. Missiles & Fire Control +24% YoY is the fastest-growing segment and the highest-margin growth driver.

**Competitive moat.** Sole-source position on the F-35 — the only fifth-generation fighter in Western production — with a now-contracted decade-long production floor. Switching costs across allied air forces are effectively infinite over any investable horizon, and the missile franchises (PAC-3, JASSM, HIMARS) face capacity-constrained rather than demand-constrained markets.

**Key risk.** Free cash flow conversion, which is precisely what BofA flagged in keeping a Neutral rating. Fixed-price development programs and classified-program charges have repeatedly surprised negatively at this company, and a record backlog accumulated at fixed prices during an inflationary period is a margin risk, not purely an asset. Secondarily, a credible Middle East de-escalation compresses the defense multiple even as backlog stays intact.

**Rating: Buy.**

### 2. MTZ — MasTec, Inc.
*(Engineering & construction — power delivery, pipeline infrastructure, communications)*

**Thesis.** MasTec is the most direct listed contractor exposure to the $1.3 trillion of U.S. utility capital spending forecast for 2026–2030, and the stock de-rated roughly 18.5% on a Q2 print that *raised* full-year guidance and delivered a record backlog — a timing-driven selloff in the communications segment that created the entry point. Buying the execution vehicle for the grid buildout is a cleaner trade than buying the commodity it will carry.

**Valuation metrics.** Against the post-Q2 price of ~$264.36 (July 31, stale — refresh before acting): roughly **28x** 2026 consensus EPS of ~$9.31 and roughly **21x** 2027 consensus of $12.67. On 2027 growth of ~36%, that is a **PEG of approximately 0.58** — the most attractive growth-adjusted valuation in today's slate. P/S, EV/EBITDA and trailing P/E were not obtainable from a consistent current vendor snapshot.

**Growth profile.** Q2 2026 revenue +23% YoY to $4.37B; adjusted EBITDA +40% to $384M; adjusted EPS +49% to $2.22. FY2026 guidance raised to $18.2B revenue, $1.6B adjusted EBITDA, $9.30 EPS. Consensus 2027 EPS of $12.67 implies 36.1% growth on top of 42.1% in 2026. Record 18-month backlog of **$21.39 billion**, with Power Delivery backlog at $6.3B and Power Delivery revenue +20% to $1.2B.

**Competitive moat.** Scale, skilled-crew availability and utility master service agreements. The binding constraint in grid construction is not capital or permits but qualified linemen, and MasTec's installed labor base and long-standing utility relationships are genuinely hard to replicate at speed.

**Key risk.** Backlog quality. The company discloses that **40% of backlog sits under master service or other service agreements, and most of those can be cancelled on short or no advance notice**. "Record backlog" is therefore a softer number than it appears. The Q2 selloff also reflected real project deferrals from 2026 into 2027, and a second deferral would break the 2027 estimate on which the entire valuation case rests. Elevated rates raise utility financing costs and can slow project starts.

**Rating: Buy.**

**Growth profile. This is the honest weak point of the pick.** FY2026 (ended March 31, 2026) revenue reached a record $2.3 billion, +2%, but net earnings of $423.8 million fell 9% and diluted EPS of $13.16 fell 4%. Q1 FY2027 revenue rose ~2.6% to ~$651 million while EPS fell to $3.29 from $3.76 and net earnings fell 17% — though that EPS still beat the $3.26 consensus, and gypsum wallboard operating earnings of ~$73 million beat the ~$67 million expected. **Earnings are declining, not growing**; the 13x multiple reflects weak momentum, not a growth multiple on sale.

**Competitive moat.** Cement is the textbook case of geographic monopoly: quarries sit next to kilns, the product is too heavy to ship economically beyond a few hundred miles, and new permits are effectively unobtainable in most U.S. jurisdictions. That yields durable regional pricing power that survives demand troughs — which is exactly what the record revenue alongside falling earnings demonstrates.

**Key risk.** Timing and event risk. Wallboard demand tracks housing starts, and with the 10-year near 5.0% and a Fed signalling a possible December hike, the trough may not be in. FY2026 capex of ~$417 million against $195 million the prior year is being spent into a downturn, which magnifies the downside if recovery is delayed. **Sources conflict on the next earnings date — Fool lists October 29, 2026, Yahoo lists November 4** — so the print may land within days. Size accordingly.

**Rating: Accumulate.**

### 5. NEM — Newmont Corporation
*(Precious metals mining — gold, with copper and silver by-products)*

**Thesis.** The cheapest large producer in a sector whose own bull-case analysts call overvalued: Newmont trades at roughly 11.1x normalized earnings and 8.4x cash flow against Agnico Eagle at 15.4x and 11.2x, while generating free cash flow at a rate that is already funding substantial buybacks. It is also the only position in this slate that is *negatively* correlated to the rest — it benefits from exactly the scenario (persistent above-target inflation, geopolitical escalation, a policy error at the December FOMC) that would damage the four rate-sensitive industrials above.

**Valuation metrics.** **Normalized P/E ~11.1x; price/cash flow ~8.4x** (Morningstar, as of October 23, 2026), versus Agnico Eagle at 15.4x and 11.2x. Spot gold ~$3,996/oz. Trailing/forward P/E from a single vendor, P/S, EV/EBITDA and PEG were not obtainable at writing; we have quoted the normalized figures rather than construct multiples from a price we could not verify.

**Growth profile.** Q3 2026 adjusted NPAT of **$2.3 billion, or $2.20 per share, up 23% year-on-year**, driven by a 22% higher realized gold price. Q2 2026 free cash flow of $2.2 billion, with $1.9 billion returned to shareholders via dividends and repurchases in the quarter. Note candidly that the growth is **price-driven, not volume-driven** — this is margin expansion on a flat production base, which is lower-quality growth than MasTec's or Lockheed's.

**Competitive moat.** Reserve life and jurisdiction. Newmont holds the largest gold reserve base in the industry across a portfolio weighted to stable mining jurisdictions, and new tier-one deposits take a decade-plus from discovery to production. That makes existing long-life, low-cost ounces a genuinely scarce and non-replicable asset.

**Key risk.** The gold price, with no hedge and full operating leverage. Morningstar's own fair values for the sector assume gold averaging ~$4,700/oz for 2026–2028, well above the current ~$3,996 — so published fair values in this group embed an optimistic price deck, and its October note records gold falling that week. If Qatari mediation succeeds and the Fed's December hike lands credibly, the safe-haven bid and the inflation hedge unwind together. Mining-specific cost inflation and country risk are secondary but persistent.

**Rating: Accumulate.**

## Risk Factors

**1. A policy error at the December FOMC — SEVERE.** The Fed hiked in September, most participants see another increase as appropriate by year-end, and inflation has exceeded target for five years. But the current impulse is a supply shock: 3 mbpd of refining capacity destroyed by missiles does not respond to the funds rate. Tightening into it compresses demand without addressing the price source. With the 10-year already at ~5.0%, a December hike accompanied by hawkish guidance would hit equity multiples and the rate-sensitive industrials in this report hardest. Wednesday's statement language, not the held rate, is the signal to read.

**2. Middle East escalation — SEVERE, and two-sided.** Reported U.S./Israeli planning for strikes on Iran *after the midterms* places a known catalyst on the calendar with an unknown date. Escalation sends Brent through its Friday high of $116.72 and re-closes Hormuz; the April 2027 contract under $100 shows the market expects a persistent but not permanent premium. The symmetric risk is underweighted: successful Qatari mediation would collapse crude, crack spreads and tanker rates simultaneously, and would take the energy sector's 2026 leadership with it.

**3. Big Tech earnings and the AI capex question — HIGH.** Microsoft and Alphabet report Wednesday within hours of the Fed, Amazon Thursday, amid 179 S&P 500 reports. The Nasdaq is on a two-week losing streak and the market is already punishing capex guidance — Tesla's capex cut alone knocked ~1% off Nvidia, Broadcom and Micron overnight. With all 33 Nvidia analysts at Buy, positioning is one-sided into the densest catalyst week of the quarter. A beat financed by raised capex is likely to be sold.

**4. Peak-cycle earnings being capitalized as permanent — HIGH, and underappreciated.** Valero's refining margin tripled to $39.24/bbl; six tanker companies earned $1.83B in a quarter against $330M a year earlier; 3-2-1 cracks are near the widest since 1986. Investors screening on trailing P/E are being handed single-digit multiples on earnings nobody models as repeatable. This is the most likely source of individual-portfolio damage over the next two quarters, and it is actively being marketed as a value opportunity.

**5. Rate-sensitive construction and housing — MODERATE.** A 10-year near 5.0%, a soft housing market, and U.S. manufacturing construction spending declining since 2024 — with IoT Analytics concluding no reshoring boom has materialized. Schwab separately flags possible data center construction delays. This risk bears directly on three of today's five picks and is why WMS and EXP carry Accumulate rather than Buy ratings.

## Bottom Line

The market is being asked to price a tightening central bank, a live military escalation and the sector's heaviest earnings week simultaneously, and it is resolving that by rotating from long-duration tech into cash-generative cyclicals — Dow up on the week, Nasdaq down for a second straight week. We think the rotation is correct but that the crowd is expressing it in the wrong instruments: refiners and tankers printing two to three times mid-cycle earnings are a bet on the absence of diplomacy, dressed up as a value screen.

The better expression is to own the **recipients** of defense, grid and infrastructure capital — Lockheed's $292B contracted backlog, MasTec's $21.4B at 0.58x PEG, Advanced Drainage at a third below peer multiples — with one de-rated cement cyclical and one gold producer at 11x normalized as an explicit hedge against the inflation-and-escalation path that would break the rest of the book. Wait for Wednesday's statement language before adding materially to anything rate-sensitive, and refresh every multiple in this report against a live quote before acting.
