The post-midterm relief rally cooled on Thursday as the market began separating what the election actually changed from what it merely made louder. ## Macro & Monetary Environment

**Policy rate:** The FOMC held at 3.75%–4.00% at its October meeting. The effective fed funds rate printed 3.88% as of late October per the Fed's H.15 release. Kevin Warsh is Fed Chair.

**The dissents matter more than the hold.** Two members — Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack — voted for a rate *increase*, citing inflation. That is an unusual configuration: dissents in the hawkish direction while the market spent most of 2025 and early 2026 debating the pace of cuts. It tells you the committee's center of gravity has moved, and it is the single most underpriced fact in this market.

**Inflation is going the wrong way.** CPI rose to 3.7% year-over-year in September from 3.4% in August. Core PCE — the Fed's preferred gauge — decelerated to 3.5% from 3.7%, so the two series are telling different stories. The Cleveland Fed nowcast points to PCE rising from roughly 3.6% in September to about 3.77% in October and 4.03% in November. Sources conflict on direction; treat the November path as genuinely uncertain rather than settled.

**What the curve is pricing.** As of November 4, futures implied a policy rate near 4.1% by February 2027 and roughly 4.5% by November 2027. The market is pricing *hikes*, not cuts. Any equity thesis built on a 2027 easing cycle is fighting the forward curve right now.

**Calendar to watch:**
- **November 18** — FOMC minutes (the October dissents will be explained here; this is the week's real event)
- **November 25** — Beige Book
- **December 8–9** — FOMC meeting and Summary of Economic Projections

**The fiscal overlay is new.** A Democratic Congress with a Republican White House is, mechanically, a recipe for fiscal gridlock. For bond markets that is mildly disinflationary at the margin — less deficit expansion — which partly explains why equities could rally while the rate curve stayed firm. It also means tax and spending legislation is effectively frozen through 2028, removing a tail risk in both directions.

## Geopolitical Factors

**Oil's war premium is deflating, fast.** Brent has reversed roughly half its advance from $70 to nearly $120, pulling back toward $95–101 depending on the source and timestamp. Crude fell more than 7% on Monday, November 2 on diplomatic signals, and dropped roughly 8% again around the midterm result. The S&P 500 Energy sector fell about 3% on November 4. An SEB analyst attributed the move to the administration stepping back from threats of immediate strikes on Iran — the read being that a Democratic Congress constrains unilateral escalation.

**This is a two-way trade, not a resolution.** The underlying conflict is unresolved. A formal settlement would extend the selling in energy equities; a diplomatic breakdown or a genuine supply disruption would reverse it just as abruptly. Shipping attacks continue to keep freight and insurance costs elevated, and Gulf energy flows have not fully normalized. Positioning in energy here is a bet on diplomacy, whether or not the holder frames it that way.

**The second-order effect is the important one.** Lower crude is a direct disinflationary impulse into a CPI print that just accelerated to 3.7%. If Brent holds near $95, the November and December headline inflation numbers get materially easier, which cuts against the hawkish dissents described above. Energy and the Fed are now the same trade.

**Domestic policy risk, newly priced.** The midterms carried a wave of data-center ballot measures and local restrictions. Texas elected Gina Hinojosa governor on a platform that included a data center moratorium. This is no longer a fringe concern — it is a permitting and siting risk that attaches to the single largest capital-spending program in the market.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: Fintech, Payments & Financial Data

*(Rotating spotlight: day-of-year 309 mod 6 = 3)*

This sector delivered the day's most instructive dispersion. Within roughly 48 hours, Block rose about 15% and FIS fell about 24%. Both are "payments companies." That spread is the entire thesis of this section: **the payments complex has bifurcated into businesses that own a distribution endpoint and businesses that rent one out.**

**The FIS collapse is a structural warning, not a buying opportunity.** FIS fell roughly 23.6% on November 4 to close near $29.76, hitting a 52-week low of $29.15, *despite beating on Q3*. The damage came from the guidance: 2026 adjusted EPS midpoint cut from roughly $6.195 to $5.855 (about −5%), plus a preliminary 2027 outlook pointing to *declining* adjusted earnings. Analyst Cristopher Kennedy noted this was the second consecutive quarter of reduced guidance, compounded by a recent CFO change. J.P. Morgan cut it from Overweight to Neutral and took its target from $53 to $38; William Blair moved to Market Perform; Mizuho held Outperform but cut its target from $60 to $46. A strategic review is underway with a substantive update expected in early 2027.

The lesson generalizes. Legacy bank-processing franchises monetize incumbency, and incumbency is being repriced. A beat on the quarter paired with a cut to the next two years is the signature of a business where the installed base is intact but the pricing power is not. "Rock-bottom valuation" arguments on FIS — and there are several circulating — require you to underwrite a strategic review whose outcome is unknown and whose CFO just left. That is a special-situations position, not a valuation position, and it is not a pick here.

**What worked instead: owning the merchant or the consumer directly.** Block's Q3 (reported November 3) showed gross profit up 22% to $3.24 billion, adjusted operating income up 92% to $923 million at a 29% margin, and adjusted diluted EPS of $1.13, up 109%. Cash App gross profit grew 27% to $2.1 billion; Square grew 14% to $1.2 billion. Full-year guidance was raised to $12.626 billion gross profit and $4.15 adjusted EPS. The blemish worth noting: Bitcoin gross profit fell 50%, which concentrates the story further onto Cash App. The stock's roughly 15% move has already captured much of the operating leverage surprise, which is precisely why it is not among today's picks.

**Corporate and B2B payments are the structurally advantaged niche.** Corpay posted Q3 revenue up about 18% to roughly $1.38 billion with cash EPS of $7.54, up about 32%, and a 57.4% adjusted EBITDA margin — a sixth consecutive quarter of double-digit organic growth. Corporate Payments grew 18% organically (20% ex-float), though management flagged roughly 100bps as one-time monetization, putting normalized segment growth near 17%. Full-year guidance moved up to about $5.35 billion revenue and about $28 cash EPS; the 2027 frame is 9–11% organic with corporate payments at mid-teens or better. The company is actively divesting vehicle payments (+8% organic) to concentrate capital on the faster-growing segment. This is the cleanest growth-at-a-reasonable-price setup in the sector.

**Core banking software is quietly compounding.** Jack Henry's fiscal Q1 2027 (quarter ended September 30) produced non-GAAP revenue of $682 million, up 7%, GAAP revenue of $701.5 million up 8.8%, GAAP diluted EPS up 16% to $2.28, and a non-GAAP operating margin of 28%, up 102bps. The number that matters: **nine competitive core wins versus four a year ago**, against a full-year target of 58–65. Core conversions are multi-year, high-switching-cost decisions; a doubling of competitive wins is a share-shift signal that shows up in revenue two to three years later. Management raised FY2027 GAAP EPS guidance to $7.47–$7.51. The stock rose about 9% on the report.

**Credit and risk data held up.** TransUnion's Q3 revenue grew 12% (8% organic constant-currency), with diluted EPS of $0.81 against $0.49 a year prior and adjusted diluted EPS of $1.25 versus $1.10. U.S. Markets grew 7% with Financial Services up 11% and double-digit growth in auto and insurance; International returned to double-digit growth led by India and Latin America. The board raised the buyback authorization by $1 billion to $2 billion total. The honest caveat: organic growth is decelerating — 10% in Q2 to 8% in Q3, and roughly 5.5% excluding FICO mortgage royalties. Sources also conflict on the margin line (a 20.4% operating margin up from 17.8%, versus an adjusted operating margin of 23.2% down 6.8 points), so verify against the filing.

**Capital markets infrastructure is consolidating.** MarketAxess reported a soft Q3 on October 23 — revenue up 1% to $210.4 million, adjusted EPS of $1.86 against a $1.95 consensus — but the fundamentals are now secondary. ICE is acquiring the company at $167 per share in cash, a 33% premium to the prior close. Underneath the deal, the operating trends were actually strong: total trading ADV up 13%, block trading ADV up 21%, portfolio trading ADV up 37%, with share gains in high yield and municipals. The signal for the sector is that electronic fixed-income venues are now scarce enough to be bought rather than built.

**Cross-border remains the weak link.** Euronet's October 27 Q3 missed, with revenue of $1,161.1 million up only 1%, operating income down 12%, and adjusted EPS of $3.55 down 2%. Management cut the 2026 outlook to roughly $4.375 billion revenue and about $725 million adjusted EBITDA, attributing pressure to U.S. immigration policy changes and macro uncertainty hitting the cross-border business. Remittance volumes are a policy-exposed revenue line, and that exposure is now visible in the numbers. Avoid.

**Sector valuation frame:** A Siblis survey put the median listed payments/fintech company at 22.5x normalized earnings as of September 30, 2026, with a 24.1x median for mid-caps. That is the bar: a mid-cap payments name materially below roughly 24x is screening cheap *relative to its own sector*, which is the comparison that matters here.

### Artificial Intelligence

The capex numbers continue to escalate beyond what most 2025 forecasts contemplated. Combined 2026 guidance for the four largest hyperscalers now sits at roughly $720–745 billion, with one tracker putting the figure near $735 billion — up about 79% from 2025. Meta alone guided to $135–145 billion. Including Oracle's fiscal 2027 plans pushes the aggregate toward $835 billion. Forward estimates for 2027 cluster at $1.2–1.3 trillion, though that is analyst projection rather than company guidance and should be treated accordingly.

Two constraints are now binding and neither is about chips:

1. **Power.** Morgan Stanley's estimate of the U.S. data center power shortfall runs to roughly 57 GW gross / 32 GW net across 2026–2028. You cannot energize capacity you cannot interconnect.
2. **Politics.** The midterm cycle turned data centers into a local ballot issue. Texas's gubernatorial outcome, decided partly on a data center moratorium platform, converts siting from a procurement question into a political one.

One analysis flagged that hyperscalers are now spending roughly 102% of cloud revenue on capex. That ratio is the thing to watch. It is sustainable while the depreciation schedule lags the revenue ramp and intolerable the moment it does not — and it is the mechanism by which an AI capex deceleration, if it comes, would transmit to earnings.

### Hardware & Semiconductors

This was the day's weak link, and the cause was demand mix rather than a broad cycle turn.

**Qualcomm** fell roughly 9–9.7% despite beating on the quarter, on a weak outlook and, critically, handset division revenue down 26% to $5.14 billion. On the move, the company was on track to shed roughly $17.5 billion in market value. Data-center momentum was real but nowhere near large enough to offset the smartphone decline.

**Arm** beat on headline numbers but royalty revenue rose only 8% to $670 million, below forecasts — and royalties are the entire long-term bull case. Shares fell about 5.28%.

The complex followed: AMD down about 3.7% premarket, Lam Research down about 3.6%, with Micron, Intel, Broadcom and TSMC all lower. Asian chip names slid overnight on the same catalysts.

**The dispersion here is the point.** Coherent gained about 7% on adjusted EPS of $2.09. AI-infrastructure-levered silicon and optics are being bid while handset-levered silicon is being sold. "Semiconductors" has not been a single trade for some time, and Thursday priced that explicitly.

Street opinion split sharply on what follows: Dan Ives warned semis could fall another 10–12%; Tom Lee called the move an overreaction. Both cannot be right, and the resolution likely depends on whether the hyperscaler capex figures above hold through the December guidance season.

*One sourcing caution:* the Arm/AMD 5% decline tied to the Delaware jury verdict in the Qualcomm licensing case is an **October 2026** event and should not be conflated with Thursday's move, though several aggregators presented them together.

### Software & SaaS

Software has been de-rating from its October highs even as fundamentals hold. Cloudflare was up about 35% for 2026 through November 2 but had fallen roughly 28% from its October 6 intraday high of $370, closing near $265 — a textbook multiple compression in a name whose operating performance did not deteriorate. It reported November 5. Datadog reported November 4 and was read as an early indicator of enterprise cloud demand.

The broader pattern: investors are no longer paying premium multiples for growth they cannot connect to a near-term earnings line. That discipline is healthy, and it is the same force that makes the spotlight sector's mid-caps — which carry visible earnings and lower multiples — relatively attractive right now.

### 1. CPAY — Corpay, Inc. *(Corporate / B2B payments)*

**Thesis:** The highest-quality growth asset in payments is trading at a below-market multiple because it is lumped in with consumer payments peers that have nothing in common with it. Six consecutive quarters of double-digit organic growth, 57% EBITDA margins, and an active portfolio reshaping toward the fastest-growing segment.

**Valuation Metrics:**
- Forward P/E: ~13.8x (sources span 13.7x–14.2x)
- Price: ~$410; market cap ~$27B
- PEG: well under 1.0 on ~30% cash EPS growth
- EV/EBITDA: not independently verified — guidance implies roughly $3.05B adjusted EBITDA on ~$5.35B revenue at the 57.4% margin

**Growth Profile:** Q3 revenue +18% to ~$1.38B; cash EPS $7.54, +32% YoY. FY2026 guidance ~$5.35B revenue and ~$28 cash EPS (+31%). 2027 framed at 9–11% organic, with Corporate Payments mid-teens or better.

**Competitive Moat:** Corporate payments is a workflow business, not a transaction business. Accounts-payable automation and cross-border B2B embed into customer ERP and treasury systems, producing switching costs closer to enterprise software than to merchant acquiring. Float income provides a rate-linked revenue stream that is a tailwind in the current hawkish environment rather than a headwind.

**Key Risk:** Acquisition-led growth with simultaneous divestitures makes organic growth genuinely hard to audit from outside. Management already flagged ~100bps of Q3 corporate payments growth as non-recurring monetization. If the divestiture of vehicle payments is executed poorly, the remaining business carries more concentration risk than the consolidated numbers suggest.

**Rating: Strong Buy** — 16 analysts polled by S&P Global carry a consensus Buy with an average target of $461 (range $390–$536); Morgan Stanley moved to $460 and J.P. Morgan to $470 post-quarter.

### 2. JKHY — Jack Henry & Associates *(Core banking & credit union software)*

**Thesis:** Competitive core wins doubled year-over-year to nine, against a 58–65 full-year target. Core banking conversions are multi-year decisions with enormous switching costs — a share shift of that size shows up in revenue for a decade, and the market is paying for the current 7% growth rather than the pipeline.

**Valuation Metrics:**
- FY2027 GAAP EPS guidance: $7.47–$7.51 (raised)
- Non-GAAP operating margin: 28%, +102bps YoY
- Market cap: ~$12B range
- P/E, P/S, EV/EBITDA, PEG: **not independently verified in this research cycle** — no current-dated multiple appeared in available sources. Verify against a live screener before sizing.

**Growth Profile:** Fiscal Q1 2027 non-GAAP revenue $682M (+7%); GAAP revenue $701.5M (+8.8%); GAAP diluted EPS $2.28 (+16%); free cash flow $83M (+19%). Core segment revenue +5% reported, 8.6% excluding certain revenue items. FY2027 guidance: GAAP revenue +6.0–6.8%, non-GAAP revenue +6.6–7.4%, GAAP EPS +7–8%.

**Competitive Moat:** The deepest in the spotlight sector. Community banks and credit unions replace core systems roughly once a generation; the conversion is operationally terrifying and the incumbent wins ties by default. Jack Henry's position with sub-$10B-asset institutions is also structurally insulated from the large-bank pricing pressure that is currently destroying FIS's guidance.

**Key Risk:** The single-digit growth rate is the whole problem — this is a 7% grower, and if the multiple compresses toward other 7% growers the stock goes nowhere regardless of core wins. Continued community bank consolidation also shrinks the customer count each year, even as revenue per customer rises.

**Rating: Buy**

### 3. TRU — TransUnion *(Credit bureau & risk data)*

**Thesis:** A rate-cycle-levered data oligopoly trading well below its own historical multiple, with international growth re-accelerating and management aggressively buying back stock. If the Fed hikes as the curve implies, this is a value trap; if oil-driven disinflation wins, the mortgage and auto lines inflect hard.

**Valuation Metrics:**
- Q3 adjusted diluted EPS $1.25 (vs $1.10); Q4 guidance $1.15–$1.22 (+7–14%)
- Market cap: ~$17B range
- P/E, P/S, EV/EBITDA, PEG: **not independently verified** — no current-dated multiple appeared in available sources. Note also that sources conflict on the margin line (20.4% operating margin up from 17.8%, versus 23.2% adjusted down 6.8 points). Verify against the 8-K.

**Growth Profile:** Q3 revenue +12% (+8% organic constant currency). Diluted EPS $0.81 vs $0.49 prior year. U.S. Markets +7%, Financial Services +11%, double-digit auto and insurance. International back to double-digit growth, led by India and Latin America.

**Competitive Moat:** Three-firm oligopoly with a regulatory-grade data asset that cannot be replicated — the bureau file is decades of contributed tradeline data that no entrant can assemble. India is a genuine second franchise, not a rounding error.

**Key Risk:** **Organic growth is decelerating and the quality of it is weakening** — 10% in Q2 to 8% in Q3, and roughly 5.5% excluding FICO mortgage royalties. That royalty dependence is a real vulnerability: it is pass-through economics on someone else's pricing decision. If FICO's mortgage pricing moderates, TransUnion's reported growth drops to mid-single digits.

**Rating: Buy** — with the explicit caveat that the thesis is partly a rates call, and the forward curve is currently against it.

### 4. NU — Nu Holdings Ltd. *(Latin American digital banking)*

**Thesis:** A 33% ROE bank with 140 million customers trading near 10x forward earnings. The valuation discount is entirely country risk — and a weaker oil complex plus a less confrontational U.S. foreign policy posture are both constructive for Latin American currencies and risk premia.

**Valuation Metrics:**
- Trailing P/E: ~15.7x
- Forward P/E: ~9.95x
- ROE: 33% (Q2 2026)
- P/S, EV/EBITDA: not meaningful for a bank; book value and ROE are the relevant frame

**Growth Profile:** Q2 2026 net income $1.1B with EPS of $0.22, beating a $0.20 forecast on revenue of $5.88B. Q3 consensus is approximately $0.223–$0.227 EPS on roughly $5.91–5.97B revenue. **Reports November 12** — the position carries binary event risk inside a week.

**Competitive Moat:** Lowest-cost deposit franchise in its markets by a wide margin, built on a digital-only cost structure that incumbent Brazilian banks cannot match without cannibalizing branch networks they still carry. 140 million customers is a distribution asset that compounds — each additional product sold against it is nearly pure incremental margin.

**Key Risk:** Brazilian credit cycle and currency. A 33% ROE in a high-rate emerging market is partly a function of high-rate spread economics; Brazilian monetary easing compresses net interest margin, while Brazilian tightening raises credit losses. The business is genuinely excellent and the macro is genuinely out of management's control.

**Rating: Buy** — though sizing should account for the November 12 print. Accumulating ahead of a binary event is a position-sizing decision, not a thesis decision.

### 5. FOUR — Shift4 Payments, Inc. *(Integrated merchant acquiring)*

**Thesis:** Beat on Q3 this morning and trades near 8–9x forward adjusted earnings — roughly a third of the 24x median mid-cap payments multiple. This is the deep-value slot in the portfolio, and it is priced for the guidance disappointments that have already happened.

**Valuation Metrics:**
- Forward adjusted P/E: ~8.7x (~$44.82 against FY2026 guidance of $5.05–$5.15)
- Market cap: ~$3.3B (smallest pick by a wide margin)
- PEG: ~0.67–0.68 per available sources
- Trailing GAAP P/E: ~69x — inflated by low GAAP earnings and not a useful figure here

**Growth Profile:** Q3 2026 EPS of $1.49 beat the $1.37 estimate by $0.12; shares up roughly 8% on the print to about $44.82. Gross revenue up 12% to $1.32B; adjusted EBITDA up 6% to $310M. FY2026 EPS guidance of $5.05–$5.15 sits slightly below the $5.19 consensus — the third guidance range this year, after $5.50–$5.70 was cut to $5.15–$5.35 in August.

**Competitive Moat:** Vertical software integration in restaurants, hospitality and stadiums. Shift4 does not sell payment processing; it sells point-of-sale and property-management software with payments attached, which is why its merchant churn is lower than standalone acquirers. 52-week range of $34.56–$76.09 shows how violently the market has repriced that moat in both directions.

**Key Risk:** **The guidance pattern is the risk, and it is serious.** Full-year EPS guidance has been cut twice and the latest range is still below consensus — the same pattern that just destroyed 24% of FIS's market value. Adjusted EBITDA growth of 6% against gross revenue growth of 12% means margins are compressing. Sources also conflict materially on the Q3 figures (a $0.37 diluted EPS figure appears alongside the $1.49 adjusted number), and the Investing.com revenue line was garbled. **Verify against the company's Q3 shareholder letter at investors.shift4.com before acting.**

**Rating: Accumulate** — deliberately the lowest rating of the five. The valuation is genuinely compelling and the moat is real, but a company that has cut guidance repeatedly deserves a smaller position and a stricter stop than its multiple alone suggests.

## Risk Factors

**1. The Fed is pricing hikes, and the equity market is not. (Severity: High)**
Two FOMC members voted for an increase in October. Futures imply ~4.1% by February and ~4.5% by November 2027. CPI accelerated to 3.7%. Equities just posted their best day since April 2025. These facts are difficult to hold simultaneously, and the November 18 minutes are the most likely catalyst for the market to pick a side.

**2. AI capex concentration risk, now with a political dimension. (Severity: High)**
Hyperscalers are spending roughly 102% of cloud revenue on capex, with 2026 totals near $735 billion and 2027 projections at $1.2–1.3 trillion. The constraint is no longer silicon — it is 32 GW of net power shortfall and a newly hostile local permitting environment following a midterm cycle in which data center restrictions became a winning ballot issue. A deceleration transmits to roughly a third of index earnings.

**3. Semiconductor demand is bifurcating, and the weak half is large. (Severity: Medium-High)**
Qualcomm's handset revenue fell 26%. Arm's royalties grew 8% against higher expectations. The AI-levered half of the complex is masking genuine weakness in the consumer-device half. If AI orders normalize at all, there is no offsetting strength underneath.

**4. Oil's war premium can reverse as fast as it deflated. (Severity: Medium)**
Brent has round-tripped from $70 to ~$120 and back toward $95–101 on diplomatic signals alone. Nothing structural has been resolved. A breakdown in talks reverses the energy-sector selloff and simultaneously removes the disinflationary impulse the Fed needs — a genuinely nasty two-factor outcome.

**5. Guidance-cut contagion in payments and legacy software. (Severity: Medium)**
FIS lost roughly a quarter of its value *after beating the quarter*, on forward guidance alone. The market is pricing forward estimates, not trailing results, and is doing so with very little patience. Any name with a pattern of successive reductions — including FOUR above — carries asymmetric downside into its next print regardless of how cheap it screens.

## Bottom Line

Wednesday was a relief rally on reduced uncertainty — a resolved election and a deflating war premium — not a repricing of earnings power, and Thursday's rotation out of semiconductors into everything else is the market beginning to sort that out. The hawkish dissents at the October FOMC and a curve pricing 4.5% by late 2027 remain the most underappreciated facts in this market, and they argue for owning cash-generative businesses at reasonable multiples rather than long-duration growth. Today's spotlight sector offered that in unusual abundance: FIS demonstrated what happens to franchises that monetize incumbency, while Corpay, Jack Henry and Nu showed what durable distribution and real switching costs are still worth — at 10–14x forward earnings in a sector whose median sits near 24x.
