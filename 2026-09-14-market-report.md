## Executive Summary

Two shocks hit the tape simultaneously this morning, and they push in opposite directions. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei called for an industry-wide slowdown in frontier AI development, OpenAI's Sam Altman agreed the industry needs to "pace the frontier," and Elon Musk backed the view — sending the AI complex into a sharp, global de-rating that took Nasdaq 100 futures down ~1.6% and dragged semis from Seoul to Eindhoven. The result is a market being repriced on both of its load-bearing assumptions at once: that AI capex compounds indefinitely, and that the Fed's next move is down. Today's report leans into the rotation that follows.

## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The Fed meets tomorrow and Wednesday (Sept 15–16), with the decision at 2:00 PM ET Wednesday and — critically — an updated Summary of Economic Projections and dot plot.** The target range has sat at 3.50%–3.75% since December 2025.

What makes this meeting unusual: the market is not debating *how much* the Fed will cut. It is weighing a hold against a **25bp hike**. The inflation data explains why:

- **Headline CPI**: 3.4% y/y in July, down from 3.5% in June — disinflating, but glacially.
- **Core CPI**: 2.5%, down from 2.6%.
- **PCE**: running at **3.7%** — described by the Committee itself as "elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal."

Softer July employment and retail sales data argue for a hold. But an oil shock arriving *two days before the meeting* is the worst possible timing for a central bank whose credibility is already stretched by a 3.7% PCE print. Crude at $107 feeds directly into headline inflation and, with a lag, into core services via freight and utilities.

**What to watch this week:** the dot plot is the whole game. If the 2027 median drifts up, the 10-year takes out 5% and the multiple compression that started in AI today spreads to everything long-duration. A hawkish hold with an unchanged dot plot is the bullish resolution — and is probably what equities are priced for.

The 10-year at 4.97% is the single most important number on the screen this week. It is the discount rate underneath every richly-valued growth name that just got an existential narrative shock.

## Geopolitical Factors

The Middle East situation escalated materially over the weekend and is now a live supply event, not a risk premium story:

- **Saudi Arabia shut a key pipeline that bypasses the Strait of Hormuz.** This is the crux. That infrastructure had been allocating roughly **7 million barrels per day** of Saudi crude to the Red Sea specifically as the workaround to halted Persian Gulf shipments under the Iranian blockade on GCC oil exports. The workaround is now offline.
- **Houthi forces claimed a large-scale missile and drone attack on Saudi Arabia**, saying they struck a military base at Sharurah.
- **UKMTO issued an alert** that a vessel inside the Strait of Hormuz was struck by an unknown projectile.
- **Diplomacy is live but unresolved:** GCC diplomats were expected to meet their Iranian counterpart Monday to discuss a temporary arrangement for managing shipping through the strait.

The asymmetry here is important. With the bypass route shut *and* the strait contested, there is no longer a physical release valve for GCC barrels. Bernstein has floated a path to $150 crude. That is a tail scenario, not a base case — but the distribution of outcomes has clearly fattened on the upside, and the diplomatic track is the only thing standing between the current $107 and something considerably worse.

For equity investors the read-through is straightforward: energy supply is now a *quantity* problem, not a price problem, and quantity problems resolve slowly.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: Industrials, Energy, Materials & "Picks-and-Shovels"

*(Rotating spotlight: day-of-year 257 mod 6 = 5.)*

Today is an unusually clean test of this bucket's core premise: that the real-economy industrial complex can carry the index when the AI trade wobbles. For most of 2026 the two have been correlated, because the biggest industrial end-market *was* AI — data center power, cooling, switchgear, interconnect. Today decoupled them, and the decoupling is the opportunity.

**The critical analytical distinction today is between industrials levered to AI capex and industrials levered to energy, defense, and physical scarcity.** The first group is not a hedge against what happened this morning — it is the same trade wearing a hard hat. Several names that screen well on growth are, on inspection, AI-datacenter derivatives:

- **Argan (AGX)** — record $2.8B backlog, revenue +61.5% to $384M, net income $53.3M, gross margins above 20%. Genuinely excellent operating momentum. But the backlog is substantially gas-fired plants *supplying electricity to data centers running AI workloads*, and sell-side work already flags the valuation as "a stretch" requiring sustained backlog growth to justify. Exposed to exactly today's risk. **Covered, not picked.**
- **Powell Industries (POWL)** — record $2.4B backlog, $934M in new orders including a $400M data center project, backlog +33% y/y. But it trades at ~28x 2027 earnings, which is rich for a project-driven industrial with lumpy revenue recognition. **Covered, not picked.**
- **Curtiss-Wright (CW)** — raised 2026 guidance to 8–9% sales growth, 14–16% EPS growth, record FCF of $585–605M, naval defense guidance up to 7–9% on Virginia-class and CVN-81, Defense Electronics bookings +~50%, book-to-bill above 1.1x, $80M Pennsylvania expansion for naval and commercial nuclear. A genuinely great business benefiting from the conflict. But at a **~42x forward P/E**, it is, as one write-up put it, "nuclear growth priced to perfection." **Covered, not picked** — this is a valuation-based report.

**Where the spotlight actually pays today: energy equipment and services.** This is the purest picks-and-shovels expression of a supply-driven oil shock. When crude moves on *supply destruction* rather than demand, the operators who restore and expand production capacity see order books inflect with a lag — and they are currently priced as though the cycle already peaked. TechnipFMC trades near **14x** with a **$15.8B subsea backlog**; Baker Hughes sits at **~18.2x**, below the energy-services industry average, having just posted record Q2 IET bookings that **doubled y/y to $7.1B**, backlog +19% to an all-time high, **$37.1B of RPO**, and the completed Chart Industries acquisition making it the global LNG liquefaction leader.

**The one place I am deliberately not going: refiners.** The 3-2-1 crack spread has widened to multi-decade highs near **$59/bbl**, nearly tripling since January. Marathon Petroleum, Valero and HF Sinclair are each up **more than 80% in 2026**; Phillips 66 more than 54%, against roughly 11% for the S&P 500. Gulf Coast refiners with WTI-based sourcing have captured the most spread because their crude costs rose more slowly than Brent-linked feedstock. The logic is sound and the move has been spectacular — which is the problem. CNBC's own framing is that the run is "nearly unprecedented" and "history says it could end soon." Buying a record-wide, mean-reverting spread after an 80% move is not a valuation trade. **Covered, not picked.**

**Materials deserve more attention than they are getting.** An energy shock is a *cost* shock for most manufacturers, but for the handful of producers sitting on structurally cheap North American feedstock it is a **relative margin windfall**, because it destroys their overseas competitors' economics first. That asymmetry is the most reliable way to own an energy shock without owning oil price beta directly — and it is the basis for one of today's picks.

**Precious metals are the other underweighted response.** Stagflation — 3.7% PCE, crude at $107, a Fed that may have to hike into slowing employment — plus an active shooting conflict is the textbook setup for gold, and gold-producer economics in 2026 are extraordinary: realized prices near **$4,483/oz** against all-in sustaining costs in the **$1,459/oz** range. That is not a levered bet on the gold price; it is a business printing roughly $3,000/oz of gross margin.

### Artificial Intelligence

The defining event of the day, and it came from inside the house.

**What was actually said:** Amodei called for an immediate slowdown in the pace of frontier AI development, outlining a three-step plan — independent reviewers embedded inside leading AI companies, coordination among frontier firms to set safety standards and limit unchecked development, and international cooperation to manage risks. He warned that swarms of rogue AI agents could take over the internet in as little as six months. Altman agreed the industry needs to slow the pace of frontier-model advances and take more steps on safety; Musk backed the view. This follows a July letter in which **more than 1,200 AI workers** across Anthropic, DeepMind, OpenAI and Meta asked Washington to help build a slowdown framework.

**It is not a settled policy outcome.** White House AI czar David Sacks pushed back, effectively telling the labs to slow down on their own rather than negotiate an industry-wide brake. That matters for the market: a voluntary, uncoordinated slowdown has very different capex implications than a regulated one.

### 1. FTI — TechnipFMC plc *(Energy equipment & services — subsea production systems)*

**Thesis:** The purest picks-and-shovels play on a supply-driven oil shock, trading at roughly **14x** earnings with a **$15.8B subsea backlog** already contracted. When the constraint is physical supply rather than demand, the companies that engineer and install offshore production capacity see order inflections that the market has not yet priced.

**Valuation Metrics:** P/E ~14x — explicitly below Baker Hughes' ~18.2x and below the energy-services group. On EV/EBITDA and P/S the discount is comparable. PEG is favorable given mid-teens earnings growth against a 14x multiple.

**Growth Profile:** Subsea backlog of **$15,833.2M** as of June 30, 2026, essentially flat versus December 31, 2025 (−$38.5M) — the important point being that it has *held* at a high level rather than drained. Q2 subsea momentum and upper-end guidance expectations support a constructive 2026 earnings setup, with cash generation improving.

**Competitive Moat:** iEPCI™ integrated subsea delivery is a genuine structural advantage — TechnipFMC is one of very few players able to bid engineering, procurement, construction and installation as a single integrated contract, which compresses project timelines for operators and makes competitive displacement expensive. The named backlog reads like a who's-who of long-cycle projects: TotalEnergies Mozambique LNG and GranMorgu, bp Tiber, Kaskida and NEP, Equinor Raia and Johan Sverdrup Phase 3. These are multi-year, contractually locked, and largely insensitive to near-term crude volatility.

**Key Risk:** Long-cycle offshore projects are sanctioned on operators' *long-term* price decks, not spot. A rapid diplomatic resolution in the Gulf that collapses crude back toward $70 would stall new final investment decisions, even though existing backlog is protected. Execution risk on fixed-price EPCI contracts is the perennial second concern.

**Rating: Strong Buy**

### 2. EXE — Expand Energy Corporation *(Oil & gas E&P — natural gas)*

**Thesis:** The largest US natural gas producer post-merger, at roughly **8x trailing earnings** and a ~$22.6B market capitalization, at the exact moment a Middle East supply disruption makes North American gas the world's most geopolitically secure molecule.

**Valuation Metrics:** Trailing P/E ~8x. Forward P/E in the **10–13x** range alongside Ovintiv and EQT. For the largest producer in the basin with this balance sheet, that is a low-single-digit-multiple discount to a commodity that has structural demand growth ahead of it.

**Growth Profile:** The swing factor is Henry Hub front-month versus the **$3.00/MMBtu** threshold — sustained moves above it drive earnings upgrades across the E&P complex. A **50% EBITDA uplift by 2028** is achievable if Henry Hub tracks EIA projections. LNG demand is projected to grow **4–5% annually**, while domestic production has been declining and raw gas demand has been running near 18.8 Bcf/d.

**Competitive Moat:** Scale in the lowest-cost basins. Marcellus economics run as low as **$1.50/MMBtu** equivalent, putting the asset base firmly in the bottom quartile of the cost curve. In a commodity business, being the low-cost producer at the largest scale *is* the moat — it is the only durable one available.

**Key Risk:** **I am underwriting this on LNG exports, not on data centers — and that distinction is the whole risk.** Part of the bull case circulating on Expand rests on AI-driven data center power demand, which is precisely the assumption that took damage this morning. If you buy this name for the data center story you are buying today's problem at a discount. The LNG export pull and declining domestic supply are the load-bearing drivers, and they stand on their own. Secondary risk: a warm winter with Henry Hub sustained below $3.00 breaks the earnings-upgrade cycle outright.

**Rating: Buy**

### 3. CF — CF Industries Holdings, Inc. *(Materials — nitrogen fertilizer)*

**Thesis:** The cleanest way to own an energy shock without owning oil price beta. Natural gas is **~36% of nitrogen production cost**, so when global gas spikes, CF's Henry Hub-linked feedstock advantage over gas-importing European competitors *widens* — an energy crisis is a competitive weapon rather than a cost problem.

**Valuation Metrics:** Trades at a mid-cycle-appropriate multiple against a **raised mid-cycle EBITDA target of $2.9B**. The key valuation insight is that the market has historically capitalized CF's earnings as cyclical peak earnings; management's guidance raise argues the industry's capital-cost floor has permanently risen, which would justify a structurally higher multiple on a structurally higher mid-cycle.

**Growth Profile:** Q1 2026 earnings beat on tight nitrogen markets; Q2 brought the mid-cycle guidance raise with management seeing **nitrogen tightness persisting through 2030**. Pricing has been the primary driver, with healthy underlying demand.

**Competitive Moat:** A production cost advantage of approximately **$300 per ton** over European peers as of early 2026 — first-quartile position on the global cost curve via Henry Hub access, plus an efficient North American asset base, unmatched distribution and logistics, and production flexibility. Critically, the asset base is **insulated from the geopolitical disruptions affecting competitors**. New nitrogen capacity requires multi-year permitting and billions in capital, so the cost-curve position is not contestable on any near horizon.

**Key Risk:** The mirror image of the thesis — a resolution in the Gulf that normalizes European gas would compress the $300/ton spread and de-rate the shares quickly. Longer term, nitrogen is still a cyclical commodity: demand destruction from farmer affordability at sustained high prices, or a heavy new-capacity cycle, would pressure the mid-cycle thesis.

**Rating: Strong Buy**

### 4. AEM — Agnico Eagle Mines Limited *(Materials — precious metals)*

**Thesis:** Stagflation plus an active shooting war is the textbook gold setup, and current producer economics are extraordinary: realized prices near **$4,483/oz** against all-in sustaining costs of **$1,459/oz**. That roughly $3,000/oz margin is being generated in the most stable mining jurisdictions on earth.

**Valuation Metrics:** Q2 2026 net income of **US$1.60B, or US$3.19 per share** — a single quarter. Total cash costs of **$1,054/oz** and AISC of **$1,459/oz** against a $4,483/oz realized price is a gross margin structure that does not require the gold price to rise for the thesis to work; it only requires it not to collapse.

**Growth Profile:** Q2 2026 production of **855,816 ounces**. The earnings trajectory is driven by the widening spread between a rising realized price and cost inflation that has stayed contained — operating leverage in its most literal form.

**Competitive Moat:** Jurisdictional quality and cost discipline. Agnico's asset base is concentrated in Canada, Australia and Finland — politically stable, established permitting regimes — which is precisely what commands a premium when the reason to own gold is geopolitical instability. A gold miner exposed to expropriation risk is a poor hedge against exactly the conditions that make gold rise. Sustained first-quartile AISC through a broad mining cost-inflation cycle demonstrates real operating discipline rather than a passing grade of ore.

**Key Risk:** Gold's negative correlation to real rates is the live threat this week. If the Fed delivers a hawkish surprise Wednesday and the 10-year breaks decisively through 5%, rising real yields would pressure bullion and the miners would fall with roughly 1.5–2x beta. This pick is explicitly a hedge against the stagflation and geopolitical scenarios — it is designed to lose money in the benign outcome.

**Rating: Strong Buy**

### 5. CCJ — Cameco Corporation *(Energy — nuclear fuel cycle)*

**Thesis:** The energy-security trade with a discrete, dateable catalyst. Cameco owns **49% of Westinghouse**, whose planned 2026 IPO is targeting a **$23–30B valuation** — a sum-of-the-parts event that forces the market to mark a stake currently carried well below any plausible public-market value.

**Valuation Metrics:** *This is the one pick where I will not claim the headline multiple is cheap.* Cameco's P/E has consistently screened rich against conventional energy, and on trailing earnings alone it is expensive. The valuation case is sum-of-the-parts: a 49% interest in a business targeting a $23–30B IPO valuation implies roughly $11–15B of attributable value from the Westinghouse stake alone, against contracted uranium earnings that are separately re-rating. The multiple is the risk; the asset backing is the case. I rate it accordingly below.

**Growth Profile:** Contracted for average annual deliveries of **over 28 million pounds of uranium over the next five years** — unusual revenue visibility for a miner. Recent additions include a nine-year agreement to supply nearly **22 million pounds to India**, valued at approximately **$2.6B**. Westinghouse's AP1000 pipeline spans **91 reactors**, with a conditional DOE commitment of **$17.5B** to accelerate deployment, underpinned by a reported $80B strategic partnership with the US government.

**Competitive Moat:** Vertical integration across the entire nuclear fuel cycle — from tier-one uranium deposits through conversion and reactor services via Westinghouse — is unique among Western producers, and the barrier is regulatory rather than capital. Nuclear fuel supply is a licensed, security-cleared, sovereign-relationship business. Following the displacement of Russian supply from Western fuel chains, Cameco's position is a matter of national energy security for multiple governments, which is about as defensible as a commercial moat gets.

**Key Risk:** Valuation is the primary risk, and the IPO is the mechanism by which it either resolves or disappoints — a Westinghouse listing that prices at the low end, or slips into 2027, removes the catalyst underpinning the multiple. Note also that part of the nuclear demand narrative rests on data center power, which took a hit this morning; I regard energy-security and grid-decarbonization demand as the durable drivers here, but the AI-adjacent component of the story is real and is now a live risk.

**Rating: Accumulate** *(rating held below Buy specifically on valuation discipline — the business quality merits more, the multiple does not)*

With PCE at 3.7% and crude up 4% two days before the decision, the dot plot may shift hawkish even on a hold. The 10-year at 4.97% has almost no buffer before 5%, a level that would force broad multiple compression across every long-duration asset. This is the risk most likely to hurt *all five* picks simultaneously, gold included.

**2. AI capex de-rating broadens — SEVERE.** Today hit semis and AI infrastructure. The unresolved question is whether it spreads to the industrials, utilities and power names whose backlogs are AI-dependent — a group that has been among 2026's strongest performers. With White House AI czar David Sacks publicly rejecting a coordinated industry brake, the policy path is genuinely uncertain, and uncertainty is what sustains a discount. A disorderly unwind of the AI infrastructure trade would take the broad index down regardless of sector positioning.

**3. Gulf escalation beyond current levels — HIGH.** With the Saudi Hormuz-bypass pipeline shut and a vessel already struck inside the strait, the physical release valve for GCC exports is gone. Bernstein's $150 path is a tail, but a tail with a mechanism. Above roughly $120 Brent, the market stops treating energy strength as a rotation opportunity and starts pricing demand destruction and a genuine global growth scare — at which point energy equities fall with everything else.

**4. Stagflation regime confirmation — HIGH.** The combination now on the board — 3.7% PCE, $107 crude, softening July employment and retail sales, a Fed potentially forced to hike into weakening data — is the definition of the regime equities handle worst. Multiple compression and earnings downgrades arrive together, and the historical playbook offers very few equity hiding places.

**5. Crowded-trade unwind in energy and refining — MODERATE.** Refiners up 80%+ YTD on record crack spreads represent significant crowded positioning. A diplomatic breakthrough on Hormuz shipping — GCC and Iranian diplomats were meeting Monday — could trigger a violent reversal that drags the entire energy complex down with it, including names whose fundamentals are unrelated to spot crude. My FTI and EXE positions carry this correlation risk even though neither thesis depends on spot price.

## Bottom Line

The market spent 2026 underwriting two assumptions — that AI capex compounds indefinitely and that the Fed's next move is a cut — and both were challenged before the opening bell today. The right response is not to sell equities but to rotate the *source* of the return: away from names whose value depends on the frontier-model buildout continuing uninterrupted, and toward physical-economy businesses with contracted backlogs, structural cost advantages, and cash flows that an oil shock improves rather than erodes.

Wednesday's dot plot matters more than anything that happened this morning. Position for stagflation, keep the hedge on, and resist buying the 80% move in refiners just because the story is good — the story being good is already the price.
