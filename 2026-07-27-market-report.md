## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The Fed is the week's dominant variable, and the risk is asymmetric.** The FOMC announces Wednesday, July 29 at 2:00 p.m. ET. Economists polled by FactSet expect the target range held at **3.50%–3.75%** — a fifth consecutive hold. The June meeting was a unanimous hold, with the Committee noting little change in unemployment and solid activity growth, but inflation still elevated against the 2% goal.

What matters more than the hold is the **direction of the dissent**. Nearly half of policymakers at the June meeting signaled they would support a **rate hike** later this year, and reporting indicates nine of eighteen participants now favor a 2026 hike versus none in March. Under Chair **Kevin Warsh**, the Fed has raised its 2026 PCE inflation projection to **3.6%**. This is the key repricing of the year: the market is no longer trading a cut-versus-hold question but a hold-versus-hike question.

**Inflation remains the binding constraint.** June CPI printed **3.5% headline** year-over-year with **core at 2.6%** — headline well above target and sticky enough to keep the hawkish wing credible.

**Why this matters for the tape:** a higher-for-longer or outright hiking path lifts discount rates on long-duration growth equities and raises financing costs for capital-intensive borrowers — precisely the AI-infrastructure and semiconductor complex that has driven index returns. Today's oil-relief rally is disinflationary at the margin and marginally *reduces* hike pressure, which is part of why the move was so broad.

**What to watch this week:** the FOMC statement and press conference Wednesday; fresh inflation data alongside it; Microsoft and Meta earnings Wednesday; Apple and Amazon Thursday. Options markets are pricing the largest single-name expected move at **Arm, at ±14.9%**.

## Geopolitical Factors

**The Iran conflict is the market's central overlay, and today was the first genuine de-escalation signal in weeks.** The 2026 war began February 28 when the U.S. and Israel commenced strikes on Iran; Iran retaliated with drones, ballistic missiles and small attack boats against shipping, and on March 4 declared the Strait of Hormuz "closed." Brent went from roughly $72 in late February to above $84 within five trading days.

The chokepoint's importance is hard to overstate: roughly **27% of the world's seaborne crude and petroleum products** and about **20.5 million barrels per day** — near 20% of global consumption — transit the Strait. The disruption has been characterized as the largest in the history of the oil market, with tanker transits far below normal and a shipping-insurance crisis amplifying the physical shortfall.

**Today's development:** the U.S. paused an almost two-week run of strikes, raising hopes that negotiations could restart. Brent fell as much as 7.4% to below $90, settling near $84.35.

**How to hold this position:** a pause is not a settlement. Analysts still see an incremental **$10–$15 per barrel** of Brent upside if disruptions re-escalate, and the risk premium can be re-priced into the market in days — as February demonstrated. Today's sector rotation (long airlines/cruise, short producers) is a high-beta bet on a diplomatic process that has not yet begun. Sizing discipline matters more than direction here.

**Today's cross-asset read:** Delta and American rose 2.6% and 3%; Royal Caribbean and Carnival each gained ~3.2%; on the other side, Occidental Petroleum fell 3.8% and Exxon Mobil 2.6%.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: Healthcare Tech, Medical Devices & Biotech (Real Revenue)

**The setup: healthcare is where capital isn't.** The single most important structural fact about this sector in mid-2026 is that U.S. MedTech has been grinding to multi-year relative lows while global capital has been absorbed by AI and semiconductors. The sector carries two distinct overhangs — **GLP-1 disruption fears** (the assumption that obesity pharmacotherapy structurally shrinks demand for bariatric, orthopedic, cardiac and diabetes-device volumes) and a **broad de-rating of high-multiple growth names** as the rate path turned hawkish. Capital has flowed out of high-P/E MedTech growth.

That combination produces exactly the condition a valuation-driven investor wants: **companies with real revenue, real approvals and real cash flow trading at multiples set by fund flows rather than by fundamentals.** Today's spotlight leans into that.

**Consolidation is validating the asset values the public market won't.** The defining sector transaction of 2026 is **Boston Scientific's agreement to acquire Penumbra for ~$14.5 billion**, announced January 15 at **$374 per share** — approximately 73% cash and 27% BSX stock, with the ~$11 billion cash portion funded from cash on hand plus new debt. Penumbra, founded in 2004 with more than 4,500 employees, was tracking ~$1.4 billion of 2025 revenue at greater than 17% growth. Boston Scientific gets scaled entry into mechanical thrombectomy and neurovascular — strategic adjacencies to its cardiovascular franchise — and accepts $0.06–$0.08 of first-full-year adjusted EPS dilution to get it.

Penumbra's own Q1 2026 print shows what BSX bought: revenue of **$374.8 million, +15.6% YoY**, with global thrombectomy at **$253.9 million (+12.1%)** and embolization and access at **$120.8 million (+23.8%)**. Note the mix — the faster-growing line is the *non*-thrombectomy business. Penumbra has withdrawn full-year 2026 guidance pending the deal and trades at a **~71.7x P/E versus 23.6x for U.S. Medical Equipment peers**, which is now a merger-arbitrage spread rather than a fundamental multiple. **Penumbra is therefore excluded from today's picks** — the return is deal-completion risk, not business value.

A second structural transaction reshaped the diagnostics landscape: **Becton Dickinson completed a Reverse Morris Trust with Waters Corporation in February 2026**, separating its Biosciences and Diagnostic Solutions business at approximately **18.9x EBITDA** and delivering BD **$4 billion in cash** plus Waters stock for distribution to BD shareholders. The read-through matters in both directions — it establishes a high-teens EBITDA clearing price for quality diagnostics assets, and it turns BD into a consumables-heavy pure-play MedTech.

**Where the real revenue is.** Screening the sector for durable commercial franchises rather than story stocks surfaces a consistent theme: the market is now paying for approvals, patent protection and steady cash flow, and is no longer funding promise. Several profitable, cash-generative names have de-rated alongside the sector without any deterioration in their own numbers:

- **Commercial rare-disease pharma** is the clearest value pocket. **Harmony Biosciences** reaffirmed full-year 2026 net product revenue of **$1.00–$1.04 billion** on July 16 and trades near **14x earnings**. **United Therapeutics** trades at roughly **17.4x** against a risk-adjusted fair multiple of **25.4x** even after a 185% run, following LungFX approval and positive Tyvaso Phase 3 data.
- **Oncology with a franchise, not a coin flip.** **Exelixis** posted Q1 2026 revenue of **$610.8 million (+10% YoY)** with CABOMETYX U.S. net product revenue up 8% to $555 million, and guides 2026 to roughly **$2.4 billion**. Its forward P/E of **14.6x** sits against peers at 24.5x.
- **Pharma services is quietly inflecting.** **Medpace** delivered a beat-and-raise on July 23 — Q2 revenue **+17.2% to $707.3 million**, EPS $4.25, net income **+34%**, net new awards **+28.2%**, **book-to-bill 1.13x**, backlog above **$3.0 billion** — and the stock jumped ~15–19%. Book-to-bill above 1.0x recovering is the leading indicator for the whole CRO group, and it implies biotech R&D budgets are re-opening.
- **Healthcare fintech is a rate-hedged compounder.** **HealthEquity** closed fiscal 2026 with net income of **$215.2 million (+123%)**, adjusted EBITDA of **$566.0 million (+20%)** and HSA assets of **$36.5 billion (+14%)**, and guides fiscal 2027 HSA cash yield to ~**3.8%** from 3.53%. Its custodial revenue rises with rates — a rare healthcare asset that *benefits* from the hawkish Fed driving the rest of the sector's multiple compression.

**The GLP-1 discount is being applied too indiscriminately.** The bear case is real for volume-levered orthopedic and bariatric exposure, but it is close to irrelevant for CRO capacity, HSA custodial economics, plasma-derived biologics, oncology franchises and CNS rare disease. The market is currently charging a sector-wide GLP-1 toll to businesses that do not pay it. That mispricing is the source of most of today's picks.

**One name failed diligence and is worth naming.** **ADMA Biologics** screens attractively on growth — ASCENIV revenue of $97.5 million in Q1 2026, **+28% YoY**, at record utilization — but the company **cut full-year 2026 revenue guidance to $530–560 million** from an implied $575–600 million on standard-IG competitive pressure, **withdrew long-term guidance**, and faces **multiple securities-fraud class actions** alleging roughly 20% revenue inflation via channel stuffing following a March 24, 2026 Culper Research report that knocked ~29% off the shares. The stock is down ~53.5% YTD near $8.31 at a ~$1.85 billion valuation. Cheap and unresolvable are different things: unquantifiable restatement risk cannot be underwritten on fundamentals, and ADMA is excluded.

### Artificial Intelligence

The AI trade in 2026 has become a capital-intensity story, and the numbers have gotten large enough that the financing question now matters as much as the demand question. The four largest U.S. hyperscalers — Microsoft, Google, Amazon and Meta — are tracking roughly **$725 billion of combined 2026 capex, up ~77% from ~$410 billion in 2025**, with Microsoft alone committing **$190 billion for CY2026**. Backlogs corroborate the demand: **AWS at $244 billion (+40% YoY)**, **Oracle at $523 billion** including Meta and NVIDIA commitments, Google Cloud growing 63% and AWS 28%.

The counterweight is monetization. Enterprise AI revenue has reached about **$37 billion, up 3x year-over-year** — real, but an order of magnitude below annual capex — and only **45% of organizations can quantify their AI ROI**. That gap is the analytical crux of the second half: spending is contractually committed, returns are not yet demonstrable, and the market has begun discriminating between the two. Wednesday and Thursday's Magnificent Seven prints are the highest-information events of the quarter on this question, because capex guidance now moves the entire supply chain more than the earnings beat does.

### Hardware & Semiconductors

Semis are the sector where the hawkish-Fed repricing has already done visible damage, and today's bounce should not be mistaken for repair. After a historic **130% twelve-month rally** in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, early July delivered a violent valuation reassessment: **Micron −13%** in a single session, erasing roughly **$138 billion** of market value, **Intel −9%**, **AMD −7%**, and the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) **−5%** after a record 71% second-quarter gain.

The selloff was a convergence, not a single event. Three factors mattered: **SK Hynix signaling a slowdown in HBM production expansion** — read by the market as a demand-growth warning rather than a supply constraint; the **Fed's hawkish turn** raising discount rates on long-duration growth and borrowing costs for capital-intensive fabs; and simple positioning after a 130% run. The HBM signal is the one to track, because memory has been the tightest link in the AI chain and the first place a demand inflection would appear.

### Software & SaaS

Enterprise software sits awkwardly between the two dominant forces: it is a primary beneficiary of broadening enterprise AI adoption and scaling inference workloads as AI moves from experimentation into production, and simultaneously the most rate-sensitive cohort in tech on a duration basis. Cloud backlogs are large and growing, which supports the consumption-revenue names; the unresolved question is whether AI attach rates convert to seat-and-consumption revenue fast enough to justify current multiples before the Fed's path is settled. Wednesday's Microsoft print — Azure growth and AI revenue disclosure in particular — is the sector's most important single data point this week.

### 2. EXEL — Exelixis, Inc.

**Sub-sector:** Commercial oncology biotechnology

**Thesis:** A profitable oncology franchise growing double digits at a **14.6x forward P/E versus 24.5x peers**, where the market is paying nothing for a late-stage catalyst calendar that includes an accepted FDA filing and two pivotal readouts this year.

**Valuation Metrics:**
- P/E (trailing): ~15.1x (versus peers at 24.5x; a "fair ratio" framework suggests 19.3x)

- P/S: ~4.2x est. (on ~$2.4B 2026 guidance midpoint)
- EV/EBITDA: not confirmed in today's sources
- PEG: below 1.0x on consensus EPS growth; precise figure not confirmed

**Growth Profile:** Q1 2026 revenue **$610.8 million, +10% YoY**. FY2025 revenue $2.123 billion; FY2026 guided to roughly **$2.4 billion** (~13% growth). Catalysts: FDA accepted the zanzalintinib NDA (with atezolizumab) in previously treated metastatic colorectal cancer in February 2026, with **STELLAR-303 and STELLAR-304 pivotal data due in 2026**, plus a new Merck trial collaboration and an ASCO 2026 data presence.

**Competitive Moat:** Cabozantinib is the **#1 TKI monotherapy and the most-prescribed TKI in combination with immunotherapy** in renal cell carcinoma and neuroendocrine tumors — an entrenched standard-of-care position that is durable because oncologists' prescribing habits and guideline inclusion change slowly. Semiconductor valuation air pocket — severity: moderate-to-high, probability: elevated.** After a 130% twelve-month SOX rally, early July already delivered Micron −13% (~$138B of value in one session), Intel −9%, AMD −7%. SK Hynix's HBM expansion slowdown is the specific signal to monitor, since memory is the tightest link in the AI chain and would show a demand inflection first.

**5. Earnings-bar risk into a crescendo week — severity: moderate.** S&P 500 Q2 earnings growth is tracking ~38% YoY, far above original expectations, with ~751 companies reporting this week. When results are already exceptional, the marginal surprise skews negative, and implied moves are wide — Arm at ±14.9%. Beats are largely priced; misses are not.

## Bottom Line

Today's broad rally is a genuine risk-premium release, not a fundamental re-rating — the market simply stopped paying for war, and did so two days before the Fed and three days before the four largest earnings reports of the quarter. That argues for taking the gift in oil-levered cyclicals rather than chasing it, and for rotating into businesses whose cash flows do not depend on Wednesday's outcome. The most durable opportunity on the screen remains healthcare: a sector at multi-year relative lows, paying a blanket GLP-1 and duration discount that a CRO with recovering book-to-bill, a consumables franchise at 12x, and an HSA custodian that earns more as rates rise simply do not owe.
