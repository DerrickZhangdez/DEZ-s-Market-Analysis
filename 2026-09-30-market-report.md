## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The regime has inverted, and this is the year's most under-appreciated fact.** Investors entered 2026 expecting rate cuts. Instead, the FOMC **raised** the target range 25bp to **3.75%–4.00%** in September 2026 — the first hike since 2023, and unanimous. Core PCE has run above 3% every single month of 2026. The Fed's own September projections put 2026 headline and core PCE at **3.7% and 3.4%** respectively, falling to 2.3% by year-end 2027.

**Today's data cut the other way, which is why the tape is bid:**
- **PCE price index: +0.3% m/m, +3.4% y/y** — below expectations. Notably, this 3.4% headline is already *beneath* the Fed's own 3.7% year-end projection, which is the real reason the front end rallied.
- **ADP private payrolls: +90,000 in September**, a clear improvement from August's +36,000. Labor is loosening but not breaking.

**The read-through:** a cooler inflation print plus a firmer-but-not-hot labor market is the combination that lets the Fed *skip* October. The consensus baseline had been one more hike in 2026; this morning's data pushes that toward year-end at the earliest, and market pricing shifted accordingly. Do not confuse "no hike in October" with "cuts are coming" — with core PCE at 3.4% against a 2% target, the bar for easing remains distant.

**What to watch this week:** Micron's fiscal Q4 (tonight) for the AI capex read-through; quarter-end rebalancing flows into today's close; and the September employment report, the next genuine test of whether the ADP improvement is signal or noise.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: Fintech, Payments & Financial Data

*(Rotation: day-of-year 273 mod 6 = **3** → Fintech, payments & financial data.)*

Today's spotlight could not have landed on a more eventful day. **The US consumer credit scoring monopoly ended yesterday**, and the repricing was violent.

#### The FICO / VantageScore shock — what actually happened

FHFA Director **Bill Pulte** announced via social media that Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac will **consolidate their loan-pricing matrices into a single grid** that formally incorporates **VantageScore 4.0** alongside FICO Classic. Because Fannie and Freddie underpin roughly **70% of the US mortgage market**, and because FICO had for decades been the *only* score those agencies accepted, this converts a monopoly into a duopoly by regulatory fiat.

Three things compounded on the same day:

1. **TransUnion extended VantageScore 4.0 mortgage pricing at $0.99 through December 2028** — locking in a price point dramatically below FICO's for more than two years.
2. **Rocket Mortgage announced it will use VantageScore as its preferred scoring model** — the first major lender to formally defect.
3. Pulte publicly stated that Equifax, Experian and TransUnion **"have been overcharging Americans"** — signalling that the bureaus themselves, not just FICO, are in the regulatory crosshairs.

**The tape:**

| Company | Sep 29 move | Read |
|---|---|---|
| **FICO** (Fair Isaac) | **−25.97%** | Monopoly rent permanently impaired |
| **TRU** (TransUnion) | −4.5% | VantageScore co-owner, but bureau pricing now political |
| **EFX** (Equifax) | −3.5% | Same, plus mortgage-inquiry volume exposure |
| **Experian** | −2.0% | Most diversified geographically, least hit |

FICO is now **down 61% year to date**, carving lower lows since an October 2 record peak of **$1,998.01**.

**The analytical point most commentary missed:** the market sold the bureaus *and* FICO. That is not a simple market-share handoff trade. VantageScore is **co-developed and co-owned by the three bureaus**, so on a naive reading Equifax, Experian and TransUnion are the direct beneficiaries of FICO's displacement. They fell anyway. The market is telling you that Pulte's "overcharging Americans" framing is read as **the opening move against the entire credit-data cost stack**, not as a gift to the bureaus. Any bull case on the bureaus from here has to underwrite that regulatory posture explicitly — you cannot hold VantageScore ownership as an asset while ignoring that the same regulator calling FICO's pricing abusive has named the bureaus in the same breath.

**We are not buying the falling knife.** FICO at −61% YTD is not a value stock; it is a business whose terminal pricing power is being actively redefined by a regulator, and there is no floor you can underwrite. We address the bureau complex below through **TransUnion**, with the regulatory overhang priced as a *primary* risk rather than a footnote — and rated accordingly.

#### The rest of the fintech complex: compressed, and unevenly so

Outside the credit-data drama, the sector's setup is one of **broad multiple compression against rising estimates** — historically a constructive combination:

> Outside of Sezzle and Dave, the median return across the remaining 21 payments and fintech companies in H1 2026 was **−19%, with multiples compressing 25%, despite EPS/EBITDA estimates *rising* 3%.**

That is the definition of a sector where price and fundamentals have decoupled. With the exception of the card networks, **most mature payments and fintech companies trade at significant discounts to long-term averages**. PayPal is cited at a ~$41.4bn market cap with a ~62% fair-value upside on some models.

The dispersion is where the opportunity sits. Three structurally distinct groups have been sold indiscriminately:

- **Rate-sensitive balance-sheet fintech** (lenders, mortgage insurers) — sold on the Fed hike, but higher-for-longer *helps* net investment income at the insurers while credit stays benign.
- **Merchant acquiring** — sold on volume-growth deceleration fears and, in leveraged names, on balance-sheet anxiety.
- **Asset & wealth platforms** — sold on fee compression and, latterly, on "AI will disintermediate advice" narratives that are considerably thinner than the multiple compression implies.

Our picks below are deliberately spread across all three, plus the credit-data and analytics layers.

An **86% gross margin** at a memory manufacturer is an extraordinary number by any historical standard for this industry and is itself the thesis and the risk in one figure. Memory has always been cyclical; the bull case is that HBM has structurally changed the margin profile.

Key items to watch:
- **HBM TAM:** Micron sees it "easily crossing **$100 billion in 2027**."
- **HBM4:** mass shipments have begun for major customer platforms; qualification samples are with multiple end customers; **HBM4E** R&D is progressing.
- **The wafer trade-off is the real story:** HBM consumes substantially more wafer capacity than conventional DRAM — roughly **3:1 for HBM3E vs DDR, rising toward 4:1 with HBM4E.** This is why conventional DRAM pricing has surged: every HBM wafer is three-to-four wafers of commodity DRAM that never gets made. That supply constraint is doing as much for Micron's margin as HBM pricing itself, and it is the thing to watch for signs of normalization.

Micron previously raised guidance on surging memory prices, and the stock jumped 14.6% after its Q3 beat — the reaction pattern is that the stock moves on **guidance and HBM commentary first**, with read-across into the broader memory and equipment complex in the following session. Expect Thursday to be a semicap day regardless of direction.

### Software & SaaS

The "**SaaSpocalypse**" remains the year's defining equity narrative, and it is the necessary backdrop for today's fintech picks. Software stocks have lost **over $1 trillion in market value in 2026** on slowing revenue, enterprise SaaS budget cuts, and the seat-compression thesis:

> If 10 AI agents can do the work of 100 sales reps, you do not need 100 Salesforce seats — you need 10.

Valuation is now segmented not by TAM but by **AI application versus AI death-risk**, technical complexity, market position and specialization depth. **Data infrastructure commands the highest multiples across all categories**, driven by the AI data boom; design and engineering software also commands a premium where AI features **enhance rather than cannibalize** the core product.

The critical structural observation: **companies are clearing their quarterly bars while their multiples compress**, because the discount rate applied to forward SaaS cash flows is rising as the *durability* of those cash flows is questioned. This is a duration repricing, not an earnings miss.

**Why this matters for today's picks:** the same seat-compression logic is being applied indiscriminately to financial-services software and platforms. Where revenue is *per-seat*, the fear is legitimate. Where revenue is **per-transaction, per-dollar-of-assets, or per-unit-of-risk-insured**, it is largely misapplied — an AI agent that replaces a human analyst still generates the credit pull, still moves the payment, still requires the insurance. Three of our six picks below are direct expressions of that distinction.

- **Thesis:** TransUnion fell 4.5% on a day it arguably *won* — it co-owns VantageScore, the score that just displaced FICO's mortgage monopoly, and it has locked $0.99 mortgage pricing through December 2028 while Rocket Mortgage defects to VantageScore as its preferred model. ### 3. ESNT — Essent Group *(Private mortgage insurance)*

- **Thesis:** A 13%+ ROE financial trading at **1.04x book** with 97% of its risk reinsured is being priced for a credit cycle that its own balance sheet is explicitly structured to survive. This is the cleanest risk-adjusted valuation in today's list.
- **Valuation Metrics:** **P/B 1.04x** trailing book value — versus a Zacks sub-industry multiple of **1.5x**, and within a five-year range of 0.87x–1.33x (median 1.08x). At $1.82 quarterly diluted EPS annualized against a ~$61 book value, this is roughly **8–9x earnings**. Consensus price target **$69.00**.
- **Growth Profile:** Q1 2026 net income **$171.8M ($1.82/diluted share)**, annualized **ROE 12.0%**, **book value per share +11% y/y to $61.20**. By Q2 2026, **ROE climbed to 13.4%**. Insurance in force **$247.9B, +1% y/y**, with **persistency at 84.7%**. Capital returned: **$214M of buybacks YTD** plus a **$0.35 quarterly dividend**.
- **Competitive Moat:** Regulatory capital requirements and GSE eligibility standards make private mortgage insurance a structurally closed oligopoly — you cannot start one. High persistency (84.7%) means the existing book is an annuity: in a higher-for-longer world, borrowers with low legacy mortgage rates do not refinance, so policies that would historically have run off keep paying premiums. **The Fed's hiking cycle is a tailwind to this specific line item**, which is precisely backwards from how the market has been trading rate-sensitive financials.
- **Key Risk:** Mortgage insurance is a levered bet on home prices and employment. A genuine housing downturn with rising unemployment drives claims, and book value compounding stops abruptly. The **97% reinsurance coverage materially caps** this but does not eliminate it. Note also that current ROE of 13.4% is **23% below its 10-year median of 17.46%** — this is a company earning below its own historical standard, and the 1.04x book multiple partly reflects that, not pure mispricing.
- **Rating: Strong Buy.** Book value compounding at 11% with buybacks at ~1x book is mathematically accretive; the downside is protected by reinsurance and the multiple is near the bottom of its own five-year range.

## Risk Factors

**Ranked by severity for the week ahead:**

1. **Inflation re-acceleration forcing another hike (HIGH).** Core PCE has exceeded 3% every month of 2026 and the Fed has already hiked once to 3.75%–4.00%. Today's cooler print is *one* month against a 3.4% core run-rate and a 2% target. The baseline scenario still contemplates one more hike in 2026. Markets are priced for a skip; they are not priced for a hike.

2. **Middle East escalation / Strait of Hormuz (HIGH).** An Iran strike or Hormuz closure scenario puts Brent **above $120–160** against a base case of $75–90. This is the highest-severity tail because it is the one risk that would *simultaneously* crush equity multiples and force the Fed's hand — the energy risk and the rate risk are not independent.

3. **AI capex circularity (MEDIUM-HIGH, rising).** Nvidia investing up to $100B into OpenAI, backing $105B of data-center financing, and guaranteeing offtake at third-party campuses creates demand that is not fully independent of the supplier's own balance sheet. This is sustainable while end-demand grows and becomes reflexive in reverse if it does not. The abandoned GPT-6.1 launch is a small crack worth watching.

4. **Regulatory intervention in financial data pricing (MEDIUM, newly elevated).** Yesterday demonstrated that a single social-media post from the FHFA director can erase 26% of a company's value and drag an entire sub-sector with it. The stated rationale — that credit bureaus have been "overcharging Americans" — signals the intervention is not finished. **This risk is specifically relevant to today's TRU position and is why it is rated Accumulate rather than Buy.**

5. **SaaS duration repricing spilling into adjacent sectors (MEDIUM).** Over $1 trillion of software value has been erased in 2026 on seat-compression fears. The genuine risk is not to software itself but to **indiscriminate contagion** — the market is applying per-seat disruption logic to per-transaction and per-asset business models where it does not apply, which creates our opportunity but can also extend the drawdown well past the point of fundamental justification.

**A note on complacency:** the VIX at **16.04** against this risk stack is not a forecast of calm — it is a cheap price for protection. Investors who want the equity exposure recommended above should consider that the hedge has rarely been better priced relative to the tails described.

## Bottom Line

A cool PCE print has bought the market a reprieve into quarter-end, but the regime has not changed: the Fed hiked in September, core inflation is running at 3.4%, and a live Middle East tail risk sits on top of both — this is a market to own selectively, not broadly, and the VIX at 16 makes the hedge cheap enough that there is no excuse for running unprotected. **The genuine opportunity this week is in the wreckage of financial data and fintech**, where a regulatory shock and an indiscriminate SaaS de-rating have left transaction-, asset- and risk-based business models trading at software-disruption multiples they do not deserve — the sector's median H1 return was −19% with multiples compressing 25% *while earnings estimates rose*. Our six names are built around that dislocation with a deliberate rate-regime hedge inside the basket, and with position sizes that respect the fact that when a regulator can take 26% out of a stock with a single post, conviction should be expressed through diversification rather than concentration.
