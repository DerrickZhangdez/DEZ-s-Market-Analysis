Medical devices and commercial-stage biotech fit that description well — these are companies with inelastic demand, contractual pricing, and in several cases net cash balance sheets that *earn more* as rates rise.

**Watch this week and next:** December 9 FOMC is the main event. Between now and then, any core CPI or PCE print at 0.3%+ pulls the 50bp scenario from tail to live. **Why now specifically.** Three things have changed simultaneously:

1. **Rate regime inverts the relative-value math.** Devices and commercial biotech generate cash *today*. When the discount rate rises, a 14x-forward business growing 11% organically becomes dramatically more attractive versus a 40x business whose cash flows arrive in 2030.

3. **Demand is inelastic and demographically underwritten.** Aging populations drive procedure volumes regardless of the Fed. Unlike AI capex, which is a discretionary corporate budget line that can be cut in a quarter, a vascular graft or an MRD oncology test is not deferrable.

Both tails are live and they point in opposite directions for different parts of the portfolio.

**4. Volatility regime instability. (Severity: Medium)**
VIX went 16.61 → 28.18 → 18.25 inside two weeks, with the VIX/VIX3M curve inverting to 1.042 at the peak. That is not a market with stable positioning; it is one where modest news generates outsized moves because liquidity is thin and hedges are being repeatedly bought and unwound.

**5. Hospital capital spending and tariff pressure on the healthcare sector. (Severity: Medium — thesis-specific)**
The explicit risk to today's spotlight. Tariffs, input inflation, or a sharper slowdown in hospital capital spending would temper device-sector growth. STE and ICUI carry the most equipment-cycle exposure of the five picks; HALO and NTRA are the most insulated.

---

## Bottom Line

The market spent Wednesday celebrating that AI capex is intact and Thursday morning discovering that the governance of its largest AI company is not — and both of those are distractions from the fact that the Federal Reserve is about to raise interest rates into 3.8% inflation on December 9. Positioning for a hiking cycle means favoring businesses that earn cash now over businesses that promise cash in 2030, and healthcare technology is currently the largest pool of double-digit organic growth trading at value-sector multiples, precisely because capital has been looking elsewhere all year. Own the pricing power and the inelastic demand: HALO, NTRA, STE, LMAT and ICUI give you five uncorrelated sub-sectors with zero exposure to whether Nvidia's board reaches a decision tonight.
