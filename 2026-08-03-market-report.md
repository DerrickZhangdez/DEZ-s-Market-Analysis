## Macro & Monetary Environment

The FOMC held the federal funds target at **3.50%–3.75%** at its July 29 meeting. There have been **no cuts in 2026**, and officials removed their prior 2026 cut projection entirely, pushing reductions into 2027–2028 while they assess how durable the Iran-war inflation impulse proves. Some market participants are now pricing a non-trivial probability of **hikes** by year-end — a full 180 from January's consensus.

The inflation problem is real, not statistical noise. Core PCE accelerated from 3.0% in December 2025 to **3.3% in June 2026**, extending an above-target run that now spans more than five years. The energy channel explains much of the impulse: WTI began 2026 near $57, reached $113 in April, and was back above $84 during the week of the July meeting. Today's 5%+ slide to ~$80 is therefore genuinely disinflationary at the margin — if it holds. New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has stood up policy-review task forces whose findings are not due until later this year, which argues for an extended hold as the base case regardless of the data.

**This week's calendar:** ISM manufacturing and services PMIs, and Friday's **July nonfarm payrolls** — the single most important print of the week. In a stagflationary setup, a weak payroll number is not the "bad news is good news" trade it was in prior cycles; with the Fed anchored on inflation, soft employment plus sticky core PCE compresses the equity multiple from both directions.

**Earnings context:** With ~300 S&P 500 companies reported, roughly **85% have beaten** estimates and aggregate profit growth is tracking above **47%** — on pace for one of the strongest seasons in years. That is the bull case and the bear case simultaneously: the bar for Q3 guidance is now extraordinarily high.

## Geopolitical Factors

The Iran–Israel–US Gulf conflict remains the dominant macro variable of 2026, and today it moved decisively toward de-escalation — provisionally.

- **What changed:** Trump called off a planned strike, claimed a deal on the Strait of Hormuz, and said denuclearization talks begin Monday afternoon. Oil fell ~5%, Treasuries rallied, yields eased.
- **What has not changed:** The Hormuz agreement is **unconfirmed and disputed by Iran**. Roughly 20% of global oil and gas trade transits the strait. Military tension has already materially reduced shipping activity there and disrupted exports from Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Iraq and the UAE.
- **The price structure:** Brent has been carrying an embedded **$10–$15 risk premium**; sell-side scenario work puts **$130** on the table if maritime flows are fully obstructed. Today's move is the market discounting part of that premium, not eliminating the risk.
- **Supply-side confirmation:** OPEC+ agreed to raise September output by **188,000 bpd**, completing the rollback of the 2023 voluntary cuts. This is the more durable bearish input for crude, because it is a decision rather than a headline.

Positioning read-through: own energy *infrastructure* with fee-based, contracted cash flows rather than commodity-price-levered E&P or oilfield services. A negotiated settlement is bearish for the barrel and bearish for service intensity, but largely neutral-to-positive for volumes moving through pipelines.

### Artificial Intelligence

The AI capex numbers have reached a scale where they function as a macro variable. The five largest US cloud/AI infrastructure providers — Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Oracle — have collectively committed to **$660–690 billion** of 2026 capital expenditure: Amazon ~$200B, Alphabet $175–185B, Meta $115–135B, Microsoft $120B+, Oracle ~$50B.

The financing structures are getting more creative, and that is the thing to watch. Nvidia and OpenAI are in talks over a backstop of up to **$250 billion** that would let OpenAI raise debt against Nvidia's credit to fund a 10-gigawatt campus in Pike County, Ohio. This follows a letter of intent to deploy at least 10GW of Nvidia systems, with the first gigawatt on the Vera Rubin platform in 2H26. Note the history: Nvidia's earlier stated intent to invest up to $100B in OpenAI never materialized as such; it ultimately contributed $30B to OpenAI's March round. Vendor financing of one's own largest customer is a late-cycle structure, and equity markets reacted accordingly when the $250B backstop was reported — AI-linked names sold off on the news rather than rallying.

The market has also drawn a sharp line on capex discipline. Recent reactions: **Microsoft +16%** and **Amazon +10%** post-earnings, versus **Meta −10%** and **Apple −4%**; Alphabet raised its 2026 capex forecast and was punished. The rule the tape is enforcing is simple and worth internalizing: *demand evidence gets rewarded, capex without demand evidence gets punished.* Palantir reports after today's close with ~70% revenue growth and accelerating AIP adoption — the highest-momentum name in software heading into a print, and therefore the one with the most to lose on any deceleration.

### Hardware & Semiconductors

Semis are the epicenter of the tech de-rating despite fundamentals that remain, on the surface, extraordinary. Global semiconductor sales hit a record **$120.6 billion in May**, up 104.1% YoY and the fifteenth consecutive monthly record. Micron is guiding to a **$50 billion revenue quarter**.

And yet the memory complex is cracking. SK hynix fell 7.47%, Micron dropped more than 5% premarket in the following session, and AMD tumbled 8.37% to $453.50 ahead of its own print — despite being up 122% in 2026 with 54 upward earnings revisions in three months and a Susquehanna target raised to $500 from $450. **AMD reports Q2 tomorrow, August 4**, with expected upside from the MI350 data-center GPU line. The setup is treacherous: parabolic YTD gains, a decelerating memory tape, and consensus already positioned for a beat. SanDisk also reports this week, giving a second read on memory.

The interpretation I favor: this is not a demand problem, it is a pricing-of-cyclicality problem. Record monthly sales up 104% YoY is, by construction, an unsustainable growth rate, and the market is beginning to discount the second derivative rather than the level. That is a normal and healthy phase of a semiconductor upcycle — and a reason to take semiconductor exposure through consumables and equipment inputs rather than through the most-owned logic and memory names.

### 1. APD — Air Products and Chemicals, Inc.
**Thesis:** The purest picks-and-shovels exposure to semiconductor and AI capacity that is not itself priced as an AI stock — high-purity specialty gases are contracted, consumable inputs to every fab, and APD has a signed build-and-operate deal with Samsung Electronics. Fiscal Q2 adjusted EPS and operating income grew 19% YoY, ahead of the company's own guidance, and management raised FY26 adjusted EPS guidance to $13.00–$13.25.

**Valuation Metrics:**

- Trailing P/E: ~22.5x
- Fair-value estimates cluster **$284–$308** (DCF-style estimate $284.21; consensus analyst target $308.00, consensus rating Buy)
- P/S, EV/EBITDA, PEG: not independently verified today; PEG estimated at **~1.6–2.0** on high-single to low-double-digit forward EPS growth — *estimate, not a sourced figure*

**Growth Profile:** Revenue forecast to compound **6.3% annually** over the next three years versus 5.2% for the chemicals industry; Q2 adjusted EPS and operating income +19% YoY; FY26 EPS guidance raised.

**Competitive Moat:** Industrial gases is a genuine oligopoly. On-site plants are built adjacent to customer facilities under 15–20 year take-or-pay contracts, making switching costs near-prohibitive and cash flows utility-like. APD and Linde effectively control the helium market. This is infrastructure revenue dressed as chemicals revenue.

**Key Risk:** It is not cheap — ~21x forward with roughly 6% revenue growth requires margin and pricing execution to work, and the stock trades within a few percent of most fair-value estimates. Legacy green-hydrogen project commitments remain a capital-allocation overhang that management is still working through.

**Rating: Accumulate**

**Thesis:** A cyclical chemicals franchise with a fresh, dated catalyst — JPMorgan upgraded LYB to Overweight from Neutral **today**, raising its target to $80 from $75 explicitly on the cash-flow outlook — trading below third-party fair-value estimates after a 43% YTD gain that still leaves the stock down 35% over five years.

**Key Risk:** Today's 5%+ oil decline cuts directly against the thesis — cheaper crude lowers naphtha costs for European and Asian competitors and **narrows LYB's ethane advantage**. ### 3. MT — ArcelorMittal S.A.
**Thesis:** European steel is being re-rated by trade policy rather than by demand, and the market has not finished discounting it. Q2 European EBITDA per tonne reached $98, up from $70 in Q1 and the highest in three years, with management stating that recent EU trade measures have not yet fully appeared in results. The stock rose 4.2% premarket to $68.46 on a headline EPS **miss**, which is the market telling you what it actually cares about here: pricing and cash generation.

**Valuation Metrics:**
- P/E: **17.94x**
- PEG: **0.12** — a deep discount to the growth rate, though PEG on trough-cycle earnings always flatters
- Consensus target $72.40; Wells Fargo raised its target to $69 from $62
- P/S and EV/EBITDA not independently verified today
- Q2: adjusted EPS $0.90 vs $1.18 expected; revenue $16.76B vs $16.99B expected; **group EBITDA +23% to $2.1B**

**Growth Profile:** FY26 strategic-project contribution of **$700 million**, against a long-term target of **$1.8 billion** — a multi-year self-help earnings bridge that does not require steel prices to rise.

**Competitive Moat:** Vertical integration into captive iron ore insulates margins when raw-material costs spike, and geographic diversification across Europe, the Americas and India spreads regional cycles. The newer and more durable moat is regulatory: EU safeguards and carbon-border measures create a structurally protected home market for a domestic producer.

**Key Risk:** Chinese export volumes remain the sector's permanent overhang, and European end-demand is still weak — the margin improvement is price-and-policy-led, not volume-led. A 17.9x P/E is not cheap in absolute terms; the case rests on earnings power normalizing upward.

**Rating: Buy**

### 4. OKE — ONEOK, Inc.
**Thesis:** The correct way to own energy into a negotiated Iran settlement. With roughly **90% of earnings fee-based** across 60,000+ miles of pipelines, processing plants and storage, ONEOK's cash flows are insulated from the ~5% crude decline that will pressure E&P and oilfield-services earnings — while a mid-15x multiple and a 4.9% yield price it as a commodity business. The company raised 2026 guidance after a Q1 in which net income and EBITDA grew 12% and 13% respectively.

**Valuation Metrics:**

- Forward dividend yield: **4.87%**, annual dividend $4.28/share
- PEG: **above 2.0** — the honest weak spot in this thesis, and above peers such as Energy Transfer and Enterprise Products
- EV/EBITDA and P/S: not independently verified today

**Growth Profile:** 2026 guidance raised post-Q1; net income +12% and EBITDA +13% in Q1; stock up ~19% YTD. This is a mid-single-digit organic grower with acquisition-driven step-ups, not a compounder — which is exactly why the PEG looks poor and the P/E looks reasonable.

**Competitive Moat:** The NGL value chain footprint is effectively irreplaceable — gathering and processing through fractionation to export, spanning the Mid-Continent, Permian and Bakken. New-build permitting and right-of-way acquisition on that scale is not realistically repeatable, and the integrated chain captures margin at every stage.

**Key Risk:** NGL volume growth ultimately depends on producer drilling activity, and today's move toward ~$80 WTI plus OPEC+ adding 188kbpd in September is precisely the environment in which US producers moderate activity. **Rating: Accumulate**

**Competitive Moat:** Low-cost North American copper-tube manufacturing at scale, vertical integration from raw metal through finished fittings and valves, and entrenched wholesale distribution relationships in a business where availability and lead time beat price. A fortress, net-cash balance sheet lets Mueller buy capacity through cycles when levered competitors cannot. **Key Risk:** Margins are normalizing, and the print showed it — Q2 net income was **$249.7M versus $245.9M** a year earlier, essentially flat despite 25% sales growth, and Q2 EPS of $1.13 **missed** estimates. **Rating: Buy**

## Risk Factors

Ranked by severity this week:

1. **Fed re-pricing toward hikes.** Core PCE at 3.3% and accelerating, no 2026 cuts, cuts pushed to 2027–2028, and some participants now pricing hikes. Equity multiples at 7,437 on the S&P are not built for a hiking cycle. This is the risk that reprices everything at once.
2. **The Iran de-escalation is unconfirmed.** Iran has disputed the President's account of the Hormuz agreement. Today's ~5% oil decline and 17% VIX collapse have already banked the good outcome. A reversal restores a $10–15 Brent risk premium immediately, with $130 in the tail if maritime flows are obstructed — and re-ignites the very inflation impulse the Fed is anchored on.
3. **AI memory/semiconductor unwind.** SK hynix −7.5%, Micron −5%+ premarket, AMD −8.4% into tomorrow's print, Nasdaq −3.2% in July. With $660–690B of 2026 hyperscaler capex now partly financed through structures like the proposed $250B Nvidia/OpenAI backstop, a demand-signal disappointment transmits into credit and financing terms, not just equity multiples.
4. **Friday's payrolls in a stagflationary regime.** A weak July NFP no longer buys a dovish Fed. Soft employment plus 3.3% core PCE compresses earnings estimates *and* keeps policy tight — the one combination for which the current tape is unprepared.
5. **The earnings bar.** 85% beat rates and +47% aggregate profit growth across ~300 reporters means good news is fully in the price. Palantir tonight and AMD tomorrow are both crowded longs where in-line guidance is a de-rating event.

## Bottom Line

The market got the geopolitical outcome it wanted today and expressed it exactly where the fundamentals have been all year — cyclicals, small caps and the physical infrastructure layer, not megacap tech. I would use the relief rally to keep rotating in that direction, but I would not pay data-center-contractor multiples to do it: Prysmian's cash takeout of Atkore at ~10x EBITDA shows where strategic buyers see value, and it is one layer removed from the names the screens have already bid up.
