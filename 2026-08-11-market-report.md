## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The Fed is on hold, but the risk has flipped direction.** The FOMC left the federal funds target at **3.50%–3.75%** in July 2026 — a fifth consecutive hold. The critical detail is the dissent pattern: **three members dissented in favor of a 25bp increase.** For most of this cycle the debate was how fast to cut. It is now whether to hike. Chair Warsh has said the Fed "won't hesitate" to stop inflation, though the bond market has registered doubts.

**Inflation is the swing variable.** US inflation eased to **3.5% in June**, its first decline in five months — progress, but still 150bp above target, and much of the stickiness reflects supply shocks concentrated in energy. That is precisely the channel the Hormuz conflict runs through, which is why the geopolitical story and the monetary story are not separable this week.

**What to watch:**
- **Wednesday, Aug 12 — July CPI.** The single most consequential release of the week. A hot print, particularly in energy or core services, converts three dissenters into a live September hike debate. A soft print lets the equity melt-up continue.
- **September FOMC.** Described by several desks as the first meaningful test of whether June's inflation improvement is durable. The door to a hike is explicitly open.
- **Long-end Treasuries.** Asia-Pacific government bonds followed Treasuries lower this week as the oil rally revived inflation concerns. Watch whether a hot CPI steepens or inverts the curve — the former is manageable for equities, the latter is not.

The equity market is priced for the Fed's next move to be either nothing or a cut. The FOMC minutes say otherwise. That gap is the primary macro risk embedded in current multiples.

## Geopolitical Factors

**The Strait of Hormuz remains the dominant geopolitical variable.** Roughly **20% of global crude oil and LNG shipments** transited Hormuz under pre-conflict conditions. The current situation involves direct military confrontation between state actors in the Persian Gulf — a threshold not crossed since the Iran–Iraq War of the 1980s.

**Today's development was constructive.** Talks between Oman and Iran over the future of shipping through the Strait are at an "advanced stage," per Qatar's foreign ministry spokesperson, and **oil pared its gains** on the news. This followed Monday's session, when climbing oil prices pressured equities and Nvidia pulled back. By mid-July, the volume of attacks — Iranian strikes on commercial shipping and regional countries, US strikes on Iran — was running higher than at any point since April 2026, materially reducing cross-Strait traffic. Conflict has abated in early August amid the talks, but the standoff has simultaneously become more *entrenched*, raising doubts about a durable deal.

**The dual-chokepoint framework.** Market participants are now underwriting concurrent stress at **both Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb**. Houthi attacks on Saudi-linked vessels and energy targets in the Red Sea corridor, plus reported attempted attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure from Iran-linked militias in Iraq, mean the conflict has multiple credible expansion vectors. A single incident at either chokepoint re-prices crude, and through crude, the entire Fed path.

**Investment implication:** the market is trading the *diplomacy* headline and discounting the *tail*. Energy was the day's laggard on Oman–Iran optimism. Given that a Hormuz closure would simultaneously spike inflation and force the Fed hawkish, the correlation between an energy shock and an equity drawdown is unusually tight right now. Portfolio-level energy exposure is functioning as a macro hedge, not merely a sector bet.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: Enterprise Software & SaaS

*(Day-of-year 223; 223 mod 6 = 1 → Enterprise software & SaaS)*

This is the most interesting sector in the market right now, because it is the only major group where **the fundamentals and the tape have completely decoupled** — and the rotation calendar has landed on it at close to maximum dislocation.

**What happened: the 2026 "SaaSpocalypse."**

Roughly **$2 trillion of software market capitalization has been erased over the past twelve months.** The Tech-Software Sector ETF (IGV) is down about 23%. Workday is down 33%. The proximate trigger was **IBM's Q2 profit warning in July, which took the stock down about 25%** and dragged Salesforce, Adobe, Workday and Accenture with it, as investors priced in a single thesis: **capex reprioritization.** Enterprise clients are diverting budget from software licenses to AI hardware and infrastructure. If that persists, it implies a near-term revenue slowdown across the whole SaaS and consulting complex.

Layered on top is a more existential fear: that AI agents structurally break the recurring-revenue model.

**The structural argument deserves to be taken seriously — but it is not uniform.**

The genuine mechanism is pricing, not demand destruction. Per-seat pricing is under real pressure because **AI has broken the correlation between value delivered and seats occupied**. When an agent performs the work of five junior employees, per-seat pricing penalizes the customer for becoming efficient. The market's response is a shift to hybrid and usage-based structures — **40% of companies with ARR above $50M now include consumption- or outcome-based revenue**, and hybrid is the single most common model at 37% adoption. Full AI monetization remains the exception in 2026; most vendors are bolting on credits, tokens, usage meters and AI add-ons inside existing contracts.

The disruption risk is **highly concentrated by architecture.** The most vulnerable category is horizontal SaaS that primarily *organizes, routes and displays* data it does not own — project management, basic CRM, IT ticketing. The most defensible categories are the inverse:

1. **Systems of record inside regulated workflows** — where the software is the audit trail, and replacing it is a compliance event, not a procurement decision (core banking, investment accounting).
2. **Regulatory content moats** — where the durable asset is a continuously maintained corpus of jurisdictional rules, not the UI (tax determination).
3. **Network businesses** — where value derives from the counterparties already on the network, which an AI agent cannot replicate unilaterally (retail–supplier EDI).
4. **Infrastructure that owns the data layer** — where AI increases, rather than cannibalizes, consumption.

**Valuation: the bar has genuinely reset.** Median public SaaS now trades near **8.5x forward revenue**, with 6–12x considered strong for a >25% grower. The Rule of 40 (revenue growth + FCF margin ≥ 40) has moved from aspiration to minimum bar — companies below it take a material discount. A reasonable P/E floor for screening is below 25, a discount to Software-Application at 29.6 and Software-Infrastructure at 28.17.

**The opportunity.** Many analysts, Dan Ives among them, argue the selloff has overshot and that AI will complement rather than replace software. That view is directionally right but too broad to trade. The exploitable inefficiency is that **the market has de-rated the disruption-resistant architectures alongside the vulnerable ones.** Cash-generative, moated software at single-digit-to-mid-teens earnings multiples is now available for the first time since 2022 — and in a few cases, at multiples that price in terminal decline for businesses that are still growing and still compounding free cash flow. Today's picks are drawn almost entirely from that pool.

**Names screened and passed on, with reasons** (rigor matters as much as selection):
- **Descartes Systems (DSGX)** — outstanding business (FY26 revenue $729M +12%, 77% gross margin, 46% EBITDA margin) but at ~36x earnings versus a ~24.7x fair ratio, the price already reflects the quality.
- **Qualys (QLYS)** — excellent fundamentals (83% gross margin, 35%+ EBIT margin, debt-free) but the stock has run **68% since March**, from $92.34 to $161.06, and DCF work now flags it ~12% overvalued. The value window closed.
- **BlackLine (BL)** — 33x forward earnings for ~10% forecast growth. The multiple and the growth rate do not meet.
- **Clearwater Analytics (CWAN)** — outstanding operating momentum, but a definitive agreement to be acquired by Warburg Pincus/Permira/Temasek at $24.55/share makes this deal arbitrage, not a valuation thesis.
- **Teradata (TDC)** — genuinely cheap at ~7.35x earnings with a new $500M buyback, but secular revenue decline makes the multiple a value trap risk rather than a discount. Watchlist, not a pick.
- **UiPath (PATH)** — trading near management's ~$11.47 average buyback price with an active repurchase program; interesting, but agentic automation is the category most directly in AI's blast radius.

### Artificial Intelligence

**Nvidia dominated the tape again, and the story has shifted from chips to project finance.** Nvidia announced a partnership with **six major asset managers to channel more than $500 billion into AI infrastructure**, and the stock gained more than 1% Tuesday, providing critical support to the S&P 500. Separately, OpenAI is in discussions with Nvidia over a **backstop of up to $250 billion** to fund the lease of a **10-gigawatt data center campus in Pike County, Ohio.** Critically, the guarantee would cover *lease and construction debt* — not the Nvidia chips inside, which are being negotiated separately.

This is the defining structural development of the 2026 AI trade, and investors should be clear-eyed about what it means. The largest supplier to the AI buildout is now underwriting the financing of its largest customer's capacity. That converts what was a cash-funded capex cycle into a **credit-funded** one, and it introduces circularity: Nvidia's guarantee supports the debt that funds the facility that houses the chips Nvidia sells. When these reports first surfaced in late July, AI stocks sold off sharply — the market's instinct was correct even if the timing was premature. The buildout is real, the demand is real, and the financing structure is nonetheless a genuine new risk vector that did not exist twelve months ago.

**Hyperscaler capex remains the load-bearing wall.** The five largest US cloud and AI infrastructure providers have collectively committed to **$660–690 billion of capex in 2026**: Amazon ~$200B, Alphabet $175–185B, Meta $115–135B, Microsoft $120B+, Oracle ~$50B. As long as these budgets hold, the semiconductor and power complex has visible demand. The moment any one of them signals moderation, the multiple compression flows downhill fast — and, as IBM's warning demonstrated, the money funding it is coming *out of* enterprise software budgets.

## Stock Picks — Valuation-Based

All five picks come from today's enterprise software spotlight, selected specifically for **architectural defensibility against AI substitution** paired with a valuation that has been marked down as if no such defensibility existed. Every one generates real free cash flow today. There are no hype picks and no story stocks here.

### 1. PRGS — Progress Software Corporation
*Sub-sector: Infrastructure software / data platform roll-up*

- **Thesis:** A disciplined serial acquirer of mature infrastructure software trading at roughly **6.5x forward earnings** — a multiple that implies terminal decline for a business that just beat, raised full-year guidance, and is guiding to record free cash flow. The market is charging nothing for the AI-adjacent asset base (Nuclia's agentic RAG, the pending $400M Domo data-platform acquisition).
- **Valuation Metrics:** Forward P/E **~6.5x** on FY26 EPS guidance of **$6.09–$6.21** against a share price near $40 (one screen showed forward P/E as low as **5.39** in early June, roughly 72% below the ~19x software industry median). Trailing P/E ~7x on FY25 EPS of $5.72. P/S ~1.8x. EV/EBITDA in the high single digits, materially above the equity multiple because of acquisition debt — this is the number to underwrite, not the P/E.
- **Growth Profile:** Q2 FY26 revenue **$253M, +7% YoY**, beating the high end of guidance; EPS **$1.62 vs. $1.49** expected. FY25 revenue $978M (+30%, acquisition-driven) with EPS +16%. FY26 revenue guidance **$990M–$1.005B**, implying only **1–2.5% growth** — organic growth is roughly flat, and the thesis must not pretend otherwise. Adjusted FCF was $247M in FY25 (+16%), guided to **$260–274M** in FY26.
- **Competitive Moat:** High switching costs in embedded infrastructure and file-transfer software, plus a genuinely repeatable capital-allocation engine — 19 acquisitions to date, bought at low multiples and run for cash.
- **Key Risk:** **Two-sided.** First, the July 2026 ShareFile incident, in which Progress ordered customers to shut down on-premises Storage Zone Controllers over a "credible external security threat" with no patch initially available. Progress has a history here, and repeated security events in a file-transfer franchise create real churn and litigation exposure. Second, leverage: the $875M ShareFile deal and $400M Domo deal are debt-funded against a business with ~1–2% organic growth. The equity is cheap partly *because* the enterprise value is not.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

### 2. SPSC — SPS Commerce, Inc.
*Sub-sector: Supply-chain network SaaS (retail–supplier EDI)*

- **Thesis:** The dominant retail–supplier EDI network, historically a 20%+ grower valued at 60–80x earnings, now available at **15.2x** after growth decelerated to mid-single digits. The network itself — tens of thousands of interconnected trading partners — is the least AI-disruptable asset structure in enterprise software, because no agent can unilaterally recreate the counterparties.
- **Valuation Metrics:** **P/E 15.2x.** P/S **3.2x** ($2.6B market cap on $775M revenue) — versus a median public SaaS multiple of ~8.5x forward revenue. EV/EBITDA in the low double digits on an EBITDA base growing 19%.
- **Growth Profile:** Q2 2026 revenue **$197.8M, +6% YoY**, with **EBITDA up 19%** — the key tell, showing operating leverage well ahead of revenue. FY26 guidance **$788.4–793.4M (5–6% growth)**. A strategic divestiture has sharpened focus on the higher-growth 1P market.
- **Competitive Moat:** True network effects. Retailers mandate SPS onboarding for suppliers; each added retailer pulls in hundreds of suppliers. Switching means renegotiating connectivity with every trading partner simultaneously — commercially near-impossible.
- **Key Risk:** Growth deceleration from 20%+ to 5–6% may reflect market saturation rather than a cyclical retail trough. If mid-single digits is the new structural rate, 15x is fair rather than cheap, and the re-rating never comes. The EBITDA-versus-revenue growth spread is the metric to monitor.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

### 3. NCNO — nCino, Inc.
*Sub-sector: Vertical SaaS — banking systems of record*

- **Thesis:** The cloud banking operating system for financial institutions, at roughly **$1.7B market cap against FY27 guidance of $135–140M in free cash flow** — approximately 12x EV/FCF for a business that just posted a 50.7% quarterly FCF margin and swung operating margin from −1% to +13.2% year over year. One value screen puts fair value at $37.38 against a ~$15.19 share price.
- **Valuation Metrics:** P/S **~2.7x** on FY27 revenue guidance of $642–646M. EV/FCF **~12x** at the midpoint. Non-GAAP operating income guided to **$166–171M**, roughly 26% margins. Trailing GAAP P/E is not the right lens here — the FCF conversion is.
- **Growth Profile:** Q1 revenue **$159.4M, +10.6% YoY**, beating estimates by 2.4%. Free cash flow **$80.8M, +54% YoY**. The five-year revenue CAGR is 22.4%, so this is a decelerating grower — but the margin inflection is doing far more for intrinsic value than the growth slowdown is taking away.
- **Competitive Moat:** System of record inside a regulated workflow. nCino sits in loan origination and account opening, where the software *is* the audit trail. Ripping it out is a multi-year compliance and examination event, not a procurement decision — the structural opposite of the horizontal SaaS most exposed to agentic substitution.
- **Key Risk:** Bank IT budget cyclicality, and the possibility that AI-era competitors attack greenfield community-bank deployments where switching costs have not yet been established. Growth settling permanently near 10% would cap the multiple even with strong cash conversion.
- **Rating:** **Strong Buy**

### 4. VERX — Vertex, Inc.
*Sub-sector: Tax determination & regulatory compliance software*

- **Thesis:** Down **64.8% over twelve months** — from a $36.75 high to a recent $12.48, near its $10.21 52-week low — while revenue grew 10.5% and adjusted EBITDA grew **33%** with margins expanding to 25%. This is the sharpest disconnect between price action and operating results anywhere in the spotlight sector.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Revenue of **$204M** in the reported quarter. Trading near 52-week lows against an analyst consensus target of **$17.46**, roughly **30% above** the recent price, with 10 of 17 analysts at Buy. The valuation case rests on EBITDA margin expansion (25% and rising) against a compressed EV/revenue multiple, not on a trailing P/E, which remains depressed by amortization and stock compensation.
- **Growth Profile:** Revenue **+10.5% YoY**; adjusted EBITDA **+33%** — profit growing at roughly 3x the revenue rate, the signature of a business past its investment peak. Q2 2026 results were reported August 3.
- **Competitive Moat:** A **regulatory content moat**, which is the most underrated defense against AI substitution in software. Vertex's durable asset is a continuously maintained corpus of tax rules across thousands of jurisdictions, embedded directly into customers' ERP systems. An LLM can draft a tax memo; it cannot assume audit liability for a wrong rate applied across a million transactions. The content must be maintained, versioned, and indemnified — and it is embedded where ERP replacement cycles run a decade.
- **Key Risk:** The 65% drawdown is not solely sentiment — it reflects a real slowdown in ERP migration activity that drives Vertex's new-logo pipeline, plus fears that AI commoditizes tax content. If ERP cycles stay frozen, 10% growth compresses further and the stock stays cheap for longer than the thesis allows. This is the highest-volatility name in today's set; size it accordingly.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

### 5. FRSH — Freshworks Inc.
*Sub-sector: CX / ITSM SaaS*

The counterargument is that at 1.6x revenue with a 25% FCF margin and raised long-term targets, the market is already pricing a severe outcome — but this is a valuation-and-cash-flow pick, not a moat pick, and it does not belong in a portfolio at the same weight as NCNO or SPSC.

The compounding danger is that an oil shock transmits directly into the CPI that determines the Fed path — this risk is not independent of Risk 1, it multiplies it.

**3. AI capex financing circularity — SEVERITY: HIGH, IMMEDIACY: MEDIUM-TERM.**
Nvidia backstopping up to $250B of OpenAI's lease and construction debt, plus a $500B+ asset-manager infrastructure vehicle, converts a cash-funded capex cycle into a credit-funded one where the primary supplier guarantees the customer's obligations. AI stocks sold off sharply when these reports first surfaced in late July. Nothing here is fraudulent or even unusual in infrastructure finance, but it means the AI trade now carries **credit** risk in addition to demand risk, and credit risk transmits faster and less linearly than earnings disappointment.

**4. Enterprise software capex reprioritization proving structural — SEVERITY: MEDIUM-HIGH, IMMEDIACY: ONGOING.**
IBM's 25% single-day decline on clients shifting budget from software to AI hardware is the direct risk to today's entire pick list. If this is a durable budget reallocation rather than a one-to-two-quarter digestion, cheap software multiples compress further regardless of individual company execution. This is the concentrated factor risk in today's basket and is flagged as such.

**5. Memory/semiconductor crowding — SEVERITY: MEDIUM, IMMEDIACY: MEDIUM-TERM.**
SOXQ up 99% YTD, Micron sold out through 2027, and a consensus narrative of supply running 20% below demand through 2030. The fundamentals genuinely support the move. But a five-year shortage thesis is precisely the kind of consensus that unwinds violently when capacity additions finally land, and positioning is heavy.

## Bottom Line

A flat tape at a 15.58 VIX on the eve of a CPI print, with three FOMC members already voting to hike and a live conflict astride the Strait of Hormuz, is not calm — it is an underpriced event window, and hedges are cheap here. The genuine opportunity is the mirror image of the crowding: semiconductors are up 99% year to date on a consensus supply-shortage narrative, while $2 trillion of software market cap has been erased on an AI-substitution narrative that the market is applying **indiscriminately across business models with radically different exposure.** Buy the moats the selloff mispriced — regulated systems of record, regulatory content, and trading networks — while acknowledging honestly that if the IBM capex-reprioritization shift proves structural rather than cyclical, cheap gets cheaper before it gets better.
