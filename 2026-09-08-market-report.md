## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The Fed is debating a hike, not a cut — and that is the single most important repricing of the year.**

The July FOMC held the target range at **3.50%–3.75%** on a divided **9–3** vote. What has changed since is not the data so much as the reaction function. Chair Kevin Warsh used Jackson Hole to emphasize restoring credibility around the 2% target, and the market took it literally: implied probability of at least one 2026 hike jumped to roughly **70.5%**, and September-specific odds now run **60–65%**. J.P. Morgan Wealth Management has moved to an explicit call for a 25bp increase on September 16. Governor Barr's comments today — that he would back a hike if inflation does not ease convincingly — removed any lingering ambiguity that the July hold was dovish.

**The data supporting the hawks:**

- **Headline CPI 3.4% y/y** (July), with the energy component still carrying Middle East supply-shock premium.
- **Unemployment ~4.1%** — a labor market stable enough to absorb tightening.
- **August payrolls +162,000**, with July revised from negative to positive. This was the print (September 3) that reignited hike pricing and knocked 260+ points off the Dow.
- FactSet has S&P 500 Q2 2026 earnings growth at **+50.4%**, the strongest since Q2 2021 — which is fundamentally supportive for equities but removes the "weak economy" argument against tightening.

**The complication:** July CPI was *cooler* on the core, yet the Fed appears set to hike anyway. That tells you the committee is reacting to the *oil-driven inflation path*, not the trailing core print. Which means the September decision is now partly a function of the Strait of Hormuz.

**What to watch this week:** August CPI is the gating event ahead of the September 15–16 FOMC. A headline print that confirms energy pass-through into services would make the hike a near-certainty and put the 10-year through 5%. Earnings flow is moderate — 29 companies reported today, 33 Wednesday, 35 Thursday.

## Geopolitical Factors

The Middle East is no longer a background risk premium; it is the marginal driver of the US rates market.

- **Oil has advanced three straight sessions** (five, on a broader count), with Brent near six-week highs, on fears of a *prolonged* rather than episodic conflict. Iran has threatened retaliation against further US action against its assets.
- **The Strait of Hormuz is the transmission mechanism.** Recent US–Iran exchanges of strikes in and around the strait have traders building a durable — not tactical — risk premium.
- **Goldman Sachs has raised forecasts** by $5, to **$85 Brent / $80 WTI for December 2026** and **$80/$75 for 2027**, explicitly assuming Middle East shipping disruption persists into 2027. Note that spot ($97 Brent) trades well *above* those forecasts, implying either the street expects mean reversion or the strip is carrying a large conflict premium that can deflate violently.
- **The Bank of England has flagged geopolitical risk as the single biggest threat to global markets** — a notable framing from a central bank.
- Late August saw the Dow drop 370 and then 400+ points on separate US strikes against Iran, so the equity market's beta to this headline flow is established and roughly -0.7% per escalation event.

**The asymmetry to understand:** a de-escalation headline would simultaneously cut oil, cut yields, and remove the Fed's hike rationale. That is a three-way tailwind, and it argues against being maximally short duration or maximally long energy here. Position for the path, not the prediction.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: Industrials, Energy, Materials & "Picks-and-Shovels"

*(Rotating spotlight bucket 5 — day-of-year 251, 251 mod 6 = 5.)*

This is where the AI trade has migrated, and it is not adequately priced as such. The clearest structural bottleneck in the entire technology economy right now is not GPUs. It is **electrons and the equipment that moves them.**

**The power arithmetic is brutal and specific:**

- US data center power demand: **31 GW (2025) → 41 GW (2026) → 66 GW (2027)**. That is a doubling in two years.
- The five largest US cloud/AI infrastructure providers (Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Oracle) have committed **$660–690 billion of 2026 capex**, nearly double 2025 levels.
- Data center natural gas demand may reach **6.1 Bcf/d by 2030**, a ~20% increase on recent annual average powerburn.

**And the supply side cannot respond:**

- Global gas turbine orders have reached the equivalent of **110 GW** against global manufacturing capacity of only **60–70 GW per year**.
- Wait times for large frame turbines (e.g., the M501JAC class) have stretched to as long as **seven years**; the average is around five.
- Turbine pricing is forecast to hit **$600/kW by end-2027 — a 195% increase from 2019.** New orders command **10–20% premiums** over existing backlog.
- Gas power plant construction costs are up **66%** on data center demand.
- Developers have announced roughly **101 GW of on-site natural gas generation** purely to bypass grid interconnection queues.

**Why this matters for how you own the theme:** when a bottleneck is this severe and this long-dated, pricing power accrues to whoever holds the backlog. Backlog *is* the moat. This is the cleanest "picks-and-shovels" setup available in public equities, and it sits in sectors that still trade at a discount to the S&P.

**The evidence in company backlogs is already emphatic:**

| Company | Backlog signal |
|---|---|
| Powell Industries (POWL) | Backlog **$2.4B** at Jun 30, **+35% from $1.8B one quarter earlier**; Q3 FY26 bookings **+158% y/y** to $934.2M |
| Primoris Services (PRIM) | Total backlog **$13.9B** (Utilities $7.7B, Energy $6.2B), up $1.9B from year-end 2025 |
| Argan (AGX) | **$2.8B** project backlog, including $2.5B added in FY26 across three US gas-fired plants totaling >3.4 GW |

**Where I part company with the crowd:** the market has already found POWL and AGX. Powell trades at ~34.7x trailing / ~28.1x forward earnings on a $6.6B cap. Argan trades at **~35–38x forward and ~25x EV/EBITDA** against a construction-industry median forward P/E of **12.7x** — that is 174% above its industry median. Both are excellent businesses; neither is a *valuation-based* purchase today. The discipline this report is built on says you buy the same theme where the multiple has not yet re-rated: **the fuel (gas E&P), the equipment installer with a fixed-price backlog (E&C), and the metal that every one of those substations and transformers consumes (copper).** That is where today's picks are concentrated.

**Materials — the copper leg.** Copper demand is robust while industry supply growth is constrained, which is the textbook setup for a sustained cost-curve squeeze. Freeport-McMoRan is expected to improve sequentially through 2026 toward ~$3.7B quarterly EBITDA as Indonesian production recovers. Among mid-caps, Hudbay (HBM) has just de-risked its Copper World development through a **$600M Mitsubishi JV for 30%**, and Ero Copper screens as the most undervalued on pure valuation ratings.

**Energy — the fuel leg.** Antero Resources screens ~29% undervalued against a $52 fair value with over half of 2026 volumes hedged; EQT trades at a ~24% discount to a $70 fair value. Baker Hughes screens ~12% undervalued at a $73 fair value estimate, and is the rare oilfield-services name with a second engine — Industrial & Energy Technology — pointed directly at the gas turbine and LNG buildout.

### Artificial Intelligence

The financing structures in AI infrastructure have crossed into territory that deserves scrutiny rather than applause.

- **Nvidia is backing $105 billion of financing** for an OpenAI data center in Ohio (PORTS-Pike Technology Campus, Pike County), built and managed by SB Energy on a 20-year lease to OpenAI, supporting an initial **4.25 GW** with an option for a further 3.75 GW.
- Separately, Nvidia and OpenAI signed an LOI for **at least 10 GW** of AI data centers with **up to $100 billion of progressive Nvidia investment** as systems deploy; the first gigawatt is expected online in **2H 2026** on the Vera Rubin platform.
- Talks have been reported for a **backstop facility of up to $250 billion**.
- **OpenAI–Broadcom**: 10 GW of custom AI accelerator hardware, deploying from 2026 through 2029, with the FT estimating a total cost of **$350–500 billion**.

**The analytical point:** a supplier financing its largest customer's purchases of its own product is vendor financing, and vendor financing is how capex cycles extend past the point where end-demand justifies them. This does not mean the AI buildout is fake — the power and memory bottlenecks are real, physical, and independently corroborated. It does mean the *equity risk* is increasingly concentrated in the financing chain rather than in demand. Owning the bottleneck (turbines, switchgear, copper, gas) is a materially different risk profile from owning the vendor-financed accelerator.

### 1. AR — Antero Resources Corporation
*Sub-sector: Natural gas E&P (Appalachia / LNG-levered)*

- **Thesis:** The single cheapest way to own the AI power buildout. Data center gas demand is heading to ~6.1 Bcf/d by 2030 while ~101 GW of announced on-site gas generation bypasses grid queues — and Antero pipes **75% of its gas into the LNG corridor** with roughly two-thirds of production linked to Gulf Coast pricing, so it captures both the domestic powerburn bid and export realizations. You are buying the fuel for the turbine shortage at single-digit forward earnings.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Trailing P/E **~11.3x** (vs. oil & gas industry ~14.3x and a wider peer group near 27.2x); **NTM P/E ~8.5x**; DCF-implied intrinsic value cited as high as **~$124.84/share** against a recent **$33.63** — I regard that particular DCF as unrealistically aggressive, but even haircutting it by half leaves a wide margin of safety. Morningstar-style fair value **$52** implies ~29% undervaluation.
- **Growth Profile:** Projected **2026 free cash flow of ~$1.71 billion** at current strip (~$1.47B on a more conservative DCF path, rising to ~$1.64B by 2030). NGLs and condensate are ~35% of production, adding ethane/propane/butane price torque on top of dry gas.
- **Competitive Moat:** The transportation portfolio is the asset. Firm capacity into the LNG corridor is not replicable at today's permitting reality — a competitor with equivalent acreage but Appalachian basis exposure earns materially less per Mcf. Over half of 2026 volumes are hedged, which converts the thesis from a gas-price bet into a cash-flow-delivery bet.
- **Key Risk:** A Middle East de-escalation that collapses the energy complex, combined with a warm winter, would compress both gas prices and the LNG arbitrage simultaneously. Hedges cushion 2026 but not 2027.
- **Rating:** **Strong Buy**

### 2. PRIM — Primoris Services Corporation
*Sub-sector: Utility & energy engineering / construction (power delivery)*

- **Thesis:** The company that physically builds the transmission, substation and distribution assets the data center boom requires, trading at roughly half its industry's multiple after a renewables-driven guidance stumble that hit the stock ~25% in 2026. **$13.9B total backlog** — $7.7B Utilities, $6.2B Energy — provides multi-year revenue visibility that the current multiple does not reflect.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Trailing P/E **~20.3x** against a construction industry average near **45.4x** and a peer group near **43.0x**; forward P/E **~19.2x** (some models show ~30x on depressed near-term EPS, which is the disagreement worth exploiting). DCF intrinsic value **~$126/share**, implying a **~26.2% discount**. Modeling 6.7% revenue growth at 5.3% operating margins on an 18x exit multiple gets to $124 within 2.5 years — i.e., the base case does not require multiple expansion.
- **Growth Profile:** Utilities segment revenue **+12.3%**, with Power Delivery posting double-digit margin growth; Q1 2026 revenue $1.6B; MSA backlog up ~$700M sequentially on power-delivery activity. Backlog additions in Energy include natural gas power generation, industrial and electrical construction, plus the PayneCrest contribution.
- **Competitive Moat:** Master service agreements with utilities are sticky, relationship-based, and effectively impossible for a new entrant to win at scale — utilities do not experiment with unproven contractors on grid-critical work. The skilled-labor constraint that limits industry capacity also protects incumbents holding the crews.
- **Key Risk:** Fixed-price project execution. The weak Q1 and lowered profit guidance came from renewables project margins, and E&C companies destroy value fast when they misprice a large fixed-scope job. Watch mix shift toward MSA/cost-plus work as the tell.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

### 3. HBM — Hudbay Minerals Inc.
*Sub-sector: Copper & gold mining (mid-tier producer with development optionality)*

- **Thesis:** Every substation, transformer, busway and switchgear cabinet in the power buildout is a copper order, and industry supply growth is constrained while demand is robust. Hudbay has just removed the funding and permitting risk from its Copper World growth engine via a **$600M Mitsubishi investment for 30%** of the JV — converting a speculative development story into a funded production ramp.
- **Valuation Metrics:** **~15x trailing earnings** on the constructive read (a headline P/E of ~21.3x on other calculations, with P/S 4.64 and P/B 3.15 near historical highs — I flag that disagreement rather than hide it). Balance sheet is clean. **EBITDA margin 60.87%**, operating margin 23.99%, net margin 22.17%. HudBay peers at ~CA$5.0B cap have been valued near **0.7× NAV** with low cash-flow multiples relative to growth.
- **Growth Profile:** Record trailing-12-month adjusted EBITDA of **$1.3 billion** and record quarterly revenue in Q1 2026. Copper output to **~124,000 tonnes in 2026 (+5%)** as British Columbia mill throughput reaches 50,000 tpd in 2H26. Copper World adds **85,000 tonnes annually over an initial 20-year life** — a **>50% increase in annual copper output** — at a JV-adjusted project IRR to Hudbay of approximately **90%** on pre-feasibility estimates. With the Arizona Sonoran acquisition, the combined district targets **>250,000 tonnes by 2030** and 350,000+ longer term.
- **Competitive Moat:** Permitted, funded, large-scale copper in Arizona. Copper World holds its final permit — in a jurisdiction where new US copper permitting takes a decade or more, the permit itself is the moat, and it is not reproducible by a competitor with capital alone.
- **Key Risk:** Copper price beta plus development execution. A global growth scare would hit realized prices and the growth multiple at once, and mid-tier miners have a long history of capex overruns on ramp. The Mitsubishi JV mitigates funding risk, not construction risk.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

### 4. BKR — Baker Hughes Company
*Sub-sector: Oilfield services & industrial energy technology (LNG / turbine equipment)*

- **Thesis:** The only large-cap way to own the gas turbine and LNG equipment bottleneck without paying an electrical-equipment multiple. The Industrial & Energy Technology segment sells directly into the LNG liquefaction and power-generation buildout — where global turbine orders (110 GW) exceed manufacturing capacity (60–70 GW/yr) and new orders carry **10–20% price premiums** — while the oilfield services segment provides cash-generative ballast.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Trailing P/E **~21.3x** (~24.0x on an alternate calculation as of Aug 31); **EV/EBITDA 12.5x** (13.07x earlier in the year); EV/Revenue **2.2x**; market cap **~$62B** on EV **~$63B**. Morningstar-equivalent fair value **$73** implies **~12% undervaluation**. Note the forward P/E of **28.7x** looks worse than trailing — that reflects conservative consensus modeling of the equipment mix, and is the crux of the disagreement here. Consensus price target **$71.52**, "Buy."
- **Growth Profile:** TTM revenue **$27.89B** with **$3.12B of profit**. Revenue grew only **1%** last fiscal year but **EBITDA grew 19%** — that spread is the whole thesis: margin expansion from mix shift toward higher-value energy technology equipment, not volume.
- **Competitive Moat:** Installed base plus a genuine oligopoly. There are very few firms globally capable of delivering LNG-scale turbomachinery and compression, and multi-decade service contracts on the installed fleet generate annuity revenue that does not reprice with the oil cycle.
- **Key Risk:** Backlog conversion timing. Multi-year equipment awards can slip, and if the market decides Baker Hughes deserves an oilfield-services multiple rather than an industrial-technology one, the re-rating thesis fails even if operations deliver. The 28.7x forward multiple leaves less cushion than the trailing figure suggests — hence the rating below.
- **Rating:** **Accumulate**

### 5. AIR — AAR Corp.
*Sub-sector: Aviation aftermarket parts, repair & software (industrials — diversifying sub-sector)*

- **Thesis:** Deliberate diversification away from the power/AI-infrastructure cluster into a demand stream driven by an entirely different structural shortage: an aging global fleet plus new-aircraft delivery delays force operators to keep older airframes flying, and every extra flight hour on an old airframe is AAR revenue. Also a beneficiary of the defense sustainment cycle underpinning today's Lockheed upgrade.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Forward P/E **~22.2x** (~26x on the stock's recent $140.89, ~20x on trailing); **EV/EBITDA 17.22x**. Aerospace & defense industry average P/E is **16.6x**, so this is the priciest name in today's slate on a relative basis — the justification is the growth rate and margin trajectory below, and the rating reflects the reduced margin of safety.
- **Growth Profile:** FY2026 revenue **$3.31 billion, +18.97%** from $2.78B. Management targets **6–10% revenue growth**, **15% annual EPS growth** and **>13% EBITDA margins** over the next three years. Over the past five years the company compounded adjusted EBITDA at a **37% CAGR** while expanding EBITDA margins ~100bp annually.
- **Competitive Moat:** Parts distribution rights and regulatory certification. Winning an OEM distribution agreement or standing up an FAA/EASA-certified MRO facility takes years, and airlines will not risk AOG (aircraft-on-ground) events on an unproven supplier. Secular market-share gains in parts distribution are the observable evidence that the moat is widening.
- **Key Risk:** Valuation leaves limited room for disappointment — at ~20–22x on a 6–10% top-line grower, the entire thesis rests on delivering the margin-mix expansion. A commercial aviation demand shock (and $97 Brent is a jet-fuel problem) would compress both earnings and the multiple.
- **Rating:** **Accumulate**

## Risk Factors

**1. A September Fed hike into an oil-driven inflation print — severity: HIGH.**
Odds above 65% and rising. The market has spent four years positioned for a cutting cycle; a hike on September 16 forces a re-rating of every long-duration equity and would likely push the 10-year through 5%. Critically, the Fed would be tightening into *supply-side* inflation it cannot control, which raises the probability of a policy error in both directions.

**2. Strait of Hormuz escalation — severity: HIGH.**
Brent at $97 already embeds a large premium, yet Goldman's own December 2026 forecast is $85. Either the strip is wrong or the forecast is. An actual closure or sustained interdiction would take crude materially higher, guarantee the hike, and hit equities on both the earnings and discount-rate legs. Recent single-day Dow drops of 370 and 400+ points on strike headlines calibrate the sensitivity.

**3. AI capex financing concentration — severity: MEDIUM-HIGH.**
$660–690B of hyperscaler capex, up to $105B of Nvidia-provided financing for a single OpenAI facility, a reported $250B backstop discussion, and a $350–500B Broadcom program. The physical demand is real; the *financing structure* is increasingly circular. This is a slow-fuse risk, not a this-week risk, but it is the one that would end the cycle.

**4. High-multiple software and cyber compression — severity: MEDIUM.**
The sector has already re-rated hard on strong prints (Okta +29%, CrowdStrike's best day ever), and it was the weakest cohort in today's tape. With yields at 4.79% and a hike being priced, these are the most rate-sensitive equities in the market. Good businesses, vulnerable prices.

**5. Complacency — severity: MEDIUM.**
The VIX has been printing new year-to-date lows even as oil rises, yields hit multi-year highs and the Fed pivots hawkish. Technical trackers note the market "has yet to become unhinged, as is notorious for this time of year." September's seasonal reputation plus record-low implied volatility plus three simultaneous macro repricings is not a combination that rewards being unhedged.

## Bottom Line

The market is quietly telling you that the AI trade and the inflation trade are now the same trade, transmitted through the power complex — and it is still pricing them as if they are separate. Own the bottleneck rather than the vendor-financed accelerator: gas, grid construction, copper and energy-technology equipment offer the same secular demand at industrial multiples instead of technology ones, and today's Eaton and Lockheed upgrades suggest the sell side is arriving at the same conclusion. With a September hike above 65% odds, Brent at $97 and the VIX at year-to-date lows, this is a week to add exposure through the un-re-rated part of the chain and to buy volatility protection while it is cheap, not to chase what has already worked.
