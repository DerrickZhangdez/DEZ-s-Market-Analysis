## Macro & Monetary Environment

**Today's data: a labor market that refuses to crack.**

- **Initial jobless claims (week ended Aug 1): 199,000**, up 1,000 from the prior week's 197,000, and *below* the 202,000 economists polled by Reuters expected.
- **Continuing claims:** forecast ~1,790,000 against a prior 1,782,000.
- **Challenger job cuts:** July planned layoffs fell to a **two-year low**.
- **Nonfarm productivity (Q2 preliminary):** expected +0.6% to +0.7% versus +0.3% prior.

Claims have fallen considerably since spiking in early June — some of that decline is seasonal-adjustment noise around summer auto shutdowns, but the layoff data corroborates it. The striking part is that layoffs have stayed very low *despite* the oil price shock from the US–Israeli war with Iran. A labor market this tight, absorbing an energy shock without shedding workers, is the profile of an economy that can tolerate higher rates.

**The Fed.** The July 29 FOMC held the federal funds target at **3.50%–3.75%**, where it has sat since December, with three regional presidents dissenting *in favor of a hike*. June minutes recorded that participants "generally noted that inflation had increased further and remained well above the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective," and the June dot plot put the median funds rate at **3.8% by year-end 2026** — a projection that only resolves with a hike. Futures have been pricing roughly an **80% probability of a September increase**. The next meeting is **September 15–16**, and it carries a Summary of Economic Projections, which makes it the natural vehicle for a move.

**Rates.** The 10-year Treasury yield held around **4.62%** into today's data, up roughly 14 basis points over the past month and 39 basis points year over year. The 30-year has been trading near **5.21%**, its highest since 2007. A long end at these levels is the single most underappreciated headwind for the long-duration AI infrastructure trade — much of what is being underwritten in data center construction assumes a cost of capital that no longer exists.

**What to watch this week.** Friday's **July nonfarm payrolls** report is the main event and will effectively settle the September debate. Today's claims print raises the bar for a dovish surprise. After the close: **Airbnb** and **Lyft**. Before the open today: **Warner Bros. Discovery**.

## Geopolitical Factors

**Hormuz is the dominant macro variable, and it is resolving.** The conflict that began with US and Israeli strikes on Iran on February 28, 2026 escalated on March 4 when Iranian forces declared the Strait of Hormuz "closed," attacking and threatening vessels attempting transit. Roughly **20 million barrels per day** — about one-fifth of global petroleum liquids consumption and more than a quarter of global seaborne oil trade — normally moves through that chokepoint. A serious disruption removes 8–10 million bpd from world supply.

The diplomatic track has moved fast this week. A senior administration official said Tuesday that an agreement to restore shipping could land "today or tomorrow," and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC "there is a chance we may have a deal" within hours. Secretary of State Marco Rubio confirmed US involvement in Oman–Iran negotiations; Qatar, the mediator, said talks continue but that no direct Iran–US meetings are scheduled.

**Market impact.** Brent fell more than **6% to below $79** on the news, having traded above $80 through the crisis. Crude has now given back more than 10% in a handful of sessions. This is the engine under the Dow's record run: energy-shock relief flows directly into transport, industrials, consumer and the broad cyclical complex, none of which are in the Nasdaq's leadership cohort.

**The risk in the other direction.** The market is now positioned for a deal that has not been signed. Iranian forces have reopened and re-closed transit before; Qatar's own language ("no direct talks planned") is not the language of an imminent signature. A collapse in the talks reverses a 10% oil move and re-prices every cyclical that has rallied on it. The asymmetry has quietly become unfavorable.

**Second-order effects.** Higher energy, fertilizer, transport, bunker fuel and marine insurance costs feed food prices and cost-of-living pressure globally — which is precisely the channel through which the Iran war pushed US inflation further above target and put a September hike on the table. Hormuz reopening is therefore *disinflationary* and, paradoxically, one of the few developments that could still talk the Fed out of moving.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: AI Infrastructure & Data Centers

*(Rotation math: day-of-year 218; 218 mod 6 = 2 → power, cooling, networking, interconnect, memory.)*

**The demand side is not in question. The stock prices are.**

The capex numbers have gone parabolic. The five largest US cloud and AI infrastructure providers — Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Oracle — have committed to **$660–690 billion of capital expenditure in 2026**, close to double 2025 levels. Adding neoclouds (~$60B), China (~$80B), sovereign programs (~$60B), Oracle, Apple and others produces roughly **$1.04 trillion of compute capex in 2026 — the first trillion-dollar year in history**. Anthropic's Google Cloud commitment alone is reported at **$200 billion over five years for 5 GW**. Nvidia and OpenAI have been in talks over a backstop facility of up to **$250 billion**.

Against that, the equity market is doing something unusual: it is refusing to pay for it. The SOX is down ~20% from its late-June peak. AMD was punished for a beat. Sandisk fell 12% after posting a quarter with an **84.6% non-GAAP gross margin** and 372% revenue growth. Investors are asking a question they did not ask in 2024 or 2025 — *when does the revenue arrive to service this capital?* — and until that question has an answer, multiple expansion in this complex is over.

**The conclusion that drives today's picks.** The AI infrastructure trade has bifurcated into layers the market has decided are "AI stocks" — optics, cooling, liquid-cooled everything — which now carry 30–60x multiples, and layers that do exactly as much AI work but are still classified as industrials, REITs or hardware, which carry 18–24x. The second group is where the risk-adjusted return is. Every pick below sits in it.

**One structural caution.** Every layer above is levered to the same variable: hyperscaler capex growth. Diversifying across power, storage, real estate, interconnect and manufacturing diversifies *company* risk, not *cycle* risk. If 2027 hyperscaler capex guidance decelerates from +77% to +20%, all five picks fall together. Position sizing should reflect that, and the risk section below treats it as the market's top risk for a reason.

### Artificial Intelligence

The dominant AI story this week is not a model release but a financing question. Nvidia's discussions with OpenAI over a backstop of up to $250 billion, following a $100 billion partnership framework tied to 10 GW of Nvidia systems (of which roughly $30 billion was ultimately contributed to OpenAI's March round), have made vendor financing a live topic. When the largest supplier of AI compute is also underwriting its largest customer's ability to buy that compute, the revenue quality question the market is now asking is not unreasonable.

Competitive dynamics are also shifting on the silicon side: reports that China's DeepSeek is developing its own AI accelerator to reduce dependence on Nvidia and Huawei added to the week's pressure on chip names. The combination — customer concentration, vendor financing, and credible custom-silicon substitution — is why the AI complex is de-rating even as its order books grow.

### 1. CMI — Cummins Inc.
**Sub-sector: on-site power generation equipment (data center gensets)**

**Thesis.** Cummins' Power Systems segment has quietly become one of the purest data center backup- and prime-power franchises in the market, and the stock is still priced as a North American truck cyclical. Q2 delivered record revenue of **$9.5 billion (+9% YoY)** with Power Systems at **$2.0 billion, +19%**, and — more importantly — segment EBITDA of **$577 million at a 29.5% margin, up from 23.6%** a year ago. That is nearly 600 basis points of margin expansion in a segment the market underwrites at industrial multiples.

**Valuation metrics.** Price ~$650.25; market cap **$89.8B**. Trailing P/E **31.5x** (33.7x on some calculations). **Forward P/E 19.75x.** P/S ≈ **2.4x** on ~$37–38B of revenue. EV/EBITDA ≈ **14x** (derived: ~18.25% margin on guided revenue, EV ≈ $95B including net debt). **PEG ≈ 0.86** (19.75x forward against ~23% 2026 EPS growth).

**Growth profile.** 2026 revenue growth guidance raised to **10–13%** from 8–11%, with EBITDA margin of 18–18.5%. Consensus EPS growth of **+23% in 2026 and +16% in 2027**; next-fiscal-year consensus EPS **$29.33**; EPS forecast to grow ~62% over three years. Management has signed a multi-year hyperscaler agreement for backup gensets and a contract to supply roughly **2 GW of high-efficiency natural gas gensets to Circe Energy between 2026 and 2030** for a behind-the-meter microgrid at a West Texas AI campus.

**Competitive moat.** Emissions certification across jurisdictions is a multi-year regulatory barrier that new entrants cannot shortcut. The installed base generates a high-margin parts-and-service annuity that smooths the equipment cycle. In a market where gas turbine and genset lead times are the binding constraint on data center energization, owning certified capacity *is* the moat.

**Key risk.** The North American heavy truck cycle. Roughly two-thirds of Cummins is still engines and components for on-highway markets, and 2027 EPA emissions rules create prebuy-then-air-pocket dynamics that could mask Power Systems' progress for several quarters. Data center genset orders are also lumpy — a single deferred hyperscaler award moves a quarter. Insider selling has been notable.

**Rating: Buy.**

### 2. JBL — Jabil Inc.
**Sub-sector: AI infrastructure manufacturing & semiconductor capital equipment**

**Thesis.** Jabil is doing **$13.6 billion of AI-related revenue in fiscal 2026 — up 50%** from $9 billion — and the market still values it as a low-margin contract manufacturer. Intelligent Infrastructure is now **49% of total revenue and grew 52%**, which means the mix question has already been settled; only the multiple has yet to catch up.

**Valuation metrics.** FY26 guidance: revenue **~$35 billion**, core operating margin **~5.8%**, core EPS **~$12.70**, adjusted free cash flow **>$1.4 billion**. Forward P/E is quoted between **18.2x and 26x** across sources and dates — a genuinely wide band that reflects a 17% share price decline inside the quarter, and readers should treat the low end as stale. P/S is roughly **0.9x**, the lowest of any pick here. Analyst price targets have moved from ~$287 to ~$378.

**Growth profile.** Cloud & Data Center Infrastructure **$10.9B, +47%**; Capital Equipment **$3.0B, +20%**; Networking & Communications **$3.1B, +29%**. The FY26 revenue outlook was raised to ~$35B during the year. Free cash flow conversion is the underappreciated line: >$1.4B on a ~$30B market cap.

**Competitive moat.** Jabil's moat is not manufacturing cost — it is design integration. Once Jabil co-engineers a hyperscaler's rack-level power or liquid-cooling assembly and qualifies the line, requalifying a competitor costs quarters. Scale across 100+ sites also lets it absorb tariff and supply-chain routing shocks that sink smaller EMS players. The Capital Equipment segment adds semicap exposure with a different cycle.

**Key risk.** The margin structure is genuinely thin — 5.8% core operating margin means a 100bp mix or pricing error erases a fifth of operating profit. Customer concentration in the AI segment is high, and EMS is historically a business where customers insource once volumes justify it. If a top hyperscaler brings rack integration in-house, the AI revenue line does not degrade gradually; it steps down.

**Rating: Buy.**

### 3. NTAP — NetApp, Inc.
**Sub-sector: enterprise storage systems & data management software**

**Thesis.** Every enterprise AI deployment needs a data layer that sits outside the hyperscaler, and NetApp's ONTAP is the default answer for the installed base. The stock trades at **18.9x forward earnings** — a small premium to a 15.6x industry average — for a business with a **20.3% free cash flow margin** and record all-flash array sales.

**Valuation metrics.** Price ~$167.80; market cap ≈ **$33B**. **Forward P/E 18.9x** (a second source cites 19.6x). P/S ≈ **4.7x** on roughly $6.8–7.0B of revenue. EV/EBITDA is not cleanly available in today's sources and is deliberately not estimated here. PEG is the weak spot: on high-single-digit to low-teens earnings growth, PEG sits near **1.5**, which is why this is an Accumulate rather than a Buy.

**Growth profile.** Q3 FY26 EPS of **$2.12** beat the $2.07 consensus and rose from $1.91 a year earlier. Revenue and earnings both grew, led by record all-flash arrays plus cloud storage and enterprise AI services. New Cisco and Splunk partnerships extend the attach motion. The stock has returned **+54.7% over 90 days** and **+62.6% over one year** — the re-rating has begun, which is the main argument against waiting.

**Competitive moat.** ONTAP is an operating system with three decades of installed base, and storage operating systems are among the stickiest software artifacts in enterprise IT — migration means revalidating every backup, snapshot and compliance workflow. Certified NVIDIA reference architectures give NetApp a specified position in enterprise AI build-outs that competitors have to displace rather than merely underprice.

**Key risk.** NAND cost inflation. The same memory shortage making Sandisk and Micron print record margins is a direct input cost for NetApp's all-flash arrays, and NetApp cannot pass it through as fast as suppliers can raise it — this is the mechanism most likely to compress gross margin over the next two quarters. Secondarily, on-premises enterprise storage remains a share-donating category to hyperscaler-native services over long horizons.

**Rating: Accumulate.**

### 4. EQIX — Equinix, Inc.
**Sub-sector: colocation & interconnection real estate**

**Thesis.** Equinix delivered the **largest single guidance raise in company history** for 2026 and Q2 AFFO per share grew **18% year over year** on a normalized constant-currency basis, yet the stock trades at a forward multiple that has compressed toward the low end of its decade range while every private data center asset trades at a premium.

**Valuation metrics.** **Forward P/E 23.4x** (versus a 16.1x sector average, which is the wrong comparison — the sector average is dominated by low-growth REITs). **Price/AFFO ≈ 25.4x**, which is expensive relative to Equinix's own history and to peers on a trailing four-year AFFO CAGR of +6.8% — and cheap relative to the 10–12% AFFO growth now being guided. Market cap ≈ **$80–85B**. Fitch investment-grade rated with covered dividend.

**Growth profile.** 2026 guidance: revenue **+11% to +12%**, AFFO per share **+10% to +12%**. Q2 AFFO/share **+18%** normalized ccy. The acceleration from a 6.8% historical AFFO CAGR to 10–12% guided is the entire thesis: this is a company whose growth rate is inflecting while its multiple assumes the old rate.

**Competitive moat.** Interconnection density. Platform Equinix's value is not floor space but the ~10,000 networks and clouds already terminated inside it — every new tenant makes the next tenant's decision easier, and no amount of capital reproduces a twenty-year accumulation of cross-connects in Ashburn, Slough or Singapore. Metro locations with power allocations are effectively unrepeatable in the current permitting environment.

**Key risk.** Capital intensity in a 5.2% long-bond world. Equinix funds expansion with debt and equity against assets underwritten at a lower cost of capital; if the 30-year stays above 5%, the spread between development yield and funding cost narrows and the growth inflection stalls. Power availability in core metros is the physical version of the same constraint.

**Rating: Buy.**

### 5. APH — Amphenol Corporation
**Sub-sector: high-speed electrical & electronic interconnect components**

**Thesis.** Amphenol is compounding earnings at 67% while trading at roughly 30x forward — a PEG near 0.6 — because the market is anchored on its trailing 44x multiple rather than the fact that IT datacom has become the largest and fastest-growing thing it does.

**Valuation metrics.** Q2 2026 record revenue **$8.8 billion**; adjusted diluted EPS **$1.35, +67%**. **Forward P/E ~29–34x** depending on source and date; trailing P/E ~44x, roughly 50% above its historical average. **PEG ≈ 0.6** against near-term growth of ~50%; note that consensus long-run estimates are far more conservative (earnings +18.3%/yr, revenue +12.9%/yr), on which PEG rises toward 1.7. Both figures are given deliberately — the gap between them *is* the debate on this stock.

**Growth profile.** IT datacom sales **+63% organically** in Q2 and **+99% YoY (+81% organic)** in Q1, when the segment passed **40% of total company revenue**. Q3 guidance: revenue **$9.3–9.4B**, adjusted EPS **$1.40–1.42**, implying ~50–53% YoY growth, with management guiding IT datacom to mid-teens *sequential* growth. Q2 orders surged 94%. The CommScope connectivity acquisition outlook was raised.

**Competitive moat.** Amphenol's advantage is portfolio breadth plus decentralized operating structure: roughly 100 largely autonomous business units let it design-in at hyperscalers across dozens of connector, cable and antenna categories simultaneously, and its three-decade M&A record converts adjacent niches into share. High-speed interconnect at 224G is a qualification-gated business where incumbency compounds.

**Key risk.** Concentration. IT datacom above 40% of revenue means Amphenol is now, functionally, a hyperscaler capex derivative wearing an industrial's clothing. The trailing multiple leaves no room for the deceleration that a 2027 capex digestion year would bring, and a move from 30x to a historical ~22x is a 27% de-rating even if estimates hold. This is the highest-beta name on the list and is sized accordingly at Accumulate.

**Rating: Accumulate.**

**Named and deliberately not picked, on valuation rather than rotation:** COHR (~31–36x forward after +424% in 52 weeks), CIEN (45x+ forward after ~+500%), AAON (~58x forward), AGX (~52x forward), POWL (~49x forward), FLEX (~40x forward), GLW (~42x forward), CRDO and ALAB (no reasonable entry), DY and MTZ (fair at ~26x and ~25x but overlapping July's PWR/EME picks), CSCO (~28x on non-GAAP forward earnings, roughly double its historical multiple). **WDC, STX and SNDK** were the hardest exclusions: Western Digital's 2026 HDD capacity is 100% sold out with 89% of revenue from hyperscaler AI cloud, and Sandisk just printed an 84.6% gross margin — but both multiples are computed on peak-cycle earnings, the available price and forward-EPS data for WDC conflicted materially across sources, and this report will not underwrite a cyclical at the top of its margin cycle on data it cannot reconcile. **NRG** was excluded because its own 2026 adjusted EPS guidance range ($7.90–$9.90) is too wide to underwrite despite a genuinely attractive setup after Wednesday's 9% decline.

No re-inclusions were needed and the one-name catalyst exception was not invoked.

## Risk Factors

**1. A September Fed hike — severity: high, probability: high.** Futures have priced roughly an 80% chance of an increase, three FOMC members already dissented for one in July, and the June dot plot's 3.8% year-end median only reconciles with a move. Today's 199,000 claims print and a two-year low in July layoffs remove the last soft-landing argument against it. A 30-year at 5.21% is already the highest since 2007; equity multiples in every long-duration category — which now includes all of AI infrastructure — are being discounted at rates the 2024–2025 buildout did not assume.

**2. AI capex digestion — severity: high, probability: medium.** The SOX has shed ~20% and more than $1 trillion in value from its June peak while orders keep setting records. That divergence resolves one of two ways. Watch Q3 hyperscaler guidance for the first sign of 2027 capex growth decelerating from the current +77% pace; every name in today's spotlight, including all five picks, is correlated to that single variable.

**3. Hormuz deal failure — severity: high, probability: low-to-medium.** The market has fully priced an agreement that is not signed. Qatar says no direct Iran–US talks are scheduled. A collapse reverses a >10% oil decline, re-inflates the CPI path the Fed is already unhappy with, and unwinds the cyclical rally carrying the Dow to records.

**4. Friday's payrolls print — severity: medium-high, probability: certain.** July nonfarm payrolls lands tomorrow into a market that has already absorbed a firm claims number. A hot print effectively confirms September; a sharply weak one contradicts today's claims and layoff data and creates its own volatility. There is no comfortable version of this release.

**5. The SaaS repricing spreading — severity: medium, probability: medium.** Two trillion dollars of software value is gone, Microsoft is down 25% year to date, and the disruption thesis has not yet been tested against a full guidance cycle. If AI agents demonstrably compress seat counts at one large-cap incumbent, the de-rating extends into adjacent categories — including the data management and infrastructure software layers that support two of today's picks.

## Bottom Line

The Dow is making records for a reason that has nothing to do with AI: oil is collapsing on a Hormuz deal and the cyclical economy is being repriced upward, while the AI complex de-rates for a third straight session on a question — where is the return on a trillion dollars of compute capex? — that will not be answered this quarter. That divergence has left the physical infrastructure of AI split into layers the market pays 30–60x for and layers doing identical work at 18–24x. Own the second group, size for the fact that all of it shares one cycle, and treat Friday's payrolls print as the event that decides whether the discount rate underneath all of it goes higher in September.
