## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The Fed is not where consensus was six months ago.** The target range has sat at **3.50%–3.75% since December 2025**. The July meeting held rates by a **9–3 vote**, with Hammack (Cleveland), Kashkari (Minneapolis) and Logan (Dallas) all dissenting *in favor of a 25bp hike* to 3.75%–4.00%. That is an unusually hawkish dissent bloc, and it materially changes the distribution of outcomes for **September 16**, when the Committee also publishes updated projections (the dot plot).

**Inflation is the binding constraint.** Headline cooled to **3.5%** in June and core sits at **2.6%** — both above the 2% objective, and both now facing an energy-driven impulse that did not exist when the July decision was taken. A sustained $100 Brent feeds directly into headline CPI and, with a lag, into core services via transport and logistics. The Fed's own minutes noted inflation had "increased further and remained well above" target.

**Rates are doing the tightening.** The 10-year at **4.796%** is near a two-decade high. This matters more for equity valuation than the funds rate itself: long-duration cash flows — precisely the profile of every AI infrastructure and high-multiple software name — get repriced off the long end. A chip stock at 100x trailing earnings is a duration instrument.

**What to watch this week:** the September 16 FOMC decision and dot plot; any CPI print landing before it; and hyperscaler commentary on **2027** capital plans, which has become the market's proxy for AI-capex durability.

## Geopolitical Factors

**Iran / Strait of Hormuz — severity: high.** The US and Iran are actively striking each other's military and commercial vessels. Iran-backed Houthi militants claimed responsibility for attacks that halted operations at Saudi energy facilities including the 400,000 bbl/d Jazan refinery. Brent hit a six-week high of ~$97.5–98 before testing $100.

**The shock absorbers are depleted.** Two factors have capped the spike so far: inventory draws and Chinese demand restraint. But the **US Strategic Petroleum Reserve has fallen below 290 million barrels — the lowest since 1982**. That is the critical detail. The buffer that has muted every oil scare since 2022 is largely spent, which means the *next* supply disruption transmits to price with far less damping. Investors should treat the current oil complacency (VIX at 15.6) as fragile.

**US–Canada trade war — severity: moderate but escalating.** Effective September 8, Canada enacted retaliatory tariffs of **15%–50% on over 700 American goods**, doubling duties on US steel and aluminum to 50% to mirror existing US tariffs, and adding levies on fish, cheese, smartphones, furniture, tools and appliances. Canada was the largest export market for US household appliances last year (>$1bn). This is a genuine earnings headwind for US industrials and consumer durables, and an underappreciated inflationary input at exactly the wrong moment for the Fed.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: Semiconductors & Semicap Equipment

*(Rotation: day-of-year 252 → 252 mod 6 = 0 → Semiconductors & semicap equipment.)*

This is the most interesting setup in the market right now, because two powerful facts are in direct conflict.

**Fact one: the spending cycle is inflecting violently upward.** Wafer fab equipment (WFE) forecasts have been revised up repeatedly and aggressively through 2026:

|---|---|---|
| Goldman Sachs | $150bn | +36% YoY |
| Semicap company collective guidance | $150–160bn | ~+30% YoY |
| Counterpoint Research | $159bn | — |
| SEMI | $143.9bn | +23.1% |
| Lam Research | $140bn | — |
| Morgan Stanley (revised) | $128.3bn | +10% (raised from +5%) |

SEMI projects **total** semiconductor equipment sales at a record **$139bn in 2026**, rising to $156bn in 2027 and $229bn by 2028. Foundry/logic WFE alone is seen +18.9% to $78.0bn. The drivers are concrete rather than speculative: DRAM and leading-edge foundry near-term, NAND and logic in the medium term, plus GAA (gate-all-around) transistor adoption, HBM4 ramp, and advanced packaging. Memory is the swing factor — the memory segment is projected above $800bn in 2026.

**Fact two: the stocks have already been repriced — twice, in both directions.** The SOX surged more than 100% to a record high in late June, then **fell as much as 29% from that peak**. The proximate causes were a memory-glut panic, questions over whether AI capex can compound at the current rate, and — critically — rising interest rates compressing the multiples of the most expensive names first.

**The synthesis, and how I am playing it.** A 29% drawdown against a 30%+ spending upcycle is exactly the kind of dislocation that produces returns — but *only* where the drawdown has outrun the fundamentals. It has not done so uniformly. Screening the space today, most of the obvious semicap names remain expensive even after the fall:

- **Entegris (ENTG)** — trades ~80x trailing, ~49x forward against ~5% Q1 revenue growth and a ~7% forward growth assumption. Against a semiconductor industry average forward P/E of ~26x, this is a premium unsupported by the growth profile. **Coverage only — not a pick.**
- **Onto Innovation (ONTO)** — genuinely excellent business (metrology/inspection, >30% 2026 revenue growth guided, Dragonfly G5 traction, Semilab acquisition), but at **~120x P/E** versus a peer average of 77x and a "fair ratio" near 59x, the price embeds perfection. **Coverage only — not a pick.**
- **Nova Ltd (NVMI)** — 43–56x forward depending on measurement date, ~15% forecast growth against a 22% industry forecast, and unpriced Israeli geopolitical exposure. **Coverage only — not a pick.**
- **FormFactor (FORM)** — the HBM4 probe-card thesis is real and structurally attractive (higher layer counts and doubled pin counts wear probe cards faster, shortening replacement cycles), but the stock ran ~226% in twelve months to an all-time high of $107.50. **Coverage only — not a pick.**
- **Axcelis (ACLS)** — ~47.6x P/E against management's *mid-single-digit* full-year 2026 revenue growth guidance. The pending Veeco merger (expected to close 2H26, pending China regulatory approval) and the DRAM/HBM implant recovery are legitimate catalysts, but 47x for mid-single-digit growth is not a valuation case today. **Coverage only — not a pick.**

The names that survive a valuation screen are in the *unglamorous* parts of the chain — test, packaging, mature-node foundry, analog, and IP licensing. That is where all five of today's picks sit. Notably, the sub-sectors that have de-rated hardest are not the ones with the weakest fundamentals; they are the ones with the least retail narrative attached.

**A structural point worth internalizing:** AI chip manufacturing requires more process-control steps per wafer than any prior generation. Every additional layer requires inspection, metrology and test. This makes *test intensity* — not unit volume — the compounding variable. It is why Teradyne's semiconductor test segment broke $1bn in a quarter for the first time with >100% YoY growth, and why probe cards and advanced packaging are structurally advantaged even in a flat unit environment.

**The market is no longer applauding.** Alphabet, Amazon and Microsoft all sold off following their earnings calls. That is a defensible strategy if the demand curve holds and a catastrophic one if it does not, and the equity market has begun pricing genuine uncertainty about which. This is the single most important open question in the market, and it is why "2027 capex guidance" has become the phrase that moves chip stocks.

### Hardware & Semiconductors

Covered in depth in the Spotlight above. The one addition worth making concerns **memory**. The "memory glut" panic that triggered the August sector drawdown sits awkwardly against a memory segment projected above $800bn in 2026 on HBM demand. Both can be true: HBM is capacity-constrained and priced accordingly, while conventional DRAM/NAND face periodic oversupply as capacity converts. The market has been treating these as one trade. They are not, and the conflation is what created the entry point in test and packaging names whose revenue is levered to HBM *test intensity* rather than to commodity bit pricing.

### Software & SaaS

The defining equity story of 2026 outside of oil. The iShares software ETF (**IGV**) is **down over 21% YTD**, with roughly **$2 trillion in market cap erased**. Salesforce is down over 30% for the year; Workday down 33%. For the first time ever, **software trades at or below a market multiple** — a historic inversion for a sector that has commanded a premium for two decades.

The bear case is specific rather than vague: AI agents capable of executing multi-step business workflows threaten the per-seat licensing model that underpins SaaS economics. Roughly **40% of IT budgets are being reallocated** from traditional SaaS subscriptions toward agentic platforms and LLM token consumption.

J.P. Morgan has argued the selloff has gone too far and that the disruption fears rest on "broken logic." The more useful observation is that what is actually occurring is **dispersion, not uniform de-rating** — some software names have clawed back double-digit gains since the trough while others remain down 60%+ from their highs. A sector trading below the market multiple with genuine terminal-value uncertainty is not automatically cheap; it requires name-level discrimination about which business models are agent-proof. I am not making a software pick today on that basis — the research required to distinguish survivors from value traps has not been done to the standard these picks demand.

### 1. TER — Teradyne, Inc. *(Automated test equipment — semiconductor & memory test)*

**Thesis.** Teradyne fell ~31% over the past month in a sector-wide memory-glut panic with, in the words of the post-mortems, "no earnings miss, no guidance cut, no lost customer" — while management guides full-year 2026 to **$6bn revenue and $9.50–$11.00 EPS** against $3.48 last year. You are buying a near-tripling of earnings at a compressed multiple because the sector sold off around it.

**Valuation Metrics.** Trailing P/E ~109x (reflecting the trough earnings base, not the run rate) · Forward P/E **~29–33x** on the FY26 EPS guide · FY27 consensus EPS ~$7.05 versus $3.48 last year *(note: the FY27 figure and the FY26 company guide come from different sources and are not directly comparable — treat the direction, not the precision, as the signal)* · P/S ~8x on the $6bn target · EV/EBITDA and PEG not verified to a primary source in today's research. On FY26 guidance the PEG is comfortably below 1.

**Growth Profile.** Q1 2026 revenue $1,282mn, of which Semiconductor Test $1,111mn — the first $1bn+ test quarter ever, **+26% sequentially and >100% YoY**. GAAP net income $398.9mn ($2.53/diluted share); non-GAAP $402.9mn ($2.56). AI-related demand reached **~70% of revenues** in Q1, up from ~60% the prior quarter.

**Competitive Moat.** Test is a duopoly (Teradyne and Advantest) with brutal switching costs — test programs are co-developed with the customer's silicon and requalifying a tester means requalifying the device. HBM and DRAM test demand is structurally rising because test intensity scales with layer count and pin count, not wafer starts. The Universal Robots/robotics segment provides an uncorrelated second leg.

**Key Risk.** Teradyne is a pure-play levered bet on AI capex continuing. Baird's downgrade to Neutral named exactly this: sustainability of hyperscaler capex growth, plus rising rates. If 2027 hyperscaler guidance disappoints, this falls further and faster than the market. The trailing multiple offers no cushion.

**Rating: Buy.**

### 2. AMKR — Amkor Technology, Inc. *(OSAT — outsourced assembly & advanced packaging)*

**Thesis.** Advanced packaging is the genuine bottleneck in AI silicon, and Amkor is the largest US-listed pure play — yet it trades at **~2.35x forward revenue against 27.5% YoY growth**, a multiple that would be unremarkable for a business growing half as fast. Full-year advanced packaging revenue is projected to **roughly triple in 2026**.

**Valuation Metrics.** Forward P/S **~2.35x** · 2026 consensus EPS **$2.08, +38.67% YoY** · implied forward P/E in the low-to-mid 30s at recent prices · trailing P/E, EV/EBITDA and PEG not verified to a primary source. On the consensus EPS growth rate the PEG sits below 1.

**Growth Profile.** Q2 2026 revenue **+26% YoY** with growth across all end markets; advanced products alone generated **$1.56bn** in Q2, up from $1.23bn a year earlier. Q3 2026 guidance: net income $180–205mn, or $0.72–$0.82 per diluted share. Management targets **$9bn revenue and 17.5% gross margin by 2028**, scaling to $11bn and 22% by 2030.

**Competitive Moat.** Scale and qualification lock-in. OSAT capacity for leading-edge packaging takes years and enormous capital to build, and customers qualify packaging lines per-product. Amkor's Arizona facility gives it geographic advantage as onshoring accelerates. The margin trajectory in management's 2030 target — 17.5% to 22% — reflects genuine mix shift toward advanced packaging, not cost-cutting.

**Key Risk.** The stock is **up 119.7% YTD**. Much of the easy re-rating is behind it, and OSAT is a capital-intensive, historically thin-margin business — a 22% gross margin target is excellent *for Amkor* and mediocre in absolute terms. Customer concentration is meaningful, and any AI-packaging digestion phase hits Amkor's utilization directly.

**Rating: Buy.**

### 3. UMC — United Microelectronics Corporation *(Specialty & mature-node foundry)*

**Thesis.** The only genuinely cheap name in this report on conventional metrics — **14.29x trailing, 13.11x forward, 5.55x EV/EBITDA** — attached to a business quietly transitioning from commodity mature-node foundry to differentiated specialty capacity. This is the valuation anchor of the slate.

**Valuation Metrics.** Trailing P/E **14.29x** · Forward P/E **13.11x** · **EV/EBITDA 5.55x** · P/S and PEG not verified to a primary source. Against a semiconductor industry forward P/E averaging in the mid-20s, UMC trades at roughly half the sector multiple.

**Growth Profile.** Q1 revenue **+5.5%** with improved utilization; management guiding to higher ASPs and **gross margins approaching 30%**. **22nm revenue hit a record 14% of total** — the single most important operating metric here, because it evidences genuine mix improvement rather than cyclical volume.

**Competitive Moat.** Mature-node capacity is *contracting* industry-wide as competitors chase leading edge, creating scarcity value in 8-inch and 12-inch specialty nodes. UMC has pricing power in embedded high-voltage and power management, with AI-adjacent optionality in power delivery, display, networking, advanced packaging, silicon photonics and thin-film lithium niobate. An ongoing buyback supports the equity.

**Key Risk.** Mature-node foundry is the segment most exposed to **Chinese capacity additions** — SMIC and domestic Chinese foundries are adding trench and specialty capacity aggressively, and mature nodes are where price competition bites first. Taiwan-domicile geopolitical risk is real and unhedgeable. Single-digit revenue growth means the thesis rests on multiple re-rating and margin expansion, not compounding.

**Rating: Accumulate.**

### 4. MCHP — Microchip Technology Incorporated *(Analog, mixed-signal & embedded microcontrollers)*

**Thesis.** A textbook early-cycle recovery. Microchip took the **deepest inventory correction in the entire analog group** — closed a fab, cut pay across an 18,000-person workforce — and is now emerging with revenue up 35% and operating margin more than doubling. Buying an analog franchise as the cycle turns, with distributor inventory normalized, is one of the more repeatable setups in semiconductors.

**Valuation Metrics.** Recent price **~$99.77** · Q4 FY26 revenue **$1.311bn, +35.1% YoY** · non-GAAP operating margin **30.6%, up from 14.0%** a year earlier · Q1 FY27 guidance $1.442–1.469bn, **+35.3% at the midpoint**. Trailing and forward P/E, P/S, EV/EBITDA and PEG not verified to a primary source in today's research — and I would flag that trailing multiples on a company exiting a trough are actively misleading. The relevant valuation question is normalized earnings power at recovered margins, which sits well below the headline multiple.

**Growth Profile.** Revenue +35% YoY and guided +35% again. The operating leverage is the story: 14% → 30.6% operating margin on a 35% revenue increase, with historical peak margins meaningfully higher still. **Bookings in July were higher than any month in the last three years**, and distributor inventory has returned inside historical norms.

**Competitive Moat.** Extreme customer stickiness — 8/16/32-bit MCUs get designed into industrial and automotive products with 10–20 year lifecycles, and the switching cost is a full firmware rewrite plus requalification. Very long-tail catalog with ~100,000 customers and minimal concentration. The "Total System Solution" strategy bundles MCU, analog, FPGA and connectivity content per socket, raising dollar content per design win.

**Key Risk.** Analog cycles are cycles. The 35% growth figures are flattered by an extraordinarily depressed base, and extrapolating them is the classic error at this point in the recovery. Industrial and automotive end markets — Microchip's core — are precisely the demand pools most exposed to $100 oil, 4.8% long rates, and the US–Canada tariff escalation. One analyst rates it Hold and would prefer entry at $62–68, a view worth respecting: at ~$100 the recovery is substantially priced.

**Rating: Accumulate.**

### 5. RMBS — Rambus Inc. *(Semiconductor IP licensing & memory interface chips)*

**Thesis.** A royalty-and-IP business with **~80% gross margins** sitting directly in the HBM4/HBM4E path, growing earnings at a ~40% average annual rate while the broader semiconductor industry grows at 3.2%. Rambus monetizes memory bandwidth demand without carrying memory's commodity price risk — structurally the highest-quality business model in this report.

**Valuation Metrics.** **This is the one name where I found materially conflicting valuation data, and I am reporting both rather than the flattering one.** One source cites a forward multiple of **~24x**; another implies a **forward P/E of 64** alongside a $118.67 price target implying ~18% *downside*. The gap likely reflects GAAP versus non-GAAP treatment and product revenue versus total revenue including royalties, but I could not reconcile it to a primary source today. **Position accordingly** — the quality of the business is well-evidenced; the entry multiple is not. Balance sheet: **$786mn in cash**. Gross margin **79.73%**.

**Growth Profile.** Record Q2 FY2026: revenue **$207.4mn, +20% YoY and +15% sequentially** — the first quarter above $200mn. Q3 guidance $210–216mn. Management expects 2H26 stronger than 1H26 with favorable conditions potentially extending into 2027. Long-term growth projected at ~20% for FY2026.

**Competitive Moat.** Patent estate and standards-body position in memory interfaces — Rambus IP is embedded in the JEDEC standards themselves, so the royalty stream is levered to industry-wide memory shipments rather than to any single customer's share. The company announced the industry's leading **HBM4E Memory Controller IP**, supporting 16 Gbps/pin for 4.1 TB/s throughput per device. Each memory generation transition (HBM3E → HBM4 → HBM4E) is a re-licensing event at higher content.

**Key Risk.** Valuation ambiguity, as above — at 64x forward this is expensive; at 24x it is not, and I cannot resolve which with confidence today. Beyond that: licensing revenue is lumpy and renewal-dependent, memory-chip momentum has shown signs of weakening per at least one analyst, and insider selling was reported around the HBM4E launch. This is the smallest-conviction sizing of the five.

**Rating: Accumulate.**

## Risk Factors

**1. Oil supply shock with a depleted buffer — severity: critical.** Brent through $100 on active US–Iran naval exchanges and Houthi strikes on Saudi infrastructure, with the SPR below 290mn barrels, the lowest since 1982. The absorptive capacity that muted prior scares is largely spent. A Hormuz disruption from here transmits to price with minimal damping, and feeds directly into the inflation print the Fed is meeting on next week.

**2. A hawkish Fed surprise on September 16 — severity: high.** Three governors already voted to hike in July, before oil went to $100. The market is not positioned for a hike, and the dot plot lands the same day. With the 10-year at 4.796%, a hawkish dot plot repricing the long end would hit exactly the long-duration equities — semis at 30–120x, AI infrastructure — that dominate index weight.

**3. AI capex durability — severity: high.** Hyperscalers are spending **102% of cloud revenue** on capex, with $775–800bn committed for 2026 and UBS projecting $4.1tn across 2026–28. Every pick in this report, and much of the index, is downstream of that spend continuing. The market has already begun to discriminate — Alphabet, Amazon and Microsoft all sold off *on* their capex announcements. Watch 2027 guidance as the tell.

**4. The SaaS terminal-value repricing — severity: moderate-to-high.** IGV down 21% YTD, ~$2tn of market cap erased, software below a market multiple for the first time ever, and 40% of IT budgets reallocating toward agentic platforms. Whether this is dislocation or correct repricing of terminal value is genuinely unresolved, and it represents a large weight of the index.

**5. Trade-war escalation — severity: moderate.** Canada's 15–50% tariffs on 700+ US goods took effect September 8, with steel and aluminum duties doubled to 50%. Direct earnings pressure on US industrials and consumer durables, plus an inflationary contribution arriving at the least convenient moment for monetary policy.

## Bottom Line

The index level is being set by oil and the long bond, but the opportunity is in the wreckage of a 29% semiconductor drawdown that ran well ahead of a 30%-plus WFE spending upcycle. The discipline that matters today is refusing to buy the *narrative* corners of that trade — metrology and probe cards at 80–120x are not cheap merely because they fell — and buying instead the test, packaging, mature-node foundry, analog and IP franchises where the multiple is defensible against 2026 numbers rather than 2028 hopes. Position with the September 16 FOMC in mind: if the dots move hawkish, everything in this report gets cheaper before it gets dearer, and the deep-value anchor (UMC at 13x) is the one that will hurt least.
