## Macro & Monetary Environment

**This is a tightening cycle, not an easing one.** That single fact should govern position sizing more than any AI headline.

- **Policy rate: 3.75%–4.00%.** The FOMC raised by 25bp in September — the **first increase since 2023** — on a **unanimous vote**. Wells Fargo characterizes it as an inflation-risk-management hike rather than the automatic start of a series. The effective fed funds rate is reported at **3.88%**.
- **Inflation:** Core PCE has run **above 3% every month of 2026**. The July minutes describe inflation as elevated relative to the 2% objective, "in part reflecting price increases associated with supply shocks in certain sectors, including energy" — i.e., the Fed is explicitly naming the oil channel. Staff expect inflation to step down next year as tariff and Middle East effects wane, reaching ~2% in 2028.
- **The dot plot is hawkish:** **16 of 18** participants projected at least one additional increase this year; **four** saw room for two more. Longer-run expectations still converge toward ~**3.25%**.
- **Market pricing (as of Oct 8):** futures imply roughly **4.1% by January** and about **4.7% by October 2027**. iShares reads the market as pricing one more hike this year, to 4.00%–4.25%.
- **Next meeting: October 27–28.** Consensus expects a hold, which makes the intervening CPI and PCE prints the highest-leverage events on the calendar.
- **The rates channel is doing real work.** The 10-year yield jumped 6bp to **5.32%**, on track for its highest close since **April 2002**. The S&P has been range-bound for roughly a month, pressured by yields and energy, supported by AI — Thursday removed the support leg for a day and the range broke lower.

**What to watch:** CPI and PCE releases ahead of October 27–28; any Fed speakers walking back or confirming the "one more hike" framing; and the 10-year's behavior around 5.35%+. A 5%+ risk-free rate is the mathematical enemy of a 40x forward multiple, which is precisely why this report's picks skew toward sub-20x and sub-35x names with real earnings.

### Artificial Intelligence

The defining AI development of the week is **a sentiment event, not a fundamental one**. The $50B-vs-$70B OpenAI discrepancy stripped roughly 3.4% from the SOX and more than 1.25% from the Nasdaq in a session, then largely reversed overnight once the net-vs-gross distinction and the year-end $70B+ expectation were reported.

The deeper signal is **how much equity value is now levered to a single private company's disclosure cadence**. When OpenAI's unaudited annualized revenue figure can move Corning, Vistra, Oracle and Astera Labs by 5–10% in an afternoon, the market is expressing AI exposure through a chain of inference several steps removed from any contract. That fragility is itself a risk factor (see Risk Factors below), and it argues for owning AI exposure through **companies with diversified revenue and visible earnings** rather than through pure-play derivatives priced for perfection.

## Risk Factors

**1. Monetary policy — the Fed is still hiking into 3%+ core inflation. (Severity: Highest)**
With the policy rate at 3.75–4.00%, 16 of 18 dots projecting at least one more hike, and futures pricing ~4.7% by October 2027, the discount rate applied to every multiple in this report is still rising. A hot CPI or PCE print before the **October 27–28** meeting converts an expected hold into a live hike and compresses high-multiple semis first and hardest.

**2. The 10-year at 5.32% — a 24-year high in the risk-free rate. (Severity: High)**
This is the quiet, mechanical risk. At 5.3%, the present value of distant cash flows falls sharply, which is precisely why nine spotlight names were rejected on valuation today. Thursday's Nasdaq −1.25% versus Dow +0.10% is a preview of how this resolves if yields keep climbing: long-duration growth de-rates while cash-generative value holds.

**3. Iran / Strait of Hormuz escalation. (Severity: High, fat-tailed)**
The Dallas Fed's scenario work puts WTI as high as **$167** under a sustained Persian Gulf export halt; Allianz sees Brent above **$130** on full closure. With Morgan Stanley estimating ~0.35% added to headline CPI per 10% oil move, an escalation does not merely raise energy costs — it forces the Fed's hand. This is the main pathway by which Risk 1 and Risk 3 become the same risk.

**4. AI narrative fragility and single-source headline risk. (Severity: Moderate-to-High)**
Thursday demonstrated that an **unaudited, definitionally ambiguous revenue figure from one private company** can erase 3.4% from the SOX and 9%+ from individual names. That is not a demand risk; it is a **positioning** risk, and it indicates crowded exposure with low conviction. Expect repeat episodes. The defense is diversified-revenue, real-earnings exposure rather than pure-play derivatives — which is how today's slate was constructed, though it remains sector-concentrated (see the concentration warning above).

**5. Q3 earnings season against a 29.5% growth bar. (Severity: Moderate)**
FactSet expects **+29.5% YoY** S&P 500 Q3 earnings growth. That is an extraordinarily high bar, and PepsiCo already illustrated the asymmetry — it **beat on EPS and revenue yet lowered its profit outlook**, with organic growth of 3.1% missing the 3.8% estimate. Delta reports today; the money-center banks, UnitedHealth and J&J land **October 13**. UnitedHealth in particular is viewed as a "show-me" quarter on commercial cost pressures.

## Bottom Line

Thursday was a positioning unwind dressed as a fundamental repricing: a net-versus-gross accounting distinction at a private company knocked 3.4% off the semis while **340 S&P names advanced**, TSMC posted record revenue, and industry estimates kept rising. The honest read is that the AI capex cycle is intact and the market's grip on it is not — which is an opportunity for buyers with a valuation discipline and a liability for anyone owning the theme at 50–450x earnings.

The larger risk is not AI sentiment but the macro regime beneath it: a Fed still hiking into 3%+ core inflation, a 10-year at a 24-year high, and an Iran supply disruption that converts every oil headline into a rate headline. **Own the semiconductor cycle through earnings, not through narrative** — the five names here average a fraction of the sector's headline multiples — and size the sleeve knowing that all five would fall together on the next OpenAI-style headline.
