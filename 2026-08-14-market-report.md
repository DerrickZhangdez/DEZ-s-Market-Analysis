# Daily Market Report — Friday, August 14, 2026

## Executive Summary

Stocks slipped modestly on Friday, giving back a sliver of the week's gains after Thursday's record close, as a genuinely ugly pair of consumer datapoints — July retail sales down 0.6% and preliminary August consumer sentiment collapsing about 8% to 51 — punctured an otherwise constructive tape. The dominant tension of the day was a market whose *inflation* problem is quietly improving (July core CPI 2.5%, PPI soft enough to knock down September rate-hike odds) while its *growth* problem is just beginning to show up in the hard data. The single most consequential headline was not macro at all: Silver Lake's reported talks to take Workday private, the first serious private-equity strike at a de-rated large-cap SaaS name and a signal that strategic capital now sees a floor under software that public markets refuse to price.

## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The Fed is not where the market thought it was six months ago.** The FOMC left the funds rate unchanged at **3.50%–3.75%** in July for a fifth consecutive meeting — but with **three dissents preferring a 25bp *hike***. That is the crucial detail. The 2026 conversation is no longer "how many cuts"; markets have repriced from expecting cuts toward pricing some probability of hikes, driven by the energy-price shock from the Iran conflict feeding into headline inflation and inflation expectations.

**The disinflation data, however, is cooperating.** July CPI (released Aug 12) rose just **0.1% m/m**, with headline at **3.4% y/y**, down a tenth from June. Core CPI rose **0.2% m/m** and **2.5% y/y**, down from 2.6%. Shelter accounted for roughly two-thirds of the headline increase — the familiar lagging component. The June and July prints together suggest the energy-led inflation burst earlier in 2026 is losing momentum, even if the level sits well above the 2% target. Thursday's July PPI was soft enough to visibly reduce September hike odds and helped drive the record close.

**Today's data was the other side of the coin, and it was bad.** July **retail sales fell 0.6% m/m** — the steepest drop since May 2025 — against expectations of a *+0.3%* gain. Preliminary August **University of Michigan consumer sentiment fell about 8% to 51**, from July's revised 55.2, ending a two-month improving streak. The weakness was described as pervasive across demographics, with outsized declines among older consumers, lower-income households, and those without a college degree.

**What to watch this week and next:** whether the consumer weakness is an energy-price tax (gasoline as a sentiment transmission channel, which would be self-correcting if Hormuz de-escalates) or genuine demand destruction; the September FOMC and whether the three hawkish dissenters gain a fourth vote; and the shelter disinflation path, which is the only mechanical route to a 2-handle headline CPI. **The uncomfortable configuration for equities is stagflation-lite: inflation too high for the Fed to ease into weakness, and a consumer that just rolled over.** A Fed that cannot cut into a slowdown is the single most underpriced risk in this tape.

## Geopolitical Factors

The war between Washington/Tel Aviv and Tehran that began **February 28** remains the market's dominant non-monetary variable, and it re-escalated rhetorically today. The U.S. stated its **naval blockade of Iranian ports could continue "indefinitely,"** reigniting concern over flows through the **Strait of Hormuz** and lifting crude ~2%. Iran–Oman talks aimed at reopening the waterway have **not** produced an accord despite optimism earlier in the week that agreement was close.

The pattern through 2026 has been episodic: attack volumes peaked in mid-July at their highest since April, then abated into early August amid negotiation, and are now caught in an unresolved standoff. For portfolio purposes the asymmetry matters more than the base case — a genuine Hormuz closure is a low-probability, very-high-severity event that would simultaneously spike energy, crush the consumer, and remove the Fed's ability to respond. Today's simultaneous rally in **both gold and oil** suggests the market is paying up for that tail rather than expressing a directional growth view.

Secondary geopolitical vectors: **pharmaceutical tariffs** remain a live overhang for healthcare (an April 2026 proclamation established a multi-tier structure including a 100% tariff on patented pharmaceuticals and APIs for non-compliant companies, softened by a December 2025 U.S.–EU in-principle deal capping drug tariffs at 15% and by company-specific reprieves negotiated in exchange for Medicaid pricing concessions). **China exposure** is compressing for semicap equipment — Applied Materials' China revenue fell to ~28% of sales from ~35% a year ago.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: Healthcare Tech, Medical Devices & Biotech with Real Revenue

*(Rotating spotlight: day-of-year 226 mod 6 = 4)*

Healthcare **fell** today alongside technology while energy, real estate and materials led — which is precisely the setup that makes this sector interesting rather than uninteresting. The structural picture:

**1. Medtech has been de-rating for two years while fundamentals held.** The U.S. medical device complex has been grinding into multi-year relative lows, with surgical robotics, heart valves, continuous glucose monitors, orthopedic implants and diagnostic imaging all heavily sold between 2025 and 2026. The de-rating was driven by macro uncertainty, tariff exposure, execution stumbles and near-term margin pressure — *not* by demand. Procedure volumes and aging-demographic demand never broke. Where those specific pressures are now easing, the gap between fundamentals and multiple is the opportunity. This is the single most important observation for today's picks: I am not buying a turnaround in demand, I am buying the expiry of a multiple compression.

**2. Policy uncertainty is resolving in the sector's favor.** The two overhangs that suppressed healthcare multiples through 2025 — drug pricing policy and tariffs — have both moved from "unknowable" to "quantified." Clarity began with pharma companies trading Medicaid price concessions for multi-year tariff reprieves, and continued with the U.S.–EU deal capping drug tariffs at 15%. Healthcare notably outperformed sharply in Q4 2025 as that uncertainty lifted, after badly lagging for three quarters. Quantified bad news is investable; unquantified bad news is not.

**3. "Real revenue" is doing more work than usual in 2026.** In a tape where the AI trade absorbs all speculative capital, pre-revenue biotech is a funding-starved asset class while *profitable* biotech is an orphaned one. The result is genuine mispricing: several commercial-stage names trade at single-digit to low-teens forward earnings with double-digit growth, multiples that make no sense against a sector average P/E north of 26.

**4. The healthcare sub-sector most insulated from the year's dominant risk is services and infrastructure, not therapeutics.** CROs, pharmacy automation, HSA custodians and diagnostics have no tariff line item, no drug-pricing exposure, and — critically — no obvious AI-disruption narrative that public markets are punishing. That last point deserves emphasis given what is happening in software (see below).

**5. But the sector has a live landmine problem, and discipline requires naming it.** Screening healthcare for "cheap" in August 2026 surfaces a striking number of names that are cheap for excellent reasons. Three that failed my diligence today, all of which would have passed a naive valuation screen:

- **CPRX (Catalyst Pharmaceuticals)** screens beautifully — ~13–14x trailing earnings, 7.9x EV/EBITDA, Firdapse guided to $435–450M (+21–26%) and Agamree to $140–150M (+20–28%), $755.9M cash and no funded debt. It is also subject to an agreed **$4.1 billion acquisition by Italy's Angelini Pharma at $31.50/share in cash**. The valuation is irrelevant; this is merger arbitrage with a fixed ceiling, not a fundamental investment. Excluded.
- **ADMA Biologics** shows a 9.3x forward P/E, FY26 guidance of $530–560M revenue and $265–300M adjusted EBITDA, gross margin expanding to 69% from 55%, and a $200M+ buyback target. It is also down ~53.5% YTD and defending **multiple securities-fraud class actions alleging ~20% inflation of 2025 revenue via channel-stuffing of ASCENIV through an undisclosed related-party distributor**, following a March 24 short-seller report that took the stock down 29% and prompted withdrawal of long-term guidance. When the integrity of the revenue line is the allegation, every multiple built on that line is unusable. Excluded.
- **AMPH (Amphastar)** trades at **6.7x trailing earnings** — and Q1 EPS collapsed to $0.14, BAQSIMI sales fell 3% while still triggering a $100M milestone obligation to Lilly, and the business is concentrated in a handful of pressured products. Cheapness without earnings durability is not value. Excluded.

Also considered and passed over on valuation-versus-growth grounds: **OMCL (Omnicell)** at ~16.5x forward is superficially attractive with a real autonomous-pharmacy moat, but guidance implies only ~4% revenue growth and — the detail that decided it — **memory component costs are actively squeezing its margins.** With SanDisk today guiding to structurally higher NAND margins for years and the memory cycle inflecting upward, Omnicell's key input cost is getting worse by design, not better. **RVTY (Revvity)** raised FY26 guidance to $2.83–2.86B and adjusted EPS of $5.30–5.40 with Diagnostics compounding at 11% organic, but Life Sciences shrank 3% and blended organic growth of 4–5% does not justify ~20x. **UTHR (United Therapeutics)** at 18.7x trailing looks cheap until you notice Q2 revenue *declined 2% y/y* to $783.3M under inhaled-prostacyclin competition and management declined to reaffirm growth expectations, pushing the story onto 2027 ralinepag and Tyvaso-IPF approvals. That is a catalyst trade, not a valuation trade.

What survived is below. The through-line: I want healthcare businesses whose economics are driven by **royalties, installed bases, backlogs and float** rather than by a single reimbursed price.

### Artificial Intelligence

The AI trade's center of gravity continued shifting from chips toward **power and financing structure**. Nvidia's **$3 billion investment in Lancium** ties compute capacity directly to power infrastructure — the clearest statement yet that electrons, not wafers, are the binding constraint. The Department of Energy projects data centers reaching **up to 12% of U.S. electricity demand by 2028**, and 2026 hyperscaler capex estimates now cluster in the **$600–800 billion** range.

The financing question is becoming the bear case. Late July brought reports of Nvidia and OpenAI discussing a backstop of **up to $250 billion** to fund infrastructure buildout — news that cracked AI stocks at the time — against OpenAI's roughly **$1.4 trillion** in total commitments. Retail sentiment on capex sustainability has visibly soured. The circularity concern is legitimate and worth stating precisely: when the dominant supplier of accelerators helps finance its largest customer's purchases of those accelerators, revenue quality degrades even as revenue growth accelerates. That does not make the buildout fake, but it does mean the marginal dollar of AI revenue in 2027 is lower-quality than the marginal dollar in 2025, and multiples should reflect that.

Beneficiary dispersion remains extreme: **Dell** at $484.50 is up ~288% YTD, having gained 9.87% on Aug 12 alone.

**Reddit surged ~14%** on confirmation it will join the **S&P 500** — a clean index-inclusion flow event rather than a fundamental re-rating. **Globant sank 12.6%** on a weak Q2, and this is the more informative move: IT services businesses built on labor arbitrage are the most direct casualty of generative AI, and the market is now pricing that as a structural rather than cyclical impairment. **T-Mobile** slipped on a downgrade citing revenue risk.

### Hardware & Semiconductors

**Applied Materials delivered a genuinely excellent quarter and fell anyway.** FQ3 revenue of **$9.12 billion (+25% y/y)** beat, non-GAAP EPS of **$3.50** beat the $3.40 consensus and set a record, operating cash flow was $3.04 billion, and $860 million went back to shareholders. FQ4 guidance was *raised* to **$10.25 billion ±$500 million** with EPS of **$3.82–$4.22**, management citing AI-driven demand and improved visibility into 2027. The stock fell ~5.4% pre-market and closed at **$504.67** (range $496.00–$523.00). Two explanations, both partly right: the stock had rallied roughly 200% into the print, and **China fell to ~28% of revenue from ~35%**, a structural derating of the addressable market that better near-term numbers do not fix.

**The memory cycle is the sector's real story.** SanDisk's Investor Day laid out a long-term model of **mid-to-high-teens annual revenue growth through fiscal 2028–2030, adjusted gross margins near 80%, operating margins around 75%, and free cash flow at roughly 50% of revenue**, sending the shares up ~7–14% and dragging Lam Research, Applied Materials and Camtek higher with it. A word of analytical caution: **those margin targets would be without precedent in NAND**, an industry historically defined by brutal capacity-driven cyclicality and mid-cycle gross margins less than half that level. Either the memory market has structurally changed shape under AI-driven demand, or these targets will not survive contact with the next capacity addition. I would underwrite the *direction* of memory pricing — corroborated by Micron's strength and by Omnicell's component-cost complaints — without underwriting that margin structure.

### Software & SaaS

The day's most important single datapoint: **Workday jumped 17.78% to close at $206.45** — its best day on record — on reports that **Silver Lake is in talks to take it private at a valuation of up to $43 billion**, in discussions running for a couple of months with no assurance of a deal.

One analyst framed it as the first **"SaaSpocalypse" casualty**, and that framing is correct. Workday entered today down ~15% YTD and **more than 40% below its 2024 peak**, de-rated on the thesis that seat-based enterprise software is structurally threatened by AI agents. What Silver Lake's interest establishes is that private capital will underwrite those cash flows at levels public markets currently refuse — context being Hg's $46.4 billion OneStream take-private and Thoma Bravo's $12.3 billion Dayforce deal.

**A discrepancy worth flagging rather than smoothing over:** Workday's market cap after today's move is reported at roughly **$51.1 billion**, *above* the reported "up to $43 billion" deal valuation. Those figures cannot both describe the same equity on the same basis. Either the $43B figure reflects a different measure (an earlier reference point, or an equity value net of Workday's substantial cash), or the market is pricing a materially higher final bid or a competing one. Anyone treating this as a clean announced-deal spread is misreading it. The durable investment conclusion is not "own Workday" — it is that a private-equity bid floor now sits under quality de-rated SaaS, which raises the value of the option embedded in every similarly de-rated name with real free cash flow.

## Stock Picks — Valuation-Based

Five names, five distinct healthcare sub-sectors, all mid-cap, none previously recommended in the past month. Common thread: economics driven by **royalties, installed bases, contracted backlog, or float** — revenue structures that resist both drug-pricing policy and AI-disruption narratives.

### 1. HALO — Halozyme Therapeutics, Inc.

- **Thesis:** A royalty business trading at a biotech-risk multiple. Halozyme's ENHANZE platform converts partners' intravenous drugs to subcutaneous injection, and 2026 royalty revenue crosses **$1 billion** for the first time — yet the market caps the whole company near $11.7B. Guidance was raised in early August, triggering a wave of upgrades.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Price ~$103, market cap ~$11.7B, enterprise value ~$10.89B. Trailing P/E ~29.6 (GAAP); **forward P/E ~11.7x** on FY26 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $8.65–$9.00 (valuation screens show as low as ~8.5x on out-year estimates). **EV/EBITDA 11.4x.** P/S ~6.3x. **PEG ≈ 0.33** against ~35% revenue growth.
- **Growth Profile:** FY26 revenue guided **$1.835–$1.910B, +31–37% y/y**; royalty revenue **$1.220–$1.245B, +41–43%**, and royalties were ~64% of Q2 revenue. Adjusted EBITDA margin above 65%. Repurchased **$333M** in Q2 at an average $69.30 — buying well below today's price.
- **Competitive Moat:** rHuPH20 is embedded in partners' approved formulations and cannot be swapped out without new clinical work and regulatory filings. That makes switching costs near-absolute for the life of each product, on top of patent and trade-secret protection and manufacturing know-how. Royalty economics mean essentially no incremental cost per dollar of partner sales.
- **Key Risk:** The royalty stream has a defined life. rHuPH20 IP expiry and any adverse partner litigation would compress the terminal value, and there is meaningful concentration in Darzalex Faspro-linked royalties. The multiple is low *because* the market is discounting a cliff — the bull case is that reinvestment and new partner conversions extend the runway past the discounted horizon.
- **Rating: Strong Buy**

### 2. HQY — HealthEquity, Inc.

- **Thesis:** The largest independent HSA custodian earns spread income on ~$30B+ of member float with **custodial yields rate-locked through 2029** — making it one of the few growth names that is *helped* rather than hurt if the Fed's hawkish dissenters win. Asset growth compounds mechanically as accounts age.
- **Valuation Metrics:** **~20x forward** non-GAAP earnings; **EV/EBITDA 13.7x**; P/S ~5.7x. FY27 guidance: revenue **$1.41–$1.42B**, GAAP net income $242–248M ($2.88–$2.95/sh), non-GAAP net income $392–398M (**$4.66–$4.73/sh**). **PEG ≈ 1.0.**
- **Growth Profile:** Q1 FY27 profit **+29%**; custodial revenue **+11% to a record $174.3M**; HSA assets **+19%**; new HSAs **+15%**; HSA investors **+18%**; **invested assets +38%**. Custodial yield **3.84%**, held up by rate locks extending to 2029. Buyback authorization expanded by **$1 billion**.
- **Competitive Moat:** Distribution is the moat — HSAs are sold through employers and health plans, and those integrations are multi-year and painful to rip out. Add custodial licensing requirements, switching friction for individual accountholders who would have to move invested balances, and scale economics on float that no subscale competitor can match. Invested assets growing 38% versus accounts at 15% means revenue per account rises without winning a single new customer.
- **Key Risk:** Rate exposure cuts both ways beyond the lock horizon — a return to a materially lower policy rate would compress custodial yield as locks roll off after 2029. Secondary risks: legislative changes to HSA tax treatment, and the company's prior cybersecurity incidents in a business built on financial and health data.
- **Rating: Strong Buy**

### 3. EXEL — Exelixis, Inc.

- **Thesis:** A profitable oncology franchise at ~13x forward earnings whose 2026 guidance **entirely excludes** its next major asset. Zanzalintinib has a **PDUFA date of December 3, 2026** in third-line-plus metastatic colorectal cancer — a free option on the print.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Trailing P/E ~16.9x; **forward P/E ~13.3x**; P/S ~4x. Peers in US biotech average ~16.8x, and one fair-value framework puts a "tailored fair" multiple at ~21.1x, implying a meaningful discount.
- **Growth Profile:** FY26 revenue guided **$2.53–$2.63B** against $2.123B in 2025. Q1 revenue $610.8M (**+10%**), Q2 $628.7M (**+11%**). Buybacks accelerating. Seven pivotal zanzalintinib trials are ongoing or starting, positioning a second franchise for the 2030s.
- **Competitive Moat:** Cabozantinib is entrenched standard-of-care in renal cell and hepatocellular carcinoma, backed by prescriber familiarity, guideline inclusion and a decade of combination data. The deeper moat is demonstrated competence at developing and commercializing multi-indication TKIs — a repeatable capability, which is why zanzalintinib is credible rather than speculative.
- **Key Risk:** Single-franchise concentration into cabozantinib loss of exclusivity around 2030–31. The near-term risk already showed up: a neuroendocrine-tumor indication slowdown forced a guidance trim at Q2. If zanzalintinib misses in December, the 2030s revenue bridge weakens materially and the discount is deserved.
- **Rating: Buy**

### 4. MMSI — Merit Medical Systems, Inc.

- **Thesis:** Accelerating organic growth and expanding margins in exactly the medtech tape that has been indiscriminately de-rated. Q2 delivered Merit's **strongest organic growth in three years** and management raised full-year guidance — the specific "execution stumble easing" case the sector selloff has stopped pricing.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Price ~$87.69, market cap ~$5.29B. **Forward P/E ~20.4x** on FY26 non-GAAP EPS of $4.25–$4.35. P/S ~3.2x. EV/EBITDA **~12–13x (my estimate**, derived from guided revenue and non-GAAP operating margin — not a sourced figure, so weight it accordingly). **PEG ≈ 1.6.**
- **Growth Profile:** FY26 revenue guided **$1.631–$1.643B (+7.6–8.4%)**, organic constant-currency raised to **+6.9–7.5%** from +6–7%. Non-GAAP EPS **$4.25–$4.35, +11–14%** — EPS growing roughly 1.6x revenue. Q2 revenue $418.8M **+10%** (+9% organic), non-GAAP operating margin **22.6%, up 142bps y/y**.
- **Competitive Moat:** Breadth, not any single product. Thousands of SKUs across peripheral intervention and endoscopy make Merit a one-stop supplier for hospital contracting, while individual devices are physician-preference items where familiarity governs choice. Replicating the catalogue plus its global regulatory registrations and distribution is a decade-long project — which is why disposables-led medtech consistently earns high returns despite unremarkable headline growth.
- **Key Risk:** Tariffs and FX on a globally distributed manufacturing base, plus hospital capital-budget sensitivity if the consumer weakness in today's data broadens into health-system finances. At ~20x with high-single-digit growth, MMSI has the least valuation cushion of these five — this pick rests on margin expansion continuing, and a single quarter of margin reversal would hurt.
- **Rating: Buy**

### 5. ICLR — ICON plc

- **Thesis:** Deep contrarian value in a top-tier CRO where **demand is demonstrably strong and only the margin mix is broken**. Three consecutive quarters of industry-leading bookings and a **$23.4B backlog** are not the signature of a business losing share — but the stock is priced as if they were.
- **Valuation Metrics:** **Forward P/E ~16.9x** versus a five-year median of 18.1x, below both the broader medical sector and the S&P 500. P/S ~1.76x. Note the GAAP P/E near 63 is distorted by impairment and should be ignored. FY26 guidance reaffirmed: revenue **$7.85–$8.15B**, adjusted diluted EPS **$10.00–$11.00**.
- **Growth Profile:** Q2 gross business wins **$3.7B**, net wins **$3.1B**, **net book-to-bill 1.51x** — a third straight quarter of sector-leading bookings — with backlog up 3% sequentially to **$23.4B**. A Microsoft AI partnership announced in June adds a credible trial-efficiency angle. The honest caveat: adjusted EBITDA margin **fell to 15.9% from 20.5%** y/y.
- **Competitive Moat:** Scale in clinical development is genuinely defensible — global site networks, regulatory infrastructure across dozens of jurisdictions, patient-recruitment data assets, and preferred-provider relationships with large pharma that are re-competed rarely. A $23.4B backlog is roughly three years of contracted revenue visibility, which is why bookings strength eventually shows up in the P&L even when current-period margins disappoint.
- **Key Risk:** The margin compression is real and is the reason this is cheap. Elevated pass-through activity inflates revenue while diluting margin, and mix may not normalize quickly. ICON also carried an accounting-review overhang earlier in 2026 that, while lifted, argues for a wider risk discount and a smaller position. Biotech funding conditions remain a cyclical swing factor on the smaller-client book.
- **Rating: Accumulate**

**Portfolio construction:** HQY and HALO carry the highest conviction and the most differentiated economics (float and royalties). EXEL is the highest-upside/highest-binary name into a December catalyst. MMSI is the quality compounder with the thinnest valuation margin. ICLR is a deliberately sized-down contrarian position where the risk is timing, not thesis. ## Risk Factors

**1. A Fed that cannot cut into a slowdown (severity: high, probability: moderate).** Three FOMC members dissented in July in favor of a *hike*. If the energy shock keeps headline CPI near 3.4% while retail sales and sentiment deteriorate, equities face the one configuration that offers no monetary put. Watch for a fourth hawkish vote in September.

**2. Consumer demand destruction (severity: high, probability: rising).** July retail sales **−0.6%** against +0.3% expected, with sentiment at **51** and declines concentrated in lower-income and older cohorts. Two datapoints are not a trend, but they are the first hard confirmation that the energy-price tax is reaching spending. Consumer-facing cyclicals and hospital capital budgets are the transmission channels.

**3. Hormuz escalation (severity: extreme, probability: low).** A blockade the U.S. says may run "indefinitely," stalled Iran–Oman talks, and Brent at $88.64. Genuine closure would spike energy, crush the consumer and neutralize the Fed simultaneously. Gold at $4,351 rallying *with* crude suggests the tail is being hedged.

**4. AI capex financing circularity (severity: high, probability: moderate).** Vendor-financed demand — reported Nvidia/OpenAI backstop talks of up to $250B against ~$1.4T of OpenAI commitments — degrades AI revenue quality even as growth continues. The Applied Materials reaction is the early warning: when a 25% beat with raised guidance sells off, expectations rather than fundamentals have become the binding constraint.

**5. Software multiple compression, or "SaaSpocalypse" (severity: moderate, probability: high — already underway).** Workday 40%+ below its 2024 peak before today, Globant down 12.6%: markets are repricing seat-based software and labor-arbitrage services as structurally AI-impaired. Private equity is establishing a valuation floor, but the public de-rating has further to run in names without hard free cash flow.

## Bottom Line

The index level says nothing happened today; the internals say the market's two-year-old script is being rewritten. Inflation is slowly cooperating while the consumer is not, and a Fed with three hawkish dissenters cannot easily rescue a slowdown it is still fighting — so the premium on businesses with contracted, non-discretionary, non-consumer revenue should keep rising. That is the case for today's healthcare rotation: a sector at multi-year relative lows, with its two policy overhangs now quantified rather than open-ended, where royalty streams, float income, backlogs and installed bases can be bought at 12–20x forward earnings while the AI complex absorbs every speculative dollar. Buy the cash-flow structures the market has stopped paying for, size the Iran tail with real hedges rather than optimism, and treat any AI name that sells off on a beat as information about positioning rather than about the business.
