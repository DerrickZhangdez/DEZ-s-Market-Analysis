### Artificial Intelligence

**The AI narrative took a real, quantifiable hit this month, and the market is finally pricing financing risk rather than demand.**

**The OpenAI revenue reset (Oct 8).** OpenAI told investors it reached roughly **$50 billion** in annualized revenue at end-September — materially below the **$68 billion** figure widely reported in late September. The read-through was immediate and brutal across the complex: **Nvidia −3%**, **Oracle −6%**, **CoreWeave −8%** on the day. OpenAI is in early discussions about a new round of roughly **$30 billion**, with no term sheet finalized.

**Broadcom's CEO breaks ranks (today, Oct 12).** Hock Tan compared parts of the AI investment boom to **gambling**, saying many investors set extreme expectations without understanding large language models. More substantively, he warned that **insufficient capital** — not insufficient demand — may be the next major brake on AI expansion, citing **Nvidia-backed Firmus Grid withdrawing a planned $5.5 billion IPO** last week.

**Why this matters more than a typical bearish soundbite.** When the CEO of one of the largest direct beneficiaries of AI capex publicly flags speculative excess and a capital constraint, that is not a short-seller's thesis — it is a supplier telling you his customers' funding is not guaranteed. Pair it with the **Nvidia/OpenAI Ohio financing at up to $105 billion** — which came in roughly **$145 billion below** the ~$250 billion guarantee reportedly under consideration, with capacity phasing in only from **2028** — and the pattern is consistent: **the headline numbers in AI infrastructure keep getting revised down, and the timelines keep getting pushed out.**

**The circularity problem, stated plainly.** A chip vendor financing its own customer's data center, which exists to buy that vendor's chips, is a structure that works beautifully while capital is abundant and reflexively unwinds when it is not. At a 5.32% 10-year, capital is becoming less abundant. **This is the most underpriced risk in the market today**, and it is why today's picks deliberately sit in financial market structure rather than in AI infrastructure.

## Risk Factors

**1. September CPI on Wednesday — severity: critical.**
Consensus expects headline inflation to **re-accelerate to ~3.6%–3.7% from 3.4%**, with core near 2.5%. The S&P 500 sits within a few points of a record. A hot print revives bets on hikes at both remaining meetings and hits equity multiples and the long end simultaneously. **This is the highest-asymmetry event on the calendar** — the index is not priced for an upside inflation surprise.

**2. AI financing circularity — severity: high, and most underpriced.**
OpenAI's annualized revenue was revised from **~$68B to ~$50B**, Nvidia's Ohio financing came in **~$145B below** the figure reportedly under consideration, **Firmus Grid pulled a $5.5B IPO**, and **Broadcom's CEO publicly warned that capital, not demand, is the next constraint**. Vendor-financed demand is reflexive: it works while capital is abundant and unwinds when it is not. At a 5.32% 10-year, capital is getting scarcer. The complex (NVDA −3%, ORCL −6%, CRWV −8% on Oct 8) has already shown how it trades on this news.

**3. Credit quality in bank earnings — severity: high.**
Rising rates plus **$100+ oil** is the textbook setup for consumer credit deterioration. Banks are expected to grow earnings **~10%**, but the sector is **down 7.5% over the past month** and the options market prices a **muted** BAC reaction — so provisions, not EPS, will drive the tape. Today's **UWM** and **Enact** downgrades suggest the rate-sensitive credit complex is already cracking.

**4. Oil supply constraint persisting through Q4 — severity: high.**
The EIA raised its Q4 Brent forecast by **$14/b to $105** and assumes **4.5 million b/d of shut-in production** persists through the quarter. This is not a headline premium that decays; it is a structural supply assumption that feeds directly into CPI and therefore into Fed policy. Iran calling the US response *"inadequate"* keeps the tail fat.

**5. The 5.32% 10-year as a valuation ceiling — severity: moderate-to-high.**
At the highest yield since **April 2002**, the discount rate on every long-duration equity has reset. Equity risk premia are compressed to levels that leave no cushion for a growth disappointment — and the index is making highs into it.

## Bottom Line

**The market is priced for a soft landing that the Fed's own dot plot no longer describes.** Equities sit at record highs while the policy rate rises, the 10-year yields more than it has since 2002, oil sits above $100 on a supply constraint the EIA expects to persist, and consensus looks for inflation to *accelerate* on Wednesday — a configuration where the index has everything to lose from a hot print and relatively little to gain from a cool one.

The actionable response is not to sell equities but to **own the cash flows that get paid when the path is uncertain rather than the ones that need the path to be benign**. That is the logic behind today's slate: **CME** and **VIRT** monetize the disagreement itself, **FIS** offers deep value on inflecting cash flow, and **SEIC** and **MORN** provide recurring-revenue ballast — with the explicit caveat that the first two are the same bet and should be sized as one. Meanwhile, the Broadcom warning deserves more weight than it is getting: when your biggest supplier tells you his customers may run out of money, that is not noise.
