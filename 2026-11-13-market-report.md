## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The regime has flipped from easing to tightening, and this is the dominant macro fact of Q4 2026.**

- **Policy rate:** Target range **3.75%–4.00%**, following a **quarter-point *increase* in September 2026** under Fed Chair **Kevin Warsh**. The committee held at the October meeting.
- **Next decision:** **December 8–9.** A Reuters poll of economists expects one more increase; traders are pricing a quarter-point move.
- **October PPI (released today, Nov 13):** Final demand **+0.3% m/m** vs. +0.4% consensus. Annual rate **rose to 6.5%** from 6.3% — highest since November 2022. **Core PPI** (ex food, energy, trade services) **+0.3% m/m / +5.0% y/y**, in line.
- **Component detail:** Foods **+1.3%**, energy **−0.1%**, transportation & warehousing **+2.0%**, trade **−0.2%**. **Crude petroleum −10.3%** and **diesel −7.9%** did the heavy lifting on the downside.
- **Context that matters:** today's soft PPI follows a **hotter-than-expected CPI** earlier this week. September PPI was running at **+1.3% to +1.4% m/m** (sources differ on the revision), so October is a genuine deceleration — but from an alarming base.

**The analytical point:** the entire PPI beat is an *energy* story. Crude petroleum fell 10.3% because oil crashed ~20% off its late-October peak. Strip that out and core is still running **5.0% y/y**. This is disinflation you rent, not disinflation you own — and it reverses the moment the Middle East supply picture tightens again. Markets are right to keep December priced for a hike.

**Structural note:** The Fed announced it will **cut rate decisions to seven per year from eight beginning in 2027**, with **no rate vote at the October 2027 meeting**. Minor for now, but it reduces the number of scheduled opportunities to course-correct — modestly raising the cost of a policy error at each remaining meeting.

**What to watch next week:** NVIDIA earnings (Nov 17) as the single largest index-level event risk; any Fed speakers walking back or confirming December; and whether the oil decline extends far enough to keep flattering headline inflation into the December print.

## Geopolitical Factors

**Oil has round-tripped a war premium, and that is quietly the most important cross-asset move of the past three weeks.**

- **Prices:** Brent **~$95.64**, WTI **above $88** as of Friday, November 13. Both are headed for a **second consecutive weekly decline**.
- **The magnitude:** crude is down roughly **20% from its late-October peak of ~$118/bbl**.
- **Why it fell:** Middle East supply is recovering. **Saudi output rebounded to ~10.56 mb/d** last month, near pre-war levels. Rising OPEC+ production and Strait of Hormuz shipping normalization have offset the risk premium.
- **Why it could snap back:** Houthi attacks continue and have shifted to **energy infrastructure** — a pumping station on the **East-West pipeline** and a facility at the port of **Yanbu**. President Trump has issued a fresh **Iran warning**. Analysts caution that the current lull in high-intensity strikes does not retire escalation risk.
- **Glut signal:** Middle Eastern benchmarks have weakened for weeks, which some read as an emerging oversupply rather than purely a geopolitical unwind.

**Investment implication:** a 20% oil decline is simultaneously (a) the reason today's PPI looked tame, (b) a margin tailwind for transports, chemicals and packaging, and (c) a direct earnings headwind for upstream E&P. Those three effects do not net out — they *rotate* leadership. Positioning for the disinflation without accounting for who pays for it is the error to avoid here.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: Industrials, Energy, Materials & "Picks-and-Shovels"
*(Rotation: day-of-year 317 mod 6 = **5**)*

This is the correct week to be looking here, for a non-obvious reason: **the AI trade is being repriced, but AI's physical supply chain is not the same asset as AI's silicon.** The semiconductor index is down ~38% from June; meanwhile the companies that pour the concrete, roll the steel, build the transmission lines and move the heat are still printing record backlogs. The market is currently treating these as a single correlated basket. They are not.

**1. The electrical infrastructure backlog is at an all-time high — and the stock sold off anyway.**

**Quanta Services (PWR)** reported a **record $63.73B backlog at September 30, up 62.7% y/y** — a **$10B sequential increase**. Q3 revenue **$12.01B (+57.4%)**, adjusted EPS **$6.06 (+82%)**, GAAP diluted EPS $4.55. Management raised FY guidance to **$41.3–41.6B revenue, $17.90–18.30 adjusted EPS, $2.5–3.0B FCF** and guided to strong double-digit adjusted EPS growth in 2027. Critically, major **765-kV transmission projects have not yet materially entered backlog**, with significant bookings potentially landing in H1 2027.

And yet the stock fell **12.3% on November 5** to $509.06, was down **~29% over one month**, before recovering to **~$608 by November 11**.

**Why I am not picking it despite loving the business:** PWR trades at roughly **45–55x trailing earnings** (sources vary; GuruFocus cites 55.1x TTM vs. a 5-year median of 50.7x). GuruFocus's own GF Value of $562 implies it is ~8% *over*valued at $608, and its earnings-based DCF flags it as significantly overvalued. A record backlog does not make 50x cheap. This is a watchlist name on a genuine valuation reset, not a buy at this multiple — and saying so is the whole point of a valuation-disciplined process.

**2. Thermal management just got an M&A bid — and it revalues the whole cooling complex.**

**Modine (MOD)** surged **~28–31%** Friday on a **Bloomberg report that Samsung Electronics is in talks to acquire it**. Modine opened at **$141.40** and touched **$146.50**; market cap was ~**$5.6B** pre-move. The context makes this especially instructive: Modine had **fallen ~15% since its November 3 earnings**, where adjusted EPS of $0.22 narrowly missed and non-GAAP gross margin compressed to **65.6% from 68.1%** — even as the **data center business grew revenue 95% y/y with record quarterly orders above $1B**.

**The lesson:** the market punished a margin wobble and ignored a 95%-growth franchise with a $1B order book. A strategic acquirer did the opposite. **The deal is unconfirmed** — no final decision has been made, talks could collapse, and another bidder could emerge — so this is not actionable as a pick. But it marks the floor valuation a strategic buyer sees in AI thermal infrastructure, and that read-through is real.

**3. Rare earths: the right theme, the wrong income statement.**

**MP Materials (MP)** posted Q3 revenue **+176% y/y to $147.7M**, swinging to an **adjusted profit of $0.04/share** from a $(0.10) loss. **NdPr sales volumes +94% to 1,020 metric tons.** Management guided to **5,000–6,000 metric tons of NdPr in 2027** and is expanding into samarium oxide separation and recommissioning chlor-alkali at Mountain Pass. MP received an analyst upgrade in this week's cycle.

But shares are **~53% below their 52-week high**, costs are expected to stay elevated through 2026–2027 because separated rare earth and magnetic materials production is structurally more expensive than concentrate, and **China's rare-earth export-control pause expired November 10** with no formal notice published as of the most recent reporting. A company earning four cents a share on a strategic narrative does not clear a valuation-based bar. **Watchlist, not a pick.** I am flagging this explicitly because it is exactly the sort of name a "spotlight sector" mandate tempts you into buying on story alone.

**4. Steel and construction materials are quietly having an excellent year.**

**Commercial Metals (CMC)** delivered **FY2026 net earnings of $655.8M versus $84.7M in FY2025** — a near-8x increase. Q4 adjusted EPS **$2.09 beat $1.96**; revenue **$2.65B beat $2.56B**. Management describes non-residential and infrastructure demand as healthy, explicitly citing **data centers, energy and infrastructure** as key end markets, with rebar and MBQ pricing showing sequential momentum in a balanced supply-demand environment. This is the AI buildout expressed as tons of rebar rather than as GPUs — same demand driver, a fraction of the multiple.

**5. Energy equipment is where LNG and data centers intersect.**

**Baker Hughes (BKR)** reported Q3 adjusted EPS **$0.70** (GAAP $0.41) against a $0.60–0.63 consensus, revenue **$8.0B**, FCF **$769M**. **RPO hit $46.4B**, including **record IET RPO of $39.4B**, with IET orders of **$5.9B** and a **1.3 book-to-bill** driven explicitly by **data center and LNG equipment demand**. The stock fell **~6%** post-print on leverage and integration concerns. Notably, BKR's **Chart Industries acquisition** brings cryogenic/LNG process technology in-house — relevant to readers of this report, since **GTLS was a prior pick in this coverage universe and now sits inside BKR**. Disclosed for transparency.

**6. Specialty chemicals: pricing power that survived a volume decline.**

**NewMarket (NEU)** is the quiet compounder of this group. Q3 net income **$124M / $13.50 per share** vs. $100M / $10.67 a year ago. Nine-month EPS **$40.64 vs. $35.78**. The striking detail: **petroleum additives shipments fell 8%**, yet **petroleum additives operating profit held at $130.9M versus $131.3M** — surcharges fully offset both the volume decline and supply chain disruption costs. Specialty Materials operating profit **nearly tripled to $16.4M from $6.0M**. Net debt/EBITDA **~1x**, with **$126M of buybacks and $83M of dividends** in the quarter. Holding profit flat on down volume is the cleanest empirical evidence of pricing power that exists.

**7. Engineered polymers: record margins and the first buyback in four years.**

**Avient (AVNT)** reported Q3 sales **+10.5% to $891M**, adjusted EPS **$0.86 (+23% y/y, $0.06 above guidance)**, GAAP EPS $0.46 vs. $0.36. **Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 80bps to a Q3-record 17.3%.** FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance was **raised to $3.22–3.32 from $3.10–3.25**, with EBITDA and FCF guidance also raised. The company repaid **$50M of debt** and **initiated its first share repurchase since 2022** — a capital-allocation signal that management believes the deleveraging phase is complete. Mike Frank is the incoming CEO.

**Spotlight synthesis:** the dispersion inside this sector is enormous. Quanta has the best fundamentals and the worst valuation. MP has the best narrative and the worst earnings. CMC, NEU, AVNT and BKR have the combination that actually matters — demonstrated earnings growth at multiples that do not require heroic assumptions. That is where today's capital goes.

### 5. FANG — Diamondback Energy, Inc.
*(Upstream oil & gas — Permian pure-play)*

## Risk Factors

**1. NVIDIA reports November 17 with no permanent CEO — severity: critical.**
Nearly half the S&P 500's value is tied to AI companies, and the largest of them enters its print with an interim CFO-turned-CEO, a contested resignation-versus-ouster narrative, a national-security dimension to the scandal, and five downgrades in 48 hours including a price target cut from $465 to $186. The binary is unusually wide and the index weight is unprecedented. This is the single largest event risk on the calendar.

**2. A December Fed hike into 6.5% headline PPI — severity: high.**
The target range is already **3.75–4.00%** after a September *increase*, October CPI ran hot, and headline PPI hit its highest annual rate since November 2022. The soft October PPI print was **almost entirely energy** (crude petroleum −10.3%, diesel −7.9%) while core held at **5.0% y/y**. If oil stabilizes or reverses, the next print has no such cushion. The Russell's **−1.66%** on an inflation-relief day says small caps already understand this.

**3. AI capex funding stress, not AI demand weakness — severity: high.**
Hyperscaler 2026 capex exceeds **$700B** while free cash flow compresses. **Tech is now a record 19.7% of junk-bond issuance**, driven by data-center financing. Barclays frames the market's question as funding rather than demand. The November 5 correction was triggered by *political* risk to data-center construction following the midterms — a reminder that this capex cycle now has a policy dependency it did not have a year ago. Credit, not earnings, is the channel to watch.

**4. Oil's 20% decline reversing — severity: moderate-to-high, and two-sided.**
Houthi attacks have shifted to energy infrastructure (**East-West pipeline pumping station**, **Yanbu**), and a fresh Iran warning is live. A supply shock re-inflates headline CPI/PPI precisely when the Fed is deciding on December. Conversely, if the glut thesis is right — Saudi at **10.56 mb/d**, OPEC+ rising, Middle East benchmarks weakening — E&P earnings estimates fall further. **Both tails hurt something in this portfolio**, which is why FANG is rated Accumulate rather than Buy.

**5. The semiconductor-versus-index divergence resolving downward — severity: moderate.**
Semis **−38.4%** from June while the S&P is **−5.9%** from its high. Historically, a drawdown of that magnitude in the market's cyclical leadership either precedes broader weakness or marks a bottom. Small-cap underperformance (Russell **−1.66%** on an up day for the S&P) tilts the evidence toward the former rather than the latter.

## Bottom Line

The market is being asked to absorb two unrelated shocks at once — a leadership vacuum at the index's largest weight and a central bank that has already started hiking — and it is resolving that tension by rotating rather than by selling. The opportunity is that the AI *physical* supply chain is being repriced alongside AI *silicon* despite record backlogs and visible earnings: Quanta's backlog is up 63% while its stock fell 29%, Commercial Metals grew earnings nearly eightfold, and Baker Hughes sold off on a beat with record LNG and data-center orders. Own the picks-and-shovels at ten to eighteen times earnings rather than the story at fifty, keep position sizes honest about the fact that today's energy-driven disinflation is rented and not owned, and do nothing large ahead of November 17.
