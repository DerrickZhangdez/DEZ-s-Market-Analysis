## Macro & Monetary Environment

### Where the Fed actually stands

The Federal Reserve left the federal funds rate unchanged at **3.50%–3.75%** in July — the fifth consecutive hold. The detail that matters more than the decision: **three FOMC members dissented in favor of a 25bp hike.** That is an unusually hawkish dissent bloc, and it leaves a September increase genuinely on the table rather than as a tail scenario.

This is the single most under-appreciated fact in the current setup. Market pricing has migrated over the course of 2026 from *expecting cuts* to **anticipating one to two hikes by year-end**. Positioning that assumes a friendly Fed is positioning against the committee's own revealed preference.

### Inflation: improving, but not enough

- **June headline CPI: 3.5% YoY** — the first decline in five months, which is progress, but the level remains far above target.
- **July CPI (Wednesday):** consensus looks for roughly **+0.09% MoM** and **~3.42% YoY** headline.
- **July core CPI:** roughly **+0.21% MoM** and **~2.52% YoY**.

The gap between headline (~3.4%) and core (~2.5%) is the crux. Fed officials — Governor Cook among them — have characterized the elevated headline print as reflecting **supply shocks, particularly in energy**, rather than broad demand-driven inflation. If that framing holds, the committee can tolerate a 3-handle headline. If core starts drifting up toward headline, the framing collapses and the three dissenters become a majority.

### The jobs-data wrinkle

Wall Street closed higher on Friday specifically because **weak July jobs data** raised hope the Fed holds steady for longer. Note the asymmetry embedded in that reaction: the market is treating labor-market deterioration as unambiguously good news. That works only while growth stays comfortably positive. Softening employment *plus* a hot CPI print Wednesday — the stagflationary combination — is the scenario for which almost nobody is positioned, and it is not far-fetched given the supply-shock character of current inflation.

## Geopolitical Factors

### Middle East diplomacy is now a *market* variable, not a background risk

The dominant geopolitical input today ran through energy. Renewed signs of **US–Iran dialogue** triggered a sharp correction in crude, with **Brent and WTI both down nearly 4%** on the diplomatic developments. TheStreet explicitly attributed the Dow's soft session to "concerns over a potential Middle East deal" — an unusual and revealing headline, because it means enough index weight now sits in energy and defense that *de-escalation* reads as a negative for the Dow even as it is a clear positive for the broad economy and for the inflation path.

For the CPI outlook, this cuts favorably. If the energy component of headline inflation is what has kept the print on a 3-handle, a sustained 4% decline in crude on diplomatic progress is disinflationary at exactly the moment the Fed needs it to be.

### The risks that have not gone away

- **Strait of Hormuz.** Roughly **20% of global oil supply** transits the strait. Any escalation threatening transit triggers immediate, severe repricing across energy and equities. The current premium compression assumes diplomacy holds; that assumption is one incident away from being wrong.
- **The Iran deal is unresolved.** Talks gained traction in June 2026 but have not concluded. Market sensitivity to deterioration remains acute — the risk premium has been *removed*, which means there is now room for it to be violently *re-added*.
- **Elevated implied volatility.** Implied vol across major indices remains above 2024 averages despite the VIX sitting at a benign 15.45 spot level. The term structure is telling you the market does not believe uncertainty has peaked, even while spot vol looks calm.

### Trade and export controls — the semiconductor overlay

Directly relevant to today's spotlight: export-control policy remains the largest single swing factor in semiconductor capital equipment. SEMI's forecasts show **China, Taiwan, and Korea leading intense equipment demand**, which means a material share of WFE revenue for every equipment and subsystem supplier is geographically exposed to a policy variable that can change without warning. This is not a theoretical risk — it is the primary reason several of the highest-quality semicap names trade at discounts to their growth rates, and it is treated explicitly as a key risk in the picks below.

SEMI's mid-year forecast revision is the most important dataset in the sector right now:

| Total equipment sales, 2028 | $229B | Record |
| WFE, 2025 → 2031 | $129B → ~$220B | ~9% CAGR, >$1.2T cumulative |

An upward revision of nearly **$9 billion in a single mid-year update** is not a rounding adjustment. It reflects accelerating investment in AI infrastructure, leading-edge logic, advanced memory, and **back-end technologies** needed to support higher compute density, HBM-related investment, and increasingly complex device architectures.

MKS Instruments' management has gone further, indicating WFE spend potentially reaching **$170–180 billion for 2027**. If that proves even directionally correct, current semicap earnings estimates are too low across the board.

#### Why the back end is where the alpha is

The structurally important shift: **process control and back-end intensity per wafer is rising faster than wafer starts.** Every additional layer of 3D NAND, every gate-all-around transition, every hybrid-bonding step, every additional HBM deck requires more inspection and metrology operations than the node before it. This is why:

- AI chip manufacturing requires **more process control steps per wafer than any prior chip generation**.
- Nova has grown optical CD metrology share from ~20% to **nearly 29% over three years** by sitting on exactly these inflections — GAA, hybrid bonding, 3D DRAM, multi-deck 3D NAND.
- Camtek's advanced packaging revenue is guided to grow **~70% between Q1 and Q4 of 2026** alone.

The investable consequence: revenue per wafer for metrology, inspection, and advanced-packaging tooling is growing faster than the WFE line item itself. Companies levered to *steps per wafer* rather than *wafers* have a second growth vector that the headline WFE number understates.

#### Memory and HBM

Korea's equipment outlays are supported by substantial investment in advanced memory including HBM; Taiwan's spending reflects large-scale leading-edge capacity builds for AI and HPC. The memory cycle has turned decisively — and it is showing up most dramatically not in the memory makers themselves but in the controller and component layer, where **Silicon Motion just posted 127% year-over-year revenue growth**.

### Artificial Intelligence

The AI capex numbers continue to escalate to a scale that is difficult to intuit:

- The five largest hyperscalers — Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta, and Oracle — are projected to spend **$660–725 billion** on capex in 2026, with roughly **75% ($450–500B)** tied directly to AI infrastructure.
- BofA has floated a **$1.2 trillion** hyperscaler capex figure in its longer-range framing.

**The custom-silicon share shift is the live competitive story.** Broadcom's AI semiconductor revenue reached **$10.8 billion in Q2 FY2026 (+143% YoY)**, with AI bookings exceeding **$30 billion in the quarter alone** and Q3 AI revenue guided to **$16 billion (+200% YoY)**. Meta has signed for **3 gigawatts of MTIA XPUs through 2028** — a multi-year, multi-generational commitment that is the clearest evidence yet that custom accelerators are a durable second architecture rather than a hedge.

NVIDIA remains dominant — data center revenue of **$193.7B in FY2026 (+68% YoY)** and roughly **75–81%** of AI accelerator revenue in H1 2026 — but that share is drifting down as custom ASICs scale, and the *direction* of the drift is what matters for supply-chain positioning.

**The read-through to today's spotlight is the point.** Custom XPUs and HBM-heavy accelerators are more advanced-packaging-intensive and more test-intensive than merchant GPUs. Every gigawatt of MTIA is more inspection steps, more thermal test handler time, more substrate and photomask demand, and more high-purity subsystem content. Whether NVIDIA or Broadcom wins a given socket, the packaging, inspection, and subsystem layer gets paid either way. That is the "picks-and-shovels of the picks-and-shovels" argument, and it is the reason today's picks sit where they do.

**The market is now differentiating on capex quality.** SpaceX (SPCX) fell **13.6%** on its first post-IPO report despite nearly doubling revenue to **$7.8 billion** on Starlink strength — investors balked at **$16 billion in quarterly capex** for AI infrastructure, compounded by the first lock-up expiration. Shopify rose **17%** by demonstrating AI integration **tripled customer traffic and orders** without eroding margins. The dividing line is no longer "does the company have an AI story" but "can it show AI returns without margin destruction."

### 1. SIMO — Silicon Motion Technology Corporation

**Thesis:** Silicon Motion is the pure-play controller layer of the NAND/SSD stack, and the AI storage build-out has taken its revenue from a cyclical trough to **127% year-over-year growth** in a single year — yet the stock still trades in the twenties on forward earnings because the market continues to price it as a commodity memory derivative rather than as the design-win-driven franchise it has become.

**Valuation Metrics:**
- **P/E (trailing):** elevated on depressed trailing earnings — the trailing figure is not the relevant metric during an earnings inflection of this magnitude
- **P/E (forward, 2026):** **~28–29x** on 2026 consensus EPS of **$8.96** (recently revised up 7.1%) against a share price around **$254–256**
- **P/E (forward, 2027):** **~21x** on 2027 consensus EPS of **$11.98** (revised up 14.6% over 60 days)
- **P/S:** roughly **4x** on a ~$2B annualized revenue run-rate
- **EV/EBITDA:** mid-teens on forward estimates, supported by an expanding margin structure
- **PEG:** **well below 0.5** — this is the crux of the case. Roughly 21–29x forward earnings against >100% revenue growth and consensus EPS revisions running *up* double digits is the single most attractive growth-adjusted valuation surfaced in today's screen.

**Growth Profile:** Q2 2026 revenue **$451 million, +127% YoY, +32% sequentially**, EPS per ADS of **$2.43**. Gross margin **50.2%** (above the 48.5–49.5% guide); operating margin **23.1%**, up from 18.2% in Q1 and above the 21–22% guide. Q3 guidance of **$519–541 million** implies **15–20% sequential** and **114–124% year-over-year** growth. Management has confirmed the company is on track to **more than double annual revenue in 2026** and expects **operating margins above 30%** by fiscal year end. Underneath the headline: SSD controller sales +50–55% YoY, embedded eMMC/UFS controllers nearly doubled, Ferri and Boot Drive solutions delivering triple-digit growth.

**Competitive Moat:** Controller design is a multi-year, firmware-heavy qualification business. Once a controller is designed into a NAND maker's or hyperscaler's SSD platform, replacement requires requalifying the entire storage stack — switching costs are high and cycles are long. Silicon Motion has extended this position into the highest-value new sockets with the **SM2524XT**, a PCIe Gen5 DRAMless controller purpose-built for AI PCs and KV-cache-heavy inference workloads, delivering up to **14 GB/s sequential read** and **2.5M IOPS random**. KV-cache offload to local SSD is becoming a standard inference architecture pattern, and it is a demand vector that did not exist two years ago.

**Key Risk:** NAND cyclicality. Controller demand is a derivative of NAND bit shipments, and the memory cycle is the most violent in semiconductors. A NAND oversupply correction would hit volumes and pricing simultaneously. The stock has also run hard — a **105.9% 30-day return** — so near-term entry timing carries real risk even if the multi-year case is sound. Consider scaling in.

**Rating: Buy**

### 2. UCTT — Ultra Clean Holdings, Inc.

**Thesis:** Ultra Clean is the outsourced subsystem manufacturer for the largest WFE OEMs, and it just delivered a quarter that blew through the top of its own guidance while trading at **1.29x sales** — roughly half its peer group at 2.5x and a fraction of the 5.8x semiconductor industry average. It is the cheapest credible way to own the 23% WFE upcycle.

**Valuation Metrics:**
- **P/E (trailing):** negative on a GAAP basis (**−14.5x**) — this is precisely why the stock is cheap and precisely why the opportunity exists. GAAP results have been suppressed by amortization and restructuring through the downturn.
- **P/E (forward):** on Q3 guidance of **$0.83–1.03 non-GAAP EPS**, the annualized exit run-rate is roughly **$3.50–4.00**, putting the stock in the **low-to-mid teens** on exit-rate earnings — remarkable for a semicap name in an accelerating cycle
- **P/S:** **1.29x**, versus **2.5x** for direct peers and **5.8x** for the US semiconductor industry
- **EV/EBITDA:** high single digits on forward estimates
- **PEG:** not meaningful on GAAP; on non-GAAP earnings growth the ratio is **well under 1**

**Growth Profile:** Q2 2026 (reported August 3) revenue of **$644.9 million** against guidance of **$565–605M** — a beat above the *top* of the range. Non-GAAP EPS of **$0.70** versus a **$0.44–0.60** guide, again above the high end. GAAP EPS **$0.19**. Q3 guidance is the striking part: revenue of **$700–750 million** (another ~13% sequential step) with GAAP EPS of **$0.67–0.87** and non-GAAP of **$0.83–1.03**. Note that GAAP is inflecting into solidly positive territory — the negative trailing P/E is about to stop being the relevant frame, which is typically when this kind of stock re-rates. Full-year 2026 outlook implies **15–20% revenue growth**, and the Q2/Q3 trajectory suggests that is conservative.

**Competitive Moat:** Ultra Clean's moat is qualification-based and physical. Its gas delivery systems, weldments, and precision subassemblies are qualified into specific OEM tool platforms, and requalifying a subsystem supplier means requalifying the tool — an expensive, slow process no OEM undertakes casually. The company's scale in ultra-high-purity manufacturing and its co-location with major OEM facilities create switching costs disproportionate to the apparently commoditized nature of the components. Its closest direct competitor, Ichor, trades at **68.5x forward earnings** — a valuation gap that is difficult to justify on business quality alone.

**Key Risk:** Customer concentration. Ultra Clean's revenue is heavily concentrated among a small number of WFE OEMs, so an order-rate deceleration at any one of them transmits directly and with amplification — subsystem suppliers sit at the front of the inventory whip and absorb both the upswing and the downswing more violently than their customers. Analyst price targets on the name are also unusually dispersed across sources, which reflects genuine disagreement about mid-cycle earnings power. The thin GAAP margin structure means execution errors compound quickly.

**Rating: Buy**

### 3. CAMT — Camtek Ltd.

**Thesis:** Camtek sits at the exact chokepoint of the AI supply chain — inspection and metrology for advanced packaging and HBM — with **$260 million of committed HBM revenue** from two manufacturers, **$600 million+ in year-to-date orders**, and record backlog. The multiple is full, but this is the one premium-valued name in the spotlight where the *booked backlog*, not the forecast, carries the valuation.

**Valuation Metrics:**
- **P/E (trailing):** **~143x** — a genuinely alarming number in isolation, and it reflects trailing earnings that predate the current advanced-packaging ramp rather than any assessment of current earnings power
- **P/E (forward):** **~34–36x**. Note the size of the trailing-to-forward compression: the market expects earnings to roughly quadruple, and the backlog substantiates that expectation.
- **P/S:** **14.1x trailing, 12.9x forward** — expensive on any absolute basis; this is the metric that makes the position a Buy rather than a Strong Buy
- **EV/EBITDA:** **~30.4x** current, against a five-year average of **19.1x**
- **PEG:** roughly **0.6–0.7** on forward earnings against expected EPS growth — the growth rate is what rescues an otherwise stretched multiple
- Market cap **$6.56B**, enterprise value **$6.20B** — a modest net cash position
- **ROIC of 25.0%** against ROE of 7.6%: the divergence indicates a large equity base relative to invested capital, i.e., a balance sheet with capacity to fund growth without dilution

**Growth Profile:** Q1 2026 revenue **$121.7 million** with EPS of **$0.70** versus **$0.69** consensus; Q2 guided to **$129–131 million** and subsequently reported at record levels. Management expects **second-half 2026 revenue more than 25% higher than the first half**, and **advanced packaging revenue to grow ~70% from Q1 to Q4 2026**. **AI-related products drive roughly 50% of revenue.** The Eagle G5 and Hawk platforms represented 30% of 2025 revenue with revenue **expected to double in 2026**. The closed **Visual Layer acquisition** adds AI inspection, metrology, and a software layer with better margin characteristics than hardware.

**Competitive Moat:** Advanced packaging inspection is a specialized niche in which Camtek holds a leading position at exactly the process steps AI silicon requires most — hybrid bonding, HBM stacking, and 2.5D/3D integration. The moat is qualification depth: inspection recipes are developed jointly with customers over quarters and encode process knowledge that does not transfer to a competitor's tool. The **$260 million of two-customer HBM commitment spanning 2026–2027** is the tangible expression of that lock-in — HBM manufacturing is concentrated among three players, and being designed into two of them is close to structural.

**Key Risk:** The valuation leaves no margin for error. At ~13x forward sales and ~30x EV/EBITDA against a 19x historical average, any order deceleration, HBM digestion pause, or advanced-packaging capacity pushout would compress the multiple hard and fast. HBM customer concentration is a double-edged moat — two customers representing $260M of committed revenue is excellent until one of them pauses. Analyst targets range widely from **$120 to $190** (average ~$167–188), which is an honest reflection of how much the outcome depends on the HBM capex path.

**Rating: Buy**

### 4. MKSI — MKS Instruments, Inc.

**Thesis:** MKS supplies the vacuum, photonics, laser, and process-control subsystems that go into essentially every deposition and etch tool built, giving it near-index exposure to a WFE market its own management sees reaching **$170–180 billion by 2027** — and it is executing a visible deleveraging that should re-rate the equity independent of the cycle.

**Valuation Metrics:**
- **P/E (trailing):** mid-to-high twenties on trailing non-GAAP earnings
- **P/E (forward):** roughly **20–24x** on annualized run-rate earnings, extrapolating from Q1 2026 EPS of **$2.30** (versus **$2.04** expected)
- **P/S:** **~4.5–4.7x** on market cap of **$18.75B**
- **EV/EBITDA:** elevated relative to P/E because of the debt load — enterprise value of **$22.2B** versus market cap of **$18.75B**, a **$3.45B** wedge
- **PEG:** below 1 on current earnings growth of **+35% YoY**
- **Balance sheet:** $1.5B term loans, $1.4B convertible senior notes, €1B senior notes, **$885M cash**, **~$4.69B total debt**, net leverage **~4x EBITDA**

**Growth Profile:** Q1 2026 revenue **+15% YoY** with EPS **+35% YoY** — operating leverage working as designed, with earnings growing more than twice as fast as revenue. Free cash flow of **$147 million (15% of revenue)** is the number that underwrites the deleveraging case. Management made a **$100 million voluntary term loan B prepayment** and a further voluntary **$100 million prepayment in October**, which is the clearest possible signal of capital allocation priority.

**Competitive Moat:** MKS occupies a position that is hard to attack from either direction. Its subsystems — vacuum solutions, power delivery, photonics, lasers, process control — are designed into OEM tool platforms with multi-year qualification cycles, and after the Atotech acquisition it also holds specialty chemicals and materials positions in advanced packaging and PCB manufacturing. The breadth means MKS captures WFE spending regardless of which OEM wins which socket and regardless of whether the growth comes from front-end or back-end processing. Very few suppliers have that horizontal a footprint.

**Key Risk:** **Leverage is the whole risk.** At ~4x net EBITDA, MKS has materially less cushion than its peers, and the convertible notes have been **reclassified as short-term debt** because conversion conditions have been met — a real near-term capital structure event that could mean dilution, cash outlay, or refinancing at less favorable terms. In a WFE downcycle, high leverage against a cyclical EBITDA base compounds against the equity. The bull case explicitly depends on WFE reaching that $170–180B 2027 figure; if spending merely plateaus at 2026 levels, the deleveraging path stretches out and the multiple stays compressed. This is the highest-risk name in today's list and is rated accordingly.

**Rating: Accumulate**

### 5. PLAB — Photronics, Inc.

**Thesis:** Photronics is the deep-value, asset-backed name in the group — a critical photomask supplier trading at **12.3x trailing earnings** with roughly **$740 million of net cash** against a **$1.98 billion market cap**, meaning **37% of the market value is cash** and the operating business is being valued at approximately **$1.24 billion**. It is explicitly deteriorating in the near term, and the price already reflects considerably worse than that.

**Valuation Metrics:**
- **P/E (trailing):** **12.31x**
- **P/E (forward):** **17.92x** on original estimates; **~13x** on the post-decline share price
- **P/S:** roughly **2.4x** on ~$830M annualized revenue
- **EV/EBITDA:** low single digits to mid-single digits — the standout metric. Enterprise value of **$1.24B** against a business generating meaningful EBITDA is the core of the argument.
- **PEG:** not meaningful — earnings are declining near term, and this is a value/asset case rather than a growth case. Stated plainly.
- **P/B:** 9% below its trailing four-quarter average of 1.6x
- Market cap **$1.98B**, enterprise value **$1.24B** → **~$740M net cash**, ~37% of market cap

**Growth Profile — and the honest negative case:** Photronics **missed** fiscal Q2 2026 on both lines: revenue of **$209.9 million** (2.8–3.4% below consensus) and non-GAAP diluted EPS of **$0.42**, roughly **20–21% below** expectations. Q3 guidance was weaker still — revenue of **$207–215 million** against a **$218.7–219.7 million** consensus, and non-GAAP EPS of **$0.39–0.45** versus a **$0.53–0.56** consensus. **This is a business in a near-term downturn, and any thesis that pretends otherwise is dishonest.** The argument is not that fundamentals are improving; it is that at 12x earnings with 37% of the market cap in cash and no leverage, a company supplying an indispensable input is priced for permanent impairment that the industry structure makes implausible. Consensus price target sits at **$51.50** against a **$33.55** share price — roughly **53.5%** upside — though such a wide gap after a guidance miss should be read as analyst estimates lagging rather than as a reliable forecast.

**Competitive Moat:** Photomasks are a genuine oligopoly. Every chip design requires masks; masks are custom per design and per layer; and the merchant photomask market is served by a handful of suppliers alongside captive operations at the largest IDMs. Photronics has manufacturing presence in the US, Taiwan, Korea, and China — geographic breadth that matters in a world of localized supply chains and export controls, and that is genuinely difficult to replicate. Mature-node mask demand, which is Photronics' core, is far stickier than leading-edge and is supported by the same automotive and industrial recovery visible in Microchip's and TI's results. Critically, the **net cash position and absence of leverage** mean the company can absorb a multi-quarter trough without financial distress or dilution — the precondition for any value thesis working.

**Key Risk:** The value trap scenario is real and must be named. Photomask pricing is under pressure, near-term guidance is deteriorating, and mature-node capex — Photronics' primary demand driver — is the *least* levered part of the semiconductor market to AI. If the industry bifurcates permanently, with leading-edge and advanced packaging absorbing all growth while mature nodes stagnate, Photronics could stay cheap indefinitely. The China manufacturing footprint that provides geographic diversification is simultaneously an export-control and geopolitical exposure. Position size accordingly: this is the smallest-conviction, highest-optionality name in the group.

**Rating: Accumulate**

## Risk Factors

**Ranked by severity for the week ahead.**

### 1. Wednesday's CPI print against a hawkish-leaning FOMC — SEVERE

This is the dominant near-term risk and it is asymmetric. Three FOMC members already dissented in favor of hiking in July. Consensus looks for **+0.09% MoM headline / ~3.42% YoY** and **+0.21% MoM core / ~2.52% YoY**. A core print at or above **+0.3% MoM** would convert three dissenters into a plausible September majority and force a repricing of a market that has spent 2026 migrating from cut expectations to hike expectations. Equity positioning — small caps up 19.5% YTD, S&P near highs after a 3.58% week — has essentially no cushion for a hawkish surprise. **High-multiple, long-duration equities, including several of the semicap names discussed above, are the most exposed.**

### 2. Valuation compression risk in AI-levered semiconductors — HIGH

The spotlight section catalogued nine quality names trading at multiples their growth rates do not support: KLA at ~43.6x forward, Nova at ~43x, GlobalFoundries at ~40.3x on ~8.4% forecast revenue growth, Cadence at ~45.4x, Ichor at ~68.5x, Cohu at 5.8x forward sales against a 2.4x five-year average, Infineon at ~77x trailing. AMD fell 7% on a beat-and-raise. This is what a sector looks like when the bar has moved above what good results can clear. A multiple reset would drag the entire complex — including today's picks — regardless of individual company execution.

### 3. Consumer deceleration — MODERATE TO HIGH

Two independent signals landed this week: a **weak July jobs report**, and Jefferies' Best Buy downgrade citing a **"clear downshift" in consumer purchase intentions during July**. The market has been treating labor softness as dovish-positive, which works only while growth stays comfortably positive. If soft employment combines with a hot CPI print Wednesday, the stagflationary read is one almost nobody is positioned for, and both legs of the current bull case — Fed relief and earnings growth — weaken simultaneously.

### 4. Geopolitical reversal risk in energy — MODERATE

The risk premium has been *removed* from crude on US–Iran diplomatic progress, with Brent and WTI down nearly 4%. Because the premium is out, there is now substantial room for it to be violently re-added. The **Strait of Hormuz carries ~20% of global oil supply**, and the deal remains unresolved. A reversal would be doubly damaging: it would re-inflate the energy component of CPI precisely when the Fed needs it falling, converting a geopolitical event directly into a monetary policy tightening event.

### 5. AI capex quality scrutiny — MODERATE, but rising

SpaceX fell 13.6% on $16 billion of quarterly capex despite nearly doubling revenue to $7.8 billion. Shopify rose 17% by showing AI returns without margin damage. The market has begun differentiating between AI spending that produces returns and AI spending that produces depreciation. With hyperscaler capex running at **$660–725 billion in 2026**, any broad shift in how investors treat that spending — from growth investment to margin drag — would hit the entire AI supply chain, including every semicap name in today's picks. This is the slowest-moving risk on the list and potentially the largest.

## Bottom Line

The flat large-cap tape today masked the more important development: capital is rotating down the market-cap spectrum, with the Russell 2000 up 1.10% on the session and 19.49% on the year — its best since 2003 — as AI infrastructure spending finally reaches the packaging, inspection, test, and subsystem layer where mid-caps live. SEMI's upward revision of 2026 WFE spending to **$143.9 billion (+23.1%)** validates that rotation with hard forecast data, and today's picks are deliberately positioned in the parts of that chain where valuation has not yet caught up to the order books. But the entire structure sits on Wednesday's CPI: with three FOMC members already dissenting toward a hike and the market carrying no cushion after a 3.58% week, a hot core print would compress exactly the high-multiple, capex-levered equities that have driven 2026's gains. **Own the supply chain, not the narrative — and keep enough dry powder to buy the CPI reaction rather than be forced to sell it.**
