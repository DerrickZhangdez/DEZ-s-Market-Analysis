## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The Fed is stuck, and the dissents are getting loud.** At the July 29 meeting the FOMC held the federal funds rate at **3.50%–3.75%** for a fifth consecutive meeting — the second decision under new Chair **Kevin Warsh** — explicitly citing "elevated" inflation. Critically, **three officials dissented**, an unusually fractured vote that tells you the committee has no consensus on direction. This was a cliffhanger meeting, not a routine hold.

**The inflation picture reversed inside of a month.** June inflation data eased *sharply*, which had markets leaning dovish. Then the Middle East escalated through July, fuel prices climbed, and the disinflation story broke down. This is the key macro fact of the moment: the current inflation impulse is **energy-driven and geopolitical**, not demand-driven. That distinction matters enormously for positioning, because an energy shock is precisely the kind of inflation the Fed cannot fix with rate policy without inducing a recession — it can only wait it out or tolerate it.

**Rates markets have flipped from cuts to hikes.** Fed funds futures now price roughly **4% by year-end**, above the current target range. Some forecasters have moved to expecting *higher* rates before December. For a market at record highs trading on long-duration AI growth stories, a repricing of the front end toward hikes is the single most underappreciated risk in the tape. Equities are currently choosing to ignore it.

**What to watch this week:** Non-farm payrolls is the marquee release, alongside a run of Fed speakers. Given the split committee, individual Fed commentary carries more signal than usual right now — dissenting members' speeches are effectively live policy debate. A hot payroll print combined with firm energy prices would be the most bearish combination available for duration-sensitive equities.

## Geopolitical Factors

Geopolitics is not a sidebar in this market; it is the primary transmission channel into inflation and therefore into the Fed path.

- **Strait of Hormuz — the central risk.** Renewed attacks on commercial shipping have left the fragile **U.S.–Iran interim peace deal hanging by a thread**. The waterway carries roughly **20% of the world's seaborne crude**. At the peak of the initial standoff, Iranian transit restrictions sent **Brent past $120/bbl**, its highest since 2022. Middle East talks were flagged as a source of uncertainty in today's session — meaning the market is actively watching the negotiation, and headline risk is two-sided and immediate.
- **Persistent volatility regime.** Hostilities erupted in February 2026 and volatility has been "the new normal" for oil ever since. Empirically, oil-price volatility during high-geopolitical-risk periods runs roughly **twice** that of calm periods — a fact worth respecting when sizing energy-sensitive positions.
- **Structurally higher floor.** ExxonMobil, Chevron, and Shell have all warned that oil prices are likely to **remain elevated even after the conflict ends**, implying the supply-side risk premium is being priced into forward curves, not just spot.
- **Venezuela.** A separate standoff is contributing to the broader oil risk premium, giving the market two independent geopolitical supply threats rather than one.
- **Semiconductor nationalism.** Less acute but strategically important: the drive toward **semiconductor self-sufficiency** across major regions is now a named driver of fab equipment spending. Trade and export-control policy is functionally an industrial subsidy for the tool makers in today's spotlight — a rare case where geopolitical friction *helps* a sector's revenue.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: Semiconductors & Semicap Equipment

This is where today's research went deepest, and the conclusion is that **semicap equipment is a structurally better place to be than semiconductor design right now** — for a specific and durable reason: spending is being driven by rising complexity per wafer, not merely by more wafers.

**The spending cycle is large, confirmed, and multi-year.** SEMI projects worldwide **300mm fab equipment spending +18% to $133 billion in 2026**, then **+14% to $151 billion in 2027**, totaling **$374 billion over the next three years**. Broader wafer fab equipment (WFE) spending is projected at a record **$135.2 billion in 2026** (+9% y/y), with total equipment sales reaching a record **$156 billion in 2027**. Citigroup expects **Samsung, TSMC, and Intel to all guide capex positively** — when the three largest spenders align, the tool order book is visible for several quarters.

**Why complexity is the real story.** Lam Research has noted that modern chip complexity has essentially **doubled the required equipment steps**: a 2nm wafer now requires **over 90 mask steps and roughly 20 EUV exposures**. Each node transition forces fab operators to spend approximately **20% more on equipment**. This is the crux of the investment case. Even in a flat-unit world, revenue per wafer rises for the companies selling steps — and it rises fastest for the vendors selling the steps that *multiply* with complexity: metrology, inspection, test, cleaning, and advanced packaging.

**Where the complexity multiplier is sharpest:**

1. **Process control (metrology/inspection).** AI chip manufacturing requires more process control steps per wafer than any prior generation — every additional layer requires inspection and metrology. This makes AI chip complexity a *structural* demand driver rather than a cyclical one. KLA has the highest multiple of the equipment majors precisely because the market understands this.
2. **Advanced packaging.** HBM and chiplet architectures have turned packaging from a commodity back-end step into a technical bottleneck. **HBM demand is forecast to grow 90% y/y in 2026**, and AI server growth is projected at a **42% CAGR**. Thermocompression bonding, electro-chemical plating, and 3D inspection are the chokepoints.
3. **Test and probe.** HBM stacks require dramatically more test coverage per unit of shipped memory. Probe card demand scales with both die count and stack height — a double multiplier.
4. **Wet processing.** Every added layer requires additional cleaning steps, making wet clean a near-linear function of process step count.

**Valuation discipline is essential here, because the sector is not uniformly cheap.** Research surfaced several names trading at multiples that already discount a perfect cycle. **Camtek (CAMT)** deserves specific mention as a name I examined and rejected: excellent operational momentum (>25% expected H2 2026 revenue growth, strong AI-linked orders, the Visual Layer acquisition) but a **trailing P/E of ~144x** and a valuation that screens roughly **330% above estimated fair value**, with one DCF pegging fair value at $37.56 against a market price implying ~80% overvaluation. Great business, unacceptable entry price. Similarly, **Nova (NVMI)** trades at **~51.6x forward** against a "fair" ratio nearer 38x, with three-year revenue growth forecast at **15% — below the 22% industry forecast** — a rare case of paying a premium multiple for below-sector growth. **Onto Innovation (ONTO)** is more defensible (>30% projected 2026 revenue growth, ~36x forward) but its **129x trailing P/E** and a fair-ratio estimate near 58x leave no margin for a single order-timing slip.

**And cheap is not automatically value.** **Photronics (PLAB)** screens as the most statistically undervalued equipment name, but the fundamentals are actively deteriorating: fiscal Q2 revenue was **flat at ~$210 million and missed estimates**, non-GAAP EPS of $0.42 came in **~20% below expectations**, and **IC revenue declined 5%** with the shortfall masked by a 13% jump in lower-quality flat-panel display masks. Shares sit far below their $56 52-week high after a prior quarter that dropped 29% on a sales miss. That is a value trap, not a value opportunity, and it is excluded from today's picks despite screening well.

The picks below are drawn from the parts of this sector where **the complexity multiplier is strongest and the multiple is still defensible**.

### Artificial Intelligence

The AI capex numbers have crossed from large into almost abstract. Aggregate hyperscaler build-out for 2026 is estimated in the **$690–725 billion** range. The headline development is that **Nvidia and OpenAI are in talks over a backstop of up to $250 billion** to fund OpenAI's plan to lease a **10-gigawatt campus in Pike County, Ohio**, with reporting suggesting the total project could approach **$500 billion** — potentially the largest data center on earth by power capacity. Structurally important detail: the guarantee would cover **lease and construction debt, not the Nvidia chips**, which are being negotiated separately. That is vendor financing of the *real estate and power*, and it tells you the binding constraint on AI scaling is no longer silicon supply — it is **power, land, and construction capital**.

Individual capex commitments: **Microsoft at ~$190 billion CY2026**; **Meta at $115–135 billion**, including a 1GW Ohio site and a Louisiana facility that could scale to **5GW**; **Google Cloud growing 63%** and **AWS 28%**. Competitively, OpenAI is racing to lock in compute against **Anthropic, Google, Amazon, and Meta**, all spending at comparable scale. The investable read-through is that the *arms race itself* is the durable trade — every participant must overbuild to avoid being compute-constrained, which guarantees demand for the picks-and-shovels layer regardless of which model provider ultimately wins.

**Today's marquee AI print: Palantir.** PLTR **surged roughly 14–16%** after posting **93% revenue growth** in Q2 and raising full-year guidance to **$7.650–7.662 billion**. ### Software & SaaS

The most interesting tension in the market. All three major cloud platforms posted growth **reacceleration** last quarter, which argues the infrastructure layer is capturing the value while the application layer fights for it. **DigitalOcean** announced a plan to repurchase up to **$500 million** in convertible notes funded by a direct stock offering, deleveraging to invest in AI-native cloud.

This is precisely why today's picks avoid application SaaS entirely. The July 30 report's software basket (ESTC, DT, NTNX, CHKP, MANH) sits in the crosshairs of the agentic-AI disruption debate — a debate that will take several quarters to resolve and which no valuation screen can currently settle.

## Stock Picks — Valuation-Based

Five picks, all in today's spotlight sector, spanning **five distinct sub-sectors** of the semiconductor supply chain. **Zero** are above $1T market cap; **all five** are outside the Magnificent Seven; **none** appear on the exclusion list. Each was selected because the complexity multiplier described above drives its revenue, and because the multiple remains defensible against that growth.

### 1. KLIC — Kulicke & Soffa Industries, Inc.
*Sub-sector: Advanced packaging assembly equipment (thermocompression bonding)*

- **Thesis:** KLIC is the purest listed play on thermocompression bonding, the specific process chokepoint for HBM stacking and chiplet assembly, and it has just inflected from a multi-year downcycle into steep growth — Q2 FY26 revenue rose **49.8% y/y** with guidance implying continued acceleration. Management has nearly **doubled 2026 capex** to expand TCB capacity toward roughly **$400 million in annual system sales**, which is management betting its own balance sheet on demand visibility.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Trailing P/E is elevated and not the right lens — the company only just swung back to GAAP profitability ($35.1M net income, $0.66 GAAP diluted EPS in Q2). The forward math is what matters: Q3 guidance of **~$1.00 non-GAAP EPS** annualizes toward a run-rate near $4.00+, placing the forward multiple in the low-to-mid 20s against ~50% growth. P/S is roughly 3–4x on a forward revenue run-rate approaching $1.2B. EV/EBITDA is distorted by the trough base; PEG screens well below 1.0x on forward estimates given the growth rate.
- **Growth Profile:** Q2 FY26 revenue **$242.6M, +21.5% sequential and +49.8% y/y**. Q3 guided to **~$310M ±$20M** (another ~28% sequential step). Q4 expected **+5–10% sequential**. Non-GAAP EPS of $0.79 in Q2 guided to ~$1.00 in Q3 — EPS is compounding faster than revenue as fixed costs lever. Fluxless TCB revenue rising sequentially across OSAT, foundry, and IDM customers.
- **Competitive Moat:** TCB is a qualified, process-integrated tool — once a bonder is designed into an HBM or chiplet production flow, requalifying a competitor costs the customer months of yield risk. KLIC's installed base in wire bonding gives it incumbent relationships across essentially every OSAT on earth, and its fluxless TCB solution is qualified at multiple customer types simultaneously.
- **Key Risk:** **KLIC reports earnings tomorrow (August 5).** This is an acute, dated, binary risk and the single largest caveat on this pick — the stock is already **up over 80% YTD**, so expectations are high and the setup is asymmetric to the downside on any guidance wobble. Investors unwilling to hold through a print should wait one day. Longer term, the capacity expansion is a fixed-cost commitment that would hurt badly if HBM packaging demand slipped.
- **Rating:** **Buy** (would be Strong Buy absent the next-day earnings risk)

### 2. FORM — FormFactor, Inc.
*Sub-sector: Test & measurement / probe cards*

- **Thesis:** Probe card demand scales with both HBM stack height and die count, giving FormFactor a double multiplier on the exact part of the memory market that is growing fastest — and the company grew its HBM probe card business **more than 50%** from H1 2025 to H1 2026 while **capacity-constrained**, meaning reported revenue is currently understating true demand. The multiple has already compressed sharply as earnings inflect: **39x forward versus above 112x trailing**.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Forward P/E **~39x**, trailing P/E **>112x** — the gap is the earnings inflection, and forward estimates imply EPS roughly tripling. Consensus for the June quarter was **$0.61 EPS on $240M revenue**, representing **+126% EPS growth** y/y. At ~$98.80, against a mean analyst target of **$144.67**, the implied upside is ~46%. P/S sits near 4x on ~$950M–1B forward revenue. PEG is comfortably below 1.0x on triple-digit EPS growth.
- **Growth Profile:** Q1 2026 revenue **$226.1M, +32% y/y**, with net income **tripling to $20.4M** — and that was achieved while absorbing heavy restructuring and factory start-up costs, so underlying margin is better than reported. Full-year 2026 revenue projected **+18.5%** with margin upside. Company forecasts **record DRAM probe card revenue** in the current quarter on another step-up in HBM.
- **Competitive Moat:** Probe cards are custom-engineered per customer design and must be qualified against specific die layouts — switching costs are high and design cycles lock in the vendor for a product generation. FormFactor is the scale leader in advanced probe cards, and in a capacity-constrained market the incumbent with the largest footprint captures the marginal order by default.
- **Key Risk:** Capacity is the bottleneck, and relief is not immediate — the Farmer's Branch facility only begins **pilot production in Q4** with the real ramp in **2027**. If HBM demand growth (forecast +90% y/y in 2026) decelerates before that capacity lands, FormFactor will have added fixed cost into a softening market and the multiple would compress hard.
- **Rating:** **Strong Buy**

### 3. RMBS — Rambus Inc.
*Sub-sector: Semiconductor IP & licensing / memory interface*

- **Thesis:** The cheapest way to own HBM growth in this report — Rambus licenses the memory interface IP embedded in essentially every meaningful AI memory architecture, earning **~80% gross margins** with none of the capital intensity or capacity risk that constrains the equipment names. At roughly **24x forward** for a business with this margin structure and a fortress balance sheet, it is the clearest valuation-to-quality mismatch I found.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Forward P/E **~24x** — materially below the semiconductor industry average near 58–64x and the cheapest multiple among today's picks. Gross margin **79.73%**. Net cash position of **$786 million** provides real downside support and optionality. P/S is elevated on headline revenue but the licensing mix means gross-profit-based multiples look far better than revenue-based ones. PEG is favorable against a forecast path toward **$963.4M revenue and $355.6M earnings by 2028** — roughly a doubling of earnings power from a 2025 base of $707.63M revenue.
- **Growth Profile:** Q1 2026 GAAP revenue **$180.2M** (licensing billings $70.8M, product revenue $88.0M, contract and other $22.6M). Q2 2026 product revenue of **$99.2M, +~22% y/y** — product is the higher-growth, higher-visibility line. In March 2026 Rambus launched **HBM4E Memory Controller IP** at **16 Gbps per pin and up to 4.1 TB/s** per device, positioning it a full generation ahead of current-volume HBM4.
- **Competitive Moat:** IP licensing with genuine standard-essential characteristics. Rambus's interface IP is designed into customer silicon at the architecture stage, making it effectively unremovable for that product generation, and the patent estate plus decades of memory-interface engineering create a moat that does not require capex to defend. Margins near 80% are themselves evidence of pricing power.
- **Key Risk:** Licensing revenue is inherently lumpier than product revenue, and the model depends on continued design wins at a small number of very large memory and compute customers. Concentration is real: losing socket position at a single major HBM manufacturer would be materially damaging, and litigation or renegotiation cycles can create sudden revenue discontinuities.
- **Rating:** **Strong Buy**

### 4. AEIS — Advanced Energy Industries, Inc.
*Sub-sector: Precision power conversion & subsystems*

- **Thesis:** AEIS sells precision power into *both* semiconductor fabs and AI data centers, giving it two independent AI-levered growth engines — and both are compounding simultaneously, with semiconductor revenue hitting a record **$278M (+33% y/y)** and management guiding semi revenue to accelerate **over 30% y/y in 2H 2026**. Margin expansion to the best levels since 2019 confirms the mix shift is structural, not cyclical.
- **Valuation Metrics:** **~23x forward EV/EBIT** — a premium, but the cheapest growth-adjusted multiple among the equipment-adjacent names given EPS growing far faster than revenue. Gross margin **40.1%, +220bps y/y**, the highest since the 2019 Artesyn acquisition. Adjusted EBITDA **$137M, +84% y/y**, which is the metric that best captures the operating leverage. PEG is below 1.0x against ~70% EPS growth. EV/EBITDA in the mid-to-high teens on forward estimates.
- **Growth Profile:** Q1 2026 revenue **$511M, +26% y/y**, beating ~$505M consensus. Non-GAAP EPS **$2.09, +70% y/y**; GAAP EPS from continuing operations **$1.59** — both above guidance midpoint. Data center computing revenue **$194M, +102% y/y** in Q1 (second consecutive record), moderating to $192M/+35% y/y in Q2. Management **raised** full-year revenue growth guidance from high-teens to **low/mid-20s%**, a second consecutive year above 20%, and lifted the data center outlook to mid-30%.
- **Competitive Moat:** Precision power supplies for plasma etch and deposition are qualified into specific process recipes — the tool OEM cannot swap the power subsystem without requalifying the entire process, making AEIS a designed-in component supplier rather than a commodity vendor. That same engineering capability transfers to high-density data center power, where efficiency at scale is the binding constraint.
- **Key Risk:** Sequential softness already visible in the data center segment (Q2 down 1% q/q) shows this line is order-lumpy, not smoothly compounding. AEIS is also one step removed from end demand — it sells to tool OEMs, so it absorbs the bullwhip effect and would see inventory corrections amplified relative to the fabs themselves.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

### 5. ACMR — ACM Research, Inc.
*Sub-sector: Wet processing — cleaning & electro-chemical plating*

- **Thesis:** Every additional process layer requires additional cleaning, making wet processing a near-linear function of the rising step count at the heart of today's spotlight thesis — and ACM is growing well above the equipment market with **ECP revenue +205% y/y** and advanced packaging **+62% y/y**. It is the only pick trading below a credible fair-value estimate, at **$62.99 against a $70.50 narrative fair value**.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Trailing P/E **~64x** versus a semiconductor industry average near 57.9x — a modest premium to sector, but against a "tailored fair" P/E of **~53.3x** the stock screens as **modestly undervalued** on a price basis given the growth delta. Forward multiple compresses materially on the guided revenue ramp. P/S is roughly 3x against the $1.08–1.175B FY26 revenue guide. PEG is near or below 1.0x against 20–30% revenue growth with faster growth in the highest-value product lines.
- **Growth Profile:** FY2026 revenue outlook **reaffirmed at $1.08–1.175 billion, +20–30% y/y**. Q1 2026 revenue **$231.3M, +34.2% y/y**, exceeding expectations. Total shipments **$240.7M, +53.6% y/y** — shipments running ahead of revenue is a leading indicator of future recognition. The mix shift is the story: **ECP +205% y/y** and **advanced packaging +62% y/y** are both higher-value than the legacy cleaning base.
- **Competitive Moat:** ACM's SAPS/TEBO megasonic cleaning technology is differentiated in removing contamination from high-aspect-ratio structures without damaging fragile features — a problem that gets strictly harder at every node, so the moat deepens with complexity. Wet tools are recipe-qualified and sticky once installed.
- **Key Risk:** **China concentration is the dominant risk and it is not small.** ACM's business is heavily weighted to Chinese fabs, placing it directly in the path of export controls and geopolitical escalation — the same "semiconductor self-sufficiency" trend that funds its order book could reverse violently via sanctions. Compounding this, research flagged **margin pressure and weaker performance in the core cleaning segment**, meaning the growth is carried by newer lines while the base business softens. This is the highest-risk pick here and is sized accordingly in the rating.
- **Rating:** **Accumulate**

## Risk Factors

**1. Rate repricing toward hikes (severity: high, underpriced).** Futures now imply **~4% by year-end** versus a 3.50%–3.75% current range. The market is at record highs on long-duration growth stories while the front end quietly prices tightening. A hot payroll print this week is the most likely trigger for a violent repricing. This is my top-ranked risk specifically *because* the tape is ignoring it.

**2. Strait of Hormuz escalation (severity: high, acute).** A fragile interim deal governs 20% of seaborne crude. A breakdown puts Brent back above $120, which feeds directly into the energy-driven inflation the Fed has already labeled "elevated" — creating the worst macro combination available: accelerating inflation the Fed cannot cut into. Headline risk is immediate and two-sided.

**3. AI capex concentration and circular financing (severity: high, medium-term).** The **$690–725 billion** build-out increasingly rests on vendor-financed structures — Nvidia potentially backstopping **$250 billion** of OpenAI's lease and construction debt is the clearest example. When the primary chip supplier guarantees the customer's real estate obligations, supplier revenue and customer solvency become correlated in a way that is invisible in good times. Any crack in AI monetization transmits straight through the picks-and-shovels complex, including today's picks.

**4. Semicap multiple compression (severity: medium, sector-specific).** Much of this sector already discounts a perfect cycle — CAMT at ~144x trailing and ~330% above fair-value estimates, NVMI at ~51.6x forward on below-sector growth, ONTO at 129x trailing. Even with defensible fundamentals, a sector-wide de-rating would hit the good names alongside the stretched ones. This is the main risk to today's basket as a group.

**5. SaaS disintermediation by agentic AI (severity: medium, structural).** The "$2 trillion erased" narrative may be overstated, but IBM's cloud slowdown and single-digit-to-low-double-digit growth at Box and DocuSign suggest genuine pressure on per-seat licensing. Unresolved for several quarters, and a reason to prefer infrastructure over applications.

## Bottom Line

The market is correctly identifying that AI capex has broken containment into industrials — Caterpillar's first $20 billion quarter is a more important datapoint for cycle durability than Palantir's 93% growth — but it is incorrectly ignoring a front end that has quietly repriced toward hikes with a three-way-dissenting Fed and an energy shock it cannot control. That combination argues for staying invested in the AI build-out while moving *down* the stack, away from application software and richly-valued design names toward the equipment, test, and IP layers where complexity per wafer does the compounding regardless of which model provider wins. Today's basket is deliberately positioned there: buy the steps, not the story — and be honest that the single cheapest name here (RMBS at ~24x with 80% gross margins) is cheap because licensing is lumpy, not because the market has missed it.
