# Daily Market Report — Tuesday, September 29, 2026

## Executive Summary

Equities sold off for a second straight session as the inflation-and-rates complex reasserted itself over the AI growth story. The S&P 500 fell 0.8% to 7,683.69 and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.9% (-248.34) to 26,820.38, with the damage heavily concentrated in the semiconductor and high-multiple technology names that have led all year — Arm Holdings alone fell roughly 8.7% after a 28% September run. The proximate cause is not an AI demand problem but a discount-rate problem: the 10-year Treasury yield near 5.24%, core PCE above 3% every month of 2026, higher oil on renewed Iran escalation, and a CME FedWatch-implied 70.3% probability of *another* 25bp Fed **hike** in October, up from 17.7% a month ago. Critically, breadth told a more nuanced story than the index prints — eight of eleven S&P sectors finished higher and Industrials (XLI) rose 1%, even as decliners beat advancers 3.55-to-1 on the NYSE, a sign that the selling was a narrow, violent de-rating of crowded momentum rather than a broad risk-off.

## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The Fed is hiking. This is the fact that most portfolios are still not positioned for.**

At its September meeting the FOMC voted *unanimously* to raise the federal funds rate 25bp to **3.75%–4.00%** — the first increase in more than three years. The statement language was blunt: "Inflation remains elevated." The September Summary of Economic Projections raised the year-end 2026 PCE inflation projection to **3.7%**, a full point above the March projection. Core PCE has printed above 3% in every month of 2026.

Market pricing has moved hard to meet the Fed:
- **October:** 70.3% implied probability of a further 25bp hike (vs. 57.6% a week ago, 17.7% a month ago).
- **December:** futures imply roughly **4.2%** by year-end; J.P. Morgan Global Research forecasts another quarter-point in December while explicitly *not* expecting a long hiking cycle.
- **September 2027:** futures imply roughly **4.8%**.

That last figure is the one worth staring at. The strip is now pricing close to 100bp of cumulative additional tightening over the next twelve months. The Fed's own dot plot points to low-4% fed funds in 2027 — meaningfully *below* what futures are pricing. **The market is more hawkish than the Fed.** That gap is the central tension in every asset price right now, and it resolves one of two ways: either the data forces the Fed up to the market (bad for duration and for long-duration equities), or inflation cooperates and the market rallies back toward the dots (a violent relief trade in exactly the names that were hit today).

The proximate inflation impulse is not wage- or services-driven — it is **energy**, imported through the Iran conflict (below). That matters for the equity response function. Energy-led inflation is a tax on margins and a supply shock the Fed cannot fix with rates, which is why the Committee simultaneously "flags job market risks" while staying cautious on inflation. A central bank hiking into an oil shock is the least comfortable position in macro, and it is where we are.

**What to watch this week:** the personal income and outlays report carrying core PCE is the single highest-stakes release; any upside surprise validates the October hike and pressures the long end further. Oil tape and any Strait of Hormuz headlines function as a real-time inflation nowcast. Quarter-end today (September 29) also means pension and target-date rebalancing flows are distorting the tape — read today's breadth with that caveat.

## Geopolitical Factors

The dominant geopolitical fact of 2026 remains the **U.S.–Israel military operation against Iran ("Operation Epic Fury")**, launched February 28, which targeted nuclear and military infrastructure. The market consequences have been non-linear and are the primary channel through which geopolitics is now setting Fed policy:

- **Oil traded at or above $100/bbl for most of Q2.** Prices then collapsed below $70/bbl in mid-June on the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz and supply/demand rebalancing.
- **The conflict is re-escalating.** By mid-July, the volume of attacks on cross-Strait traffic was higher than at any point since April. On September 28, reporting indicated President Trump rebuffed an Iran peace deal, and oil prices rose on the news — that headline is the direct cause of today's inflation-driven selloff in semiconductors.
- **Roughly 20% of global oil and gas trade transits the Strait of Hormuz.** This is the entire fat tail. Any sustained closure is a $100+ oil scenario, a 4%+ core inflation scenario, and a forced-hiking scenario simultaneously.
- **Financial warfare is escalating in parallel.** Treasury Secretary Bessent's "economic onslaught" added restrictions on roughly 60 entities, including Iranian oil revenue networks and shadow-fleet vessels.
- **J.P. Morgan's end-2026 Brent forecast is $78/bbl**, cut from a prior $95 — the sell-side consensus is positioned for de-escalation.

**Energy (XLE) fell 0.9% today even as oil rose.** That divergence is worth flagging: energy equities are not being bought as an inflation hedge, which suggests the market views the oil move as a risk-premium spike rather than a durable demand-driven re-rating. If that view is wrong, energy is deeply under-owned.

Second-order: U.S.–China technology competition and trade tension continue to fragment supply chains, which is a structural cost-push force independent of the Middle East and a reason to doubt that goods disinflation returns as a reliable offset.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: AI Infrastructure & Data Centers — Power, Cooling, Networking, Interconnect, Memory

*(Rotating spotlight: day-of-year 272 mod 6 = 2.)*

The single most important asymmetry in this market is the gap between **the financial fragility of AI model developers** and **the contractual durability of the people selling them physical infrastructure.** Today's selloff hit the former's proxies (Arm -8.7%, Qualcomm -5.9%, Marvell -4.0%) while Industrials rose 1%. That is not noise — it is the market beginning to distinguish between the two, and it is the entire thesis of today's report.

**The demand signal is now contractual, not aspirational.** The September announcement flow is extraordinary even by 2026 standards:

- **OpenAI and NVIDIA** signed a letter of intent to build at least **10 gigawatts** of AI data centers, with NVIDIA committing up to **$100 billion** progressively as systems deploy. Separate reporting describes talks over a backstop of up to **$250 billion** to let OpenAI raise debt against a 10GW campus in Pike County, Ohio.
- **Anthropic** committed **$50 billion** to U.S. data centers with neocloud provider Fluidstack, with facilities in Texas and New York coming online through 2026.
- **xAI's** Memphis-area Colossus 2 may more than double its NVIDIA chip count by year-end.
- **American Electric Power** plans **$78 billion** of investment 2026–2030 specifically to serve data center electricity demand.
- **Gartner** expects total data center spending of **$822 billion in 2026**, +62.5% year over year.
- **Global data center power demand** is now forecast to grow **+220% from 2023 levels to 1,350 TWh by 2030**, a figure revised significantly *upward* from prior estimates.

**Where the bottleneck actually is.** It has moved. Through 2024–2025 the constraint was accelerator supply. In 2026 the binding constraints are, in order: (1) **grid interconnection and generation capacity**, (2) **skilled electrical and mechanical labor**, (3) **thermal management at rack densities that copper-and-air cannot serve**, and (4) **high-speed interconnect** as clusters scale past 100,000 accelerators and optics displaces copper. Every one of those is served by an industrial supply chain with pricing power, multi-year backlogs, and — critically — valuations that have not run to the same place as the semiconductor complex.

**Power and stored power.** The grid cannot deliver firm capacity on the timeline hyperscalers want, which is forcing on-site generation, behind-the-meter arrangements, and — increasingly — large-scale battery energy storage to shave peaks and bridge outages. This is why industrial battery and DC-power businesses that were valued as sleepy GDP-plus compounders are seeing data center order books inflect. **EnerSys (ENS)** guided to stronger second-half growth driven specifically by data centers after a Q1 FY2027 adjusted EPS of $3.66 that beat by 29.8% on record revenue of $935.6M (+5% YoY). The revenue growth is unremarkable; the earnings leverage and the mix shift are not.

**Thermal.** The liquid cooling market is the cleanest secular story in the complex: **$4.8B in 2025 → $6B in 2026 → $27.1B by 2035, an 18.2% CAGR.** **nVent Electric (NVT)** is the most direct mid-cap expression — Q2 2026 net sales of **$1.47B, +53% YoY (+47% organic)**, guidance raised to 37–39% reported / 32–34% organic growth, new products contributing more than 30 points of growth, another liquid-cooling manufacturing expansion announced, and **data center sales expected to exceed $2 billion in 2026, more than double the prior year.** The Zacks consensus for 2026 revenue is $5.45B, +39.96%. **Modine (MOD)** — which this report recommended on September 11 and which is therefore excluded today — guided to 50–70% annual data center growth for two fiscal years, confirming the category rather than the single name.

**Interconnect and optics.** This is where the demand is most certain and the valuations are most dangerous. Optical connectivity has become the defining bottleneck as fiber displaces copper inside the cluster. The growth is real: **Astera Labs (ALAB)** grew revenue **272% YoY** to $268M in Q2 FY2026 and guides to >170% growth for the year; **Lumentum (LITE)** grew fiscal Q2 revenue 66% to $665.5M and is up 166% YTD (+1,269% over one year); **Coherent (COHR)** grew fiscal Q3 revenue 21% to $1.806B and is up 97% YTD; **Credo (CRDO)** acquired DustPhotonics in April to bring silicon photonics in-house across 800G/1.6T/3.2T. **But the multiples are indefensible on any fundamental framework: ALAB at ~251.6x, COHR at ~157.8x, LITE at ~131.5x, CRDO at ~95.3x earnings.** The sell-side describes Credo as trading at a "47.1% discount" to that peer group — which is a statement about the peer group, not about Credo. We are not buying photonics at these prices. We are buying the *cabling and connectivity* layer underneath it, where **Belden (BDC) trades at 16.63x forward earnings** against a sector average of 24.58x and Amphenol's 29.7x.

**Memory and storage.** The most violent repricing in the entire market. **Sandisk (SNDK)** posted fiscal Q4 revenue of **$8.97B, +372% YoY**, with gross margin at **84.6%**, a contracted backlog of **$41.6–42.0 billion**, 2026 enterprise AI storage capacity **entirely sold out** under long-term agreements, and data center now 38% of portfolio (up from 12%). The stock is **+490% YTD and +4,043% over 52 weeks.** This is a genuinely transformed business — and it is also a stock that has moved more than thirty-fold off its low. The honest formulation of the risk is the one the sell-side itself uses: the question is not whether Sandisk can briefly earn 80% gross margins, but whether NAND pricing holds long enough to prevent a violent earnings reversal as supply expands. **We are taking memory exposure today through a systems integrator at 19x forward earnings, not through the pure-play at the top of a 40-bagger.**

**Capacity and land.** The landlords are converting the buildout into contracted rent. **Digital Realty (DLR)** booked **$707 million of annualized GAAP base rent** in Q1 2026 at 100% share, anchored by a **200-megawatt AI inference lease — the largest hyperscale deal in company history** — and guided to roughly 8% core FFO growth. **Equinix (EQIX)** raised 2026 AFFO guidance to $4.20–4.28B (+9–11%) and signed a deal with NVIDIA. **Iron Mountain (IRM)** is +42% YTD. Equinix's interconnection stickiness earns its premium; Digital Realty's wholesale model is lumpier but is the more direct claim on raw megawatts, and trades accordingly.

**Construction and the labor bottleneck.** The contractors' backlogs are the hardest demand evidence available anywhere. **Comfort Systems (FIX)** reported a **record $14.1B backlog, +73% YoY**, with >74% of revenue from new construction including data centers. **IES Holdings (IESC)** backlog reached **$4.5B**, with Communications segment revenue +35% YoY to $367.7M on data center demand — but IESC carries a **trailing P/E of 61**. And note the tape: **Sterling fell ~18% in a week, IES ~18%, and Powell ~14%** in recent trading. Record backlogs did not protect these stocks. That is the clearest possible warning that in this sub-sector, **the backlog is in the price and then some** — and it is why today's picks lean toward the parts of the chain where it demonstrably is not.

**The bear case, stated fairly.** The financing structures are getting more baroque: vendor backstops (NVIDIA financing OpenAI's debt capacity), neocloud intermediaries, and off-balance-sheet special purpose vehicles are now load-bearing parts of the buildout. Industry reporting explicitly notes the financing "gets harder." If credit conditions tighten — and a Fed hiking to 4.8% is precisely how they tighten — the marginal gigawatt does not get built. The correct response is not to avoid the theme but to own the parts of it with **investment-grade counterparties, contracted backlog, real current earnings, and multiples that do not require the buildout to accelerate.** That is the filter applied to every pick below.

### Artificial Intelligence

September's news flow was dominated by capital commitments rather than capability releases, which is itself informative about where the industry is in its cycle. The OpenAI–NVIDIA 10GW letter of intent (up to $100B progressive investment, with a separately reported backstop of up to $250B), Anthropic's $50B Fluidstack partnership across Texas and New York, and xAI's Colossus 2 expansion collectively represent commitments that exceed the annual capex of most national infrastructure programs.

The structural development worth flagging is **vendor financing becoming central to the demand chain.** When the primary accelerator supplier is also underwriting the debt capacity of its largest customer, the revenue quality of that relationship changes. This is not a fraud allegation — it is a standard late-cycle capital-goods pattern, and it historically ends with the vendor holding the asset. Investors underwriting NVIDIA's revenue should be aware that a growing share of it is being financed by NVIDIA.

Separately, CNBC's reporting on **"model fatigue"** — AI labs shipping new model versions at a frenetic pace with diminishing market reaction — is an early signal that capability releases have lost their ability to move equity prices. Both observations point the same direction: the differentiated investment exposure in AI has migrated from the model layer to the physical layer.

### Hardware & Semiconductors

The chip complex was the epicenter. **Arm Holdings (ARM) fell ~8.7%**, closing around $283.33 in the prior session, after a September in which it had gained more than 28% including a 17% single-day jump on September 21. ARM remains **+158% to +235% YTD** depending on the measurement window. **Qualcomm (QCOM) fell ~5.9%**, **Marvell ~4.0%**, with **Intel** and **AMD** down over 5% and 3% respectively in the adjacent session, and the Nasdaq 100 ETF off more than 1%.

Three distinct forces compounded:

1. **Rates.** Higher oil revived inflation fear, which lifted yields, which compressed the group's multiples. This is the dominant factor and it is mechanical.
2. **Profit-taking in the most extended names.** ARM fell "well beyond the semiconductor fund," meaning this was name-specific unwinding, not sector beta.
3. **A genuinely novel reflexivity risk in ARM specifically:** SoftBank increased a **$25 billion margin loan secured by Arm shares.** This creates a feedback loop in which ARM's stock price is an input to its largest holder's borrowing capacity — a structure that amplifies drawdowns mechanically. Investors in ARM are now underwriting SoftBank's balance sheet whether they intend to or not. We regard this as a material, under-discussed, and non-diversifiable risk in the name.

The read-through to the spotlight sector is constructive rather than negative: **none of today's semiconductor weakness reflected a change in data center demand.** The capex commitments detailed above were made in the same month. When the same macro force marks down a 95x-earnings optics name and a 16x-earnings cabling name equally, the latter becomes materially more attractive on a relative basis.

### Software & SaaS

The sector's 2026 narrative has completed a full round trip. In April, the market violently repriced software on AI-agent disruption fears (NET -12%, SNOW -9%, NOW -7% in a single session). That thesis has since been substantially unwound: four major enterprise names reported within nine days in late August/early September and **all four beat**, with CrowdStrike, Salesforce, and Snowflake each posting double-digit-to-mid-30s revenue growth, raising guidance, and rallying double digits. Salesforce's CEO publicly dismissed the "SaaSpocalypse" framing, and the group rallied on it.

The differentiation that emerged is the one that matters: **Zoom grew revenue 4.9% and fell 7% despite a 12% EPS beat.** The market is no longer paying for software as a category — it is paying for demonstrated AI-driven acceleration and punishing its absence, regardless of profitability. Slow-growth software is being valued as a melting ice cube even when it beats.

We took extensive enterprise software exposure in the September 28 report (SSNC, INTA, WK, AGYS, PCOR, DBX) and that positioning stands; today's rotation into hard infrastructure is deliberate diversification away from it rather than a change of view.

**Construction principle for today:** own the AI buildout through **contracted, currently-earning, short-duration industrial cash flows at defensible multiples** — explicitly avoiding both the photonics complex (95–251x earnings) and the memory pure-plays (+490% YTD) where the fundamentals are excellent and the entry prices are not. Every name below earns money today, has identifiable data center demand pull, and trades at a multiple that does not require the buildout to accelerate from here.

### 1. ENS — EnerSys *(Stored & DC power — industrial batteries, BESS, data center backup)*

**Thesis.** EnerSys is priced as a cyclical industrial battery maker while its data center and energy-storage mix inflects; Q1 FY2027 adjusted EPS of **$3.66 beat estimates by 29.8%** on record revenue of $935.6M and management guided to **stronger second-half growth driven specifically by data centers.** At a **14.48x forward P/E**, it is the cheapest genuine data center growth exposure we can find in the entire spotlight sector.

**Valuation Metrics.** Market cap ~$6.12B; enterprise value ~$6.85B. **Trailing P/E 20.56; forward P/E 14.48; EV/EBITDA 11.63.** P/S approximately 1.6x on ~$3.7B trailing revenue.

**Growth Profile.** Revenue +5% YoY in the most recent quarter — deliberately unremarkable, and the point. The earnings line is where the leverage shows: a ~30% EPS beat on 5% revenue growth is mix and margin, not volume. The forward-to-trailing P/E compression from 20.6x to 14.5x embeds roughly 40% consensus EPS growth. If the data center-driven H2 acceleration lands, that estimate is too low.

**Competitive Moat.** Installed-base economics. Industrial batteries are mission-critical, spec'd into equipment and facilities, and replaced on maintenance cycles that follow the original vendor. Switching costs in data center DC power and backup are high because the failure mode is total facility downtime. This is a business that earns its returns from being already installed.

**Key Risk.** Lead and raw-material cost pass-through, and the risk that the data center contribution remains too small a share of total revenue to move the consolidated line in the timeframe the multiple implies. The legacy motive-power and specialty businesses are genuinely GDP-linked, and a Fed hiking to 4.8% is not a friendly backdrop for them. This is a re-rating story that requires two consecutive quarters of data center mix disclosure to work.

**Rating: Strong Buy.** The best risk/reward in the report. You are paying a below-market multiple for an optionality on the single most durable demand trend in the economy, with a real earnings floor underneath.

### 2. BDC — Belden Inc. *(Interconnect, cabling & enterprise/data center networking)*

**Thesis.** Belden is the cheapest way to own the copper-and-fiber layer of the AI buildout — **16.63x forward earnings versus a 24.58x sector average and Amphenol's 29.7x** — while the orders have already inflected: record Q2 2026 orders of **$836M, +19% YoY and +23% sequentially, book-to-bill 1.11**, with a **$20M Tier-1 hyperscaler data center contract** won in the quarter. The Ruckus Networks acquisition, closed July 1, was immediately accretive to revenue, EPS, and EBITDA.

**Valuation Metrics.** **Forward P/E 16.63x** (sector 24.58x, APH 29.7x). Q2 2026 revenue $750.2M; GAAP EPS $1.74; adjusted EPS $2.34. Adjusted EBITDA $146M at a **19.5% margin**, +28% YoY. Adjusted gross margin 39.6%. The Ruckus deal was ~$1.87B, announced April 30 and closed July 1 — leverage is the number to monitor, and is the reason the multiple is where it is.

**Growth Profile.** Revenue **+12% YoY (+8% organic)** to a record $750M. Adjusted EPS **+24% YoY**. EBITDA +28% YoY — a clean margin-expansion profile in which earnings grow at double the rate of revenue. Solutions mix accelerated past 20% post-Ruckus, which is the structural story: Belden is converting from a components vendor to a solutions vendor, and solutions carry higher margins and stickier revenue.

**Competitive Moat.** Specification lock-in and certification. Industrial and data center cabling is designed into facilities, certified to standards, and carries performance guarantees — it is not a spot-market commodity. The Ruckus addition brings wireless networking that bundles with the physical layer. A 1.11 book-to-bill with 19% order growth in a business with this installed base is a durable pricing position.

**Key Risk.** Integration and leverage. A $1.87B acquisition against a company of Belden's size is a significant bite, and integration disappointment in a rising-rate environment would be punished twice — once on execution and once on the balance sheet. Belden also faces genuine competitive pressure in enterprise cabling and building-network infrastructure, notably from Amphenol, which acquired CommScope's Connectivity and Cable Solutions business in January 2026 and commands an estimated **33% share** of AI data center interconnect. Belden is the challenger here, not the incumbent, and the multiple reflects that.

**Rating: Buy.** *Adjacency disclosure: Belden has partial overlap with **CIEN** (Ciena), picked September 16 and inside the one-month look-back, on networking infrastructure. We regard them as materially different businesses — Ciena is coherent optical transport systems and software; Belden is physical-layer cabling, connectors and enterprise wireless — but investors holding CIEN should size accordingly rather than treating these as independent positions.*

### 3. PENG — Penguin Solutions, Inc. *(AI systems integration & advanced memory)*

**Thesis.** Penguin gives you the memory and AI-systems exposure that is driving Sandisk to a 490% YTD gain, at **18.95x forward earnings** — a discount to the sector median — with **Q3 revenue +48% YoY** and management raising 2026 revenue growth guidance to **22%** with adjusted EPS around **$2.60**. This is the valuation-disciplined route into the most explosive sub-sector in the market.

**Valuation Metrics.** **Forward P/E 18.95x**, explicitly at a discount to sector median. 2026 adjusted EPS guidance ~$2.60 on revenue growth raised to 22%. The multiple is the entire argument: PENG trades at roughly one-fifth the earnings multiple of the optics names (ALAB 251.6x, COHR 157.8x, LITE 131.5x, CRDO 95.3x) for exposure to the same buildout.

**Growth Profile.** Q3 revenue **+48% YoY**; full-year 2026 revenue growth guidance **raised to 22%**. The gap between the 48% quarterly print and the 22% annual guide tells you the acceleration is recent and the guide is conservative — the setup for upward revisions. Growth is driven by AI infrastructure and the integrated memory segment specifically, which is the exact demand vector.

**Competitive Moat.** Systems integration and qualification cycles. Penguin builds and supports complete AI compute clusters, which means it sits between commodity component supply and the customer's uptime requirement. Advanced memory modules require qualification with OEMs and hyperscalers — a slow, sticky process. The moat is narrower than EnerSys's or Belden's, which is reflected in the rating below.

**Key Risk.** Memory is a cycle, and this is the most dangerous part of it. Penguin's integrated memory segment benefits from the same NAND/DRAM pricing that took Sandisk's gross margin to 84.6% — and that pricing will normalize. The honest question, as the sell-side puts it for the whole complex, is whether pricing holds long enough to prevent earnings from falling sharply as supply expands. Penguin is also a smaller, less liquid name with meaningful customer concentration in AI systems. **Size this below the other positions.**

**Rating: Buy.** Real growth at a real multiple — but underwrite it as a cyclical with a favorable entry price, not as a compounder.

### 4. AZZ — AZZ Inc. *(Metal coatings & power infrastructure — galvanizing for grid and data center steel)*

**Thesis.** Every megawatt of new data center capacity requires transmission towers, substation structures, cable tray, conduit and structural steel — all of which must be hot-dip galvanized or coated to survive outdoor service. AZZ is the largest provider of that service in North America, earning a **15.5% operating margin** with a **17.3% five-year revenue CAGR**, at **20.3x forward earnings** — a true picks-and-shovels position one layer below the names everyone is crowding into.

**Valuation Metrics.** Share price ~$150.34; **20.3x forward P/E.** Five-year annual revenue growth **17.3%**; operating margin **15.5%**. For a business with these margins in a consolidated industry, a 20x forward multiple is undemanding relative to the 24.58x sector average that Belden is also cheap against.

**Growth Profile.** The 17.3% five-year revenue CAGR is the relevant number — it demonstrates the company compounded through the pre-AI period, so the data center and grid-investment demand layered on top (AEP's $78B program 2026–2030 is one utility of many) is incremental rather than the whole thesis. Margin durability at 15.5% through that growth indicates pricing discipline rather than volume-chasing.

**Competitive Moat.** Geography and permitting. Galvanizing kettles are environmentally permitted, capital-intensive facilities, and the economics of shipping heavy steel mean each plant serves a defined radius. New entrants face permitting timelines measured in years. This is effectively a network of regional local monopolies in a service that cannot be offshored — structural steel is too heavy to ship for coating. It is the most defensible moat in today's list.

**Key Risk.** Zinc input costs and steel-fabrication volume cyclicality. AZZ's demand derives from construction activity, and a Fed hiking toward 4.8% raises project financing costs across non-data-center end markets — utility, transportation, industrial construction — that still constitute the majority of volume. A broad construction slowdown would not be offset by data center strength alone.

**Rating: Buy.** The highest-quality moat here, at a fair rather than cheap price, with genuine cyclical exposure that demands patience.

### 5. DLR — Digital Realty Trust, Inc. *(Data center REIT — wholesale hyperscale capacity)*

**Thesis.** Digital Realty is the most direct listed claim on raw contracted megawatts, and it just proved it: **$707 million of annualized GAAP base rent bookings in Q1 2026** at 100% share, anchored by a **200-megawatt AI inference lease, the largest hyperscale deal in company history.** At roughly 8% core FFO growth, it is the cheaper of the two large data center REITs and the one with more direct leverage to the buildout's physical scale.

**Valuation Metrics.** Market cap ~$71B; dividend yield **2.59%**; **+23% YTD.** Guided to approximately **8% core FFO growth** for 2026. The comparison set: Equinix at ~$102B market cap, a 1.99% yield, +37% YTD, with 2026 AFFO guidance raised to $4.20–4.28B (+9–11%); Iron Mountain at ~$34.7B, 2.96% yield, +42% YTD. **DLR has underperformed both while booking the largest lease in its history** — that gap is the opportunity.

**Growth Profile.** ~8% core FFO growth, below Equinix's 9–11% AFFO growth, with a record leasing backlog providing revenue visibility into 2026 and beyond. Record leasing across the 0–1MW plus interconnection segment shows the retail/colocation business is healthy alongside the hyperscale wins. The growth rate understates the position: wholesale leases signed today convert to revenue over subsequent years, so current FFO reflects past leasing, not the 200MW deal.

**Competitive Moat.** Land, power interconnection rights, and permits in constrained metros. The scarcest asset in the AI economy is a site with secured grid interconnection — and those are held, not built quickly. Digital Realty's existing portfolio of powered shells and interconnection agreements cannot be replicated on any relevant timeline, which is precisely why hyperscalers sign 200MW leases rather than developing themselves.

**Key Risk.** Lease lumpiness, development risk, and rate sensitivity. Digital Realty's wholesale model carries more lease concentration and development execution risk than Equinix's interconnection-driven model. More acutely: **REITs are the most rate-sensitive equity structure that exists**, and the futures strip pricing 4.8% fed funds by September 2027 is a direct headwind to both the cap rate and the cost of development capital. A secondary and growing risk is local political pushback against data center siting, now a live issue in multiple jurisdictions. **Own this for the contracted megawatts, and accept that the rate path may hold the multiple down for several quarters.**

**Rating: Accumulate.** Scale in rather than initiating a full position. The asset quality and the record lease justify the exposure; the rate environment forbids paying up or sizing aggressively. This is the one pick in today's list where the macro is actively working against the fundamentals.

### 6. NVT — nVent Electric plc *(Liquid cooling, electrical connection & protection)*

**Thesis.** nVent is the purest mid-cap expression of the liquid cooling supercycle — **data center sales expected to exceed $2 billion in 2026, more than double the prior year**, inside a company delivering **53% total sales growth** — and it trades at **~26.9x trailing earnings** against that growth, a fraction of the multiples the optics names command for comparable exposure.

**Valuation Metrics.** **TTM P/E 26.93; P/S 4.92; EV/EBITDA 24.57; forward P/S 4.19x.** Last close referenced at $167.16 against a widely-followed fair value estimate of $220. 2026 consensus revenue **$5.45B, +39.96% YoY.** This is the most expensive name in today's list on every metric, and it is the one with the highest growth — the PEG math is defensible; the absolute multiple is not cheap.

**Growth Profile.** Q2 2026 net sales **$1.47B, +53% YoY (+47% organic)** — record sales and record EPS. Full-year guidance **raised** to 37–39% reported and 32–34% organic, up from 26–28% and 21–23%. New products contributed **more than 30 points** of sales growth, and management announced another liquid cooling manufacturing expansion. The addressable market backdrop: liquid cooling at **$6B in 2026 growing to $27.1B by 2035 (18.2% CAGR).**

**Competitive Moat.** Thermal engineering plus the installed electrical-connection franchise. nVent's advantage is that it sells liquid cooling distribution and high-density intelligent PDUs into the same customers and the same racks as its legacy enclosures and electrical fastening products. Liquid cooling designs are qualified per-platform and carry warranty and service obligations — once specified into a rack architecture, the vendor persists across the deployment.

**Key Risk.** Valuation and the base effect. Organic growth of 47% cannot be annualized; the 2027 comparison will be brutal, and at 26.9x trailing earnings the stock has no room for a guidance disappointment. More specifically: if the financing environment for the marginal gigawatt tightens — a real possibility with fed funds heading toward 4.8% and industry reporting that the buildout's financing "gets harder" — new-build thermal orders are among the first line items to slip, because they attach to capacity that has not broken ground.

**Rating: Accumulate.** The business is excellent and the category is the most certain in the spotlight; the entry multiple requires the growth to persist. Scale in, size it below ENS and BDC, and treat any guidance moderation as the signal rather than the noise.

## Risk Factors

**1. The Fed hikes further than the dots and the market has to price 5% terminal. (Severity: Critical)**
This is the dominant risk and it is already partially in motion. Core PCE above 3% every month of 2026, a year-end projection of 3.7%, a 70.3% implied October hike probability, and futures pricing ~4.8% by September 2027 against a dot plot showing low-4% — the market is already more hawkish than the Committee. If energy-led inflation proves persistent and the Committee follows the market rather than leading it, the 10-year does not stop at 5.24%. Every long-duration equity in the market is mispriced in that scenario, and the damage is not confined to technology — it reaches REITs, small caps, and anything financed at the margin.

**2. Strait of Hormuz disruption. (Severity: Critical, probability moderate)**
Roughly 20% of global oil and gas trade transits the Strait. Attacks on cross-Strait traffic have been running above any level since April, President Trump reportedly rebuffed an Iran peace deal on September 28, and oil rose on the news. A sustained closure produces $100+ oil, a step-change in headline inflation, and forces the Fed to tighten into a growth shock simultaneously. Note that consensus is positioned the other way — J.P. Morgan cut its end-2026 Brent forecast to $78 from $95 — which means the pain trade is higher oil, and positioning offers no cushion.

**3. AI capex financing tightens and the marginal gigawatt does not get built. (Severity: High)**
The buildout's financing structures have grown materially more complex: NVIDIA committing up to $100B progressively (with talks of a $250B backstop) to fund its own customer's debt capacity, neocloud intermediaries like Fluidstack sitting between Anthropic and physical assets, and industry reporting explicitly noting the financing "gets harder." These structures are robust in easy credit and reflexive in tight credit. A Fed at 4.8% is how credit gets tight. This is the primary risk to today's picks, and the reason every one was selected for current earnings and contracted backlog rather than projected demand.

**4. Momentum unwind broadens from semiconductors into the whole market. (Severity: High)**
Today was a narrow, violent de-rating: ARM -8.7% after +28% in a month, QCOM -5.9%, MRVL -4.0%, with decliners beating advancers 3.55-to-1 on the NYSE — while eight of eleven sectors closed *higher* and Industrials gained 1%. That combination is a positioning event. The ARM/SoftBank $25B margin loan is a live mechanical amplifier: a falling share price impairs the borrowing capacity it collateralizes. Crowded momentum unwinds rarely stay contained to one name. **The VIX at 16.07 is not pricing this.**

**5. Backlog-in-the-price risk across the AI industrial complex. (Severity: Moderate)**
Comfort Systems reported a record $14.1B backlog (+73% YoY) and IES $4.5B — and in recent trading Sterling fell ~18% in a week, IES ~18%, Powell ~14%. Record fundamental news did not prevent an 18% drawdown, because at 61x trailing earnings (IESC) the good news was already capitalized. This is an execution risk for the spotlight sector broadly and is the specific reason today's picks cluster in the 14–27x range rather than among the backlog champions.

## Bottom Line

The market spent today marking down the *price* of AI exposure without marking down the *demand* for it — and that distinction is the entire opportunity. A rate shock compressed multiples indiscriminately, hitting a 95x optics name and a 16.6x cabling name with roughly equal force, while in the same month OpenAI, Anthropic and xAI committed well over $150 billion to physical capacity and Gartner marked 2026 data center spending to $822 billion, +62.5%. Own that spread: contracted, currently-earning, short-duration industrial cash flows at 14–27x earnings, not the photonics and memory complex at 95–250x where the businesses are magnificent and the entry prices already assume it. With the futures strip pricing fed funds at 4.8% by late 2027 — above the Fed's own dots — and the VIX at 16, the correct posture is to be fully invested in the plumbing, sized conservatively in the rate-sensitive names, and holding enough cash to buy the momentum unwind if it broadens.
