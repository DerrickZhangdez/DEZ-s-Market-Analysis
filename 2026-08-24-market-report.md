## Macro & Monetary Environment

The monetary setup in August 2026 is the mirror image of the consensus that prevailed a year ago. **The risk being priced is a hike, not a cut.** Chair Warsh, after the late-July hold, explicitly acknowledged that Treasury yields have risen since the prior meeting and signaled the Fed "won't hesitate" to stop inflation — while the bond market registered visible doubt. Sell-side positioning now has the Fed **on hold through 2026, with easing pushed into early 2027**.

Key coordinates:

- **10-year Treasury at 4.74%**, near a 20-month high; the 30-year touched its highest level in nearly two decades earlier this month (Aug 18). The long end, not the front end, is doing the tightening.
- **Inflation:** the most recent core print came in subdued, easing some pressure on the Fed, but inflation remains elevated and is outpacing wage growth. Analysts characterize the current episode as largely a **supply-shock environment**, which is precisely the setup in which a central bank has "no urgency to hike" but also no room to cut.
- **September hike odds** fell to roughly 30% from 44% the prior week — meaningfully lower, but a non-trivial tail that keeps duration risk live.

**What to watch this week:**
1. **Jackson Hole, Aug 27–29** — theme: "Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy." Note that the formal topic is *not* the rate path, which raises the odds that any rate signal comes from the sidelines rather than the keynote.
2. **Nvidia earnings, Aug 26** — the single largest swing factor for the AI-infrastructure complex (detail below).
3. **Retail earnings** — Walmart, Target, Home Depot, Dollar Tree and Dollar General land this week and will test consumer strength after July's retail slump. With the consumer already absorbing tariffs, this is the week's cleanest read on whether cost pass-through is reaching the household.
4. **FOMC minutes** — likely to be scrutinized for how seriously the hawkish wing entertained a hike.

The macro tension to hold in mind: a 4.74% 10-year is a genuine headwind to long-duration equity valuations, and it arrives exactly as AI-infrastructure names carry their richest multiples of the cycle.

## Geopolitical Factors

**Iran sanctions — the live catalyst.** Treasury Secretary Bessent is set to unveil what Washington has billed as its **most aggressive sanctions package yet**, targeting countries and companies purchasing Iranian crude. Counterintuitively, oil fell on the news (WTI −1.62%, Brent −1.38%) as traders faded the headline and Tehran publicly dismissed the pressure. Underneath the price action, the physical market is tightening: Iran has begun limiting crude offered to China, and both crude inventories and oil-on-water are drawing down as Middle East exports stay restricted. The Strait of Hormuz remains the tail risk that would reprice energy, inflation, and the Fed path simultaneously.

**US-Canada trade breakdown — the new one.** Talks collapsed late Friday night in an unprecedented rupture with the second-largest US trading partner. The US imposed **50% tariffs** on a range of Canadian goods on Saturday, with Trump threatening Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 to add further 50% duties on alcohol, hockey equipment, cement and dairy. **Critically for markets, energy, potash and critical minerals were carved out** — which is why this is a consumer-goods and industrials story rather than an energy shock. Canada retaliates **September 8** with tariffs on steel, dairy, appliances, agricultural equipment, pulp, paper and electronics. Carney called the US position "too much asked, too little offered" and the tariffs "a miscalculation."

The September 8 retaliation date matters for the electrical-equipment and industrial names in today's spotlight: appliances, electronics and steel in the Canadian retaliation list touch input costs and cross-border assembly for several US industrials.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: AI Infrastructure & Data Centers — Power, Cooling, Networking, Interconnect, Memory

*(Rotating spotlight: day-of-year 236 mod 6 = 2)*

This is the deepest section of today's report, and the research produced a genuinely uncomfortable conclusion worth stating plainly up front: **the AI-infrastructure thesis is more intact than ever, and the stocks that express it most directly are the most expensive they have been this cycle.** A valuation-based process cannot simply buy the theme today. It has to find the parts of the stack the market has not yet re-rated.

**The demand side is not the problem.** Microsoft, Google, Meta and Amazon have all *raised* 2026 and 2027 capex guidance. Nvidia is expected to report Q2 FY27 revenue of roughly $92–95B (management guided ~$91B) against data-center revenue potentially exceeding $80B, versus $71.6B in Q1 FY27 (+93% YoY); consensus EPS sits near $2.06. Nvidia is separately reported to be preparing to backstop roughly **$100 billion** in OpenAI financing. Anthropic has launched **Theseus Infrastructure** with Macquarie Asset Management and GIC — purpose-built US data centers with Anthropic as anchor tenant. Microsoft, meanwhile, is publicly wrestling with the physical limits of data centers and electricity. Morgan Stanley frames the constraint as a **38-gigawatt power gap**. Demand, capital and political will are all aligned.

**Where the re-rating has *not* happened** — and where today's picks come from:

1. **Colocation / interconnection REITs are actively out of favor.** BofA states listed data centers are "likely to remain out of favor in 2026" *despite* solid fundamentals — positive leasing spreads, improving occupancy, stable returns. Equinix trades at a **19.5x AFFO multiple** (Digital Realty 22.2x) and BofA retained a **Buy on Equinix as a "self-help story,"** while cutting Digital Realty to Neutral (PT $170 from $210) because its development pipeline's scale and location are a poor fit for AI-driven demand. That is a rare thing in this sector: a discounted multiple with an identified, company-specific improvement path. Equinix separately raised its 2026 outlook to roughly $10.21–10.29B revenue (+11–12%).
2. **EMS / cloud-and-power manufacturing is growing like AI infrastructure but priced like contract manufacturing.** Flex and Jabil sit at ~25x and ~27x forward respectively — near the ~23x electronics-industry average — while Flex guides its Cloud & Power Infrastructure segment to **+65–75% in FY27 and +80%+ in FY28**, and Jabil guides AI-related revenue to **$12.1B in FY26, +35% YoY**, with Cloud/DCI raised $600M to $9.8B.
3. **Grid and T&D equipment at a market multiple.** Hubbell at ~25.4–26.9x forward against a market average near 26.3x, having raised FY26 adjusted EPS guidance twice (to $20.25–$20.55) on T&D and data-center strength.
4. **Fiber-route and building construction at a discount to its peers.** Dycom at ~28.2x forward with an all-time-high backlog of **$11.91B, +46.5% YoY**, versus Primoris at 46x on a comparable backlog of $11.6B.

**The memory sub-sector deserves its own warning.** Memory equities are selling off hard *despite* record AI demand, and the reasons are structural, not sentiment: HBM supply is catching up from three directions at once, **SK Hynix — the strongest player — has already guided softer**, and **China's CXMT raised $8.6B** to add conventional DRAM supply at scale. Against that, SK Hynix says its entire 2026 HBM supply is sold out and shipped HBM4 first for Nvidia's Rubin generation; GB300 requires HBM4 in volumes current production cannot satisfy. The bull case is real but the marginal-supply news flow is against it. **We are taking no memory exposure today** — the risk/reward does not clear our bar when the strongest operator in the group is guiding down.

**Bottom line on the spotlight:** own the buildout through the layers where a reasonable multiple still absorbs an execution stumble. Avoid the layers where a 45–50x multiple requires everything to go right into a Nvidia print and a Jackson Hole meeting in the same week.

### Artificial Intelligence

The competitive picture has shifted materially. **Anthropic has pulled ahead of OpenAI on revenue trajectory**, with OpenAI reported to be posting disappointing revenue growth and mounting losses; ChatGPT's growth has stalled while Anthropic's Claude Code has been a standout success with enterprise customers. The counter-datapoint: OpenAI is growing faster among *business users* quarter-to-date than Anthropic, so the enterprise contest is genuinely close rather than settled.

Notable moves: OpenAI cut prices 80% and made **GPT-5.6 Luna** the free ChatGPT default, offered Zero Data Retention for frontier models, expanded ChatGPT Ads across Europe, and is funding 14 policy/research projects ($1M each plus up to $1M in model credits). Nvidia is reportedly preparing to back ~$100B in OpenAI financing. Anthropic launched Theseus Infrastructure with Macquarie and GIC. Google acquired Spirit Airlines' data assets. Palantir is up 93% on the year.

The strategically important theme is the **shift from "tokenmaxxing" to efficiency** in customer AI spending. An efficiency-oriented customer base is mildly bearish for raw token volume and unambiguously bullish for whoever supplies power, cooling and interconnect per unit of useful compute — which is the spotlight sector's structural argument.

Alibaba opened down roughly 8% in Hong Kong, touching HK$111 (−10% intraday at worst), after confirming a **$10B raise via 710 million HK shares at a 3.6% discount** (UBS and CICC arranging) to fund AI ambitions — a raise that roughly matches a full quarter of its capex. The signal worth extracting: when a major AI spender funds capex with discounted equity rather than cash flow, the market is being asked to finance the buildout directly. That is a late-cycle financing tell, and it is a point in favor of owning the *suppliers* being paid in cash over the *spenders* issuing paper.

### Hardware & Semiconductors

The semiconductor tape is bifurcating sharply. **Memory is the epicenter of weakness** for the supply reasons detailed above. **Optical and interconnect are the epicenter of strength** — LITE, AAOI and COHR have all posted YTD gains above 100%, helped by Nvidia's $6B of investment in optical connectivity. The technical driver is durable: copper interconnect cannot support GPU clusters at ten-thousand-card scale, making optical the default for power, distance and density.

Marvell's own guidance illustrates where the value is migrating: **optical interconnect revenue guided to +70%+ YoY in FY27, outpacing custom AI silicon at 20%+**. Note also the custom-silicon market structure — Broadcom holds roughly 70% design share against Marvell's 20–25% — which tempers the "Marvell is the ASIC winner" narrative.

Everything now routes through Wednesday's Nvidia print. With ~96% expected YoY revenue growth already in consensus, the asymmetry is unattractive: an in-line quarter is unlikely to re-rate the group upward, while any gross-margin or guidance wobble hits every name in the table above.

### 1. EQIX — Equinix, Inc.
*Sub-sector: colocation & interconnection REIT*

- **Thesis:** The only place in the AI-infrastructure stack where a leading operator trades at a *discounted* multiple with an identified improvement path. BofA says listed data centers stay out of favor in 2026 despite positive leasing spreads, improving occupancy and stable returns — and kept a **Buy on Equinix specifically as a "self-help story"** while downgrading its closest peer. Being unloved for sector-sentiment reasons rather than company reasons is the setup this process exists to find.
- **Valuation Metrics:** **19.5x AFFO** (peer Digital Realty 22.2x) — the relevant multiple for a REIT. Management notes that including non-revenue-generating capex would push the figure into the low 20s, which is a fair caveat to carry. P/E trailing & forward, P/S, EV/EBITDA, PEG: `n/a — not verified today` (and of limited use for a REIT versus AFFO).
- **Growth Profile:** 2026 outlook raised to **$10.21–$10.29B revenue, +11–12%**. Mid-teens-plus AFFO/share growth is the historical pattern but was not verified in today's research.
- **Competitive Moat:** Interconnection density. Equinix's value is the accumulated tenant-to-tenant cross-connect fabric inside its facilities, which cannot be replicated by building comparable square footage — the classic network effect in physical form, and precisely what Digital Realty's downgrade thesis (wrong scale, wrong locations) shows is hard to copy.
- **Key Risk:** Power. If electricity availability, not floor space, is the binding constraint on AI capacity, incumbents with legacy metro footprints may capture less of the AI wave than greenfield campuses sited next to generation. The Morgan Stanley 38-GW power gap is a risk to Equinix, not just an opportunity.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

### 2. FLEX — Flex Ltd.
*Sub-sector: cloud & power infrastructure manufacturing (EMS)*

- **Thesis:** Growing like an AI-infrastructure company while priced like a contract manufacturer. Flex guides its **Cloud & Power Infrastructure segment to +65–75% in FY27 and +80%+ in FY28** — and the whole company trades at roughly the electronics-industry average multiple. This is the widest gap between growth and multiple found anywhere in today's screen.
- **Valuation Metrics:** **~25x forward P/E**, against a ~23x electronics-industry average and ~26x market. Trailing P/E, P/S, EV/EBITDA, PEG: `n/a — not verified today`. On a segment-growth basis the implied PEG is well below 1; on *consolidated* EPS growth it is materially higher, and honesty requires stating that the 65–75% figure is a segment, not the company.
- **Growth Profile:** Data-center segment revenue expected to rise at least 35% in the current fiscal year, with CPI accelerating into FY28. Consolidated revenue growth is far more modest — the story is mix shift toward higher-value power and cooling content, not a company-wide growth inflection.
- **Competitive Moat:** Scale plus qualification lock-in. Power-shelf, busbar and rack-integration programs are designed in with the customer and carry multi-year switching costs once qualified; Flex has moved up the value chain from assembly into power infrastructure it designs itself.
- **Key Risk:** Customer concentration in a capex cycle. EMS margins are structurally thin, so a hyperscaler push-out hits operating income hard and fast. This is a levered bet on AI capex continuing, with less multiple cushion than the headline 25x suggests.
- **Rating:** **Strong Buy**

### 3. JBL — Jabil, Inc.
*Sub-sector: diversified EMS / AI hardware*

- **Thesis:** The same mispricing as Flex with a more diversified end-market base — healthcare, regulated industries and autos underneath the AI growth — for roughly two turns more multiple. **AI-related revenue guided to $12.1B in FY26, +35% YoY**, with Cloud and DCI raised $600M to $9.8B.
- **Valuation Metrics:** **~27x forward P/E** versus ~23x electronics-industry average. Trailing P/E, P/S, EV/EBITDA, PEG: `n/a — not verified today`.
- **Growth Profile:** AI revenue +35% YoY to $12.1B in FY26; Cloud/DCI revised upward mid-year to $9.8B, a positive-revision pattern that matters more than the absolute figure.
- **Competitive Moat:** Breadth of qualified manufacturing footprint and a diversified revenue base that funds AI capacity investment without betting the company on it. The non-AI segments are the moat here — they are what let Jabil underwrite AI capacity while a pure-play cannot.
- **Key Risk:** Correlated exposure with Flex (see portfolio note below) and the same thin-margin, concentrated-customer profile. Jabil's diversification cuts both ways: slower-growing legacy segments cap the upside if the AI mix re-rates.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

### 4. HUBB — Hubbell Incorporated
*Sub-sector: electrical T&D and grid equipment*

- **Thesis:** The quality way to own the 38-GW power gap at a market multiple rather than a thematic one. While Powell trades at ~33x and Vertiv at ~34x, Hubbell sits near the market average with guidance revised **upward twice this year** on T&D and data-center demand.
- **Valuation Metrics:** **~25.4–26.9x forward P/E** (sources vary; one comparison cites ~23.0x) versus a ~26.3x market average — and versus Powell's ~33x in the same end-market. Trailing P/E, P/S, EV/EBITDA, PEG: `n/a — not verified today`.
- **Growth Profile:** FY26 guidance raised to **8–11% total sales growth** and then to **$20.25–$20.55 adjusted EPS** (from an initial $19.30–$19.85), driven by T&D and data centers. EPS growth is running well ahead of sales growth — the margin story is the earnings story.
- **Competitive Moat:** Utility specification lock-in. Grid components are approved into utility standards over years and rarely re-bid on price, which is why the business sustains high margins and superior returns on capital through cycles. Grid modernization and electrification are secular demand independent of AI.
- **Key Risk:** The September 8 Canadian retaliation list explicitly includes **steel, appliances and electronics** — direct input-cost and cross-border exposure for US electrical manufacturers. A project-driven order book also means execution and timing risk on the data-center portion.
- **Rating:** **Accumulate**

### 5. DY — Dycom Industries, Inc.
*Sub-sector: specialty construction — fiber routes & data center build*

- **Thesis:** Someone has to physically build the fiber and the buildings, and Dycom carries an all-time-high backlog at a **~18-point multiple discount to Primoris on a comparable backlog**. It is the cheapest verified entry point into the physical buildout found today.
- **Valuation Metrics:** **~28.2x forward P/E** — a premium to its industry, but against Primoris at ~46x forward (vs ~34x construction average) on a similar-sized book. Trailing P/E, P/S, EV/EBITDA, PEG: `n/a — not verified today`.
- **Growth Profile:** Backlog at an all-time high of **$11.91B, +46.5% YoY**. FY26 contract revenue guided to **$5.35–$5.425B, +13.8–15.4% YoY**. Shares +21.2% YTD, roughly in line with the heavy-construction industry's +23.6% — this name has *not* run away from its group.
- **Competitive Moat:** Skilled-crew scale and master service agreements. The binding constraint in specialty construction is qualified labor and long-standing carrier/hyperscaler relationships, not capital — which is why incumbents keep winning as scope expands from fiber into building systems.
- **Key Risk:** **Backlog duration.** Most of the 46.5% backlog growth sits beyond 12 months, which raises real questions about near-term cash conversion — a paper backlog that converts slowly cannot support a 28x multiple. At least one analyst has published doubts ahead of earnings on exactly this point.
- **Rating:** **Accumulate**

## Risk Factors

**1. Nvidia's August 26 print — severity: high, immediacy: 48 hours.**
Consensus already embeds ~96% YoY revenue growth and $80B+ in data-center revenue. The asymmetry is poor: an in-line quarter probably does not re-rate a group trading at 45–50x, while a gross-margin miss or soft guide transmits instantly to every optical, cooling, power and construction name in this report. This is the week's dominant single-name systemic risk.

**2. The long end of the curve — severity: high, immediacy: ongoing.**
A 10-year at 4.74% near 20-month highs, with the 30-year at its highest in nearly two decades, is a direct discount-rate assault on exactly the long-duration growth equities that have led this year. The market is pricing a ~30% chance of a September *hike*. Rising yields plus peak multiples in the same sector is the core vulnerability of the current tape.

**3. Trade-war escalation — severity: medium-high, immediacy: September 8.**
The US-Canada rupture is unprecedented with the second-largest US trading partner. Energy, potash and critical minerals were carved out, which caps the macro damage — but Canada's September 8 retaliation on steel, dairy, appliances, agricultural equipment, pulp, paper and electronics lands squarely on industrials and the consumer. Watch this week's retail earnings for evidence of tariff pass-through reaching households.

**4. Memory oversupply spilling into the broader AI complex — severity: medium, immediacy: weeks.**
SK Hynix guiding softer while CXMT adds $8.6B of DRAM supply is a genuine supply-side deterioration. The risk is narrative contagion: if the market reads memory weakness as "the AI cycle is topping" rather than "memory-specific supply," multiples compress across the whole buildout regardless of the power and interconnect bottlenecks that remain very real.

**5. AI capex funded by discounted equity — severity: medium, immediacy: quarters.**
Alibaba raising $10B at a 3.6% discount — roughly one quarter of its capex — alongside Nvidia reportedly backstopping ~$100B of OpenAI financing, points to a buildout increasingly financed by paper and vendor arrangements rather than operating cash flow. Combined with OpenAI's reported revenue disappointment and mounting losses, this is the slow-developing structural risk to watch. It is also the strongest argument for owning cash-paid suppliers over equity-issuing spenders.

## Bottom Line

The AI infrastructure buildout is real, funded, and physically constrained by power — but the equities that express it most directly are priced for flawless execution heading into a Nvidia print and Jackson Hole in the same week. Today's discipline is to own the buildout where the multiple still absorbs a stumble: an out-of-favor interconnection REIT at 19.5x AFFO, two manufacturers growing their AI segments 35–75% at electronics-industry multiples, grid equipment at a market multiple, and specialty construction at a large discount to its peer. We are declining the optical, networking and memory layers entirely — not because the businesses are weak, but because at 45–50x forward there is no margin for the very risks this week is most likely to deliver.
