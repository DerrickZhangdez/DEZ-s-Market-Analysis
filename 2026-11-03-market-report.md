## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The single most important fact for positioning: the Fed is tightening, not easing.**

- **Current target range: 3.75%–4.00%**, held at the **October 28** meeting.
- That hold followed a **25bp hike in September 2026 — the first increase since 2023.**
- The decision drew **two dissents**, both from voters who wanted to hike *immediately*.
- The post-meeting statement retained the language **"Inflation remains elevated."**
- **Chair Warsh has deliberately withheld forward guidance**, raising the information value of each individual data release and the variance around each meeting.

**What's priced:** Futures indicate a strong probability of **another hike at the December 9 FOMC**, with the possibility of one or two more in 2027. Schwab's house view is two further hikes this cycle, contingent on the breadth of inflation.

**Why this matters more than the election.** A market that spent 2023–2025 trained to buy duration on every dip is now operating under the opposite regime. Rising policy rates do two things to equity valuation simultaneously: they raise the discount rate applied to distant cash flows, and they raise the opportunity cost of owning a non-earning asset. That is precisely why the compression has been concentrated in long-duration software and pre-profit AI infrastructure rather than in the broad index. **Morgan Stanley's framing is the correct one: inflation, growth and earnings will dominate the election for broad market performance.**

**Economists' concern** is that inflation has **broadened beyond energy** and beyond the Iran-war impulse — meaning the December decision will likely turn on core services breadth rather than on the oil price unwind now underway.

**This week to watch:** midterm results (tonight/Wednesday), the read-through to the December FOMC, and whether the oil decline begins to show up in headline inflation expectations.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: Enterprise Software & SaaS
*(Rotation: day-of-year 307 mod 6 = **1** → Enterprise Software & SaaS)*

**The 2026 "SaaSpocalypse" is the single most consequential equity story of the year, and it is now entering its discrimination phase.**

**What happened.** Beginning in late January 2026 following Anthropic's Claude Cowork announcement, the software complex entered a historic de-rating. Roughly **$1 trillion** of software market value was erased by February; by April, cumulative estimates reached **~$2 trillion**. The core fear is structural rather than cyclical: **if one worker using AI agents can do the work of several, per-seat licensing — the economic foundation of modern SaaS — contracts rather than expands.**

**The damage, in prices:**

| Name | Drawdown |
|---|---|
| Salesforce | **−33% YTD** |
| ServiceNow | **−45% over the past year** |
| Workday | **−30% YTD** |
| iShares Expanded Tech-Software (IGV) | **−23%** |
| Adobe / Salesforce | ~**50% discount** to early-2025 levels |

**The October 29 aftershock.** ServiceNow guided Q4 **contracted revenue growth to 18.5%** — a deceleration that the market read as confirmation of the agent thesis. **NOW fell ~19%**, dragging **CRM −6%** and **WDAY −5%**. When the highest-quality name in enterprise workflow software guides cRPO down, the market stops treating the AI-disruption narrative as a story and starts treating it as a forecast input.

**The counter-argument — and why it deserves weight.** **JPMorgan** strategists argue the market is pricing **"worst-case AI disruption scenarios that are unlikely to materialise."** Several high-quality names have fallen **40%+ while still growing revenue and generating substantial free cash flow.** **Bain's** more sober read: the reset reflects genuine AI disruption, slowing net retention, and a **widening divide between incumbents and future winners** — with **smaller software firms most exposed**.

**The analytical framework I am using today.** The market is currently de-rating software as a *category*. That is lazy, and it is where the opportunity lives. The relevant question is not "is this a SaaS company?" but **"what is this company's unit of monetization, and does AI agent adoption increase or decrease it?"**

Three buckets:

1. **Per-seat, human-headcount-linked (structurally impaired).** Classic HCM, CRM, and productivity seats. AI agents are a direct substitute for the billing unit. Requires a genuine valuation discount to own.
2. **Consumption / telemetry-linked (structurally advantaged).** Observability, data infrastructure, security telemetry. **Agent proliferation increases the number of services, API calls, logs, traces and machine identities to monitor.** AI adoption is *accretive* to the billing unit — more agents means more to observe, secure and store. The market is currently de-rating these alongside per-seat names. **That is the mispricing.**
3. **Regulated / system-of-record workflow (defensible).** Vertical SaaS embedded in compliance, claims, or public-sector workflow — where switching costs are legal and procedural, not merely technical.

**Why the spotlight matters today specifically:** **Dynatrace reported this morning and fell ~15%**, handing today's report a live, dated test case for exactly this framework (see Pick #1).

**A name I examined and rejected:** **CCC Intelligent Solutions (CCC)** screens beautifully — Q3 revenue $294.1M (+10.1%), adjusted EBITDA $130.5M, FCF $82.5M, trading near 52-week lows around $7.70. **But it has suspended guidance and cancelled its earnings call because of a pending take-private with GTCR and Elliott.** That makes it a merger-arbitrage position, not a valuation-based investment. **It is excluded from today's picks on process grounds.** Flagging it because the screening data alone would mislead.

**A second rejection, for the same discipline:** **Qualys (QLYS)** appeared in my initial screen as a cheap, 43%-operating-margin security compounder "down 26%." **That data was ~260 days stale.** QLYS actually opened Monday at **$224.12**, against a 12-month range of **$74.51–$235.55** — it has round-tripped to near its highs, trades at a P/E in the high-20s/30s, and was **downgraded to Hold by Wall Street Zen on November 2**. It reports Q3 **after the close tonight** (guidance: revenue $185.5–187.5M, +9–10%; non-GAAP EPS $1.91–1.98; consensus $1.96 on $186.65M). **Not a value pick at this price.**

Per CNBC's sourcing, **the market is not pricing a Democratic sweep**, making it the asymmetric outcome for Wednesday. Reuters/Morningstar map the sector consequences: **clean energy, housing and healthcare benefit; AI, data centers and defense draw legislative scrutiny; financials and industrials come under pressure.** Note the overlap with the already-stressed AI complex — a sweep would add political risk to a trade already carrying valuation risk. Counterweight: the S&P 500 was higher one year after **every** midterm since the early 1980s, though BTIG's Krinsky disputes the forward applicability.

**4. Memory cost inflation compressing hardware and device margins. [MODERATE-HIGH]**
Apple's holiday-quarter margin warning is the leading indicator, not an idiosyncratic stumble. Memory capacity is insufficient, DRAM rose high-teens last quarter, and **every device OEM and server builder faces the same input cost.** This is a margin-transfer story — from assemblers to memory makers — and it is a reason the MU pick above is paired against a Mag-7 hardware warning rather than against a forecast.

**5. Oil's two-sided geopolitical binary. [MODERATE, high variance]**
Brent has fallen from $123 (Oct 27) to ~$107 on diplomacy alone, with no supply change. **The premium can re-inflate as fast as it deflated.** Strait of Hormuz remains the binary, sanctions-license status is genuinely unclear, and the EIA's $84/bbl 2027 forecast embeds successful de-escalation. Airlines and consumers are long this unwind; energy producers are short it.

---

## Bottom Line

**The market is no longer de-rating assets; it is re-sorting them — and it is currently doing so with a blunt instrument.** The AI trade has split cleanly between those who sell compute and those who finance it, while the software complex is being marked down as a single category despite containing businesses whose monetization **increases** with agent adoption. Today's picks lean into that specific confusion: consumption-linked observability and security sold off alongside per-seat SaaS, plus one memory cyclical the market discounted for hypothetical future supply on the very morning Apple confirmed present pricing power.

**Position with humility into tonight.** Two genuine binaries resolve within 36 hours — an election whose most consequential outcome is unpriced, and a Fed that has stopped offering guidance five weeks before a live meeting. Both of my lowest-conviction ratings (ACCUMULATE on MU and PAYC) reflect exactly that: compelling valuations whose theses depend on conditions I cannot verify today. **The discipline that matters this week is refusing to size a conditional thesis like an unconditional one.**
