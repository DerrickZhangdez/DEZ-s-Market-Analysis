## Macro & Monetary Environment

**The Fed is on hold with a hawkish tail.** The July FOMC left the target range at **3.50%–3.75%**, but with **three dissents in favor of a hike** — an unusually fractured committee. That framing matters more than the level: the market is not pricing an easing cycle, it is pricing the probability that inflation forces the Fed back into tightening. Following today's CPI, traders shifted toward the Fed holding steady in September after being roughly 50-50 the day before, with **September hike odds near 42%**.

**July CPI, in detail:**

| Metric | July 2026 | Prior / Context |
|---|---|---|
| Headline CPI, m/m | +0.1% (seasonally adj.) | Tame; second consecutive moderate month |
| Headline CPI, y/y | 3.4% | Down from 3.5% in June; in line with consensus |
| Core CPI, m/m | +0.2% | Consistent with a ~2.5% annualized run rate |
| Core CPI, y/y | 2.5% | The genuinely encouraging number |

The composition is the bullish part. The **energy-driven burst earlier in 2026 is fading**, which is precisely what you would expect given the crude correction described below, and core at 2.5% year-over-year is close enough to target that the hawks lose their strongest argument. The caveat that analysts are attaching to the print is that energy prices remain volatile and hostage to Middle East conditions — a cooling headline number built on falling oil is a number that can reverse if the Strait of Hormuz situation deteriorates.

**Why this print did not force a hike.** The read across the sell side today is that inflation rose about as expected but not enough to compel action. With no FOMC meeting until September, the committee gets another full month of data — August CPI, two more PPI prints, and payrolls — before it has to commit. That extended runway is itself supportive for equities: it removes the near-term event risk that a hot print would have created.

**What to watch the rest of this week:**

- **Thursday, August 13 — July PPI and weekly initial jobless claims.** PPI is the more important of the two right now. It measures inflation at the producer level and functions as an early signal before prices reach consumers; a hot PPI would undercut today's benign CPI narrative and revive the September hike trade.
- **Friday, August 14 — July retail sales.** The read on consumption, which is the main engine of the US economy. Given that the Fed's hawkish faction is arguing from demand strength, a strong retail sales number is paradoxically an equity risk this week.

**Rates and positioning.** With the policy rate at 3.50%–3.75% and core CPI at 2.5%, real policy rates remain meaningfully positive — restrictive on paper. The equity market's willingness to pay up for long-duration growth assets in that environment rests on the AI capex cycle delivering earnings, which is why the infrastructure earnings discussed below carry more weight than usual.

## Geopolitical Factors

**Iran and the Strait of Hormuz remain the dominant macro-geopolitical variable, and the direction of travel has recently been toward de-escalation.** Renewed signs of US–Iran dialogue triggered a sharp correction in crude. Brent and WTI both opened higher on persistent Middle East uncertainty before reversing to declines of nearly 4% on diplomatic developments; **Brent front-month futures were at $84.89/bbl as of August 4**, having recovered 1.3% after a prior-session 7% drop to a three-week low. Treasury Secretary Bessent has indicated negotiations continue to progress and that an agreement could restore maritime transit through the region. As of early August, conflict had abated amid Iran–Oman talks concerning the Strait.

**Why this is the single largest tail risk in the market.** Roughly **20% of global oil supply transits the Strait of Hormuz**. Any escalation threatening that transit would trigger immediate and severe repricing across energy and equity markets — and critically, it would do so through the *inflation* channel at exactly the moment the Fed has three members already voting to hike. An oil shock in the current configuration is not merely an energy-sector event; it is a monetary-policy event. That asymmetry is why the VIX at ~15 looks complacent relative to the headline risk.

**The counter-trade is getting harder.** Investors who were long the Iran-war oil trade have already collected, and the observation across the sell side is that staying long that trade from here is trickier: you are now short a peace deal with a negative carry. That cuts both ways for equities — energy earnings estimates are at risk of a de-rating, while transports, chemicals, and consumer discretionary get a tailwind.

**Secondary geopolitical vectors:** geopolitical fragmentation is accelerating, with Middle East conflict, US–China technology competition, and trade tensions jointly exposing global supply-chain vulnerabilities. For the spotlight sector specifically, US–China technology restrictions remain the structural risk to any company with a material China revenue line or a China-dependent supply chain — relevant to storage, networking, and electrical components alike.

## Sector Deep Dives

### Today's Spotlight: AI Infrastructure & Data Centers

*(Rotation basis: day-of-year 224 → 224 mod 6 = 2 → AI infrastructure & data centers — power, cooling, networking, interconnect, memory.)*

This is the sector where the 2026 investment thesis has most decisively shifted, and today's earnings tape underlines it.

**The capex number is the whole argument.** Hyperscalers are on track to spend close to **$700 billion on data centers in 2026** — Amazon around $200B, Alphabet $175–185B, Meta $115–135B, Microsoft toward $120B, and Oracle around $50B. Independent estimates of the total AI capex cycle for 2026 run from roughly $690B to $775–800B depending on what is counted. Whatever the exact figure, it is a committed, contracted, multi-year spending program, and the marginal dollar is no longer going where it went in 2023–24.

**The bottleneck has moved.** The consensus framing across 2026 research is explicit: the focus has shifted from GPUs to **power, cooling, networking, and related infrastructure**, because those have become the binding constraints as GPU supply eases. AI's constraints are now increasingly *physical and economic* — compute, power, cost, and community acceptance — even as model capability keeps improving. This is the single most important structural observation for a valuation-driven investor, because it tells you where the pricing power has relocated.

**Today's confirming datapoints:**

- **CoreWeave (CRWV) +18%** on a Q2 beat — the GPU-cloud neocloud model is still clearing expectations.
- **Super Micro (SMCI) +9%** — beat on earnings, *missed* on revenue, but guided the coming quarter above expectations. The pattern of "revenue constrained, demand not" is the signature of a supply-limited market.
- **Cerebras Systems (CBRS)** reports after the close, having guided core revenue to roughly **$194 million, +88% year-over-year**, against a consensus adjusted loss near $0.17/share. The number that matters is gross margin: guidance of **36–38% versus 47% in Q1**, and the stock fell ~10% after its prior report despite strong revenue precisely because investors focused on margin compression. This is the key tension in AI infrastructure — top-line growth is nearly universal, margin durability is not.
- **Cisco (CSCO)** reports fiscal Q4 after the bell, with the street at **$1.17 EPS (+18.2% y/y) on $16.83B revenue (+14.7% y/y)**.
- **Nvidia** locked in a reported **$500 billion financing alliance** with major Wall Street institutions — a datapoint about how the buildout is being funded, which is becoming as important as demand itself.
- **Anthropic revealed custom chip plans**, joining the in-house silicon trend, which is incrementally negative for merchant accelerator vendors and incrementally positive for the interconnect, memory, and power layers that every architecture requires regardless of who designs the compute.

**Sub-sector by sub-sector — where the fundamentals and the valuations actually sit:**

**1. Storage and memory — the most under-appreciated hard constraint.** This is the cleanest supply-demand imbalance in the sector. **Western Digital states that 100% of its 2026 HDD capacity is sold out** on multiyear agreements extending toward 2028–2029. Morgan Stanley's Asia supply-chain checks see HDD shortages potentially stretching **through 2028**, with demand growing ~40–50% year-over-year against supply expanding only ~30–35%. That gap has already converted to price: **HDD prices rose roughly 50% this year**, with nearline pricing targeting **$25–30/TB**. Seagate is running **47% gross margins** with nearline capacity committed through mid-2026. Both WDC and Seagate grew EPS more than 100% in their latest quarters. Critically, the revenue mix has transformed the business quality: **Western Digital derives 89% of revenue from hyperscaler AI cloud versus 5% from consumer** — this is no longer a PC-cycle company. On valuation, WDC trades at a lower forward multiple than Seagate with stronger estimate revisions and expanding gross margins. SanDisk, by contrast, screens at a demanding **~60x P/E with NAND pricing risk** and is not comparable value.

**2. Cooling and thermal management — real growth, mixed valuations.** The liquid cooling market was **$4.8B in 2025, $6B in 2026, and is forecast to reach $27.1B by 2035 (18.2% CAGR)**. The operating results are extraordinary: **nVent's** organic orders surged roughly 65% in a recent quarter on large liquid-cooling hyperscaler programs, Q1 2026 organic sales grew **34%**, the company posted record quarterly sales, orders, and backlog, and it now expects **more than $2 billion in 2026 data-center sales** — with a third liquid-cooling capacity expansion in three years (a 160,000 sq ft Minnesota facility opening in H1 2027, part of 400,000+ sq ft added). **AAON** raised its 2026 revenue growth outlook to **40–45%** on a record **$2.1 billion backlog, up 107.4% year-over-year**, with liquid cooling sales up **186.8%** on a trailing-twelve-month basis — but the stock trades at a **P/E of ~104**, which disqualifies it on valuation discipline regardless of how good the operations are. **SPX Technologies** is the more defensible entry: data center cooling revenue is projected to grow from **$200M in FY2025 to $350M in 2026** with a far more moderate multiple. **Modine** guides to 50–70% annual data center growth over the next two fiscal years but was recommended in this series on July 31 and is excluded today.

**3. Power generation and electrical equipment — the hardest physical constraint.** Power is where the buildout actually stalls. **NRG** signed a landmark **1.2 GW data center agreement**, delivered Q2 adjusted EBITDA up **34%** year-over-year, and projects that announced data center agreements plus a lead "bring your own power" opportunity could drive contracted FCF to roughly **$2.9 billion by 2033** — about 95% of the 2026 free-cash-flow-before-growth midpoint. **Talen** expanded its Amazon PPA to **1.92 GW**, is evaluating gigawatt-scale X-energy Xe-100 SMRs, and agreed to acquire 2,451 MW of gas assets (Waterford, Darby, Lawrenceburg) for **$3.45 billion** — but at ~$386/share against $1.75–2.05B of 2026 adjusted EBITDA, the equity already embeds a great deal. On the equipment side, **Powell Industries** reported a record **$2.4 billion backlog** with visibility through fiscal 2028, record orders of **$934 million**, a **$400M+ data center mega-order** (Phase 1 of a multiphase behind-the-meter on-site generation design), and a debt-free balance sheet with $634M of liquidity — genuinely excellent, but the sell-side bull case runs on **~49x forward non-GAAP earnings**, which is not a valuation-based entry.

**4. Networking and optical interconnect — outstanding fundamentals, unacceptable prices.** This is where I am declining to pick, and the reason is worth stating explicitly. **Ciena** grew fiscal Q2 revenue **39.5% to $1.57 billion** with adjusted EPS up **290%**, raised its FY2026 outlook to **$6.3 billion (~32% growth)**, and grew backlog by ~$2B to nearly $7B — but trades at a **P/E near 208** against a communications-industry average near 40, described in one analysis as more than 3x estimated fair value. **Coherent's** Datacenter & Communications segment grew **over 40%** year-over-year and now represents **75% of total revenue**, with 1.6T transceivers and optical circuit switch products ramping into a datacom market forecast to compound at ~35% from $19B in 2025 to $47B by 2028 — but after a 376–448% rally the forward P/E has expanded to **~44.6x**. **Marvell** has 50%+ anticipated data center growth and an expanding custom XPU pipeline, but sits at a forward P/E of roughly **59x**, about 54% above the semiconductor industry median, and screens as significantly overvalued against fair-value estimates. **Corning** has genuinely transformative contracts — an Nvidia optical connectivity partnership, a Meta deal worth up to **$6 billion through 2030**, and Amazon data center supply — targeting a **$20 billion annualized run rate** by end-2026 with optical communications now 44% of revenue, but after a 140% move it trades at **36–46x forward** against an electronic-industry average of 21x. Every one of these is a good business. None is a good price today.

**5. Data center REITs — the cleanest way to own the physical asset, with one clear relative value.** **Digital Realty** guides to 8% core FFO growth and 10%+ revenue growth in 2026 with a record **$1.4 billion backlog, up 95.8% year-over-year**, commencing into FY26–27 — but trades near **29x P/AFFO**, which is difficult to defend against that growth rate, and the case depends on whether backlog converts on schedule or whether power and construction constraints push commencements out. **Iron Mountain** is the better-priced alternative and is underwritten by a second, unrelated cash-flow engine: Q2 2026 revenue of **$2.0 billion, +18.5%** year-over-year, adjusted EBITDA of **$727 million (+15.7%)**, AFFO of **$433 million or $1.44/share (+17%)**, data center revenue of **$263 million, +39%**, ALM revenue of **$288 million, +88%**, and **110 MW leased through July** (13 MW in Q2 plus 75 MW in July) against an original full-year target of 100 MW that management now expects to meaningfully exceed. Roughly **325 MW** is expected to be energized over the next 24 months toward total developable capacity near **1.4 GW**, with backlog supporting **$370 million of revenue growth beyond 2026**. Guidance was raised to **$7.94–8.01B revenue, $2.945–2.975B adjusted EBITDA, and $5.87–5.93 AFFO/share**.

**The valuation summary for the spotlight sector.** AI infrastructure names generally trade at **18–35x forward earnings versus Nvidia's ~47x**, with the sector still less crowded than AI silicon — "less hype, smaller crowd, similar capex tailwind." That framing was accurate six months ago and is now only *selectively* true: the optical/interconnect and premium electrical-equipment sub-sectors have fully repriced past it, while storage, moderate-multiple thermal, merchant power, and digital-infrastructure REITs still offer defensible entries. Today's picks are drawn from the latter group.

### Artificial Intelligence

The AI narrative today is about **funding structures and physical constraints** rather than model releases. Nvidia's reported **$500 billion financing alliance** with major Wall Street institutions is the most consequential item: it signals that the buildout has outgrown hyperscaler operating cash flow and is moving into structured finance, which introduces a credit dimension to what has so far been an equity story. **Anthropic's disclosed custom chip plans** extend the in-house silicon trend already established by Google, Amazon, and Meta — a competitive negative for merchant accelerators at the margin, and a reminder that the layers *below* compute (memory, interconnect, power, cooling) are architecture-agnostic and therefore capture demand regardless of which silicon wins. **OpenAI** shipped a purpose-built cybersecurity model for defenders, continuing the vertical-specialization trend. On the infrastructure-commitment side, the previously announced OpenAI–Nvidia arrangement to build **10 GW** of AI data centers around a **$100 billion** strategic partnership remains the anchor datapoint for multi-year power demand.

The investable read-through is unchanged and reinforced: model-layer competition is intensifying and margins there are uncertain, while the physical layer has contracted, sold-out capacity. That is why this report's picks sit in the physical layer.

### 1. WDC — Western Digital Corporation
*Sub-sector: enterprise storage / nearline HDD*

- **Thesis:** Western Digital has **sold out 100% of its 2026 HDD capacity** under multiyear agreements extending toward 2028–2029, into a market where demand is growing ~40–50% annually against ~30–35% supply growth — and the business has quietly transformed into an AI-cloud infrastructure company, with **89% of revenue from hyperscaler AI cloud versus 5% consumer**. You are buying contracted, capacity-constrained volume with realized pricing power at a market multiple.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Forward P/E **~24.9–25.9** (sources: 24.93 as of Aug 6, 25.88 more recently) against a trailing multiple distorted by the earnings inflection. EV/EBITDA **~37.0** — note this reflects trailing EBITDA and overstates the forward burden given the guided earnings ramp; the forward P/E is the more honest number here. PEG is favorable on a 24% three-year revenue CAGR, though the deceleration in the out-years (net income forecast +5.7% next year versus a 50% tech-industry forecast) is the reason the market has not paid more.
- **Growth Profile:** Q3 2026 revenue **$3.34 billion, +46%** year-over-year; EPS **$9.26 versus $2.17** in the year-ago quarter. FY2026 estimates have moved up from $11.9B to **$12.4 billion** revenue and from $9.04 to **$11.89** EPS. Revenue is forecast to grow **24% per year** over the next three years versus 9.7% for US tech. Peer Seagate is running 47% gross margins on the same dynamic, and both grew EPS more than 100% last quarter.
- **Competitive Moat:** A structural duopoly in nearline HDD with multi-year capacity constraints. Areal-density leadership requires decades of accumulated process knowledge, and — decisively — new HDD capacity takes years to build, so the shortage cannot be competed away inside the investment horizon. Multiyear sold-out agreements convert that scarcity into contracted revenue visibility.
- **Key Risk:** This is a cyclical industry with a history of destroying capital at cycle peaks. Two specific threats: (a) the shortage resolves faster than the 2028 consensus if supply additions accelerate or AI storage intensity disappoints, and (b) QLC NAND/SSD substitution encroaches on nearline economics if NAND pricing breaks. The out-year earnings deceleration already embedded in estimates suggests the market shares this concern.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

### 2. NVT — nVent Electric plc
*Sub-sector: liquid cooling distribution & electrical connection/protection*

- **Thesis:** nVent sits at the exact chokepoint the AI buildout has moved to — liquid cooling distribution and intelligent power distribution — and the order book proves it: **organic orders up ~65%** in a recent quarter on large hyperscaler liquid-cooling programs, Q1 2026 organic sales **+34%**, record quarterly sales, orders, and backlog in Q2, a raised 2026 outlook, and **more than $2 billion of expected 2026 data-center sales**. It trades at roughly half the multiple of the premium cooling comparables.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Trailing P/E **26.9**, P/S **4.92**, EV/EBITDA **24.57**. Analyst fair value has been raised from $116.20 to **$145.21** on updated growth, margin, and terminal-multiple assumptions. Versus AAON at ~104x P/E and Powell at ~49x forward on comparable data center exposure, nVent is the value entry in the thermal/electrical complex — the PEG is defensible on 30%+ organic growth against a high-20s multiple.
- **Growth Profile:** 34% organic sales growth with orders growing roughly twice that rate — the order-to-sales spread is the leading indicator, and it is widening. Backlog is at a record. Capacity is being added ahead of demand: a **160,000 sq ft** Minnesota liquid-cooling facility opens in H1 2027, the third liquid-cooling expansion in three years and part of **400,000+ sq ft** added. The addressable market compounds at **18.2%** ($6B in 2026 → $27.1B by 2035).
- **Competitive Moat:** Liquid cooling distribution is a qualification-gated business — hyperscalers design nVent's manifolds, CDUs, and busway into rack architectures years ahead of deployment, and switching mid-program is impractical. The electrical connection and protection franchise supplies a broad installed base of utility and industrial customers, providing a non-AI earnings floor that the pure-play cooling names lack.
- **Key Risk:** Order growth of 65% against sales growth of 34% means expectations are now anchored to a very high bar; any hyperscaler capex digestion phase would hit orders first and visibly. Additionally, capacity is being committed against demand that is concentrated among a handful of customers — high-20s multiples do not survive a single large program deferral.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

### 3. SPXC — SPX Technologies, Inc.
*Sub-sector: engineered cooling towers & medium-voltage power transmission equipment*

- **Thesis:** SPX gets paid twice by the same buildout — evaporative cooling for data centers plus transformers and detection/measurement equipment for the grid upgrades that data centers require — and unlike its cooling peers it still trades at a defensible multiple with a **PEG of 1.66**. Data center cooling revenue is scaling from **$200 million in FY2025 to a projected $350 million in 2026**, a 75% increase off a base that is still small enough to compound.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Trailing P/E **45.4**, forward P/E **28.29** (P/E non-GAAP forward **24.36**), EV/EBITDA **24.40–24.58**, EV/FCF **44.96**, **PEG 1.66**. 2026 EPS estimates have been revised up to **$7.98** from $7.95. Consensus price target **$266.67**. The gap between the 45x trailing and ~24–28x forward multiple is the earnings inflection the market has only partly capitalized.
- **Growth Profile:** Data center cooling +75% year-over-year in 2026 on the segment level; total company EPS estimates drifting upward; the stock is up **43.6–49.8%** over the past 52 weeks, which is strong but materially less than the 140–450% moves in the optical complex — meaning less of the future has been pulled forward. Zacks Rank #2 (Buy).
- **Competitive Moat:** Cooling towers and medium-voltage transformers are long-lead-time, engineered-to-order, code- and utility-qualified products with entrenched aftermarket attachment. Transformer capacity in particular is globally scarce with multi-year lead times — a genuine supply-side moat that is not a function of AI sentiment. The Detection & Measurement segment adds regulated, non-cyclical revenue.
- **Key Risk:** Data center cooling is still a minority of consolidated revenue, so the AI thesis is diluted by slower-growing legacy segments — the stock can be right on thesis and mediocre on results. A 28x forward multiple on a company with meaningful industrial cyclicality also leaves limited margin for execution error, and the EV/FCF of ~45 indicates free cash conversion lags reported earnings.
- **Rating:** **Accumulate**

### 4. NRG — NRG Energy, Inc.
*Sub-sector: independent power generation & retail energy*

- **Thesis:** Power is the hardest physical constraint on the AI buildout, and NRG is the rare way to own it at a **forward P/E of roughly 17–18** — a discount to the equipment and REIT layers by a wide margin. The company signed a landmark **1.2 GW data center agreement**, grew Q2 adjusted EBITDA **34%** year-over-year, and management reaffirmed 2026 guidance while targeting **at least 14% adjusted EPS and free cash flow per share growth over the next five years**. A recent pullback on Texas weakness and capex concerns is the entry.
- **Valuation Metrics:** Forward P/E **17.18–18.02**. Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA **$1.2 billion** and free cash flow before growth **$1.025 billion**, both up year-over-year. Projected free cash flow of **$4.5 billion by 2030**, with 2026–2029 in a **$3.1–4.0 billion** range. Contracted FCF could reach approximately **$2.9 billion by 2033** from announced data center agreements plus the lead BYOP opportunity — roughly **95% of the 2026 FCFbG midpoint**, which is the single most important number in this thesis: it means the announced pipeline alone could nearly replicate today's entire free cash flow base with contracted, not merchant, cash flow.
- **Growth Profile:** Adjusted EBITDA +34% year-over-year, driven by the acquired LS Power portfolio, higher PJM capacity values, and Smart Home growth. The ≥14% five-year adjusted EPS and FCF/share growth target is management-committed and supported by buybacks against a growing FCF base. The LS Power acquisition brings natural gas to **75% of the portfolio** — dispatchable capacity, which is what data centers actually need.
- **Competitive Moat:** Dispatchable generation capacity in constrained markets (ERCOT, PJM) is close to unreplicable on a relevant timeline — interconnection queues and permitting are multi-year. The retail energy and Smart Home businesses provide a natural hedge to wholesale price volatility and a recurring-revenue overlay that pure merchant generators lack. Rising PJM capacity prices accrue directly to owners of existing capacity.
- **Key Risk:** Merchant power earnings remain weather- and commodity-exposed — the recent share weakness came precisely from Texas demand softness and capex concerns, and the Q2 report missed on earnings even while EBITDA grew. A capital-intensive growth program (funded partly through equity, per the upsized $2.3B offering as LS Power exited part of its stake) can dilute the per-share growth math if returns disappoint. **Disclosure:** this is the same broad sub-sector as Vistra (VST), picked on July 31 and on today's exclusion list; NRG is a distinct issuer with a materially different business mix (retail + Smart Home + newly acquired gas fleet versus VST's nuclear-weighted portfolio), but investors running this series as a portfolio should note the thematic adjacency.
- **Rating:** **Buy**

### 5. IRM — Iron Mountain Incorporated
*Sub-sector: digital infrastructure REIT & information management*

- **Thesis:** Iron Mountain offers data center growth rates comparable to the pure-play REITs at a materially lower multiple, funded by a legacy records-management annuity that no competitor can replicate. Data center revenue grew **39%** to **$263 million** in Q2 with **110 MW leased through July** against an original 100 MW full-year target management now expects to "meaningfully exceed" — while Digital Realty, growing core FFO at 8%, trades near **29x P/AFFO**.
- **Valuation Metrics:** 2026 guidance (raised): revenue **$7.94–8.01 billion**, adjusted EBITDA **$2.945–2.975 billion**, AFFO **$5.87–5.93 per share**. Against a share price implied by the current dividend (**$0.864 quarterly, $3.456 annualized**) at a **2.6–3.1% yield**, that puts the stock at roughly **19–22x AFFO** — a clear discount to DLR's ~29x P/AFFO on comparable or better growth. GuruFocus flags GF Value at $107.15 ("modestly overvalued") with a GF Score of 85/100; treat that as the bear-case anchor on price.
- **Growth Profile:** Q2 2026 revenue **$2.0 billion, +18.5%** year-over-year; adjusted EBITDA **$727 million, +15.7%**; AFFO **$433 million / $1.44 per share, +17%**. Data center revenue **+39%**, ALM (asset lifecycle management) revenue **+88% to $288 million** and approaching $1 billion annualized. Organic growth recently hit a 25-year high. Forward visibility: **325 MW** to be energized over the next 24 months toward **~1.4 GW** developable capacity, plus backlog supporting **$370 million of revenue growth beyond 2026**, with renewal pricing in the low teens.
- **Competitive Moat:** The records-management business is one of the highest-retention franchises in the market — physical storage contracts renew almost automatically because the cost and risk of moving archives exceeds the cost of storing them, producing decades-long customer duration and consistent pricing power. That cash flow funds data center development without the equity dilution pure-play REITs require, and ALM monetizes the same customer relationships in the opposite direction (decommissioning IT assets). Growth businesses now represent **35% of revenue**, so the mix shift is real, not aspirational.
- **Key Risk:** Leverage. This is a capital-intensive REIT building data centers into a market where power and construction constraints are the binding issue, and the 110 MW leased is a *signing* number — revenue depends on energization schedules that management does not fully control. The dividend yield at 2.59% sits **52% below its 10-year median of 5.41%**, meaning the security has already re-rated substantially from a records-storage income vehicle to a growth multiple; a growth disappointment would compress it against a still-elevated payout obligation.
- **Rating:** **Accumulate**

## Risk Factors

Ranked by severity for the week ahead:

**1. Strait of Hormuz / Iran escalation reversal — severe, moderate probability.** Roughly **20% of global oil supply** transits the Strait. The recent ~4% crude correction and the retreat from a three-week low were driven by diplomatic progress and Bessent's comments on restoring maritime transit — a fragile foundation. Escalation would trigger immediate severe repricing across energy and equities, and crucially it would arrive through the inflation channel while three FOMC members are already voting to hike. This is the one risk that can simultaneously break the earnings and the discount-rate legs of the bull case.

**2. Thursday PPI and Friday retail sales undoing the CPI relief — high probability, moderate severity.** Today's benign CPI is one datapoint built partly on falling energy prices. **PPI (Thursday)** is an early signal of producer-level inflation before it reaches consumers, and a hot print would revive the September hike trade that today's print just suppressed. **Retail sales (Friday)** is the perverse one: because the hawkish case rests on demand strength, a *strong* consumption number is an equity risk this week.

**3. AI infrastructure margin compression, not demand failure — moderate probability, high severity for the spotlight sector.** The tell is already visible. Cerebras guided gross margin to **36–38% from 47%** the prior quarter and fell ~10% after its previous report on exactly that issue despite strong revenue. Super Micro beat on earnings but **missed on revenue**. The market has been paying growth multiples for volume; the moment it starts underwriting margins, the 44–208x names in optical and cooling have very long distances to fall. This is the specific risk today's valuation screen was built to avoid.

**4. Financing-structure risk in the capex cycle — low near-term probability, very high severity.** Nvidia's reported **$500 billion financing alliance** with Wall Street institutions and Intel's **$15 billion equity offering** both indicate that ~$700B of annual data center spending has outgrown internally generated cash. Once the buildout is debt- and structured-finance-funded, a credit event or a rise in funding costs becomes a transmission channel into AI capex that did not exist when hyperscalers were self-funding. Watch spreads, not just orders.

**5. Semiconductor forecast dispersion and cycle-peak risk — moderate probability, moderate severity.** JPMorgan models **$1.68 trillion** in 2026 industry sales (+111%); WSTS models **$975 billion** (+26%). That is not a rounding difference — it is a disagreement about whether this is a super-cycle or a blow-off. Fifteen consecutive record months and +104% year-over-year growth are, historically, late-cycle readings. The August 9–10 sell-off, when Intel led chips lower on Iran and rate fears, showed how quickly the group de-rates on non-fundamental news.

## Bottom Line

Today's CPI bought the market time rather than a rate cut — with core at 2.5% and the Fed on hold at 3.50–3.75% with three hawkish dissenters, the September debate is about a hike, and this week's PPI and retail sales prints can undo the relief. The genuinely durable opportunity remains in the physical layer of the AI buildout, where roughly $700 billion of 2026 hyperscaler capex meets sold-out capacity, but the character of that trade has changed: optical, interconnect, and premium electrical equipment have repriced to 44–208x and no longer offer a margin of safety, while storage, moderate-multiple thermal and grid equipment, merchant power, and digital infrastructure REITs still do. Own the constraint, not the narrative — and at these multiples, insist on being paid for it.
